\title{\privoke: A Client-Side Privacy Firewall for LLM Prompts}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous Institution}\country{Anonymous}}


\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to hosted language models can disclose personal or confidential information before a provider processes them. We examine how local inspection, request enforcement, guarded model revision and operational reporting connect in one workflow. \privoke combines local inspection, ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK handling on supported requests (ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels), version-bound detector updates with a retention guard, and separate randomized telemetry. On 1,560 authored cases with provisional targets, updated packages improved agreement; 33 installed receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths. All matched full-model candidates improved aggregate agreement but failed the configured retention guard. Registered fresh detector-layer tests found that the shipped rework passed the 0.5-point BLOCK-recall margin (difference $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$) while its union-recall result stayed inconclusive and a 1.05-point loss against the original detectors did not meet the promotion rule. Controlled telemetry fixtures verified delivery and accounting; finite-arithmetic loss slightly exceeded nominal budgets and simulated estimation error was substantial. The findings support bounded mechanism claims, not validated contextual judgments, universal interception, an accepted update, useful live monitoring or user benefit. End-to-end evasion by fixed transforms succeeded on 2.99\% of protected pairs (6.52\% counting fail-closed BLOCKs as misses; a floor, not an adaptive attacker) while 65.5\% of clean control prompts are already non-ALLOW; update attacks were rejected except where mechanisms are missing (no cumulative bound, no publisher authentication or credentials); masking a warned prompt prevented 0.545 of sensitive transmissions against 0.413 (regex and NER layers, development data).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Hosted large language models (LLMs) support writing, coding and analysis, but prompts may include another person's details or confidential material~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing,SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Provider controls on retention act after the initial transfer. Running the application model locally is one alternative, but changes the chosen service and its computational demands~\cite{Llama2023}. We study the intervening point: inspecting selected requests on the user's device before handoff.

The privacy question is contextual: the same information may be appropriate or inappropriate to disclose depending on whose information it is, the audience and purpose~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}. Recognition alone cannot establish authorization. Nor does a warning necessarily prevent transfer. PriVoke combines rules, named entity recognition (NER) and semantic classification to represent sensitivity, audience and overlapping categories, then selects ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. ALLOW and WARN send the original body; BLOCK cancels a recognized request. WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. Because an early rule BLOCK ends the detector cascade, both detector order and request enforcement affect observed behavior. Work on reproducing sensitive prompt information in chatbot outputs concerns a later point in the lifecycle~\cite{priyanshu2023chatbots}; pre-transmission inspection cannot reverse that disclosure.

The system also separates detector maintenance from reporting. A server runtime fits candidates from supplied labelled examples, and a version-bound retention guard may reject a candidate before publication. This addresses the risk that aggregate gains conceal regressions on previously correct cases~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, but only for the guard's declared examples and criteria. A separate client randomizes coarse telemetry under a local budget ledger; neither prompts nor telemetry train the detector. The contribution is the evaluation of these connected but distinct mechanisms in one workflow, not a new detector or privacy primitive.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt]
\item An implemented client path connecting local layered inspection to explicit request handling on supported browser requests, specified as a workflow (Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow}) and a decision policy.
\item A version-bound update workflow with a casewise retention guard and a formal acceptance predicate (Definition~\ref{def:accept}), distinguished from the read-only standard V3 path and evaluated through matched candidates that improved aggregate agreement but did not qualify for publication.
\item A separate randomized telemetry path evaluated for finite event-content privacy, budget accounting, delivery and estimation error.
\item Explicit security goals and threats linked to the experiments that test them, with unmet properties shown as such, and an empirical account of trade-offs across prediction, enforcement, update eligibility and reporting that does not treat provisional targets as contextual truth.
\end{enumerate}
The implementation, evaluation recipes and research records are available at an anonymous repository (link withheld for review).

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\textbf{RQ1}: does \privoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively; \textbf{RQ2}: can it update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior; and \textbf{RQ3}: what systems and privacy overhead does it introduce? RQ1 separates agreement with declared targets from observed transmission; RQ2 asks whether aggregate gains satisfy a casewise retention rule; RQ3 separates resident cost and event-content privacy from estimation error and selection. These distinctions define what the evidence can support and where independent contextual judgment remains absent.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local intervention and contextual decisions.} Casper combines browser-side rules, entity recognition and topic analysis, replacing identifiers and requiring acknowledgement for topic warnings~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}. Hide and Seek (HaS) hides entities before cloud inference and recovers them in responses, ProSan learns selective word replacement from task-importance and privacy proxies~\cite{HaS2024,ProSan2024}, and PromptShield classifies enterprise prompts by role and policy~\cite{PromptShield2025}. These works address sanitization, task continuation or policy classification; \privoke instead measures a request contract in which supported hooks act automatically and WARN forwards unchanged content without acknowledgement.

\textit{Retaining behavior during model revision.} Negative-flip research measures when an update turns a previously correct prediction into an error; Yan et al. propose positive-congruent training for image classifiers~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. This motivates paired gains and losses but not privacy-policy correctness; \privoke applies the distinction to an update decision, rejecting candidates under a configured guard limited to its declared cases and harm rule.

\textit{Privacy of operational reporting.} RAPPOR applies randomized response to client-side statistics and addresses the privacy cost of repeated reporting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}; \privoke combines categorical event reports with a local budget ledger. Event-content protection does not conceal participation or network metadata, and accurate marginals would not by themselves establish representative monitoring.

\section{Threat Model and Security Goals}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
\privoke addresses prompt text in recognized browser requests before handoff to an external language-model provider. Protected assets are the request text, local detector and model state, update artifacts and credentials, and randomized telemetry with its budget ledger. The user, enabled extension, page hook, local runtime and workstation are trusted to inspect and to preserve local state; compromised local components are out of scope and could expose prompts or alter decisions and accounting. The provider and network observer are not trusted: the provider receives the original prompt after ALLOW or WARN, and an observer may see report presence and transport metadata that event-content randomization does not hide.

We consider two active adversaries. A \emph{prompt author} modifies text to evade detection. An \emph{update sender} submits stale, replayed, duplicate, malformed or unauthorized candidates, or presents an unauthorized service identity. The implementation has normalization and detector layers for prompt variation, and parent-version, tensor-bound, serialized-publication and receipt checks for updates (Section~\ref{sec:updates}); the inspected proxy requires client certificates on model-streaming and telemetry routes. Section~\ref{sec:security_eval} reports negative tests of these controls: they hold where a mechanism exists, but authentication and authorization, particularly for update submission, are not established.

The boundary covers recognized prompt-bearing POST requests sent through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} (XHR). Workers, attachments, unsupported endpoints and other transports are outside measured coverage, users can disable protection, and the optional hosted-inference configuration is excluded. Table~\ref{tab:threat_defense} (Appendix~\ref{app:policy}) maps each threat to its defense and experiment. Each goal in Table~\ref{tab:security_goals} is therefore conditional, and none implies correct contextual decisions or comprehensive protection. Unmet properties are reported as unmet rather than omitted.

\section{PriVoke Design}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
\privoke has three runtime paths (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): local inspection and enforcement in the browser and client runtime, a legacy server-side update path, and a separate randomized telemetry channel; standard V3 packages are read-only. The subsections below specify each path and Section~\ref{sec:properties} states the properties each is designed to satisfy.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Enforcement}
\label{sec:enforcement}
\textit{Request enforcement (G1).} Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} gives the client workflow. When the active page hook recognizes a supported request and the runtime returns BLOCK, the hook cancels it before the page's native sender hands it off. ALLOW and WARN release the original body, and WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. The installed probes (Section~\ref{sec:installed_probes}) test this contract on selected paths.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\privoke request workflow. Layers are ordered rules $<$ NER $<$ semantic; $\max$ uses ALLOW $<$ WARN $<$ BLOCK.}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require intercepted request $r$ with prompt text $x$; requested layers $\Lambda$
\State \textbf{if} protection is disabled or $r$ is unsupported \textbf{then return} forward $r$
\State $x_c\gets\textsc{Normalize}(x)$ with offsets; $x_\ell\gets\textsc{Lowercase}(x_c)$
\State $a\gets\text{ALLOW}$;\; $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{false}$
\If{rules $\in\Lambda$}
  \State $a\gets\textsc{Rules}(x_c)$
  \If{$a=\text{BLOCK}$} \textbf{goto} line~\ref{alg:enforce} \EndIf \Comment{skip NER and semantics}
\EndIf
\ForAll{$\ell\in\Lambda\setminus\{\text{rules}\}$ \textbf{concurrently}}
  \State $F\gets\ell(x_c)$ for NER; $\ell(x_\ell)$ for semantic
  \If{$\ell$ failed} $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{true}$
  \Else{} $a\gets\max\bigl(a,\max_{f\in F}\pi(f)\bigr)$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$\mathit{failed}\wedge a=\text{ALLOW}$} $a\gets\text{BLOCK}$ \EndIf
\State \textbf{enforce:}\label{alg:enforce} \textbf{if} $a=\text{BLOCK}$ cancel $r$ before transmission
\State \textbf{else} release the original body (WARN first shows a notice, without waiting)
\State offer a coarse event to the separate telemetry channel (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The runtime normalizes text while retaining offsets. Regex and NER receive case-preserved text; semantic models receive lowercased normalized text. Rules run first and an early rule BLOCK ends the cascade; otherwise the strongest action is retained. A failed requested layer turns an otherwise-ALLOW result into BLOCK. If the parameter stream is lost, however, full mode can continue with rules and NER and forward without semantic inspection. We therefore distinguish the returned action, executed layers and receiver observation.

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic model predicts sensitivity $s\in\{\mathrm{S0},\dots,\mathrm{S3}\}$ (S0 no identified risk; S3 highly sensitive information, identifiers or secrets), audience (visibility) $v\in\{\mathrm{P0},\dots,\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{PU}\}$ (public, semi-public, restricted/group-private/personal-private, unspecified) and a category set $C$ (health, politics, religion, criminal, financial, sexual, child, location, identity and third-party information). With $c$ the finding confidence and $I=C\cap\{\mathrm{IDENTITY},\mathrm{LOCATION}\}$, the policy $\pi$ maps a finding to an action:
\begin{equation}
\pi(f)=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{WARN} & c<0.5\wedge\bigl(s=\mathrm{S3}\vee(s=\mathrm{S2}\wedge I\neq\varnothing)\bigr)\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & c<0.5\ \text{otherwise}\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & s=\mathrm{S0}\wedge C=\varnothing\\
\mathrm{BLOCK} & s=\mathrm{S3}\\
\mathrm{WARN} & s=\mathrm{S2}\ \vee\ |I|=2\ \vee\ (I\neq\varnothing\wedge v\in\{\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4}\})\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with rows evaluated in order and a missing confidence treated as $c\ge0.5$. The score $c$ is not calibrated joint confidence or evidence of authorization. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives the order and its observable consequences. The pretrained V3 packages use BERT encoders~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}; the installed default is V3 balanced.

\label{sec:detector_precision_design}
\textit{Detector precision controls.} Rules and NER precede semantic classification, and a rule BLOCK ends the cascade. The precision revision therefore targets unnecessary early findings while retaining the possibility of missed detections. The policy mapping and semantic models are unchanged.

\subsection{Guarded Update Lifecycle}
\label{sec:updates}
\textit{Standard V3 versus the legacy update path.} Two model paths coexist and must not be conflated (Table~\ref{tab:paths}). The \emph{standard V3 path} serves the installed default: pinned, read-only pretrained packages streamed as hash-verified assets, with no gradient route; the streaming service's default alias is configuration-owned, and a legacy publication cannot replace it. The \emph{legacy update path} applies version-bound gradient updates to a mutable V1 artifact; it is opt-in (an explicit training profile), the updater and fuzzer services exist only there, and standard V3 identifiers are rejected by it. All guarded-update evidence in this paper concerns the legacy mechanism and controlled continuation studies; the paper reports installed V3 inference, not a deployed V3 learning loop.

In the legacy workflow, the updater requests labelled batches and held-out guard cases; the server runtime computes a bounded change against its cached base. Training is centralized on supplied examples, not user gradients or federated data. A candidate $u=(\mathit{src},\mathit{mid},v_b,G,\mu)$ carries a source, model identity, base version $v_b$, named gradients $G=\{(n_i,\sigma_i,w_i)\}$ with shape $\sigma_i$ and values $w_i$, and metadata $\mu$. Let $A$ be the active artifact with version $v_A$, parameters $P$ and configured bounds: $B$ (default 1.0) per value, $L$ values per tensor (4,096; larger for the full-encoder strategy) and $T=65{,}536$ values in total.

\begin{definition}[Acceptance of a candidate]\label{def:accept}
Candidate $u$ replaces its parent $A$ if and only if all of the following hold, where (a)--(c) are checked by the update service, re-evaluated on the artifact reloaded under the writer lock, and (d) is checked by the trainer before submission:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,label=(\alph*),itemsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Valid.} $\mathit{mid}$ equals the service's model and $A$'s; $\mathit{src}$ and $v_b$ are non-empty; $1\le|G|\le256$, names are distinct, $\sum_i|w_i|\le T$ and $|\mu|\le64$; for every $i$, $\sigma_i$ is positive with $\prod\sigma_i=|w_i|\le L$, every value is finite with $|x|\le B$, and $n_i$ names a trainable parameter of $A$ with shape $\sigma_i$; and $A$'s architecture admits gradient updates (scratch-presence and pretrained-context artifacts allow only offline release fitting).
\item \emph{Fresh.} $v_b=v_A$ (compare-and-swap on the exact parent).
\item \emph{Not a conflicting retry.} Let $\kappa=H(\mathit{src},\mathit{request\_source},\mathit{request\_id})$. If a receipt for $\kappa$ exists, $u$ is not re-applied: an identical payload digest returns the recorded outcome and a different digest is rejected. Updates without a request identifier create no receipt.
\item \emph{Guarded.} With held-out exact match $e$, sensitive recall $r$, clean specificity $p$, minimum exact match $\theta$ and target-capped regression rate $\rho$ (a row regresses when its severity or action falls below the lower of its target and the base prediction), $e_{\mathrm{train}}>\theta\wedge e_u\ge e_A\wedge r_u\ge r_A\wedge p_u\ge p_A\wedge\rho_u=0$. Retention policies additionally need zero per-row ORIGINAL violations; the sixteen-row window guard needs non-decreasing counts and $\rho=0$, with its selected-checkpoint evidence bound to the artifact.
\end{enumerate}
On acceptance, $A'=A\oplus u$ has version \texttt{release+train.}$(k{+}1)$ and a recomputed checksum, is written to a temporary file, flushed and atomically renamed over $A$, and its receipt is committed; a best-effort audit record follows. Otherwise $A$ is unchanged.
\end{definition}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:update} gives the control flow. The service recovers a receipt left by an interrupted commit before serving the next update, so a crash between rename and receipt does not allow a second application. Rejection at a later stage does not undo an earlier head stage already accepted. Clients verify delivered artifacts before refreshing.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Guarded update of the legacy path (Definition~\ref{def:accept}).}
\label{alg:update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require candidate $u$ with base version $v_b$, request identifier, gradients $G$
\State \textbf{trainer:} fit $u$ on supplied examples; \textbf{if not} Guarded$(u)$ \textbf{then} discard $u$
\State submit $u$ to the update service; \textbf{if not} Valid$(u,A)$ \textbf{then reject}
\State \textbf{lock} writer;\; recover any receipt left by an interrupted commit
\State reload $A$; revalidate $u$ against it (a replay of a committed payload passes this step)
\State \textbf{if} receipt for $\kappa(u)$ exists \textbf{then} \textbf{return} recorded outcome \textbf{or reject} (digest differs)
\State \textbf{if} $v_b\ne v_A$ \textbf{then reject} (stale); $A'\gets A\oplus u$; new version and checksum
\State write $A'$ to a temporary file; flush; \textbf{atomically rename} over $A$; commit receipt
\State \textbf{unlock}; \textbf{return} $A'$ version; clients verify and refresh later
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The checks constrain stale, duplicate and malformed updates but do not authenticate the sender, bound the aggregate change beyond the per-value limit $B$, or guarantee behavioral safety. Attack outcomes against the live service are in Section~\ref{sec:eval_update}. In the matched RQ2 study, all full-model candidates failed the configured guard and none was published.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
The official client emits five coarse randomized fields: action, risk bucket, precedence-selected category, model-version bucket and four-hour time-of-day bin; the schema omits the prompt, identifiers, exact confidence and per-layer findings. Test code checks the field set and aggregate storage, and controlled operation tests delivery and accounting, not absence of prompt text from every local log, memory surface or channel. The collector estimates lifetime marginals but lacks denominators and time-window queries for latency or error rates, and telemetry provides no training labels.

\textit{Event-content privacy (G4).} Randomized response limits how much the distribution of a released tuple can change between possible event contents~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}, for a fixed reporting opportunity and public configuration; it does not randomize whether a report exists. The event budget $\varepsilon$ is divided equally, $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$; for a domain of size $K_i$ the true value has reporting probability $p_i$ and each alternative $q_i$:
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}.
\end{equation}
Under independent ideal sampling, composition multiplies the field likelihood ratios to bound the event ratio by $e^\varepsilon$~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}; that is the nominal mechanism, not an exact guarantee for the finite implementation. The client compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw, $J/2^{53}$, with a binary64 threshold $\widetilde p_i$ and chooses alternatives by rejection sampling, so its finite probabilities and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite audit (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_results}) found $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ slightly above nominal $\varepsilon$ for the audited host settings, so the result is an implementation-specific bound under independent uniform randomness, not exact nominal epsilon across builds. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves nominal budget before packet construction (default one per event and eight per Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day) without refund for failed construction or delivery; it depends on retained state and a trusted clock and is not user-level accounting across devices or days. Report presence, accepted counts, timing, source IP, contribution selection and other network metadata remain observable or outside the content guarantee, and the collector's inversion and clipping do not by themselves establish useful or representative monitoring.

\subsection{Security Properties}
\label{sec:properties}
Let $r$ be a recognized supported request, $a(r)$ the action of Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow}, $\mathrm{sent}(r)$ the event that its body reaches the page's native sender, $A$ the active artifact, $A'$ the active artifact after candidate $u$ is submitted, and $\mathcal M$ the telemetry mechanism. The properties hold under the trust assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:threat_model}, and Table~\ref{tab:security_goals} links each to its test.

\begin{property}[Enforcement, G1]\label{prop:g1}
$a(r)=\mathrm{BLOCK}\Rightarrow\neg\mathrm{sent}(r)$ on the hooked \texttt{fetch}/XHR path; ALLOW and WARN release the original body (WARN+MASK, off by default, a rewritten body).
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Update non-replacement, G2]\label{prop:g2}
$\neg\mathrm{Accept}(u,A)\Rightarrow A'=A$ at the guarded publication stage.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Telemetry schema, G3]\label{prop:g3}
Every official telemetry packet is a tuple $(x_1,\dots,x_5)\in\prod_{i=1}^{5}\mathcal D_i$ of coarse fields from fixed finite domains, so it contains no raw prompt text.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Event-content LDP, G4]\label{prop:g4}
For events $e,e'$ admitted at one reporting opportunity under a public configuration and every output $y$, $\Pr[\mathcal M(e)=y]\le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal M(e')=y]$ for the ideal mechanism, and with $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ for the audited implementation, under independent uniform OS randomness; report presence, selection, timing and metadata are outside the property.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Evasion resilience, G5]\label{prop:g5}
For a declared transform set $T$, the end-to-end $\mathrm{ASR}_{\mid}(\tau)$ of Section~\ref{sec:metrics} is small for every $\tau\in T$; the property is estimated, not guaranteed.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Update admission, G6]\label{prop:g6}
$\mathrm{Accept}(u,A)$ holds exactly when conditions (a)--(d) of Definition~\ref{def:accept} hold; only then is $A'\ne A$.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Authenticated service, G7]\label{prop:g7}
A request $q$ to the model, update or telemetry service is served or applied only if $\mathrm{principal}(q)\in\mathrm{Authorized}$.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Intervention effect, G8]\label{prop:g8}
An intervention $I$ on WARN prompts reduces sensitive transmission when $\mathrm{prev}(I)>\mathrm{prev}(\text{WARN})$, where $\mathrm{prev}$ is the fraction of sensitive prompts not sent with an intact sensitive span.
\end{property}

\textit{Design rationale.} Rules run first and a failed requested layer fails closed, so a cheap early decision cannot be weakened and unavailable analysis cannot silently allow; candidates are bound to their exact parent so a rejected or stale candidate cannot overwrite the active model; and telemetry is a separate coarse channel, so neither prompts nor reports train the detector.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
The browser extension injects page hooks for supported \texttt{fetch} and XHR POST requests and routes extracted text through a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to the local Python runtime; native messaging launches the supervisor, and the native-host manifest restricts launches to the registered extension identity. The classifier packages are the verified, pretrained V3 balanced default (efficient and quality tiers and older V1 profiles are selectable), and standard V3 inference is read-only. The official reporter randomizes five coarse fields, and the telemetry collector stores lifetime marginals. The legacy updater, fuzzer and parameter service implement candidate fitting, validation, version-bound publication and artifact refresh. These are distinct runtime paths: neither prompt inspection nor telemetry supplies detector-training labels.

\subsection{Baselines and Comparison Protocols}
The RQ1 comparison processes the same cases through the product handler with rules and policy fixed. Historical and updated packages differ in tokenizer window, threshold, capacity and training history, so results compare complete packages rather than isolate architecture or pretraining. The revised-detector rerun holds the cases, packages and policy constant. RQ2 matches the starting weights and ordered training exposures for head-only and full-model continuation, then compares aggregate assessment with a separate sixteen-case guard mapping that passes a candidate only with zero action harms and more joint-correct guard cases than the base (Appendix~\ref{app:retention}). The assessment reuses 1,080 provisional cases; hyperparameters and artifact identity are in Appendix~\ref{app:retention}.

\textit{Detector precision protocol.} This study explicitly requested regex and NER, excluding semantics and product actions, and compares the pre-change configuration with the first revision on design, development, synthetic and previously opened endpoint data; post-review and one-shot status are disclosed with the results. \subsection{Fresh Held-Out Protocol}
\label{sec:heldout_protocol}
Registered tests sampled fresh PIIMB sentences~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026} from source groups absent from development, training, validation and earlier studies. Data, source trees, scripts, hypotheses and margins were frozen before scoring; every call requested and verified [2,3]. Registration v2 compared N with O on F1 (16,446 sentences: 10,000 positive, 6,446 negative), F3 (2,000 long positive documents) and F2 (6,000 unlabelled natural prompts). After its recall gate failed, the owner reworked the rules. Registration v3 compared K on F4, a new set of 8,646 positive sentences from 5,745 source documents. F4 is positive-only; specificity checks reuse F1 negatives previously seen during rework and are not fresh confirmation.

The registered non-inferiority margins were 1.0 point for union recall, 0.5 for BLOCK recall, and the stated specificity guards. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample source groups (10,000 replicates, seed 20261024), and a claim passes only if its bound lies beyond the margin; post-hoc analyses are exploratory, and F2 results are flag rates because its prompts are unlabelled and unadjudicated.

\subsection{Adversarial Transformations}
\label{sec:adv_design}
The seed set is all 8,646 labelled-sensitive F4 rows (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_protocol}). This is a secondary, non-registered second use of F4: its registered use (H7, H8) is complete, the rows are disjoint from every set used to design the default-off robustness flags and from K's design, and no detector changed, but it is not a fresh confirmation. Twenty fixed, non-adaptive transforms in eight families (Unicode substitution, inserted spaces, spelling, template paraphrase, indirect reference, encoded identifiers, long-context placement, six-language translation) act on the annotated identifier spans; the frozen set of 169,371 applicable pairs was committed before scoring. The in-process runtime requested layers [2,3,4] (regex first, executed layers asserted) in two paired arms: shipped defaults and the four default-off robustness flags. The intended action is the action on the untransformed prompt, and ASR (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}) is primary at the action level, where the runtime's fail-closed BLOCK on a layer error counts as protection; the detection level counts such a BLOCK as a miss.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
For case $j$ with target sensitivity $s_j$, audience $v_j$, category set $C_j$ and allowed-action set $\mathcal A_j$, and prediction $(\hat s_j,\hat v_j,\hat C_j,\hat a_j)$, label, action and joint agreement average
\begin{equation}
L_j=\mathbf1\{\hat s_j=s_j,\hat v_j=v_j,\hat C_j=C_j\},\ \
A_j=\mathbf1\{\hat a_j\in\mathcal A_j\},\ \
J_j=L_jA_j .
\end{equation}
A case is binary-positive when $\hat s_j\ne\mathrm{S0}$ or $\hat C_j\neq\varnothing$; recall is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ and specificity $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$ over targets that are provisional labels on the contextual workload and source annotation presence on the final endpoint. For a paired correctness indicator $Z$, gains $G$ and losses $\Lambda$ count cases with $Z^{\rm old}=0,Z^{\rm new}=1$ and the reverse; the net change is $(G-\Lambda)/N$, always reported with both counts.

\textit{Attack success rate.} For a frozen labelled sensitive set $D$ and a prompt transformation $\tau$, let $d(x)=1$ when the evaluated pipeline's action on $x$ is WARN or BLOCK. A prompt \emph{evades} when it was detected before the transform and is missed after it. The conditional and unconditional rates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E(\tau)&=\{x\in D:d(x)=1,\ d(\tau(x))=0\},\\
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mid}(\tau)&=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|\{x\in D:d(x)=1\}|},\qquad
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm u}(\tau)=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|D|},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
each reported with a Wilson interval for $k$ successes in $n$ trials, $\bigl[\hat p+\tfrac{z^2}{2n}\pm z\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}+\tfrac{z^2}{4n^2}}\,\bigr]\big/\bigl(1+\tfrac{z^2}{n}\bigr)$ with $\hat p=k/n$ and $z=1.96$. Baseline detection $|\{d(x)=1\}|/|D|$ on untransformed prompts accompanies every rate. This is a product end-to-end measure and is never pooled with LLM-only results; its results are in Section~\ref{sec:eval_evasion}.

\subsection{Environments and Operational Protocols}
Thirty-three installed Linux Chromium probes exercised the extension, native installer, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector against a local HTTPS receiver that checked original-body sends, BLOCK outcomes and late arrivals; these synthetic observations do not establish provider authentication or cross-platform compatibility. Historical timing covered three synthetic actions over fetch and XHR (twelve profiles, 360 rules/NER decisions). V3 timing used two fresh profiles per transport, a fixed benign prompt, four detector arms in reversed order, 80 warmups and 480 timed requests; its off arm retains extension infrastructure, so within-profile median differences estimate resident activation, not total or cold-start cost (Torch 2.11 CPU, four threads, Python 3.12.3, Chromium 153.0.8010.12).

The telemetry audit evaluated exact finite kernels on fingerprinted Windows CPython 3.11.9 for budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 under independent uniform OS bits, using outward 100-digit arithmetic. Simulation covered 180 prespecified cells (four contributor counts, five field domains, three histogram types) with 10,000 repetitions per cell (seed 20261014; NumPy 1.26.4/PCG64), and separate controlled fixtures tested the reporter, ledger and collector without a detector or real users. Report-call timing excludes background delivery. These studies have distinct units and are not a user study.

\textit{Artifact availability.} The code, evaluation recipes and dated research records are available at an anonymous repository (link withheld for review). They identify configurations through source, model and data fingerprints and environment receipts, and distinguish product and ablation dependencies, including historical uncommitted sources, from matched continuation, final semantics, historical and standard-V3 client captures, telemetry, and the detector-precision unit (records \path{detector-precision-20261024.md} and \path{detector-precision-followup-20261024.md}). Some pinned checkpoints and raw run artifacts remain local and ignored, so exact reruns need more than a Git checkout, and public distribution of those assets has not been established.

\subsection{Statistical Procedure}
Proportions carry Wilson 95\% intervals; paired outcomes use exact McNemar tests on discordant rows; cluster bootstraps resample whole source or scenario groups (2,000 to 10,000 replicates with recorded seeds). No multiplicity correction is applied, exploratory analyses are labelled as such, and intervals over repeated or correlated observations are noted where they are optimistic.

The fixed-package ablation examines this explanation (Table~\ref{tab:targeted_ablation}, Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}). \subsubsection{Agreement on a Separate Annotation Target}
\label{sec:final_annotation}
The final endpoint asked whether the packages distinguished positive from negative rows under a different annotation target. All 2,988 semantic-only calls completed without errors and verified exclusive execution of the requested layer. Updated recall reached 80.51--93.64\%, but specificity remained at only 13.36--21.76\% (Figure~\ref{fig:rq1}b; confusion counts in Table~\ref{tab:final_presence}). Efficient and balanced lost specificity relative to their historical versions, while quality's small increase remained uncertain. Paired net-accuracy gain ranges from frozen source-group resampling were nevertheless positive: 7.41--17.37, 5.35--15.03 and 1.02--10.71 percentage points, respectively. Recognizing additional positives improved average accuracy despite poor discrimination of annotation-negative rows. This endpoint complements rather than reproduces the contextual comparison: it assesses source annotation presence through semantics alone, whereas the product comparison assesses provisional labels and actions through a cascade, so the difference is not a controlled estimate of distribution shift or detector contribution. Neither endpoint independently establishes whether disclosure is authorized.

\subsubsection{Detector-Layer Precision Revision}
\label{sec:detector_precision}
The precision revision of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision_design} targets unnecessary early findings at the detector layers; Table~\ref{tab:detprec} (Appendix~\ref{app:detprec}) compares the pre-change and installed configurations on the splits of Section~\ref{sec:detprec_protocol}. These are detector-layer measurements, not comparable with the product or semantic-only results. On the previously opened 498-row endpoint, regex specificity rose from 0.985 to 0.996 while recall fell from 0.653 to 0.640; neither paired change was significant (McNemar $p=0.25$), and the regex/NER union showed no detectable change, which does not establish equivalence. Synthetic-set specificity gains were larger but came from design material. \subsubsection{Full-Product Rerun with the Revised Detectors}
\label{sec:product_rerun}
The rerun of the 1,560 cases and six packages with the revised detectors (omitting the student-identifier rule and K) completed 40,560 calls without errors, and the clean pre-change export reproduced the earlier results exactly. Over-restrictions fell in every package: the 139 shared unnecessary restrictions fell to 108 (112 in the union; 103 of the original cases remain). V3 balanced allowed-action agreement rose by 29 cases (36 improved, 7 regressed; $p<0.001$) with no new binary misses in any V3 tier. Removing rules still traded restriction for protection (103 actions corrected, 18 lost; net 85; $p<10^{-14}$). Revised NER findings changed 48 identity-name cases; without NER 1,526 of 1,560 outputs matched the full product, and removal improved 7 actions and 25 joint outcomes with no regression. These are repeated measurements on provisional cases, not fresh confirmation, and K is not evaluated in the installed full-product study (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).

\subsubsection{Registered Fresh Held-Out Evaluation}
\label{sec:heldout}
The preceding detector-layer results were consumed or post-review. Registered scoring on new sentences (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_protocol}) tested the first revision N and then the reworked K against the original detectors O. Table~\ref{tab:heldout} (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}) gives the registered cells and the context cells on the same rows; the key cells are quoted below. N increased F1 regex and union specificity, but union recall fell 1.37 points and failed its registered non-inferiority test; BLOCK recall fell 1.90 points. K recovered some of N's loss, but its 1.05-point union-recall loss against O left H7 inconclusive, so it did not pass the registered promotion rule. For BLOCK recall, K passed H8's 0.5-percentage-point non-inferiority margin: the estimated K$-$O difference was +0.01 points (95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$). This does not establish equal BLOCK recall. \subsubsection{Supplementary Detector Checks}
\label{sec:supplementary}
The supplementary checks did not change the shipped configuration: larger NER models were mixed and source-dependent, GLiNER and amplifier-only policies established no union benefit, and seeded transformations exposed digit and zero-width evasion that default-off canonicalizations reversed only where they applied (Section~\ref{sec:eval_evasion} reports the end-to-end rate). \subsubsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
\label{sec:installed_probes}
Across thirty-three correctness probes, the installed-browser capture connected decisions to eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes. Correlated runtime and receiver records, including late-arrival checks, established these outcomes without a decision broker. Healthy full-pipeline and sole-semantic probes also verified execution, linking request disposition to the detector path actually exercised.

Fault probes separated keeping a request path available from preserving its protection. When the parameter stream was interrupted, semantic-only requests were blocked, but configured full-mode fallback ran rules and NER and forwarded the request, maintaining reduced analysis without equivalent semantic protection; restarting restored full execution, and disabling protection or all layers forwarded by design. These observations were kept separate from nine earlier failed captures.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke improved agreement with provisional contextual policy and enforced its declared request contract on the tested installed paths. Action errors, unchanged-content forwarding under WARN and weak specificity on the separate annotation-presence task constrain the broader effectiveness claim. A later detector-layer revision targets the rule and NER false detections behind part of that restriction. A full-product rerun measured the first revision's effect on actions, but the shipped rework was evaluated only at the detector layers, where registered fresh tests found a real union-recall cost against the original detectors alongside a specificity gain. The results do not yet establish independent contextual accuracy, provider compatibility, prevention for every sensitive prompt or practical usability.

Padding with 800 characters of benign filler inside the 512-token window let 15.05\% of protected prompts reach ALLOW and lowered the clean non-ALLOW rate from 65.5\% to 16.0\%: the semantic decision depends on length as well as the identifier. These rates are a floor, not an estimate of a capable attacker: the transforms are fixed and non-adaptive, the paraphrase lexicon is small and no language model generated text, so an adaptive or LLM-generated attacker is future work. Pair intervals are optimistic, 347 labelled-sensitive rows are unprotected before any transform, and the runtime was called in process, not through the installed client.

\subsection{RQ2: Guarded Updates and Attack Resistance}
\label{sec:adaptation_results}
Candidate eligibility follows the acceptance predicate of Definition~\ref{def:accept}. RQ1 packages differ in several respects, so RQ2 instead matches the starting model and training exposure and asks whether full-model continuation improves agreement over head-only fitting and whether its candidate satisfies a separate retention rule.

\subsubsection{Matched Continuation and Guard Decisions}
All six 64-step fits completed with finite parameters. HEADS preserved the encoder tensors, while FULL changed 39 and produced nonzero gradients in later steps. The six candidates and unchanged base completed 7,560 error-free native in-process semantic assessments, each covering the same 1,080 situations. These repeated assessments provide neither additional independent cases nor measurements of deployed network execution.

Full-model continuation improved joint and allowed-action agreement in each seed (Figure~\ref{fig:rq2}; Table~\ref{tab:matched_continuation}, Appendix~\ref{app:retention}). For seeds 42/43/44, joint gains/losses were 79/14, 69/26 and 90/20; action gains/losses were 123/38, 124/41 and 110/19. Under-enforcement distance increased on 33/37/15 cases and over-enforcement distance on 17/13/17; these overlapping measures cannot be summed as distinct harms. Aggregate gains therefore coexisted with lost protection on the reused assessment set.

\subsubsection{Guarded-Update Attacks (G2, G6)}
\label{sec:eval_update}
Ninety-one rows attack the live legacy update service (a host process on loopback over a private copy of the mutable artifact) and the streaming service, snapshotting the active artifact and what the streaming service serves before and after each attack (``unchanged'' means byte-identical). Inputs and state are synthetic; this is a negative-test observation, not a penetration test. A rejected attack meets its property only if the model was unchanged, replays and duplicates must apply at most once, and a missing mechanism is unmet whatever the attack did. The standard V3 path has no update route to attack: a legacy update for a V3 model id and an update call to the streaming port were rejected, and its release store was unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:updsec}, Appendix~\ref{app:secdetail}).

All three stale submissions were rejected with the model unchanged. A byte-identical replay returned the original version without a second application, while the same payload without a request identifier, under another source, or with a reused identifier and different payload was rejected; eight concurrent identical submissions applied once. All 23 malformed-tensor, seven NaN or Inf and five wrong-shape attacks were rejected, including a same-size transposed shape and a two-tensor update with one wrong tensor, which applied nothing. Interrupted updates behaved safely: a 1 ms deadline then retry applied once; ten hard kills of the updater left a valid artifact each time with exactly one application on retry; a stray temporary file did not block the next update; and a truncated artifact made the updater unhealthy and the streaming service unavailable (fail closed).

Two properties are unmet. The magnitude bound is per coordinate only: 25 successive maximum-magnitude updates were all accepted (cumulative per-coordinate drift 26), as was one update moving 41,876 coordinates by 1.0 (L2 norm 204.6), so no cumulative or aggregate bound exists. And the service has no publisher authentication: a peer that reaches the port with no credential and any source identifier published an update, and the artifact then records the forged source. These are absent mechanisms, not bypasses of an implemented check, and no service code was changed; the production stack publishes the update service only on its internal network, a compensating control outside the service.

\subsubsection{Authentication and Communication Security (G7)}
\label{sec:eval_auth}
The services open plaintext ports with no credential, token, expiry or peer-identity check, so transport identity is delegated to the ingress. The certificate rows ran against a stand-in mutual-TLS terminator, not the production ingress: they show the TLS stack's certificate semantics, while the ingress configuration is covered only by static checks and a smoke test not executed here. Missing, self-signed and wrong-usage client certificates, untrusted or mismatched server certificates and a plaintext client were all refused (7 of 7), as were expired and not-yet-valid certificates (3 of 3) and a foreign-CA certificate with the legitimate subject. The unmet properties concern identity inside the system: any claimed source identifier was accepted, a bogus bearer token and API key were ignored, and a second valid installation certificate had identical access, so installations cannot be individually authorized or revoked and no revocation list exists. Retrieval needs no credential at the service (an arbitrary or empty consumer identifier was served the legacy model, the V3 manifest and an asset), and the receipt-status lookup answered unauthenticated peers; retrieval replays were read-only, and unknown models, path traversal, a wrong manifest pin and non-allowlisted assets were rejected. Gaps and controls: Appendix~\ref{app:secdetail}.

\subsection{RQ3: Systems Overhead}
Historical V1 timings (Table~\ref{tab:browser_cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:browser}) end at the local rules/NER decision. All 240 ALLOW/WARN decisions ran both detectors, whereas the 120 BLOCK decisions stopped after rules, so cells include different amounts of work; medians span 27.9--35.0 ms, and each maximum describes sixty requests across two profiles, excluding receiver completion and semantic processing.

Installed V3 balanced timings appear in Figure~\ref{fig:latency} (Table~\ref{tab:v3_installed_cost}). The four within-profile median increments per arm span 26.1--26.7 ms for rules/NER, 9.4--17.0 ms for semantics and 31.9--33.5 ms for full detection; these compare profile medians rather than individual requests. All 480 timed attempts returned ALLOW. Including 80 warmups, 144 scaffold calls and ten post-cost checks, the 714 calls yielded 574 analysis RPCs and 712 unchanged-body arrivals without execution errors. Quality blocked the same benign prompt once on each transport, accounting for both non-arrivals; correct package loading thus coexisted with an inappropriate decision.

These comparisons isolate detector activation for one short input, excluding total overhead relative to an absent extension and the cost of cold download or loading. With two profile clusters per cell and observed maxima up to 341.0 ms, they establish neither a service-level objective nor a tail guarantee. Product code, dependencies and images also differ between V1 and V3, preventing attribution of cross-study speed differences to the model alone. Both studies timed the pre-change rules and NER, so the rules/NER increments may differ for the installed configuration. A sequential detector-layer pass (Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision}) found no meaningful per-prompt latency difference between configurations, but it is not a browser measurement.

\subsection{Telemetry Privacy and Utility}
\label{sec:telemetry_results}
The finite audit qualifies the privacy guarantee suggested by the ideal formula. On the fingerprinted host, maximum event loss exceeded nominal budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 by approximately $(1.294,1.503,1.226)\times10^{-15}$. Although tiny, this excess means the kernel does not satisfy the exact nominal bound. Eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$ under the same independent uniform-entropy assumptions. The result bounds admitted event content under the audited conditions, without certifying other builds or deployment entropy.

The two prespecified balanced-integer category cells (Table~\ref{tab:telemetry_utility}, Appendix~\ref{app:reporting}), at event budget one with $K=11$ and 10,000 repetitions, illustrate clipping bias and root-mean-square error (RMSE) for the first category. At $n=80$ (truth 7) the clipped bias was $+22.84$ and RMSE 42.32 with clipping in 85.62\% of repetitions; at $n=8000$ (truth 727) they were $+237.50$ and 1028.47 with clipping in 28.32\%. Independently clipped marginals failed to sum to $n$ in 99.99\% and 99.78\% of repetitions.

Accounting checks admitted eight default reservations, denied the ninth, preserved the cap after reopening and reset it on the next UTC day. Clock rollback and corrupt state failed closed; sixteen of forty concurrent epsilon-0.5 requests were admitted. These checks support bounded admission under the tested state and clock conditions, but admission also determines which events are represented. In a constructed 32-event sequence containing 25\% BLOCK overall, reversing the order changed BLOCK representation among the first eight contributors from 100\% to 0\%.

The controlled operational run offered 308 opportunities across 23 ledgers, producing 184 reservations, 168 accepted reports, sixteen submission failures and 124 pre-queue suppressions. Reopen, recovery and invalid-packet checks passed, and noisy and estimated summaries survived collector restarts. Ordered ALLOW-then-BLOCK inputs exhausted budgets before BLOCK, reproducing the selection effect in fixtures rather than users. Across all 308 opportunities, including suppressions, synchronous report-call p50/p95/max was 56.157/143.356/189.324 ms. Separate operator Health probes detected outage and recovery without demonstrating autonomous monitoring.

\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} Installed measurements quantify resident V3 detector activation alongside historical infrastructure costs, leaving total and cold-start overhead unresolved. Controlled telemetry tests verify delivery and accounting, but finite privacy excess, estimation error and admission selection persist, and the channel lacks performance fields.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
RQ1 shows why detector order is part of the policy: an early rule BLOCK prevents later semantic evidence from changing the action. Removing rules corrected 139 unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen previously correct actions, and the revised rerun still gained 85 actions against the same eighteen losses. Fresh detector-layer tests found a specificity/recall trade-off: the first revision failed its recall margin; K passed H8 (difference $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$) but lost 1.05 points of union recall against the original detectors and failed H7. The loss is source dependent and the owner shipped K despite the unmet promotion rule: a trade-off, not a universally suitable setting.

The security evaluation adds a second picture. Low ASR (2.99\%) rests on high baseline over-flagging (65.5\% of clean control prompts non-ALLOW) and on fail-closed BLOCKs that also block every long benign prompt, so it does not show that identifiers are recognized; indirect references (13.22\%) and short-window dilution (15.05\%) are real evasion routes, and the flags help only the two families they target. The update path rejects every malformed, stale and replayed submission it was shown but has no publisher authentication, credentials or cumulative bound, so G2 and G6 hold for the attacks that reach the checks and not for an unauthenticated peer; the standard V3 path avoids the problem by having no update route. Masking prevents more warned transmissions than plain WARN (0.545 against 0.413) but only what the detectors cover and at a measured over-redaction cost. Unlike Casper, HaS and ProSan~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}, \privoke sends unchanged content after WARN unless masking is enabled and leaves task completion unresolved after BLOCK.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
Independent contextual ground truth remains the largest scientific gap because the authored targets are assistant-provisional; freezing the annotation-presence endpoint limits post-selection but does not validate contextual authorization. A stronger evaluation would define whose information may flow to which audience and purpose before annotators see system outputs, with at least two independent annotators; the blinded double-annotation tooling is ready, but no human labels exist and the study has not been run.

Group and text exclusions do not establish template independence or absence of pretraining exposure; the final endpoint (308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row) is English and synthetic or machine-generated, and neither intervals nor three seeds give a population guarantee. The detector-layer studies use one small English spaCy model and annotation labels with unquantified noise; the 498-row endpoint is consumed, the student-identifier rule rests on one template, F4 is positive-only so K's specificity rests on reused negatives and unlabelled prompts whose flag rates are not error rates, and no multiplicity correction was applied. K's recall cost against the original detectors is real and source dependent, and neither the full-product rerun nor the installed probes and cost studies include K.

The new units are limited too: the evasion experiment reuses F4 non-adaptively in process, the update tests used synthetic legacy state and a stand-in TLS terminator, and the WARN experiment is detector-layer on development data with an analytic confirmation bound.

Further limits of what the results establish: the installed probes cover selected paths and not unsupported transports; cluster resampling addresses known grouping but not independent templates or population coverage; the matched continuation does not establish population-level generalization or the security of the publication path; one repeated benign prompt cannot establish a false-positive rate; the two illustrative telemetry cells do not establish utility acceptance or describe every cell, additional reports alone do not ensure reliable counts, acceptable application error remains unmeasured, and a content-privacy bound alone cannot establish representative monitoring.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
Consistent with negative-flip research~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, all matched full-model candidates improved aggregate assessment yet failed the guard, and the weak zero-head base and small guard constrain the significance of a pass; the study guard also differs from the service predicate. This paper reports installed V3 inference and bounded continuation evidence, not a deployed V3 learning loop (Table~\ref{tab:paths}); authenticated providers, Windows, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified, and practical utility and human benefit remain open.

Matched resident arms omit extension-absent and cold-load comparisons, the telemetry bound assumes independent uniform OS bits and a trusted clock, and exact reruns need the referenced weights and inputs. Next steps are adaptive attackers, authenticated publication and retrieval with a cumulative update bound, a semantic-layer WARN evaluation, contextual adjudication and user evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke connects local prompt inspection to supported request enforcement, guarded candidate updates and separate randomized telemetry. Receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths, except Edge on Copilot. Updated packages improved agreement on 1,560 authored cases but annotation-presence specificity was low, and all matched full-model candidates failed retention.

The detector revisions expose the central trade-off: K, shipped by owner decision, passed H8's 0.5-point BLOCK-recall margin but retained a 1.05-point union-recall loss against the original detectors (95\% interval $-1.70$ to $-0.41$). Fixed transforms evade the product on 2.99\% of protected pairs (37.63\% of prompts by at least one variant), with long inputs protected only by indiscriminate fail-closed BLOCKs; the update service rejects every stale, replayed, malformed and non-finite submission but lacks publisher authentication, credentials and a cumulative bound; and masking prevents more warned transmissions than WARN at an over-redaction cost bounded by span recall. The evidence supports bounded, inspectable mechanisms only (Section~\ref{sec:discussion} lists what it does not establish). Contextual adjudication, adaptive attackers and user outcomes remain future work.

For other findings, the baseline checks are ordered. S0 with an empty category set yields ALLOW. S3 yields BLOCK and S2 yields WARN. For the remaining S0/S1 cases, IDENTITY together with LOCATION yields WARN; either category with visibility P2, P3 or P4 also yields WARN. Otherwise the finding yields ALLOW. PU denotes an unspecified audience and is not treated as restricted/private by this comparison. A category set is multilabel, so retaining one sensitive category is not equivalent to matching the exact target set.

The cascade then constrains what reaches aggregation. An early rule BLOCK ends inspection; later requested layers have not evaluated the text. Otherwise, aggregation preserves the strongest enforcement decision rather than replacing it with the last detector's action. A failed requested layer changes an otherwise ALLOW result to BLOCK, while a valid non-ALLOW finding remains visible. These semantics differ from a configuration in which an unavailable parameter stream leaves fewer enabled layers: a successfully completed reduced cascade is not a successful semantic execution.

Finally, a policy action and a receiver observation are different records. ALLOW and WARN release the original supported request, while BLOCK cancels it. Notice rendering under WARN does not imply acknowledgement, consent or removal of information from the body. For a probe expecting a send, the relevant observation is receiver arrival with the original body; for a no-send probe, the observation is absence of arrival within the declared observation window, including checks for a late send. The installed probes establish these bounded outcomes, not an absence of leakage through every browser transport.

\section{Candidate Identity, Guarding and Reproduction}
\label{app:retention}
The matched continuation comparison starts both HEADS and FULL from the same intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads. That ORIGINAL control is distinct from both the historical V1 classifier and the selected updated package used in RQ1. The shared 4,096 exposures describe repeated training occurrences, not 4,096 independently sampled situations. Each seed produces a terminal candidate; the assessment selects no intermediate checkpoint.

A candidate, a guard decision and an effective artifact must remain distinguishable in the record. The candidate is the fitted set of weights, even if rejected. The decision applies the fixed sixteen-case criterion of this study: improved joint correctness and zero action harms against its derived target actions. This study mapping differs from the maintained service predicate of Definition~\ref{def:accept}(d), which requires non-decreasing exact, recall and specificity counts and no target-capped regression, with progress required separately at selection. The effective-artifact mapping describes which artifact that rule would retain: a rejected candidate maps to the unchanged ORIGINAL. Because this experiment publishes no candidate, that mapping does not establish a server replacement or a client refresh. Counting only effective artifacts would erase the rejected candidates' measured gains and harms; counting every fitted candidate as an update would erase the guard.

For reproduction, the maintained records identify the exact base and candidate artifacts, parameter scope, ordered exposure schedule, exclusions, scoring target and actual sole-semantic execution. The model-family label alone is insufficient to recover those inputs. The ideal mechanism and audited finite implementation in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism} are analyzed for the content of a fixed reporting opportunity under public configuration. The finite bound includes the reported excess over nominal privacy loss. For a category whose true contributor count is $h$ among $n$ reports, and observed randomized count $Y$, the nominal inverse-response estimate before clipping is
\begin{equation}
\widehat h=\frac{Y-nq}{p-q}.
\end{equation}
The collector clips each estimated marginal separately to $[0,n]$. Inverting the nominal probabilities is not the same as inverting the exact finite kernel, and clipping is nonlinear: the expected clipped value need not equal $h$. Independently clipped categories also need not sum to $n$. These properties explain why privacy loss, bias, RMSE and total coherence are separate checks.

The synthetic trials fix the contributor histogram before sampling the randomized reports. Their error therefore excludes uncertainty about which people or events enter that histogram. Increasing $n$ means more admitted reports, pooled across installation-days where necessary, rather than relaxing the per-installation daily cap. A precision requirement for an operational decision was not prespecified; the two illustrative category cells cannot establish one after the fact.

The operational accounting also has distinct denominators. An opportunity may be suppressed before queuing, a reservation may be consumed without a delivered packet, and an accepted report represents a received randomized event. The observed totals reconcile as $308=184+124$ opportunities and $184=168+16$ reserved events, where the latter partition is accepted reports plus submission failures in the controlled fixtures. These are not counts of users, unique prompts or detector decisions in a deployment.

The ledger reserves before construction and does not refund failed construction or delivery. That ordering protects accounting against retries within retained state, but cannot hide whether a report was sent. A cap can also change event composition before randomization, as the retained order-reversal example demonstrates. Correcting the randomized counts would still leave that admission mechanism to justify. The finite audit and controlled operation are recorded separately in \path{telemetry-evidence-20261014.md} and \path{rq3-telemetry-evidence-20261017.md}; neither supplies training examples or establishes useful live monitoring.

\subsection*{Detector-layer protocol details}
\label{sec:detprec_protocol}
This detector-layer study explicitly requests regex and NER, never semantics. It compares pre-change detectors with the first revision; the later student-identifier rule and K rework are absent. The 502-row development data are split by source-group hash into dev-TUNE (267; 148 positive) and dev-CHECK (235; 116 positive); design material also includes 2,000 personalized and 1,000 OpenAI-generated rows. The post-filter used dev-TUNE and personalized data; dev-CHECK and OpenAI results for its revised version are post-review. The inputs consulted while designing regex repairs were not separately recorded, so those splits are treated as design material. The initial endpoint contains 498 rows and was scored once per configuration after defaults froze, but had already been opened in another unit; paired outcomes were later reconstructed and the endpoint is now consumed. The student rule was diagnosed after this scoring. Labels are source annotation presence, not contextual authorization.

A positive is any nonzero sensitivity or nonempty category. We report confusion counts, recall, specificity and latency for regex, NER and their union. Paired McNemar tests use discordant rows; paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 replicates, fixed seed) resample documents on real sets and rows on synthetic sets, with scenario-label sensitivity analyses (20 personalized and 10 OpenAI scenarios). No multiplicity correction is applied. Runtime action and weighted-error comparisons are separate outcomes.

\textit{Rule gates.} Kinship terms and the word ``family'' need a possessive determiner (my, our, his, her, their), optionally followed by up to two modifier words; child cues need a possessive and a child noun, an age expression, or an age and a person noun (a five-year-old laptop does not match). Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns after a possessive, and location keywords need a connector such as ``live in'' followed by a non-closed-class capture. Structured identity values stay on one line (at most five tokens) and a phone label needs a number-like value. Checksum validators reject shape-only hits for card numbers (Luhn~\cite{Luhn1960Patent}), IBANs (mod-97~\cite{ISO13616Part1_2020}), NHS numbers (mod-11) and US social security numbers (issuable ranges), and context matching is word-bounded. Password placeholders, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. A separate student-identifier rule (S3 identity) needs a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student; it postdates Sections~\ref{sec:detector_precision} and~\ref{sec:product_rerun} and is part of N and K in Section~\ref{sec:heldout}.

\textit{Labelled identifiers and downgrades (K).} Two rules (S3 and S2 identity) match a label noun (id, identifier, number, no, num, nr, code or reference, in words, snake or camel case, key-value form, or a hash sign after the qualifier) followed by a value. S3 qualifiers cover national and government identity documents, tax, health and insurance, bank account and payment-card labels, and the acronyms SSN, TFN, NINO, EIN, ITIN, MRN and NRIC need no noun; S2 qualifiers cover customer, account, member, employee, user, policy, claim, plate, loyalty and similar labels. A value is tokens of letters, digits and underscores joined by hyphens or single spaces, with a digit in its first token (or a one- to three-letter prefix before a digit group), five to twenty-four compact characters, and not a date; software licence names and order, build, ticket, part and promotion codes are excluded. Downgrades keep a hit whose checksum fails when a card, banking or social-security word is in context (S3), and keep an eight-character token with five or more digits at S2 after an IBAN check fails without a banking word. Digit spaces are removed before matching.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 1.} Five span rules apply in turn to the output of the small English spaCy model~\cite{Honnibal2020SpaCy}, which uses capitalization as evidence for names: placeholder masks; identifier-like tokens (e-mail, URL, underscore, code); spans inside headings (except multi-token or cue-introduced names); short acronyms without a locating or affiliation preposition; and single-token person candidates that the model's word list contains and the tagger reads as non-proper nouns without a corroborating cue.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 2.} The first matching rule decides: a person linked with a corroborated place or organization yields S2; that place or organization yields S2; a corroborated person yields S2; a place or organization tied to the speaker by a relation cue (or in the same prompt as one) keeps its default; a facility beside a number yields S2; a single uncorroborated person yields S1; and a lone place or organization is dropped. Corroboration means evidence about the entity itself (a multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring person, or a place or organization with a personal-relation cue); a bare locating preposition does not corroborate.

\textit{Development-only ablations.} Table~\ref{tab:detprec_ablation} holds the repaired rules fixed and varies the NER input and filter on design and development splits (dev-CHECK and OpenAI are post-review; none is held out). Case preservation raises or preserves union recall but lowers specificity wherever there is margin to lose (to 0.824, 0.832 and 0.814 on dev-TUNE, dev-CHECK and OpenAI, and 0.919 on the personalized set) because capitalized non-names enter; the post-filter recovers most of it on case-preserved input and, on lowercased input, lowers recall slightly while raising specificity. Counting only non-organization entities moved development NER-only recall from 0.170 to 0.333 and specificity from 0.996 to 0.937 between lowercased and case-preserved input.

The generic labelled-identifier rule fires at S2 on technical key and value text, and its qualifier vocabulary mirrors the labels of the development spans, so its gains may not transfer.

\textit{Spans.} Counting a prompt as detected when any rule fires overstates masking: on F1, N's union detects 80.92\% of positive sentences yet fully masks every annotated span in only 37.16\% (37.31\% for O), and on the 502 development sentences any-hit recall was 0.761, every mention was masked in 107 of 264 prompts (0.405) and 0.625 of gold characters were covered. On F1, N's union overlap recall rose 1.19 points and its over-masking fell by 12.75 characters per thousand, whereas on F4 K raised gold-character coverage by 5.00 points over O but masked 10.82 more characters per thousand outside annotated spans than N. Letting NER extend masking only where the regex layer already fired recovers part of the NER span gain without creating a positive.

\textit{Full-product rerun detail.} The comparison and ablation of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_comparison_results} were rerun as full-product evidence (rules, NER and semantics, cascade execution recorded) on the same 1,560 cases, six packages, policy, rule-first ordering and one pass per cell, comparing a clean export of the pre-change commit with the revised detectors without the student-identifier rule. The pre-change arm reproduced every historical outcome (no discordant pair), the semantic layer is unchanged by construction, all 40,560 requests completed without errors, and intervals resample the 35 scenario families.

The revised detectors reduced over-restriction in every package: the 139 unnecessary restrictions shared by the V3 tiers fell to 108 (112 in union; 103 among the original 139), so over-enforcement was largely but not entirely a rule artefact. Allowed-action agreement rose for every package (V3 balanced 1,409 to 1,438: 36 improved, 7 regressed, $p<0.001$), and binary false positives for that package fell from 119 to 95 with false negatives unchanged at 8. The mechanism was 36 cases moving from BLOCK to ALLOW as early rule BLOCKs fell from 289 to 253, against 7 clean cases moving from ALLOW to WARN because NER now emitted findings. Joint agreement did not change significantly for V3 ($p$ from 0.26 to 0.81) and fell by five for V1 balanced (2 improved, 7 regressed, $p=0.18$): the rule repairs alone added 28 joint-correct cases without a loss while the new NER findings altered the label set on 25 cases, each in favour of removing NER. V3 stayed ahead of V1 in every tier (allowed-action gains against losses 388/5, 413/12 and 337/3, against 389/3, 389/6 and 348/0 historically).

The ablation persists but shrinks: removing rules corrected 103 actions and lost 18 (net 85, $p<10^{-14}$; family interval for the action-rate gain 2.4 to 8.9 points, against 3.5 to 12.7 historically), and under-restriction again rose from 12 to 30. Revised NER converts 48 findings, all identity names (alone it flags 36 of the 810 sensitive and 12 of the 750 clean cases); No-NER equals the full product on 1,526 of 1,560 cases, and removing NER improves 7 action and 25 joint outcomes with no regression, so on this fixture NER is a net negative at the label level, which does not establish its value or harm elsewhere. These are measurements on the same provisional labels and correlated families, scored twice, so not a fresh confirmation; the ablation uses one balanced package, and neither the student-identifier rule nor K is part of the rerun, so the shipped configuration has not been run through the full product.

\textit{Evasion design.} The transforms act on annotated identifier spans: Cyrillic or Greek homoglyphs and zero-width characters, a space between every span character, one transposition, deletion or doubling, template paraphrase from a declared 35-entry lexicon and six frames, initials or a trailing-digits cue and a family-keyed pointer (the pointer removes the value, so the table also reports rates without it), base64, hex and spelled digits, 800-character and 6,000-character filler placements, and six-language translation templates. The runtime is called through its native servicer in process with V3 balanced loaded through the PIPE3 adapter; the benign control uses 1,000 clean F1 rows with the entity-free variants. Intervals are Wilson over pairs, with a source-document bootstrap for the overall rate (2.88--3.10 at the action level).

Update and authentication rows used the private copy of the legacy artifact, snapshotted before and after each attack, and ten kill delays on one machine, with Windows process termination standing in for a kill signal; they show no violation, not its absence. Test identities were generated per run. A kill during the atomic write can leave a temporary file beside the artifact that no startup code removes; this was seen only in unrecorded development runs and its effect on serving was not tested. The update tests exercised the legacy artifact only and did not measure the effect of a bounded hostile update on detection quality; telemetry authentication, the fuzzer service and the browser extension were outside them. The evasion experiment's stored scores and frozen sets contain F4-derived text and stay local; the records carry the aggregates and file hashes. Its first launch ran out of memory in five of twelve baseline shards and the loader was changed to stream per shard before scoring resumed; a duplicate orchestration produced no duplicate or conflicting records. Frozen sets, transforms and analysis definitions did not change.
