\title{\privoke: A Client-Side Privacy Firewall for LLM Prompts}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous Institution}\country{Anonymous}}


\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to hosted language models can disclose personal or confidential information before a provider processes them. \privoke connects local inspection, ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK handling of supported requests (ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels), guarded and signed updates of its V3 detectors, and separate randomized telemetry. On 1,560 authored cases with provisional targets, updated packages improved agreement; on an earlier prototype build, 33 installed receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths. All matched full-model candidates improved aggregate agreement but failed the configured retention guard. Registered fresh detector-layer tests found that the shipped rework passed the 0.5-point BLOCK-recall margin (difference $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$), while its union-recall result stayed inconclusive and a 1.05-point loss against the original detectors did not meet the promotion rule. Fixed transforms evaded the product on 2.99\% of protected pairs (6.52\% counting fail-closed BLOCKs as misses; a floor, not an adaptive attacker), while 65.5\% of clean control prompts are already non-ALLOW. Masking a warned prompt prevented 0.545 of sensitive transmissions against 0.413 (regex and NER layers, development data). On the prototype, telemetry fixtures verified delivery and accounting, finite-arithmetic loss slightly exceeded nominal budgets, and update attacks were rejected except where mechanisms were missing (cumulative bound, publisher authentication, credentials); the current Rust services add these mechanisms, \pendingStageThirteen{and their evaluation is pending}. Simulated telemetry estimation error was substantial. The findings support bounded mechanism claims, not validated contextual judgments, universal interception, an accepted update, useful live monitoring or user benefit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Hosted large language models (LLMs) support writing, coding and analysis, but prompts may include another person's details or confidential material~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing,SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Provider controls on retention act after the initial transfer. Running the application model locally is one alternative, but changes the chosen service and its computational demands~\cite{Llama2023}. We study the intervening point: inspecting selected requests on the user's device before handoff.

The privacy question is contextual: the same information may be appropriate or inappropriate to disclose depending on whose information it is, the audience and purpose~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}. Recognition alone cannot establish authorization, nor does a warning prevent transfer. PriVoke combines rules, named entity recognition (NER) and semantic classification to represent sensitivity, audience and overlapping categories, then selects ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK: ALLOW and WARN send the original body without waiting for acknowledgement, and BLOCK cancels a recognized request. Because an early rule BLOCK ends the detector cascade, detector order and request enforcement both affect observed behavior. Work on reproducing sensitive prompt information in chatbot outputs concerns a later point in the lifecycle~\cite{priyanshu2023chatbots}; pre-transmission inspection cannot reverse that disclosure.

The system also separates detector maintenance from reporting. Server services written in Rust fit candidates for the V3 detectors from frozen labelled releases, and drift, guard and retention gates may reject a candidate before a signed publication. This addresses the risk that aggregate gains conceal regressions on previously correct cases~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, but only for the declared rows and criteria. A separate reporter randomizes coarse telemetry under a local budget ledger; neither prompts nor telemetry train the detector. The contribution is the evaluation of these connected but distinct mechanisms in one workflow, not a new detector or privacy primitive.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt]
\item An implemented client path connecting local layered inspection to explicit request handling on supported browser requests, specified as a workflow (Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow}) and a decision policy.
\item A guarded, signed V3 update workflow with casewise guard and retention gates and a formal acceptance predicate (Definition~\ref{def:accept}), with V1 models kept for detection only, and matched candidates that improved aggregate agreement but did not qualify for publication.
\item A separate randomized telemetry path evaluated for finite event-content privacy, budget accounting, delivery and estimation error.
\item Explicit security goals and threats linked to the experiments that test them, with unmet properties shown as such, and an empirical account of trade-offs across prediction, enforcement, update eligibility and reporting that does not treat provisional targets as contextual truth.
\end{enumerate}
The implementation, evaluation recipes and research records are available at an anonymous repository (link withheld for review).

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\textbf{RQ1}: does \privoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively; \textbf{RQ2}: can it update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior; and \textbf{RQ3}: what systems and privacy overhead does it introduce? RQ1 separates agreement with declared targets from observed transmission; RQ2 asks whether aggregate gains satisfy a casewise retention rule; RQ3 separates resident cost and event-content privacy from estimation error and selection.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local intervention and contextual decisions.} Casper combines browser-side rules, entity recognition and topic analysis, replacing identifiers and requiring acknowledgement for topic warnings~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}. Hide and Seek (HaS) hides entities before cloud inference and recovers them in responses, ProSan learns selective word replacement from task-importance and privacy proxies~\cite{HaS2024,ProSan2024}, and PromptShield classifies enterprise prompts by role and policy~\cite{PromptShield2025}. These works address sanitization, task continuation or policy classification; \privoke instead measures a request contract in which supported hooks act automatically and WARN forwards unchanged content without acknowledgement.

\textit{Retaining behavior during model revision.} Negative-flip research measures when an update turns a previously correct prediction into an error; Yan et al. propose positive-congruent training for image classifiers~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. This motivates paired gains and losses but not privacy-policy correctness; \privoke applies the distinction to an update decision, rejecting candidates under a configured guard limited to its declared cases and harm rule.

\textit{Privacy of operational reporting.} RAPPOR applies randomized response to client-side statistics and addresses the privacy cost of repeated reporting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}; \privoke combines categorical event reports with a local budget ledger. Event-content protection does not conceal participation or network metadata, and accurate marginals would not by themselves establish representative monitoring.

\section{Threat Model and Security Goals}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
\privoke addresses prompt text in recognized browser requests before handoff to an external language-model provider. Protected assets are the request text, local detector and model state, update artifacts and credentials, and randomized telemetry with its budget ledger. The user, enabled extension, page hook, local controller and workstation are trusted to inspect and to preserve local state; compromised local components are out of scope and could expose prompts or alter decisions and accounting. The provider and network observer are not trusted: the provider receives the original prompt after ALLOW or WARN, and an observer may see report presence and transport metadata that event-content randomization does not hide.

We consider two active adversaries. A \emph{prompt author} modifies text to evade detection. An \emph{update sender} submits stale, replayed, duplicate, malformed or unauthorized candidates, or presents an unauthorized service identity. The implementation has normalization and detector layers for prompt variation; for updates it has parent-bound compare-and-swap, receipts, drift, guard and retention gates and Ed25519-signed publication (Section~\ref{sec:updates}); and every server listener requires mutual TLS with a per-call role policy. Section~\ref{sec:security_eval} reports negative tests on an earlier prototype build, whose controls held where a mechanism existed but which lacked update authentication, a cumulative bound and in-service credentials; \pendingStageThirteen{tests of the current build are pending}.

The boundary covers recognized prompt-bearing POST requests sent through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} (XHR). Workers, attachments, unsupported endpoints and other transports are outside measured coverage, users can disable protection, and the optional hosted-inference configuration is excluded. Table~\ref{tab:threat_defense} (Appendix~\ref{app:policy}) maps each threat to its defense and experiment. Each goal in Table~\ref{tab:security_goals} is therefore conditional, and none implies correct contextual decisions or comprehensive protection.

\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The subsections below specify each path and Section~\ref{sec:properties} states the properties each is designed to satisfy.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Enforcement}
\label{sec:enforcement}
\textit{Request enforcement (G1).} Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} gives the client workflow. When the active page hook recognizes a supported request and the runtime returns BLOCK, the hook cancels it before the page's native sender hands it off. ALLOW and WARN release the original body, and WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. The installed probes (Section~\ref{sec:installed_probes}) test this contract on selected paths.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\privoke request workflow. Layers are ordered rules $<$ NER $<$ semantic; $\max$ uses ALLOW $<$ WARN $<$ BLOCK.}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require intercepted request $r$ with prompt text $x$; requested layers $\Lambda$
\State \textbf{if} protection is disabled or $r$ is unsupported \textbf{then return} forward $r$
\State $x_c\gets\textsc{Normalize}(x)$ with offsets; $x_\ell\gets\textsc{Lowercase}(x_c)$
\State $a\gets\text{ALLOW}$;\; $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{false}$
\If{rules $\in\Lambda$}
  \State $a\gets\textsc{Rules}(x_c)$
  \If{$a=\text{BLOCK}$} \textbf{goto} line~\ref{alg:enforce} \EndIf \Comment{skip NER and semantics}
\EndIf
\ForAll{$\ell\in\Lambda\setminus\{\text{rules}\}$}\Comment{concurrent in the Python worker}
  \State $F\gets\ell(x_c)$ for NER; $\ell(x_\ell)$ for semantic
  \If{$\ell$ failed} $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{true}$
  \Else{} $a\gets\max\bigl(a,\max_{f\in F}\pi(f)\bigr)$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$\mathit{failed}\wedge a=\text{ALLOW}$} $a\gets\text{BLOCK}$ \EndIf \Comment{runtime; the extension warns}
\State \textbf{enforce:}\label{alg:enforce} \textbf{if} $a=\text{BLOCK}$ cancel $r$ before transmission
\State \textbf{else} release the original body (WARN first shows a notice, without waiting)
\State offer a coarse event to the separate telemetry channel (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The runtime normalizes text while retaining offsets. Regex and NER receive case-preserved text; semantic models receive lowercased normalized text. Rules run first and an early rule BLOCK ends the cascade; otherwise the strongest action is retained. A failed requested layer turns an otherwise-ALLOW runtime result into BLOCK, but the installed extension forwards a request whose analysis failed without a detector BLOCK, with a warning. We therefore distinguish the returned action, executed layers and receiver observation.

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic model predicts sensitivity $s\in\{\mathrm{S0},\dots,\mathrm{S3}\}$ (S0 no identified risk; S3 highly sensitive information, identifiers or secrets), audience (visibility) $v\in\{\mathrm{P0},\dots,\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{PU}\}$ (public, semi-public, restricted/group-private/personal-private, unspecified) and a category set $C$ (health, politics, religion, criminal, financial, sexual, child, location, identity and third-party information). With $c$ the finding confidence and $I=C\cap\{\mathrm{IDENTITY},\mathrm{LOCATION}\}$, the policy $\pi$ maps a finding to an action:
\begin{equation}
\pi(f)=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{WARN} & c<0.5\wedge\bigl(s=\mathrm{S3}\vee(s=\mathrm{S2}\wedge I\neq\varnothing)\bigr)\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & c<0.5\ \text{otherwise}\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & s=\mathrm{S0}\wedge C=\varnothing\\
\mathrm{BLOCK} & s=\mathrm{S3}\\
\mathrm{WARN} & s=\mathrm{S2}\ \vee\ |I|=2\ \vee\ (I\neq\varnothing\wedge v\in\{\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4}\})\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with rows evaluated in order and a missing confidence treated as $c\ge0.5$. The score $c$ is not calibrated joint confidence or evidence of authorization. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives the order and its observable consequences. The pretrained V3 packages use BERT encoders~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}; the installed default is V3 balanced.

\label{sec:detector_precision_design}
\textit{Detector precision controls.} Because rules and NER precede semantic classification, the precision revision targets unnecessary early findings at the risk of missed detections; the policy mapping and semantic models are unchanged.

\subsection{Guarded Update Lifecycle}
\label{sec:updates}
\textit{Detection-only V1 and routine V3 training.} Two model families coexist (Table~\ref{tab:paths}). V1 models remain available to the detection pipeline but are not trained: the parameter-update and gradient calls answer \texttt{UNIMPLEMENTED}. Routine training, an opt-in deployment profile, retrains all three V3 tiers. The scheduler in the parameter-update service (param-update) visits efficient, balanced and quality in turn, head-only (HEADS) and then full-encoder (FULL) fitting, journals each request identifier, seed and expected base, and asks the V3 fuzzer for a cycle; the V3 runtime fits the candidate from its parent on frozen labelled taxonomy, benign and audience releases. Lineage starts at each tier's pinned standard release. Training is centralized on these releases, not on user prompts, gradients or telemetry.

A candidate $u=(m,\sigma,p,o,\theta_u,E,\rho)$ names a tier $m$, scope $\sigma\in\{\text{HEADS},\text{FULL}\}$, parent manifest $p$, original $o$, parameters $\theta_u$, fit evidence $E$ and request identifier $\rho$. Let $A$ be $m$'s active version with manifest $a$ and parameters $\theta_A$, $\theta_R$ the tier's pinned release, and $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ the L2 norm over all tensors.

\begin{definition}[Acceptance of a candidate]\label{def:accept}
Param-update publishes $u$ as $m$'s new version if and only if all of the following hold; at commit, (a) is re-checked and (b)--(d) scored before the transaction, inside which the receipt replay, (e) and the recorded verdicts decide:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,label=(\alph*),itemsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Admitted.} $o$ is the pinned release's original; configuration and tensor inventory are unchanged, a HEADS candidate changes only head tensors, every stored byte matches its hash, and $E$ names the pinned frozen source.
\item \emph{Bounded.} $\lVert\theta_u-\theta_p\rVert\le\delta_{m,\sigma}$ (calibrated per tier and scope) and $\lVert\theta_u-\theta_R\rVert\le100$ (cumulative).
\item \emph{Guarded.} $\rho$ was allocated by the scheduler; param-update rebuilds the sixteen guard rows from the frozen release with that allocation's seed and scores parent and candidate itself, requiring more joint-correct rows and zero action harms. The trainer's guard claims are not trusted.
\item \emph{Retaining.} On the approved 200-row bank, the candidate falls below the pinned release's sensitivity, severity or action on at most 5\% (rounded down) of the rows on which that release predicts a sensitive label with union margin and sensitivity gap above $10^{-6}$.
\item \emph{Fresh and not conflicting.} $p=a$ (compare-and-swap); if a receipt for $\rho$ exists, an identical payload returns the recorded outcome and a different one is refused without a receipt.
\end{enumerate}
On acceptance the transaction allocates sequence $k{+}1$, signs the statement $(\mathit{key},m,\text{manifest},p,o,k{+}1)$ with an Ed25519 publication key that is not a TLS key, moves $m$'s authority row to $u$ and commits the receipt. A stale base or a refusal under (b)--(d) records a rejected receipt, an admission failure or a conflicting reuse of $\rho$ records none, and nothing is signed or published.
\end{definition}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:update} gives the control flow. Published files derive from the authority row and are repaired from it at startup, so an interrupted commit serves the old or the new version. The controller loads a version only if its statement verifies under a compiled key, matches the model, manifest, parent and original, descends from the compiled pin and raises the persisted sequence high-water mark; a rollback is a new, higher-sequence statement issued only to the publisher role, and refresh is enabled per model.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Guarded V3 update (Definition~\ref{def:accept}).}
\label{alg:update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require tier $m$, scope $\sigma$; active version $A$ with manifest $a$
\State \textbf{scheduler:} journal $(\rho,\mathit{seed},a)$; request a cycle; runtime fits and stages $u$
\State \textbf{if} receipt for $\rho$ exists \textbf{then return} it, \textbf{or reject} without receipt (payload differs)
\State \textbf{if not} Admitted$(u)$ \textbf{then reject} (no receipt; retry allowed)
\State $v\gets$ verdict of Bounded, Guarded, Retaining (scored before the transaction)
\State \textbf{begin transaction}; repeat the receipt replay check
\State \textbf{if} $p\ne a$ \textbf{or} $v$ refuses \textbf{then} record rejected receipt (stale or $v$)
\State \textbf{else} sign $(\mathit{key},m,\text{manifest},p,o,k{+}1)$; set $m$'s authority to $u$; record receipt
\State \textbf{end transaction}; promote staged files; update the published pointer
\State clients admit the signed version on their next refresh
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The gates bound change and tie publication to a signing key, but the guard and retention criteria cover only their declared rows, authorization rests on certificate and key custody, and behavioral safety is not guaranteed. The attacks of Section~\ref{sec:eval_update} targeted the prototype's V1 update service; \pendingStageThirteen{tests of these gates are pending}.

\subsection{Privacy-Preserving Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
The controller's reporter emits five coarse randomized fields (action, risk bucket, precedence-selected category, model-version bucket and four-hour time-of-day bin) over mutual TLS with a shared reporter credential; the schema omits the prompt, identifiers, exact confidence and per-layer findings. Test code checks the field set and aggregate storage, and controlled operation of the prototype tested delivery and accounting, not absence of prompt text from every local log, memory surface or channel. The collector estimates lifetime marginals but lacks denominators and time-window queries for latency or error rates, and telemetry provides no training labels.

\textit{Event-content privacy (G4).} Randomized response limits how much the distribution of a released tuple can change between possible event contents~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}, for a fixed reporting opportunity and public configuration; it does not randomize whether a report exists. The event budget $\varepsilon$ is divided equally, $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$; for a domain of size $K_i$ the true value has reporting probability $p_i$ and each alternative $q_i$:
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}.
\end{equation}
Under independent ideal sampling, composition multiplies the field likelihood ratios to bound the event ratio by $e^\varepsilon$~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}; that is the nominal mechanism, not an exact guarantee for the finite implementation. The reporter compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw with a binary64 threshold and chooses alternatives by rejection sampling, so its finite event loss $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ (Equation~\ref{eq:finite}, Appendix~\ref{app:reporting}) differs from $\varepsilon$. The finite audit of the prototype's reporter (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_results}) found $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ slightly above nominal $\varepsilon$ for the audited host settings, so the result is an implementation-specific bound under independent uniform randomness, not exact nominal epsilon across builds; \pendingStageThirteen{the current reporter is not covered by that audit}. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves nominal budget before packet construction (default one per event and eight per Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day) without refund for failed construction or delivery; it depends on retained state and a trusted clock and is not user-level accounting across devices or days. Report presence, accepted counts, timing, source IP, contribution selection and other network metadata remain observable or outside the content guarantee, and the collector's inversion and clipping do not by themselves establish useful or representative monitoring.

\subsection{Security Properties}
\label{sec:properties}
Let $r$ be a recognized supported request, $a(r)$ the action of Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow}, $\mathrm{sent}(r)$ the event that its body reaches the page's native sender, $A$ the active artifact, $A'$ the active artifact after candidate $u$ is submitted, and $\mathcal M$ the telemetry mechanism. The properties hold under the trust assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:threat_model}, and Table~\ref{tab:security_goals} links each to its test.

\begin{property}[Enforcement, G1]\label{prop:g1}
$a(r)=\mathrm{BLOCK}\Rightarrow\neg\mathrm{sent}(r)$ on the hooked \texttt{fetch}/XHR path; ALLOW and WARN release the original body (WARN+MASK, off by default, a rewritten body).
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Update non-replacement, G2]\label{prop:g2}
$\neg\mathrm{Accept}(u,A)\Rightarrow A'=A$ at the guarded publication stage.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Telemetry schema, G3]\label{prop:g3}
Every official telemetry packet is a tuple $(x_1,\dots,x_5)\in\prod_{i=1}^{5}\mathcal D_i$ of coarse fields from fixed finite domains, so it contains no raw prompt text.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Event-content LDP, G4]\label{prop:g4}
For events $e,e'$ admitted at one reporting opportunity under a public configuration and every output $y$, $\Pr[\mathcal M(e)=y]\le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[\mathcal M(e')=y]$ for the ideal mechanism, and with $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ for the audited implementation, under independent uniform OS randomness; report presence, selection, timing and metadata are outside the property.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Evasion resilience, G5]\label{prop:g5}
For a declared transform set $T$, the end-to-end $\mathrm{ASR}_{\mid}(\tau)$ of Section~\ref{sec:metrics} is small for every $\tau\in T$; the property is estimated, not guaranteed.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Update admission, G6]\label{prop:g6}
$\mathrm{Accept}(u,A)$ holds exactly when conditions (a)--(e) of Definition~\ref{def:accept} hold; only then is $A'\ne A$.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Authenticated service, G7]\label{prop:g7}
A request $q$ to the model, update or telemetry service is served or applied only if $\mathrm{principal}(q)\in\mathrm{Authorized}$.
\end{property}
\begin{property}[Intervention effect, G8]\label{prop:g8}
An intervention $I$ on WARN prompts reduces sensitive transmission when $\mathrm{prev}(I)>\mathrm{prev}(\text{WARN})$, where $\mathrm{prev}$ is the fraction of sensitive prompts not sent with an intact sensitive span.
\end{property}

\textit{Design rationale.} Rules run first, so a cheap early decision cannot be weakened; failed analysis does not pass silently (the runtime BLOCKs an otherwise-ALLOW result; the extension forwards with a warning); candidates are bound to their exact parent and clients load only signed descendants of the pinned release, so a rejected or stale candidate cannot replace the active model; and telemetry is a separate coarse channel, so neither prompts nor reports train the detector.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}
The browser extension injects page hooks for supported \texttt{fetch} and XHR POST requests and passes extracted text over native messaging to a local controller written in Rust; the extension manifest requests no loopback permissions. The controller runs analysis in sandboxed worker processes: by default a Python analysis worker serves every layer, and an optional Rust analysis worker (rules, NER with spaCy embedded, and semantics when a model cache is configured) serves the selections it claims. It fetches packages over mutual TLS with an installation certificate, checks pins and signed statements before use, and owns the telemetry reporter. The installed default is the pinned V3 balanced package; efficient, quality and older V1 profiles remain selectable. The server services (model streaming, telemetry collection, param-update with its checkpoint store and scheduler, the V3 fuzzer and V3 runtime) are written in Rust and run as containers, with routine training in an opt-in profile; each listener terminates mutual TLS against a client CA and revocation list and applies a per-call role policy, without a reverse proxy.

The installed probes, cost and telemetry measurements and security tests of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} were taken on an earlier prototype build, in which a loopback gRPC-Web bridge connected the extension to a Python runtime, the services sat behind a TLS ingress, and an opt-in V1 update service accepted gradient updates; \pendingStageThirteen{their re-measurement on the current build is pending}. Detector and model results used the prototype's Python analysis code, still the default worker; the optional Rust worker matches its regex and NER outputs byte for byte (NER via the spaCy pool) and its semantic-only decisions on pinned-release samples, without a full-pipeline rerun.

\subsection{Baselines and Comparison Protocols}
The RQ1 comparison processes the same cases through the product handler with rules and policy fixed. Historical and updated packages differ in tokenizer window, threshold, capacity and training history, so results compare complete packages rather than isolate architecture or pretraining. The assessment reuses 1,080 provisional cases; hyperparameters and artifact identity are in Appendix~\ref{app:retention}.

\subsection{Fresh Held-Out Protocol}
\label{sec:heldout_protocol}
Registered tests sampled fresh PIIMB sentences~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026} from source groups absent from development, training, validation and earlier studies. Data, source trees, scripts, hypotheses and margins were frozen before scoring; every call requested and verified [2,3]. Registration v2 compared N with O on F1 (16,446 sentences: 10,000 positive, 6,446 negative), F3 (2,000 long positive documents) and F2 (6,000 unlabelled natural prompts). After its recall gate failed, the owner reworked the rules. Registration v3 compared K on F4, a new set of 8,646 positive sentences from 5,745 source documents. F4 is positive-only; specificity checks reuse F1 negatives previously seen during rework and are not fresh confirmation.

The registered non-inferiority margins were 1.0 point for union recall, 0.5 for BLOCK recall, and the stated specificity guards. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample source groups (10,000 replicates, seed 20261024), and a claim passes only if its bound lies beyond the margin; post-hoc analyses are exploratory, and F2 results are flag rates because its prompts are unlabelled and unadjudicated.

\subsection{Adversarial Transformations}
\label{sec:adv_design}
The seed set is all 8,646 labelled-sensitive F4 rows (Section~\ref{sec:heldout_protocol}), a secondary, non-registered use after its registered use (H7, H8); the rows are disjoint from the design sets of the robustness flags and of K, but this is not a fresh confirmation. Twenty fixed, non-adaptive transforms in eight families (Unicode substitution, inserted spaces, spelling, template paraphrase, indirect reference, encoded identifiers, long-context placement, six-language translation) act on the annotated identifier spans; the frozen set of 169,371 applicable pairs was committed before scoring. The in-process runtime requested layers [2,3,4] (regex first, executed layers asserted) in two paired arms: shipped defaults and the four default-off robustness flags. The intended action is the action on the untransformed prompt, and ASR (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}) is primary at the action level, where the runtime's fail-closed BLOCK on a layer error counts as protection; the detection level counts such a BLOCK as a miss.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
For case $j$ with target sensitivity $s_j$, audience $v_j$, category set $C_j$ and allowed-action set $\mathcal A_j$, and prediction $(\hat s_j,\hat v_j,\hat C_j,\hat a_j)$, label, action and joint agreement average
\begin{equation}
L_j=\mathbf1\{\hat s_j=s_j,\hat v_j=v_j,\hat C_j=C_j\},\ \
A_j=\mathbf1\{\hat a_j\in\mathcal A_j\},\ \
J_j=L_jA_j .
\end{equation}
A case is binary-positive when $\hat s_j\ne\mathrm{S0}$ or $\hat C_j\neq\varnothing$; recall is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ and specificity $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$ over targets that are provisional labels on the contextual workload and source annotation presence on the final endpoint. For a paired correctness indicator $Z$, gains $G$ and losses $\Lambda$ count cases with $Z^{\rm old}=0,Z^{\rm new}=1$ and the reverse; the net change is $(G-\Lambda)/N$, always reported with both counts.

\textit{Attack success rate.} For a frozen labelled sensitive set $D$ and a prompt transformation $\tau$, let $d(x)=1$ when the evaluated pipeline's action on $x$ is WARN or BLOCK. A prompt \emph{evades} when it was detected before the transform and is missed after it. The conditional and unconditional rates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E(\tau)&=\{x\in D:d(x)=1,\ d(\tau(x))=0\},\\
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mid}(\tau)&=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|\{x\in D:d(x)=1\}|},\qquad
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm u}(\tau)=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|D|},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
each reported with a Wilson 95\% interval. Baseline detection $|\{d(x)=1\}|/|D|$ on untransformed prompts accompanies every rate. This is a product end-to-end measure and is never pooled with LLM-only results; its results are in Section~\ref{sec:eval_evasion}.

\subsection{Environments and Operational Protocols}
All installed, timing and telemetry runs used the earlier prototype build (Section~\ref{sec:implementation}). Thirty-three installed Linux Chromium probes and 73 headless Windows probes per browser (Chrome 157, Edge 157, a Playwright Firefox 159 build) ran against local synthetic receivers; historical timing covered 360 rules/NER decisions in twelve profiles and V3 timing 480 timed requests in four profiles; telemetry evidence comprises a finite audit, a 180-cell simulation and controlled fixtures without a detector or real users. These studies have distinct units and are not a user study; Appendix~\ref{app:environments} gives versions and protocols. Code, recipes and dated research records are at an anonymous repository (link withheld); some pinned checkpoints and raw artifacts remain local, so exact reruns need more than a Git checkout.

\subsection{Statistical Procedure}
Proportions carry Wilson 95\% intervals; paired outcomes use exact McNemar tests on discordant rows; cluster bootstraps resample whole source or scenario groups (2,000 to 10,000 replicates with recorded seeds). No multiplicity correction is applied, exploratory analyses are labelled as such, and intervals over repeated or correlated observations are noted where they are optimistic.

\subsubsection{Agreement on a Separate Annotation Target}
\label{sec:final_annotation}

\subsubsection{Guarded-Update Attacks (G2, G6)}
\label{sec:eval_update}
Ninety-one rows attacked the earlier prototype build's live V1 update service (a loopback host process over a private copy of the mutable artifact) and streaming service with synthetic inputs, snapshotting what each served before and after every attack; this is a negative-test observation, not a penetration test. A rejected attack meets its property only if the model is byte-identical afterwards, replays and duplicates must apply at most once, and a missing mechanism is unmet whatever the attack did. That build's standard V3 path had no update route: a legacy update for a V3 model id and an update call to the streaming port were rejected (Table~\ref{tab:updsec}, Appendix~\ref{app:secdetail}).

All three stale submissions, all 23 malformed-tensor, seven NaN or Inf and five wrong-shape attacks were rejected with the model unchanged. A byte-identical replay returned the original version without a second application, other replay variants were rejected, and eight concurrent identical submissions applied once. Interrupted updates behaved safely: a 1 ms deadline then retry applied once; ten hard kills left a valid artifact with exactly one application on retry; and a truncated artifact failed closed. Two properties were unmet. The magnitude bound was per coordinate only: 25 successive maximum-magnitude updates were accepted (cumulative per-coordinate drift 26), as was one update moving 41,876 coordinates by 1.0 (L2 norm 204.6). And the service had no publisher authentication: a peer reaching the port with no credential and any source identifier published an update. These were absent mechanisms, not bypasses of an implemented check. \pendingStageThirteen{The current build has no V1 update route; tests of its V3 gates and signed publication are pending.}

\subsubsection{Authentication and Communication Security (G7)}
\label{sec:eval_auth}
The prototype's services opened plaintext ports with no credential, token, expiry or peer-identity check, delegating transport identity to an ingress. Certificate rows ran against a stand-in mutual-TLS terminator, not that ingress: missing, self-signed and wrong-usage client certificates, untrusted or mismatched server certificates and a plaintext client were refused (7 of 7), as were expired and not-yet-valid certificates (3 of 3) and a foreign-CA certificate with the legitimate subject. Identity inside the system was unmet: any claimed source identifier was accepted, a bogus bearer token and API key were ignored, a second valid installation certificate had identical access, so installations could not be individually authorized or revoked, and retrieval needed no credential at the service. Further rows and controls are in Appendix~\ref{app:secdetail}. \pendingStageThirteen{The current services terminate mutual TLS themselves with a revocation list and role policy; these rows await re-measurement.}

\subsection{RQ3: Systems Overhead}
All RQ3 measurements were taken on the earlier prototype build. Historical V1 timings (Table~\ref{tab:browser_cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:browser}) end at the local rules/NER decision: all 240 ALLOW/WARN decisions ran both detectors, whereas the 120 BLOCK decisions stopped after rules; medians span 27.9--35.0 ms, and each maximum describes sixty requests across two profiles. For installed V3 balanced (Figure~\ref{fig:latency}, Table~\ref{tab:v3_installed_cost}), the four within-profile median increments per arm span 26.1--26.7 ms for rules/NER, 9.4--17.0 ms for semantics and 31.9--33.5 ms for full detection. All 480 timed attempts returned ALLOW, while quality blocked the same benign prompt once on each transport (request accounting in Appendix~\ref{app:browser}). These comparisons isolate detector activation for one short input, not total overhead relative to an absent extension or cold loading; with two profile clusters per cell and observed maxima up to 341.0 ms, they establish no service-level objective or tail guarantee. Product code, dependencies and images also differ between V1 and V3, and both studies timed the pre-change rules and NER; a sequential detector-layer pass found no meaningful latency difference between configurations (Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision}), but not in a browser.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
Detector order is part of the policy: an early rule BLOCK prevents later semantic evidence from changing the action, so removing rules corrected 139 unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen correct actions (85 net after revision). Fresh detector-layer tests found a specificity/recall trade-off: K passed H8 but lost 1.05 points of union recall against the original detectors, failed H7, and was shipped by owner decision despite the unmet promotion rule; the loss is source dependent. Low ASR (2.99\%) rests on baseline over-flagging (65.5\% of clean controls non-ALLOW) and fail-closed BLOCKs, so it does not show that identifiers are recognized; indirect references (13.22\%) and short-window dilution (15.05\%) are real evasion routes. The prototype's update path rejected every malformed, stale and replayed submission but lacked publisher authentication, credentials and a cumulative bound, so G2 and G6 held only for attacks that reached its checks; the current build adds these mechanisms, \pendingStageThirteen{but whether they close the gaps is not yet measured}. Masking (0.545 against 0.413) helps only where the detectors cover the span, at an over-redaction cost. Unlike Casper, HaS and ProSan~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}, \privoke sends unchanged content after WARN unless masking is enabled and leaves task completion unresolved after BLOCK.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
Independent contextual ground truth is the largest gap: the targets are assistant-provisional, and a frozen annotation-presence endpoint limits post-selection without validating authorization. Blinded tooling for at least two independent annotators is ready, but no human labels exist.

Exclusions do not establish template independence or absence of pretraining exposure; the final endpoint (308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row) is English and synthetic or machine-generated, and neither intervals, cluster resampling nor three seeds give population coverage. The detector-layer studies use one small English spaCy model and noisy labels; the 498-row endpoint is consumed, the student-identifier rule rests on one template, K's specificity rests on reused negatives and unlabelled flag rates, no multiplicity correction was applied, and no product or installed study includes K. The evasion experiment reuses F4 non-adaptively in process; the WARN experiment is detector-layer on development data; the installed, cost, telemetry and security measurements describe the earlier prototype build (security rows with synthetic legacy state and a stand-in TLS terminator); and the cross-platform probes are single headless runs on synthetic fixtures, leaving live providers, headed and packaged installs, release Firefox and Opera GX unverified. Installed probes cover selected paths; resident arms omit extension-absent and cold-load comparisons; one benign prompt cannot give a false-positive rate; the telemetry bound assumes independent uniform OS bits and a trusted clock, and neither the two telemetry cells nor a content-privacy bound establish utility or representative monitoring. The matched continuation establishes neither generalization nor publication-path security, the weak zero-head base and small guard limit what a pass would mean, and no routinely published V3 version is evaluated. Next steps are re-measuring the current build, adaptive attackers, a semantic-layer WARN evaluation, contextual adjudication and user evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke connects local prompt inspection to supported request enforcement, guarded and signed V3 updates and separate randomized telemetry. On an earlier prototype build, receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths, except Edge on Copilot. Updated packages improved agreement on 1,560 authored cases but annotation-presence specificity was low, and all matched full-model candidates failed retention. K, shipped by owner decision, passed H8 but retained a 1.05-point union-recall loss against the original detectors (95\% interval $-1.70$ to $-0.41$). Fixed transforms evade the product on 2.99\% of protected pairs (37.63\% of prompts by some variant), and masking prevents more warned transmissions than WARN at an over-redaction cost. The prototype's update service lacked publisher authentication, credentials and a cumulative bound, which the current Rust services add (\pendingStageThirteen{evaluation pending}). The evidence supports bounded, inspectable mechanisms only.

For other findings, the baseline checks are ordered. S0 with an empty category set yields ALLOW. S3 yields BLOCK and S2 yields WARN. PU denotes an unspecified audience and is not treated as restricted/private by this comparison. A category set is multilabel, so retaining one sensitive category is not equivalent to matching the exact target set.

The cascade then constrains what reaches aggregation. An early rule BLOCK ends inspection; later requested layers have not evaluated the text. Otherwise, aggregation preserves the strongest enforcement decision rather than replacing it with the last detector's action. In the runtime, a failed requested layer changes an otherwise ALLOW result to BLOCK (the extension forwards it with a warning), while a valid non-ALLOW finding remains visible. These semantics differ from a configuration in which an unavailable parameter stream leaves fewer enabled layers: a successfully completed reduced cascade is not a successful semantic execution.

Finally, a policy action and a receiver observation are different records. ALLOW and WARN release the original supported request, while BLOCK cancels it. Notice rendering under WARN does not imply acknowledgement, consent or removal of information from the body. For a probe expecting a send, the relevant observation is receiver arrival with the original body; for a no-send probe, the observation is absence of arrival within the declared observation window, including checks for a late send. The installed probes establish these bounded outcomes, not an absence of leakage through every browser transport.

\section{Telemetry Estimation and Admission}
\label{app:reporting}
The reporter compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw, $J/2^{53}$, with a binary64 threshold $\widetilde p_i$ and chooses alternatives by rejection sampling, so its finite probabilities and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The ideal mechanism and audited finite implementation in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism} are analyzed for the content of a fixed reporting opportunity under public configuration. The finite bound includes the reported excess over nominal privacy loss. For a category whose true contributor count is $h$ among $n$ reports, and observed randomized count $Y$, the nominal inverse-response estimate before clipping is
\begin{equation}
\widehat h=\frac{Y-nq}{p-q}.
\end{equation}
The collector clips each estimated marginal separately to $[0,n]$. Inverting the nominal probabilities is not the same as inverting the exact finite kernel, and clipping is nonlinear: the expected clipped value need not equal $h$. Independently clipped categories also need not sum to $n$. These properties explain why privacy loss, bias, RMSE and total coherence are separate checks.

The synthetic trials fix the contributor histogram before sampling the randomized reports. Their error therefore excludes uncertainty about which people or events enter that histogram. Increasing $n$ means more admitted reports, pooled across installation-days where necessary, rather than relaxing the per-installation daily cap. A precision requirement for an operational decision was not prespecified; the two illustrative category cells cannot establish one after the fact.

The operational accounting also has distinct denominators. An opportunity may be suppressed before queuing, a reservation may be consumed without a delivered packet, and an accepted report represents a received randomized event. The observed totals reconcile as $308=184+124$ opportunities and $184=168+16$ reserved events, where the latter partition is accepted reports plus submission failures in the controlled fixtures. These are not counts of users, unique prompts or detector decisions in a deployment.

The ledger reserves before construction and does not refund failed construction or delivery. That ordering protects accounting against retries within retained state, but cannot hide whether a report was sent. A cap can also change event composition before randomization, as the retained order-reversal example demonstrates. Correcting the randomized counts would still leave that admission mechanism to justify. \subsection*{Detector-layer protocol details}
\label{sec:detprec_protocol}
This detector-layer study explicitly requests regex and NER, never semantics. It compares pre-change detectors with the first revision; the later student-identifier rule and K rework are absent. The 502-row development data are split by source-group hash into dev-TUNE (267; 148 positive) and dev-CHECK (235; 116 positive); design material also includes 2,000 personalized and 1,000 OpenAI-generated rows. The post-filter used dev-TUNE and personalized data; dev-CHECK and OpenAI results for its revised version are post-review. The inputs consulted while designing regex repairs were not separately recorded, so those splits are treated as design material. The initial endpoint contains 498 rows and was scored once per configuration after defaults froze, but had already been opened in another unit; paired outcomes were later reconstructed and the endpoint is now consumed. The student rule was diagnosed after this scoring. Labels are source annotation presence, not contextual authorization.

A positive is any nonzero sensitivity or nonempty category. We report confusion counts, recall, specificity and latency for regex, NER and their union. Paired McNemar tests use discordant rows; paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 replicates, fixed seed) resample documents on real sets and rows on synthetic sets, with scenario-label sensitivity analyses (20 personalized and 10 OpenAI scenarios). No multiplicity correction is applied. Runtime action and weighted-error comparisons are separate outcomes.

\textit{Rule gates.} Kinship terms and the word ``family'' need a possessive determiner (my, our, his, her, their), optionally followed by up to two modifier words; child cues need a possessive and a child noun, an age expression, or an age and a person noun (a five-year-old laptop does not match). Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns after a possessive, and location keywords need a connector such as ``live in'' followed by a non-closed-class capture. Structured identity values stay on one line (at most five tokens) and a phone label needs a number-like value. Checksum validators reject shape-only hits for card numbers (Luhn~\cite{Luhn1960Patent}), IBANs (mod-97~\cite{ISO13616Part1_2020}), NHS numbers (mod-11) and US social security numbers (issuable ranges), and context matching is word-bounded. Password placeholders, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. A separate student-identifier rule (S3 identity) needs a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student; it postdates Sections~\ref{sec:detector_precision} and~\ref{sec:product_rerun} and is part of N and K in Section~\ref{sec:heldout}.

\textit{Labelled identifiers and downgrades (K).} Two rules (S3 and S2 identity) match a label noun (id, identifier, number, no, num, nr, code or reference, in words, snake or camel case, key-value form, or a hash sign after the qualifier) followed by a value. S3 qualifiers cover national and government identity documents, tax, health and insurance, bank account and payment-card labels, and the acronyms SSN, TFN, NINO, EIN, ITIN, MRN and NRIC need no noun; S2 qualifiers cover customer, account, member, employee, user, policy, claim, plate, loyalty and similar labels. A value is tokens of letters, digits and underscores joined by hyphens or single spaces, with a digit in its first token (or a one- to three-letter prefix before a digit group), five to twenty-four compact characters, and not a date; software licence names and order, build, ticket, part and promotion codes are excluded. Downgrades keep a hit whose checksum fails when a card, banking or social-security word is in context (S3), and keep an eight-character token with five or more digits at S2 after an IBAN check fails without a banking word. Digit spaces are removed before matching.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 1.} Five span rules apply in turn to the output of the small English spaCy model~\cite{Honnibal2020SpaCy}, which uses capitalization as evidence for names: placeholder masks; identifier-like tokens (e-mail, URL, underscore, code); spans inside headings (except multi-token or cue-introduced names); short acronyms without a locating or affiliation preposition; and single-token person candidates that the model's word list contains and the tagger reads as non-proper nouns without a corroborating cue.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 2.} The first matching rule decides: a person linked with a corroborated place or organization yields S2; that place or organization yields S2; a corroborated person yields S2; a place or organization tied to the speaker by a relation cue (or in the same prompt as one) keeps its default; a facility beside a number yields S2; a single uncorroborated person yields S1; and a lone place or organization is dropped. Corroboration means evidence about the entity itself (a multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring person, or a place or organization with a personal-relation cue); a bare locating preposition does not corroborate.

\textit{Development-only ablations.} Table~\ref{tab:detprec_ablation} holds the repaired rules fixed and varies the NER input and filter on design and development splits (dev-CHECK and OpenAI are post-review; none is held out). Case preservation raises or preserves union recall but lowers specificity wherever there is margin to lose (to 0.824, 0.832 and 0.814 on dev-TUNE, dev-CHECK and OpenAI, and 0.919 on the personalized set) because capitalized non-names enter; the post-filter recovers most of it on case-preserved input and, on lowercased input, lowers recall slightly while raising specificity. Counting only non-organization entities moved development NER-only recall from 0.170 to 0.333 and specificity from 0.996 to 0.937 between lowercased and case-preserved input.

Allowed-action agreement rose for every package (V3 balanced 1,409 to 1,438: 36 improved, 7 regressed, $p<0.001$), and binary false positives for that package fell from 119 to 95 with false negatives unchanged at 8. Joint agreement did not change significantly for V3 ($p$ from 0.26 to 0.81) and fell by five for V1 balanced (2 improved, 7 regressed, $p=0.18$): the rule repairs alone added 28 joint-correct cases without a loss while the new NER findings altered the label set on 25 cases, each in favour of removing NER. V3 stayed ahead of V1 in every tier (allowed-action gains against losses 388/5, 413/12 and 337/3, against 389/3, 389/6 and 348/0 historically).

The ablation persists but shrinks: removing rules corrected 103 actions and lost 18 (net 85, $p<10^{-14}$; family interval for the action-rate gain 2.4 to 8.9 points, against 3.5 to 12.7 historically), and under-restriction again rose from 12 to 30. Revised NER converts 48 findings, all identity names (alone it flags 36 of the 810 sensitive and 12 of the 750 clean cases); No-NER equals the full product on 1,526 of 1,560 cases, and removing NER improves 7 action and 25 joint outcomes with no regression, so on this fixture NER is a net negative at the label level, which does not establish its value or harm elsewhere. These are measurements on the same provisional labels and correlated families, scored twice, so not a fresh confirmation; the ablation uses one balanced package, and neither the student-identifier rule nor K is part of the rerun, so the shipped configuration has not been run through the full product.

\textit{Evasion design.} The transforms act on annotated identifier spans: Cyrillic or Greek homoglyphs and zero-width characters, a space between every span character, one transposition, deletion or doubling, template paraphrase from a declared 35-entry lexicon and six frames, initials or a trailing-digits cue and a family-keyed pointer (the pointer removes the value, so the table also reports rates without it), base64, hex and spelled digits, 800-character and 6,000-character filler placements, and six-language translation templates. The runtime is called through its native servicer in process with V3 balanced loaded through the PIPE3 adapter; the benign control uses 1,000 clean F1 rows with the entity-free variants. Intervals are Wilson over pairs, with a source-document bootstrap for the overall rate (2.88--3.10 at the action level).

Further update rows: a replay without a request identifier, under another source, or with a reused identifier and different payload was rejected; a same-size transposed shape and a two-tensor update with one wrong tensor applied nothing; a stray temporary file did not block the next update; a truncated artifact made the updater unhealthy and the streaming service unavailable; the V3 release store was unchanged; a forged publication left the forged source in the artifact; and that stack published the update service only on its internal network. Further authentication rows: the certificate rows show the TLS stack's semantics, while the ingress configuration was covered only by static checks and a smoke test not executed here; an arbitrary or empty consumer identifier was served the legacy model, the V3 manifest and an asset; the receipt-status lookup answered unauthenticated peers; retrieval replays were read-only; and unknown models, path traversal, a wrong manifest pin and non-allowlisted assets were rejected.

Update and authentication rows used the private copy of the legacy artifact, snapshotted before and after each attack, and ten kill delays on one machine, with Windows process termination standing in for a kill signal; they show no violation, not its absence. Test identities were generated per run. A kill during the atomic write can leave a temporary file beside the artifact that no startup code removes; this was seen only in unrecorded development runs and its effect on serving was not tested. The update tests exercised the legacy artifact only and did not measure the effect of a bounded hostile update on detection quality; telemetry authentication, the fuzzer service and the browser extension were outside them. The evasion experiment's stored scores and frozen sets contain F4-derived text and stay local; the records carry the aggregates and file hashes. Its first launch ran out of memory in five of twelve baseline shards and the loader was changed to stream per shard before scoring resumed; a duplicate orchestration produced no duplicate or conflicting records. Frozen sets, transforms and analysis definitions did not change. The records are \path{adversarial-evasion-20261116.md}, \path{update-security-20261116.md} and \path{warn-intervention-20261116.md} under \texttt{paper/research/}.
