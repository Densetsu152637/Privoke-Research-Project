\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to hosted language models can disclose personal or confidential information before a provider processes them. Prior work offers local inspection, sanitization and policy-classification approaches; we examine how these mechanisms connect to request enforcement, guarded model revision and operational reporting in one workflow. PriVoke combines local inspection, ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK handling on supported requests (ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels), version-bound detector updates with a retention guard, and separate randomized telemetry. On 1,560 authored cases with provisional targets, updated packages improved agreement; 33 installed receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths. All matched full-model candidates improved aggregate agreement but failed the configured retention guard. Registered fresh detector-layer tests found that the shipped rework retained level BLOCK recall but lost 1.05 points of union recall against the original detectors and did not meet the promotion rule. Telemetry delivery and accounting worked in controlled fixtures, while finite-arithmetic loss slightly exceeded nominal privacy budgets and simulations showed substantial estimation error. These findings support bounded mechanism claims, not independently validated contextual judgments, universal interception, an accepted retention-qualified update, useful live monitoring, or user benefit. Adaptive end-to-end evasion and communication-authentication tests remain outstanding.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Local differential privacy, local inference, model regression, prompt privacy, randomized telemetry, request enforcement.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Hosted large language models (LLMs) support writing, coding and analysis, but prompts may include another person's details or confidential material~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing,SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Provider controls on retention act after the initial transfer. Running the application model locally is one alternative, but changes the chosen service and its computational demands~\cite{Llama2023}. We study the intervening point: inspecting selected requests on the user's device before handoff.

The privacy question is contextual: the same information may be appropriate or inappropriate to disclose depending on whose information it is, the audience and purpose~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}. Recognition alone cannot establish authorization. Nor does a warning necessarily prevent transfer. PriVoke combines rules, named entity recognition (NER) and semantic classification to represent sensitivity, audience and overlapping categories, then selects ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. ALLOW and WARN send the original body; BLOCK cancels a recognized request. WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. Because an early rule BLOCK ends the detector cascade, both detector order and request enforcement affect observed behavior. Work on reproducing sensitive prompt information in chatbot outputs concerns a later point in the lifecycle~\cite{priyanshu2023chatbots}; pre-transmission inspection cannot reverse that disclosure.

The system also separates detector maintenance from reporting. A server runtime fits candidates from supplied labelled examples; a version-bound retention guard may reject a candidate before publication. This addresses the risk that aggregate gains conceal regressions on previously correct cases~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, but only for the guard's declared examples and criteria. A separate client randomizes coarse telemetry under a local budget ledger. Neither prompts nor telemetry train the detector. We study these connected but distinct mechanisms empirically; the contribution is their evaluation in one workflow, not a new detector or privacy primitive. We measure policy agreement, receiver-observed enforcement, update eligibility, runtime cost and event-content privacy separately.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An implemented client path connecting local layered inspection to explicit request handling on supported browser requests.
\item A version-bound update workflow with a casewise retention guard, evaluated through matched candidates that improved aggregate agreement but did not qualify for publication.
\item A separate randomized telemetry path evaluated for finite event-content privacy, budget accounting, delivery and estimation error.
\item An empirical account of trade-offs across prediction, enforcement, update eligibility and reporting, without treating provisional targets as validated contextual truth.
\end{enumerate}

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1}: Does PriVoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively?
\item \textbf{RQ2}: Can PriVoke update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior?
\item \textbf{RQ3}: What systems and privacy overhead does PriVoke introduce?
\end{enumerate}
RQ1 separates agreement with declared targets from observed transmission; RQ2 asks whether aggregate gains satisfy a casewise retention rule; RQ3 separates resident system cost and event-content privacy from estimation error and selection. These distinctions define what the evidence can support and where independent contextual judgment remains absent.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local intervention and contextual decisions.} Casper combines browser-side rules, entity recognition and topic analysis; its reviewed prototype replaces identifiers and requires acknowledgement for topic warnings~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}. Hide and Seek (HaS) hides entities before cloud inference and recovers them in responses, while ProSan learns selective word replacement from task-importance and privacy proxies~\cite{HaS2024,ProSan2024}. PromptShield classifies enterprise prompts by role and policy~\cite{PromptShield2025}. These works address sanitization, task continuation or policy classification. PriVoke instead measures a request contract in which supported hooks act automatically and WARN forwards unchanged content without acknowledgement. Across these approaches, detector labels alone do not establish contextual authorization; that question requires its own targets and evaluation.

\textit{Retaining behavior during model revision.} Negative-flip research measures when an update turns a previously correct prediction into an error; Yan et al. propose positive-congruent training for image classifiers~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. This motivates paired gains and losses, but does not establish privacy-policy correctness. PriVoke applies the distinction to an update decision: candidates are compared with their declared base and can be rejected under a configured retention guard. The measured candidates show why aggregate improvement is insufficient for acceptance; the guard remains limited to its declared cases and harm rule.

\textit{Privacy of operational reporting.} Local randomization supports aggregate collection without directly releasing each underlying value. RAPPOR applies randomized response to client-side statistics and addresses the additional privacy cost of repeated reporting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}. PriVoke instead combines categorical event reports with a local budget ledger. We examine finite arithmetic, estimation error and contribution selection separately: event-content protection does not conceal participation or network metadata, and accurate marginals would not by themselves establish representative monitoring.

\textit{Contribution and comparison boundaries.} PriVoke does not claim a new detector, regression method or privacy primitive. Its contribution is the empirical connection of established mechanisms: RQ1 pairs local decisions with receiver observations; RQ2 separates learning gains from version-bound update eligibility; RQ3 measures system cost and distinguishes event-content privacy from estimation and admission. The cited systems use different tasks, targets and denominators, so no numerical ranking is justified.

\section{Threat Model and Security Goals}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
PriVoke addresses prompt text in recognized browser requests before handoff to an external language-model provider. Protected assets include the request text, local detector/model state, update artifacts and credentials, and randomized telemetry plus its budget ledger. The user, enabled extension, page hook, local runtime and workstation are trusted to perform inspection and preserve local state. The provider and network observer are not trusted: a provider receives the original prompt after ALLOW or WARN, while an observer may see report presence and transport metadata. Event-content randomization does not hide these facts.

We consider prompt authors who modify text to evade detection and update senders who submit stale, duplicate, malformed or unauthorized candidates. The implementation has normalization and detector layers for prompt variation, and parent-version, tensor-bound, serialized-publication and receipt checks for updates. These are implemented controls, not evidence of an adaptive attack or comprehensive security campaign. The inspected proxy requires client certificates on model-streaming and telemetry routes, but end-to-end authentication and authorization are not established, particularly for update submission. Negative tests of invalid or expired credentials, impersonation, unauthorized retrieval/publication and cross-identity replay have not been reported as executed. We exclude compromised workstations, operating systems, extensions and local runtimes, which could expose prompts or alter decisions and accounting.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
\textit{Request enforcement (G1).} When the active page hook recognizes a supported request and the runtime returns BLOCK, the hook cancels it before the page's native sender hands it off. ALLOW and WARN release the original body, and WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. The installed probes in Section~\ref{sec:results} test this contract on selected paths; they do not establish coverage of unsupported transports.

The runtime normalizes text while retaining offsets. Regex and NER receive case-preserved text; semantic models receive lowercased normalized text. Rules run first and an early rule BLOCK ends the cascade; otherwise, the strongest action is retained. A failed requested layer turns an otherwise-ALLOW result into BLOCK. If the parameter stream is lost, however, full mode can continue with rules and NER and forward without semantic inspection. We therefore distinguish the returned action, executed layers and receiver observation.



\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic model predicts sensitivity, audience (called visibility in the implementation) and overlapping information categories. Sensitivity ranges from S0 (no identified risk) through S1 and S2 to S3 (highly sensitive information, identifiers or secrets); visibility spans public (P0), semi-public (P1), restricted/group-private/personal-private (P2--P4) and unspecified (PU). Categories include health, politics, religion, criminal, financial, sexual, child, location, identity and third-party information.

The policy ordinarily maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 allow unless identity and location co-occur, or either occurs with P2--P4; these cases WARN. S0 without categories always allows. Confidence below 0.5 overrides this mapping: S3 becomes WARN, S2 remains WARN only with identity or location, and other cases ALLOW. This score is not calibrated joint confidence or evidence of authorization. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives the complete order. The compact-model precedent motivates these package comparisons~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}, but they change multiple factors and do not isolate an architectural cause. The pretrained V3 packages use BERT encoders~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}; the installed default is V3 balanced. Model revisions and package identities are retained in the research records.

\subsection{Detector Precision Controls}
\label{sec:detector_precision_design}
Rules and NER precede semantic classification, and a rule BLOCK ends the cascade. The precision revision therefore targets unnecessary early findings while retaining the possibility of missed detections. The policy mapping and semantic models are unchanged.





\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
A candidate is eligible only at the publication stage whose configured quality and retention tests it passes. In the legacy workflow, the updater requests labelled batches and held-out guard cases; a server runtime computes bounded changes against its cached base. Training is centralized on supplied examples, not user gradients or federated data. Rejection at a later stage does not undo an earlier head stage already accepted.

Before publication, the service checks model identity, parent version and tensor names, shapes, finiteness and magnitude. Serialized publication rechecks the parent, atomically replaces the artifact, and records durable receipts so identical retries recover the prior outcome. Clients verify delivered artifacts before refreshing. Standard V3 remains a pinned, read-only inference path. These controls are intended to constrain stale, duplicate and malformed updates; they do not authenticate the sender or guarantee behavioral safety. Test code covers validation and receipt scenarios, but is not itself a passing security campaign. In the matched RQ2 study, all full-model candidates failed the configured guard and none was published.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
The official client emits five coarse randomized fields: action, risk bucket, precedence-selected category, model-version bucket, and four-hour time-of-day bin. The schema omits the prompt, identifiers, exact confidence and per-layer findings. Client and collector test code checks the field set and aggregate storage; controlled operation tests delivery and accounting, not absence of prompt text from every local log, memory surface or channel. The collector estimates lifetime marginals, but lacks denominators and time-window queries needed to estimate latency or error rates. Telemetry does not provide training labels.

\textit{Event-content privacy (G4).} The randomized-response mechanism is intended to limit how much the distribution of a released tuple can change between possible event contents~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. This comparison fixes a reporting opportunity and public configuration; it does not randomize whether a report exists. The event budget $\varepsilon$ is divided equally across fields as $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. For a domain of size $K_i$, the true value has reporting probability $p_i$ and each alternative has probability $q_i$:
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}.
\end{equation}
Under independent ideal sampling, composition multiplies the field likelihood ratios to bound the event ratio by $e^\varepsilon$~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. That is the nominal ideal mechanism, not an exact guarantee for the finite implementation. The client compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw, $J/2^{53}$, with a binary64 threshold $\widetilde p_i$, choosing alternatives by rejection sampling. Its finite probabilities and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite audit reported in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_results} found $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ slightly above nominal $\varepsilon$ for the audited host settings. It therefore supports only the corresponding implementation-specific finite bound under the audit's independent-uniform-randomness assumption, not exact nominal epsilon across builds or deployment environments. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves nominal budget before packet construction, defaulting to one per event and eight per Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day. Failed construction or delivery does not refund that reservation; accounting depends on retained ledger state and a trusted clock. The installation-local cap is not user-level accounting across devices or days.

Report presence, exact accepted counts, timing, source IP, contribution selection, delivery and other network metadata remain observable or outside this content guarantee. The collector estimates marginals by inverting nominal response probabilities and clipping counts to their feasible range, so the privacy mechanism alone does not establish useful or representative monitoring. The controlled telemetry results in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_results} report accounting and delivery behavior separately from the finite privacy audit and estimation error.
\subsection{Implementation}
The browser extension injects page hooks for supported \texttt{fetch} and XHR POST requests. The extension background routes extracted text through a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to the local Python runtime; native messaging launches the supervisor, and the native-host manifest restricts launches to the registered extension identity. The default local package is the verified, pretrained V3 balanced model; standard V3 inference is read-only. The separate legacy updater, fuzzer, and parameter service implement candidate fitting, validation, version-bound publication, and artifact refresh. The official reporter randomizes its five coarse fields and reserves budget in installation-local state before delivery; the collector stores lifetime marginals. These are distinct runtime paths: neither prompt inspection nor telemetry supplies detector-training labels.

\subsection{Detector Precision Protocol}
This study explicitly requested regex and NER, excluding semantics and product actions. It compares the pre-change configuration with the first revision on design, development, synthetic and previously opened endpoint data; post-review and one-shot status are disclosed with the results. \subsection{Fresh Held-Out Protocol}
\label{sec:heldout_protocol}
To obtain new detector-layer evidence, registered tests sampled fresh PIIMB sentences~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026} from source groups absent from development, training, validation and earlier studies. Data, source trees, scripts, hypotheses and margins were frozen before scoring; every call requested and verified [2,3]. Registration v2 compared N with O on F1 (16,446 sentences: 10,000 positive, 6,446 negative), F3 (2,000 long positive documents) and F2 (6,000 unlabelled natural prompts). After its recall gate failed, the owner reworked the rules. Registration v3 compared K on F4, a new set of 8,646 positive sentences from 5,745 source documents. F4 is positive-only; specificity checks reuse F1 negatives previously seen during rework and are not fresh confirmation.

The registered non-inferiority margins were 1.0 point for union recall, 0.5 for BLOCK recall, and the stated specificity guards. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals resample source groups (10,000 replicates, seed 20261024); a claim passes only if its bound lies beyond the margin. Discordant-row counts and exact McNemar values accompany deltas. Post-hoc layer, rule and stratum analyses are exploratory. F2 results are flag rates because its prompts are unlabelled and the flagged sample has not been adjudicated.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Telemetry Protocols}
Thirty-three installed Linux Chromium probes exercised the extension, native installer, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector. A local HTTPS receiver checked original-body sends, BLOCK outcomes and late arrivals; these synthetic observations do not establish provider authentication or cross-platform compatibility.

Historical timing measured three synthetic actions over fetch and XHR: twelve profiles and 360 measured rules/NER decisions. V3 timing used two fresh profiles per transport, a fixed benign prompt, detectors-off/rules-NER/semantic/full arms, reversed order, 80 warmups and 480 timed requests. Its off arm retained extension infrastructure; within-profile median differences estimate resident detector activation, not total or cold-start cost. Runtime and package execution were asserted on Torch 2.11 CPU (four threads), Python 3.12.3 and Chromium 153.0.8010.12.

The telemetry audit evaluated exact finite kernels on fingerprinted Windows CPython 3.11.9 for budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 under independent uniform OS bits, using outward 100-digit arithmetic. Simulation covered 180 prespecified cells across four contributor counts, five field domains and three histogram types, with 10,000 repetitions per cell (seed 20261014; NumPy 1.26.4/PCG64); nominal inversion and clipping matched the collector. Separate controlled fixtures tested the reporter, ledger and collector without a detector or real users, including caps, persistence, ordering, outage/recovery and malformed packets. Report-call timing excludes background delivery. These studies have distinct units and are not a user study.

\textit{Artifact availability.} Tracked recipes in \texttt{evaluation/} and dated records in \texttt{paper/research/} identify configurations through source, model and data fingerprints and environment receipts. They distinguish product/ablation dependencies, including historical uncommitted sources, from matched continuation, final semantics, historical and standard-V3 client captures, telemetry, and the detector-precision unit with its follow-up (\path{detector-precision-20261024.md} and \path{detector-precision-followup-20261024.md}). Some pinned checkpoints and raw run artifacts remain local and ignored. Exact reruns therefore require more than a Git checkout, and public distribution of those assets has not been established.

These gains concern reused provisional targets; no package was promoted.

Execution records identify a shared source of unnecessary restriction. All updated packages over-restricted the same 139 cases: rules blocked 109 clean controls before later detectors could run and warned on thirty low-risk controls. Rules ran on every case, ending the cascade on 289 and leaving NER and semantics to process 1,271. A better downstream semantic model cannot undo these earlier decisions. These rule-block counts are historical measurements of the pre-change rules; Section~\ref{sec:product_rerun} reports the rerun with the revised detectors, under which the shared figure falls to 108. In the historical configuration NER produced zero converted PriVoke findings after supported-label filtering, although this does not mean that the recognizer was inactive or returned no raw entities; that statement does not hold for the revised detectors.

\label{sec:detector_ablation}
The fixed-package ablation examines this explanation by comparing agreement with under- and over-restriction (Table~\ref{tab:targeted_ablation}). Removing rules corrected all 139 shared unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen previously correct actions. Its net gain of 121 actions therefore came at the cost of missed protection under the provisional policy. Paired action-rate gains ranged from 3.46--12.70 percentage points by family and 5.96--9.23 by audience expression. These separate descriptive ranges support the direction of the tradeoff without establishing that its losses are acceptable. \subsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
Across thirty-three correctness probes, the installed-browser capture connected decisions to eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes. Correlated runtime and receiver records, including late-arrival checks, established these outcomes without a decision broker. Healthy full-pipeline and sole-semantic probes also verified execution, linking request disposition to the detector path actually exercised.

Fault probes exposed the difference between keeping a request path available and preserving its protection. When the parameter stream was interrupted, semantic-only requests were blocked, but configured full-mode fallback ran rules and NER and forwarded the request. These completed observations were kept separate from nine earlier failed captures.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke improved agreement with provisional contextual policy and enforced its declared request contract on the tested installed paths. Action errors, unchanged-content forwarding under WARN and weak specificity on the separate annotation-presence task constrain the broader effectiveness claim. A later detector-layer revision targets the rule and NER false detections behind part of that restriction. A full-product rerun measured the first revision's effect on actions, but the shipped rework was evaluated only at the detector layers, where registered fresh tests found a real union-recall cost against the original detectors alongside a specificity gain. The results do not yet establish independent contextual accuracy, provider compatibility, prevention for every sensitive prompt or practical usability.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
RQ1 shows why detector order is part of the policy: an early rule BLOCK prevents later semantic evidence from changing the action. Historically, removing rules corrected 139 unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen previously correct actions. The revised full-product rerun lowered shared unnecessary restrictions to 108, yet rule removal still gained 85 actions while losing the same eighteen. Both results rely on provisional targets. Fresh detector-layer tests found a specificity/recall trade-off: the first revision improved union specificity but failed its recall margin; the shipped rework restored level BLOCK recall but still lost 1.05 points of union recall against the original detectors. That loss is source-dependent, and some specificity checks reused negatives seen during rework. The owner shipped K despite its failure to meet the registered promotion rule. These findings describe trade-offs, not a universally suitable detector setting.

Comparison with local sanitization clarifies who controls continuation. Casper's acknowledged warnings leave a decision to the user, while HaS and ProSan transform content to support continued use~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}. PriVoke instead sends unchanged content after WARN and leaves task completion unresolved after BLOCK. Receiver compliance verifies that contract, not whether it is desirable. That judgment needs evidence about disclosure authorization, warning comprehension and the effort to reformulate prompts.

Execution traces and ablation answer complementary questions. Traces locate the layer that produced an action and identify layers that did not run; matched removal measures the resulting action changes on the fixture. Neither alone determines whether the trade-off is acceptable to users. Fewer unnecessary restrictions and more missed protections remain distinct outcomes.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
Contextual integrity concerns appropriate information flows~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}, for which entity recognition supplies only part of the judgment. Independent contextual ground truth remains the largest scientific gap because PriVoke's authored targets are assistant-provisional. Freezing the separate annotation-presence endpoint before opening limits post-selection, but neither its different target nor its sole-semantic execution validates contextual authorization. Improved authored-target agreement and poor final specificity consequently answer different questions, neither establishing human agreement, appropriate intervention or task utility.

A stronger evaluation should define whose information may flow to which audience and for what purpose before annotators see system outputs. At least two independent annotators should label a fresh sample, with agreement and adjudication reported. The existing annotation-presence endpoint remains informative about discrimination against its source labels, including its low specificity, but it cannot validate contextual authorization.

Group and text exclusions reduce declared overlap, while paired cluster analyses show sensitivity to known dependence. Neither establishes template independence, paraphrase exclusion or absence of pretraining exposure. The final endpoint comprises 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row. These English synthetic or machine-generated cases do not represent other languages or organizational policies, and neither unadjusted intervals nor three seeds provide a population guarantee. Confirmation after opening this endpoint requires a new protected successor.

The detector-layer study uses one small English spaCy model and source annotation labels with unquantified noise; S1 findings count as positive. The post-filter was developed on design material, and dev-CHECK/OpenAI became post-review after revisions. The 498-row endpoint was already opened for another unit and is now consumed; its paired rerun does not create fresh confirmation. The student-identifier rule was added later and rests on one template. The fresh F4 draw tests that rule but contains positives only; specificity checks reuse negatives seen during rework. Synthetic splits lack template identifiers, no multiplicity correction was applied, and small discordant counts make interval estimates uncertain. These English benchmark-derived data do not establish multilingual or population performance.

The fresh held-out evidence has its own limits. F4 contains positive sentences only, so K's specificity rests on negatives that O and N had already been scored on and whose results informed the rework, and on unlabelled natural prompts whose flag rates cannot be read as error rates. The benchmark pool is exhausted, so no further confirmation draw is possible without a new source. Its annotation is incomplete by construction and includes attribute spans (age, title, gender) for which PriVoke has no detector, which is why recall is far from 100\%, and label noise was not audited. The F2 adjudication and the label-noise audit remain unperformed. The K qualifier vocabulary mirrors labels present in the development spans, so labelled-identifier gains are tied to the benchmark's labelling conventions. The registered promotion rule was not met, and K is shipped by owner decision. K's recall cost against the original detectors is real, source dependent and partly attributable to the NER layer it shares with the first revision. Exploratory layer, rule, stratum and span analyses were produced after the registered results and cannot confirm anything. Neither the full-product rerun nor the installed probes and cost studies include K, and the default-off robustness fixes and larger NER models are not part of the shipped configuration. All detector-layer results use one small English spaCy model and annotation-presence labels, not contextual authorization.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
Matching the base and exposure reduces RQ2 configuration confounding without retrospectively isolating the RQ1 package effect. Consistent with negative-flip research~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, all matched full-model candidates improved aggregate assessment yet failed the guard. Under the configured rule, higher scores cannot override casewise rejection, and no candidate was published. The weak zero-head base and small guard also constrain the significance of a pass, motivating independently reviewed retention cases and explicit criteria for harm.

The meaning of a harm criterion matters as much as its pass rate. A label change that preserves an allowed action differs from a change that releases previously blocked content; an unnecessary BLOCK imposes a different cost from under-restriction. The maintained guard uses a single derived action and the assessment uses allowed sets, so their disagreements need not be contradictions. A larger, independently justified guard could alter eligibility in either direction. The present result establishes the configured decision and the measured gains and losses, without establishing that the guard represents every relevant harm or that rejected weights lack all useful information.

Operational version checks address another failure mode. Binding a candidate to its exact parent can prevent an update evaluated against one model from overwriting a different revision, but cannot make its labels correct. Likewise, a verified download shows artifact integrity, not behavioral suitability. Standard V3's pinned inference path and the separately enabled legacy update service must remain distinguishable: this paper reports installed V3 inference and bounded continuation evidence, not a deployed V3 learning loop.

Blocking when analysis is unavailable can prevent disclosure while interrupting an authorized task; WARN avoids that interruption but proceeds without informed acknowledgement. Heuristic confidence cannot resolve the choice because it is not calibrated joint correctness. Installed Linux evidence now covers historical V1 enforcement and standard V3 loading and cost, yet the quality-tier benign-prompt BLOCKs demonstrate why installation alone is not accuracy validation. Authenticated providers, Windows, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified. No WARN+CONFIRM or WARN+MASK/EDIT intervention was evaluated. Without evidence on authorized task completion, unnecessary intervention, warning comprehension and reformulation effort, practical utility and human benefit remain open.

\subsection{Measurement Boundaries and Next Steps}
Matched resident arms quantify semantic and full-detector activation, but retain instrumentation and omit extension-absent and cold-load comparisons needed to establish total overhead. For reporting, RAPPOR motivates repeated-report accounting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}; PriVoke's ledger depends on retained state and a trusted clock, and its finite bound assumes independent uniform OS bits. Accounting leaves estimation error and admission-order effects unresolved, so privacy, estimation and representative admission require separate justification. The current deployment has too few concurrent users for a live-usage usefulness study, and more users alone would not repair the missing fields. Lifetime categorical marginals and an operator Health probe cannot replace latency/error and usage-denominator instrumentation, nor has a deployment-usefulness threshold been established. Participation, delivery selection and metadata remain outside the bound, and aggregates supply no corrected training labels.

A reporting design must consequently specify its intended estimand before increasing collection. The distribution among admitted, successfully delivered events can differ from the distribution among all attempted decisions, installations or users. A daily cap selects early events; delivery failures can further select which reservations become visible. Even an exact estimator of the admitted histogram would not correct those differences without additional assumptions. Adding richer fields or denominators could help answer operational questions, but would change both the disclosure surface and the privacy analysis. Such a redesign and its validation are future work, rather than benefits established by the current collector.

Reproducibility has a related boundary. A readable recipe and a fingerprint allow an available artifact to be identified, while a successful fresh rerun additionally requires the referenced weights, inputs, compatible dependencies and permissions. The retained records disclose local and ignored assets so that absence from a public checkout is not mistaken for a complete reproduction package. Future release work should resolve distribution and environment requirements independently of the manuscript's empirical conclusions.

A human double-annotation protocol for contextual targets, with blinded stratified sampling and agreement statistics, has been prepared but not run; running it, together with adjudicating a flagged sample of natural prompts, is the most direct route to independent evidence for the detector findings above.

Across the lifecycle, early restrictions can survive later model improvements, improved candidates can remain ineligible, and admitted reports can remain unrepresentative. These findings motivate separate acceptance evidence rather than validate a remedy. Contextual adjudication, broader receiver and retention checks, total-cost measurements, and user/task evidence are needed to support stronger claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke connects local prompt inspection to supported request enforcement, guarded candidate updates and separate randomized telemetry. Receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths, including unchanged-body sends under WARN. On 1,560 authored cases, updated packages improved agreement with provisional targets, but a separate annotation-presence evaluation had low specificity. All matched full-model candidates failed retention. Installed V3 measurements isolated resident detector activation; controlled telemetry fixtures verified delivery and accounting, while the finite audit found privacy loss slightly above nominal and simulations showed substantial estimation error.

The detector revisions expose the central trade-off. The first revision improved union specificity but failed its registered recall test. K, shipped by owner decision, restored level BLOCK recall but retained a 1.05-point union-recall loss against the original detectors (95\% interval $-1.70$ to $-0.41$), concentrated in Privy. The evidence supports bounded, inspectable mechanisms, not independently validated contextual accuracy, universal interception, an accepted retention-qualified update, representative monitoring or user benefit. Human annotation, adaptive end-to-end evasion, update-security and communication-authentication tests, total cost, and user/task outcomes remain outstanding.

\section{Policy Decision Order and Observable Outcomes}
\label{app:policy}
The contextual attributes in Section~\ref{sec:pii_categorisation} describe a finding before enforcement. For a finding with numeric confidence below 0.5, the low-confidence rule takes precedence: S3 yields WARN; S2 yields WARN only if IDENTITY or LOCATION is present; all remaining cases yield ALLOW. Missing or nonnumeric confidence does not activate that rule. This is a decision threshold, not an empirical confidence calibration.

For other findings, the baseline checks are ordered. S0 with an empty category set yields ALLOW. S3 yields BLOCK and S2 yields WARN. For the remaining S0/S1 cases, IDENTITY together with LOCATION yields WARN; either category with visibility P2, P3 or P4 also yields WARN. Otherwise the finding yields ALLOW. PU denotes an unspecified audience and is not treated as restricted/private by this comparison. A category set is multilabel, so retaining one sensitive category is not equivalent to matching the exact target set.

The cascade then constrains what reaches aggregation. An early rule BLOCK ends inspection; later requested layers have not evaluated the text. Otherwise, aggregation preserves the strongest enforcement decision rather than replacing it with the last detector's action. A failed requested layer changes an otherwise ALLOW result to BLOCK, while a valid non-ALLOW finding remains visible. These semantics differ from a configuration in which an unavailable parameter stream leaves fewer enabled layers: a successfully completed reduced cascade is not a successful semantic execution.

Finally, a policy action and a receiver observation are different records. ALLOW and WARN release the original supported request, while BLOCK cancels it. Notice rendering under WARN does not imply acknowledgement, consent or removal of information from the body. For a probe expecting a send, the relevant observation is receiver arrival with the original body; for a no-send probe, the observation is absence of arrival within the declared observation window, including checks for a late send. The installed probes establish these bounded outcomes, not an absence of leakage through every browser transport.

\section{Measurement Definitions and Reused Evidence}
\label{app:measurement}
Let case $j$ have target sensitivity $s_j$, audience $v_j$, category set $C_j$ and allowed-action set $\mathcal A_j$. Let the corresponding prediction be $\hat s_j,\hat v_j,\hat C_j,\hat a_j$. Define the three indicators
\begin{align}
L_j&=\mathbf1\{\hat s_j=s_j,\ \hat v_j=v_j,\ \hat C_j=C_j\},\\
A_j&=\mathbf1\{\hat a_j\in\mathcal A_j\},\\
J_j&=L_j A_j.
\end{align}
Label agreement, action agreement and label-and-action joint agreement average $L_j$, $A_j$ and $J_j$, respectively, over the declared cases. Exact category agreement counts both omitted and spurious categories as mismatches. For action direction, the ordering is ALLOW $<$ WARN $<$ BLOCK; the retained contextual analysis distinguishes an action below the allowed set from one above it.

The binary projection is positive when $\hat s_j\ne\mathrm{S0}$ or $\hat C_j$ is nonempty. Recall is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ and specificity is $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$, with denominators fixed by the declared positive and negative targets. On the contextual workload those targets are provisional sensitivity labels; on the final endpoint they are source annotation presence. The same prediction projection does not make those reference tasks equivalent.

For any binary correctness indicator $Z_j$, the paired gain count is the number of cases with $Z_j^{\rm old}=0$ and $Z_j^{\rm new}=1$; the loss count reverses those values. The net rate difference is $(G-L)/N$. Reporting only that difference would hide the number of previous successes lost. The case identity must remain paired when a cluster is resampled, and repeated appearances of a sampled group carry its rows with their multiplicities. Row weighting is retained, so a larger group contributes more cases; the method does not give each group equal influence.

The 1,560 contextual cases are shared by the package comparison and targeted ablation. The 1,080-case continuation assessment is a reused provisional subset after the declared exclusions. These are related development observations. The 498-row final endpoint supplies a different frozen target and source composition, with all six arms retained, but its 464 observed group keys do not certify independent templates. A later analysis of already opened material cannot retroactively make it protected confirmation. The full confusion counts and adverse outcomes remain in the main results, rather than being displaced by this methodological detail.

The matched continuation comparison starts both HEADS and FULL from the same intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads. That ORIGINAL control is distinct from both the historical V1 classifier and the selected updated package used in RQ1. The shared 4,096 exposures describe repeated training occurrences, not 4,096 independently sampled situations. Each seed produces a terminal candidate; the assessment selects no intermediate checkpoint.

A candidate, a guard decision and an effective artifact must remain distinguishable in the record. The candidate is the fitted set of weights, even if rejected. The decision applies the fixed sixteen-case criterion: improved joint correctness and zero action harms against its derived target actions. The effective-artifact mapping describes which artifact that rule would retain: a rejected candidate maps to the unchanged ORIGINAL. Because this experiment publishes no candidate, that mapping does not establish a server replacement or a client refresh. Counting only effective artifacts would erase the rejected candidates' measured gains and harms; counting every fitted candidate as an update would erase the guard.

The same requirement applies to the RQ1 package comparison and final endpoint: source, tokenizer, backbone and task-head identities must match the recorded configuration. \section{Installed-Browser Observation Boundaries}
\label{app:browser}
The historical rules/NER cost capture and standard-V3 activation study use different timing endpoints. Historical page-observed decision latency ends when the analysis result returns and excludes the receiver response. V3 request-to-receiver completion includes a successful send to the synthetic receiver. Their distributions should therefore not be subtracted to estimate a generation speedup. In the V3 study, subtraction occurs only within a profile between its resident detector arm and its detectors-off baseline, using the same endpoint and fixed prompt.

The off baseline retains the extension hooks, native launcher, supervisor, bridge and model service. It estimates the increment from activating a detector in that arrangement, not the total cost of installing the extension. Reversing the arm order in the second profile replicate reduces reliance on one fixed ordering without creating an extension-absent control or a cold-load experiment. The warmup requests are excluded from the timed distributions, and the untimed tier-selection probes remain separate from those distributions.

Historical resource windows include browser startup and request/readiness activity before teardown, with resident services and instrumentation already present. Cgroup CPU counters aggregate descendants, while charged memory includes cache and kernel charges~\cite{LinuxCgroupV2Interfaces2026}. They are not per-detector CPU or process RSS/PSS measurements. Positive sampling gaps remain part of the record; a sampled maximum does not establish the continuous peak. Observer cost is retained rather than subtracted through an unverified attribution model.

Each historical transport/action cell pools thirty measurements from each of two profiles. Its nearest-rank p99 is the observed maximum of sixty measurements, rather than evidence of a well-estimated population tail. The V3 distributions likewise contain repeated executions within only two profiles per transport. These restrictions matter when interpreting apparent latency differences and when extrapolating beyond the fixed synthetic prompts. \section{Telemetry Estimation and Admission}
\label{app:reporting}
The ideal mechanism and audited finite implementation in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism} are analyzed for the content of a fixed reporting opportunity under public configuration. The finite bound includes the reported excess over nominal privacy loss. For a category whose true contributor count is $h$ among $n$ reports, and observed randomized count $Y$, the nominal inverse-response estimate before clipping is
\begin{equation}
\widehat h=\frac{Y-nq}{p-q}.
\end{equation}
The collector clips each estimated marginal separately to $[0,n]$. Inverting the nominal probabilities is not the same as inverting the exact finite kernel, and clipping is nonlinear: the expected clipped value need not equal $h$. Independently clipped categories also need not sum to $n$. These properties explain why privacy loss, bias, RMSE and total coherence are separate checks.

The synthetic trials fix the contributor histogram before sampling the randomized reports. Their error therefore excludes uncertainty about which people or events enter that histogram. Increasing $n$ means more admitted reports, pooled across installation-days where necessary, rather than relaxing the per-installation daily cap. A precision requirement for an operational decision was not prespecified; the two illustrative category cells cannot establish one after the fact.

The operational accounting also has distinct denominators. An opportunity may be suppressed before queuing, a reservation may be consumed without a delivered packet, and an accepted report represents a received randomized event. The observed totals reconcile as $308=184+124$ opportunities and $184=168+16$ reserved events, where the latter partition is accepted reports plus submission failures in the controlled fixtures. These are not counts of users, unique prompts or detector decisions in a deployment.

The ledger reserves before construction and does not refund failed construction or delivery. That ordering protects accounting against retries within retained state, but cannot hide whether a report was sent. A cap can also change event composition before randomization, as the retained order-reversal example demonstrates. Correcting the randomized counts would still leave that admission mechanism to justify. The finite audit and controlled operation are recorded separately in \path{telemetry-evidence-20261014.md} and \path{rq3-telemetry-evidence-20261017.md}; neither supplies training examples or establishes useful live monitoring.

\textit{Paired detector-layer results.} On the held-out final endpoint, the regex layer's specificity point estimate moved from 0.985 to 0.996 (false positives 4 to 1) while its recall moved from 0.653 to 0.640 (true positives 154 to 151). The design and development splits show the same direction at similar size: dev specificity 0.971 to 0.983 and recall 0.652 to 0.640. The synthetic sets show larger specificity gains: regex false positives fell from 23 to 0 on the personalized set and from 154 to 34 on the OpenAI set, where regex recall fell from 0.920 to 0.896. Because the rules were developed with these sets in view, the synthetic gains are optimistic, and the held-out difference is a net difference of three rows.

Paired tests on the same rows (Table~\ref{tab:detprec_paired}) show that the held-out regex shifts are not statistically significant. Three rows became correct and none incorrect for specificity, and none became correct and three incorrect for recall; the exact McNemar $p$-value is 0.25 for each, and the 95\% intervals for the differences, [0.000, 0.026] and [$-$0.029, 0.000], reach zero. The development split shows the same three-against-zero counts. The point estimates above are therefore consistent in direction but compatible with no change. The synthetic-set regex changes are significant: personalized false positives fell on 23 rows with none added ($p<0.001$, although an interval that resamples its 20 scenario labels touches zero, [0.000, 0.073]), and the OpenAI set gained specificity on 121 rows against 1 ($p<0.001$; scenario-label interval [0.174, 0.329]) while losing recall on 12 rows against none ($p<0.001$).



The union shows no detectable change on held-out data: recall 0.767 to 0.763 (181 to 180 true positives; 13 rows gained and 14 lost, $p=1.0$) and specificity 0.958 to 0.962 (11 to 10 false positives; 7 gained and 6 lost, $p=1.0$). This supports no detectable change, not equivalence: the intervals for the differences, [$-$0.047, 0.039] for recall and [$-$0.023, 0.031] for specificity, remain compatible with shifts of three to five points either way. The development-set rise in union recall from 0.731 to 0.761 (12 rows gained, 4 lost) is not significant by the exact test ($p=0.077$) and did not reproduce on the held-out endpoint. The NER layer alone did not improve there: false positives rose from 7 to 9 and recall fell from 0.237 to 0.212. It also lost recall on both synthetic sets (personalized 0.800 to 0.695, OpenAI 0.290 to 0.170), and gained recall on the development set (0.208 to 0.246, with specificity 0.975 to 0.966) and on dev-CHECK, where its false positives stayed at four while recall rose from 0.172 to 0.207. The direction of the trade is regex specificity for a small recall cost, but on real text neither side is statistically established; only the synthetic-set changes are.

\textit{NER models.} Two larger spaCy models and a GLiNER-based personal-data model were compared with the shipped small model on dev-CHECK and the OpenAI set, with the GLiNER label preset and threshold frozen on the design splits first. No union difference among the candidates with the post-filter on was statistically reliable (smallest exact $p=0.125$). The larger spaCy model had the most consistent gains on those post-review splits (dev-CHECK 5 true positives gained and 1 lost, 4 false positives removed; OpenAI 1 gained, 3 removed) and was neutral on the design splits. The GLiNER model lost NER recall (dev-CHECK $-$0.103, $p=0.008$), left the union level or lower, and documents no training data, so it was dropped. The transformer spaCy model is a comparator at roughly six times the NER-layer latency. On the fresh F1 set the larger model raised union specificity over N by 1.71 points (1.17 to 2.24) and union recall by 1.86, but it lowered specificity in the Privy sentences (union $-$4.34, NER-only $-$7.59), so the pooled gain depends on the source mix. It also adds about 550 MB on disk and 762 MB resident. None of the evaluated models changed a BLOCK decision on the post-review splits, because their entities are at most S2. The owner held the larger model, and the shipped NER model is unchanged.

\textit{NER as amplifier.} A study on the development splits asked whether NER is better used only to corroborate or extend regex findings. Corroboration-only policies never beat the shipped union on the design splits, and amplifier-only policies cannot create a positive by construction, so they surrender NER's recall gain (negative result for NER as an amplifier-only detector). Modest independent recall gains from the shipped NER layer remain.

\textit{Robustness probe and fixes.} A perturbation probe applied seeded transformations to the development, personalized and OpenAI sets. Digit obfuscation caused the largest recall gap: splitting digits with dashes or spelling them out cost the revised union about 20 to 29 points on development and OpenAI text, and on OpenAI the precision repairs made it worse than O (regex recall fell 34.4 points against 15.2 under dashes). Zero-width characters cost 11 points of development union recall and inflated NER false positives; lowercase and uppercase text reduced NER recall; digit spacing, full-width digits and bracketed at-signs were already neutralized. Four default-off canonicalizations were then built and scored on F1's 58,899 perturbed items: stripping zero-width characters ($+$9.11 points of union recall), rejoining split or spelled digits ($+$17.82), spelled e-mail separators ($+$30.90) and a caseless gate on NER ($+$1.30), together $+$11.77, all passing their registered tests. These passes are largely circular. Stripping restores the unperturbed result on every item by construction (zero mismatches), the digit gain comes from the dash and spelled transforms, the others having been handled already, the caseless gain is entirely in uppercase text, and the no-harm test passes because no clean sentence changed outcome. These are detector-layer transformation results, not an end-to-end attack-success rate: no adaptive attacker, intended-action denominator or receiver-observed attack outcome was evaluated, and the tests do not estimate how often these inputs occur. The documentation prerequisite registered for promotion was unmet at the frozen commit; the owner waived it, and the fixes remain off and outside K.

\subsection*{Detector-layer protocol details}
\label{sec:detprec_protocol}
This detector-layer study explicitly requests regex and NER, never semantics. It compares pre-change detectors with the first revision; the later student-identifier rule and K rework are absent. The 502-row development data are split by source-group hash into dev-TUNE (267; 148 positive) and dev-CHECK (235; 116 positive); design material also includes 2,000 personalized and 1,000 OpenAI-generated rows. The post-filter used dev-TUNE and personalized data; dev-CHECK and OpenAI results for its revised version are post-review. The inputs consulted while designing regex repairs were not separately recorded, so those splits are treated as design material. The initial endpoint contains 498 rows and was scored once per configuration after defaults froze, but had already been opened in another unit; paired outcomes were later reconstructed and the endpoint is now consumed. The student rule was diagnosed after this scoring. Labels are source annotation presence, not contextual authorization.

A positive is any nonzero sensitivity or nonempty category. We report confusion counts, recall, specificity and latency for regex, NER and their union. Paired McNemar tests use discordant rows; paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 replicates, fixed seed) resample documents on real sets and rows on synthetic sets, with scenario-label sensitivity analyses (20 personalized and 10 OpenAI scenarios). No multiplicity correction is applied. Runtime action and weighted-error comparisons are separate outcomes.

\textit{Rule gates.} Kinship terms and the word ``family'' require a possessive determiner (my, our, his, her, their), optionally followed by up to two hyphen- or space-joined modifier words, so ``my ex-wife'' matches while a non-possessive self-statement does not. Child cues require a possessive and a child noun, an age expression, or an age followed by a person noun; object ages such as a five-year-old laptop do not match. Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns (for example salary, loan, mortgage, savings, credit score) after a possessive with up to two modifiers; a bare reference to an account does not match. Location keywords require a connector such as ``live in'' followed by a capture that is not a closed-class word. Structured identity values stay on one line and capture at most five tokens, and a phone label needs a value that begins like a number. Checksum validators reject shape-only hits for card numbers (Luhn), IBANs (mod-97), NHS numbers (mod-11) and US social security numbers (issuable area, group and serial ranges), and context matching is word-bounded rather than substring-based. Password placeholders, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. A bare reference to a student is not a child cue; a separate student-identifier rule (S3 identity) needs a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student, so generic student and identifier mentions stay negative. It postdates the measurements of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision} and Section~\ref{sec:product_rerun} and is part of N and K in Section~\ref{sec:heldout}.

\textit{Labelled identifiers and downgrades (K).} Two rules, S3 and S2 identity, match a label followed by a value. The label noun is id, identifier, number, no, num, nr, code or reference, in words, snake case, camel case or key-value form, or a hash sign directly after the qualifier. S3 qualifiers are passport, licence, national, social security or insurance, tax, identity, identification, citizenship, voter, visa, residence permit, immigration, military, birth certificate, patient, medical record, health plan, insurance, Medicare, Medicaid, beneficiary, bank account and payment card; the acronyms SSN, TFN, NINO, EIN, ITIN, MRN and NRIC need no noun, and a colon after Passport, ID card, identity card, Medicare or Medicaid labels its own value. S2 qualifiers are customer, client, account, member, subscriber, employee, staff, personnel, user, policy, claim, registration, plate, store, loyalty, library, rewards, club and gift cards, and reference. A value is tokens of letters, digits and underscores joined by hyphens or single spaces; its first token carries a digit or is a one- to three-letter prefix before a digit group, every spaced group carries a digit, it contains five to twenty-four compact characters and it is not a date. Software licence names, order, build, ticket, part and promotion codes are not qualifiers. Downgrades keep a hit whose checksum fails when a card, banking or social-security word is in context (S3) and, after an IBAN check fails without a banking word, keep a token of eight or more characters with five or more digits and a letter at S2. Because digit spaces are removed before matching, a spaced card number reaches the rule as one digit run.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 1.} Five span rules apply in turn. The placeholder rule removes masks (repeated letters, spans without letters, a pluralized capital letter, and spans inside template delimiters). The identifier rule removes e-mail, URL, underscore and code-like tokens, which belong to the regex layer. The structural rule removes spans inside headings, except multi-token or cue-introduced person names. The short-acronym rule requires a locating or affiliation preposition before a place of at most three letters or an organization of at most four. The common-phrase rule removes a single-token person candidate when the model's installed word list contains it and the tagger, reading the lowercased token in isolation, finds no proper-noun reading and no context cue corroborates it.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 2.} The first matching rule decides: (1) a person linked with a place or organization, when either side is independently corroborated, yields S2; (2) the corresponding place or organization yields S2; (3) a corroborated person yields S2; (4) a place or organization tied to the speaker by a relation cue keeps its default sensitivity; (5) one in the same prompt as such a tied entity keeps its default; (6) a facility or location directly beside a number yields S2; (7) a single uncorroborated person yields S1; and (8) a lone place or organization is dropped. Independent corroboration means evidence about the entity itself: a multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring person, or a place or organization with a personal-relation cue. A bare locating preposition does not corroborate.

\textit{Rule gates.} A narrative heuristic that matched on length alone was removed. Kinship, family and child cues now require a possessive determiner, optionally with modifier words, or an age expression combined with a person noun. Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns after a possessive, and location keywords require a connector followed by a name rather than a closed-class word. Structured identity values remain on one short line. Rules derived from Presidio patterns regained validators: Luhn checksums~\cite{Luhn1960Patent} for card numbers, mod-97 for IBANs~\cite{ISO13616Part1_2020}, mod-11 for NHS numbers, issuable ranges for US social security numbers and whole-word context terms for weakly shaped licence and date patterns. Placeholder passwords, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. Appendix~\ref{app:detprec} lists the gates.

\textit{Labelled identifiers and checksum downgrades.} The validators and context gates also missed some genuine identifiers. The later K rework therefore added label-based rules and retained certain checksum failures when surrounding words support an identifier interpretation. Explicit labels, rather than a country pattern or checksum alone, supply evidence; values still need identifier-like shape. Detailed qualifier and token rules remain in Appendix~\ref{app:detprec}. The student-identifier rule was added after earlier scoring when an action-level check found adult student records downgraded from BLOCK to WARN; that evidence came from a single template.

\textit{Case-preserved input and NER filtering.} Regex rules are case-insensitive, but the small English spaCy model~\cite{Honnibal2020SpaCy} uses capitalization as evidence for names. Case-preserved NER input recovered entities that lowercasing suppressed, while also admitting headings and ordinary capitalized words. A post-filter removes likely placeholders, identifiers, structural headings, short uncorroborated acronyms and common words, then uses corroborating context to distinguish a lone place or organization from information linked to the speaker. These controls are on by default and individually switchable for rollback. The detailed gates and ablations are in Appendix~\ref{app:detprec}.

The pre-change detectors are O; N is the first precision revision and K is the shipped rework. The initial precision and product-rerun tables precede the student-identifier rule and K; the fresh held-out results include them. Default-off canonicalizations and alternative NER models are not part of K. This chronology matters because K has detector-layer evidence but no installed full-product evaluation.

\textit{The first revision failed its recall test.} N raised regex specificity by 1.75 points and union specificity by 3.32 points on F1, but its union recall fell 1.37 points against O, and the whole interval lies below zero as well as below the $-1.0$ margin ($p=2\times10^{-6}$). The loss is larger at the BLOCK level: 190 positives moved from BLOCK to a lesser action and none moved the other way, all through the regex layer. By the registered rule N was not promotable; N was already the installed default, and the registration's stated consequence was the trade-off itself, not a revert. An exploratory exact layer swap placed the recall loss in the regex layer: keeping the old regex layer with the new NER layer gave $+$0.08 points of union recall ($-$0.43 to $+$0.61), whereas the new regex layer with the old NER layer gave $-$1.49. The rules behind the 146 positives lost through the regex layer were mainly the weakly shaped licence recognizer, IBAN and its validator, concentrated-sensitive-data, the card and social-security recognizers, and the child and family cues; the child and family hits fell mostly on text the annotation does not mark. This attribution is post hoc and uses no new data. It prompted the owner's decision to rework the regex rules that cost recall.

\textit{The rework recovered part of the loss and not all of it.} On F4, K lost 1.05 points of union recall against O (342 gained, 433 lost). The estimate sits 0.05 points beyond the margin, the interval straddles it, and H7 is inconclusive. Relative to the margin that means the data neither show a loss within 1.0 point nor show that the loss exceeds it; relative to zero, the upper limit $-0.41$ shows K is significantly worse than O on union recall (exact McNemar $p=0.0012$). BLOCK-level recall is level (H8). Against N, K dominates on every recall rule: 69 union gains and no losses, and 135 BLOCK gains and none lost. K restores about 43\% of N's union-recall loss on F4 ($-1.85$ for N, $-1.05$ for K) and all of its BLOCK-level loss ($-1.55$ for N, $+0.01$ for K). The 5 clean F1 rows K adds against N, all from the generic labelled-identifier rule, are the cost of the new rules, and the G2 pass is a second look. By the registered rule K was not promoted, because H7 did not pass. It is nonetheless the shipped configuration: the owner decided on 25 October 2026 to ship the rework, and an independent critic reviewing the registered cells advised that if the first revision shipped, K was the better version. The critic's summary concluded that K's recall loss against O is real, that BLOCK-level recall is equal, and that reverting to O would buy back about one point of union recall through both layers (in Privy mainly through O's noisy NER; the critic's corrected wording, w3-critic-3), at a cost of about three points of union specificity.

\textit{Source heterogeneity.} K's union-recall loss is concentrated in Privy: $-8.00$ points against O (95\% interval $-9.57$ to $-6.41$), almost unchanged from N's $-8.04$. Outside Privy, K's exploratory estimate is positive, but source mix changes the pooled result. Because annotations are incomplete and prior NER often flagged unannotated text, the observed losses cannot be classified entirely as false-positive reduction or true misses. Source and span analyses are in Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}.

\textit{Flag rates on natural prompts.} Of 6,000 unlabelled prompts, O flagged 24.4\% (1,465), whereas N and K each flagged 16.7\% (1,001), and K flagged exactly the prompts N did. BLOCK fell from 130 prompts to 65. These are flag rates, not error rates; whether flagged prompts contain personal data awaits human adjudication, so no specificity claim is made on natural prompts. The generic labelled-identifier rule fires at S2 on technical text such as a user-identifier key and value, and its qualifier vocabulary mirrors the labels of the development spans, so its gains may not transfer to text that does not use such labels.

\textit{Spans.} Counting a prompt as detected when any rule fires overstates masking. On F1, N's union detects 80.92\% of positive sentences by that definition, yet fully masks every annotated span in only 37.16\% of them (37.31\% for O). Span-level scoring on the 502 development sentences showed the same gap (union any-hit recall 0.761, every mention masked in 107 of 264 prompts, 0.405, and 0.625 of gold characters covered). Span measures also moved differently from the presence measures: on F1, N's union overlap recall rose 1.19 points and its over-masking fell by 12.75 characters per thousand, whereas on F4, K raised gold-character coverage by 5.00 points over O but masked 10.82 more characters per thousand outside annotated spans than N. Letting NER extend masking only where the regex layer already fired recovers part of the NER span gain without creating a positive.

\textit{Full-product rerun detail.} The comparison and ablation of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_comparison_results} were measured with the pre-change detectors. They were rerun as full-product evidence (rules, NER and semantics, actual cascade execution recorded), not as detector-layer or semantic-only results. The rerun used the same 1,560 cases, six packages, policy, rule-first ordering and one first pass per cell, and compared a clean export of the pre-change commit with the revised detectors without the student-identifier rule. The pre-change arm reproduced every historical outcome and action on all 1,560 cases for all six packages and seven layer sets, with no discordant pair, so differences arise from the detectors rather than the harness. The semantic layer is unchanged by construction, and its sole-semantic outcomes were identical in both arms. All 40,560 scored requests completed without errors. Intervals resample the 35 scenario families.



The revised detectors reduced over-restriction in every package, and the 139 unnecessary restrictions shared by the three V3 tiers fell to 108 (112 in union); 103 of the 108 lie among the original 139. The residual shows that the over-enforcement was largely, but not entirely, a rule artefact. Allowed-action agreement rose for every package, for example V3 balanced from 1,409 to 1,438 (36 cases improved, 7 regressed, $p<0.001$), and binary false positives for that package fell from 119 to 95 with false negatives unchanged at 8; no V3 tier gained a binary miss. The mechanism for V3 balanced was 36 cases moving from BLOCK to ALLOW as rule false positives disappeared (early rule BLOCKs fell from 289 to 253), against 7 clean cases moving from ALLOW to WARN because NER now emitted findings. Joint agreement did not change significantly for V3 ($p$ from 0.26 to 0.81) and fell by five for V1 balanced (2 improved, 7 regressed, $p=0.18$). Two effects cancelled: the rule repairs alone added 28 joint-correct cases without a loss, while the new NER findings altered the label set on 25 cases, each in favour of removing NER. The updated V3 packages remained ahead of historical V1 in every tier, with allowed-action gains of 388 against 5 losses for efficient, 413 against 12 for balanced and 337 against 3 for quality, compared with 389/3, 389/6 and 348/0 historically.



The central ablation result persists but shrinks. Removing rules versus the full product corrected 139 actions and lost 18 historically (net 121), and with the revised detectors corrected 103 and lost 18 (net 85, $p<10^{-14}$; family-resampled interval for the action-rate gain 2.4 to 8.9 percentage points, against 3.5 to 12.7 historically). The lost actions are again 18, and under-restriction again rises from 12 to 30, so the tradeoff between unnecessary restriction and missed protection remains. The revised detectors also change the NER findings. Historically NER produced no converted findings on this fixture and removing it left every outcome unchanged. With case-preserved input and the post-filter it converts 48 findings, all identity names: alone it flags 36 of the 810 sensitive-labelled cases and 12 of the 750 clean cases. No-NER now equals the full product on 1,526 of 1,560 cases, and removing NER improves 7 action outcomes and 25 joint outcomes with no regression. On this fixture the NER layer is therefore a net negative at the label level, which does not establish its value or harm elsewhere.

These are full-product measurements on the same assistant-provisional labels and correlated families as before. The detector changes were tuned on development and personalized sets rather than on these cases, but this partition has now been scored twice, so the rerun is not a fresh confirmation. The ablation uses one balanced package, the intervals use the family grouping only, and neither the student-identifier rule nor the later rework (K) is part of the rerun: the shipped configuration has not been run through the full product.
