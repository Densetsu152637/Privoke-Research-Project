\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to web-hosted language models can disclose personal and organizational information before a provider's controls apply. To address this exposure, we present \privoke, which inspects supported prompts on the user's workstation and enforces an allow, warn or block decision before transmission. Its update service trains detector candidates from supplied labelled examples, checks version and retention constraints, and streams accepted parameters to clients; independently randomized telemetry remains separate from training. We evaluate this protection and update lifecycle through detection and enforcement measurements, retention checks during updates, and assessments of systems and privacy costs. Updated packages improve agreement on an authored contextual workload, although action failures remain and performance is weak on a separate annotation-presence task, where specificity ranges from 13.36 to 21.76\%. In a matched continuation study, aggregate gains do not ensure retention: every full-model candidate fails the retention guard. Installed-browser probes further show bounded request handling, while the cost and telemetry audits reveal limits in measurement and estimation. Together, these results describe an implemented and auditable protection and update lifecycle, while independent contextual ground truth, practical utility and reliable generalization remain open questions.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Prompt privacy, local inference, request enforcement, guarded model updates, local differential privacy.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Web-hosted large language models support writing, coding and analysis~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing}, often drawing on contextual information that can also disclose a person's health, a colleague's details or an organization's secrets. Reported workplace disclosures illustrate the risks associated with including such material in prompts~\cite{SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Because transmitted content is already available to the provider, provider-side safeguards address a different point in the disclosure path~\cite{Athey2017,barbera2025ai,King2025}. Local intervention therefore offers an opportunity to inspect a supported request before it leaves the browser.

Although local inspection provides a point of intervention, recognizing an entity does not establish whether its disclosure is authorized, and even a correct warning may allow the original content to be transmitted. Similarly, a detector update may improve average accuracy while losing a previously protected case. Monitoring can itself disclose information unless the reporting path has a separate privacy boundary. These distinctions motivate \privoke, a client-side prompt firewall that joins local inspection, explicit request enforcement, guarded detector updates and separately randomized telemetry.

\privoke assigns contextual sensitivity, intended audience and information categories, then maps these findings to ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. On supported requests, ALLOW and WARN forward the original body, while BLOCK cancels transmission. Since WARN only attempts to display a notice without waiting for acknowledgement, it establishes neither consent nor prevented disclosure. In the configuration studied here, the detector runs locally, while a distinct server-side training path uses supplied labelled examples, validates candidate updates against their exact base, and distributes accepted parameters without rebuilding the extension. User prompts and telemetry do not supply training labels.

The closest work already establishes local prompt protection. Casper combines rules, entity recognition and topic detection in a browser extension~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}; Hide and Seek trains local hiding and recovery models~\cite{HaS2024}; ProSan trains a local sanitizer that trades privacy hiding against task utility~\cite{ProSan2024}. Building on this prior work, our contribution is an implemented, version-bound detector lifecycle coupled to observable request actions, together with evidence that distinguishes useful gains from regression and rejection. Within this lifecycle, a retention check provides a bounded gate rather than a guarantee of safe adaptation.

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1: Does PriVoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively?}
\item \textbf{RQ2: Can PriVoke update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior?}
\item \textbf{RQ3: What systems and privacy overhead does PriVoke introduce?}
\end{enumerate}
The answers to these questions remain conditional: authored contextual cases show substantial package gains alongside remaining enforcement harms, while separate annotation-based tasks expose weak specificity. Although the update path works mechanically, aggregate improvement does not ensure retention, and the browser and telemetry measurements establish bounded behavior and costs without demonstrating deployment utility. Keeping these answers together makes the system's contribution and its unresolved gaps explicit.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local protection and sanitization.} Casper's three layers directly precede PriVoke's layered inspection~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024}. In addition to detecting sensitive content, Casper substitutes identifiers, restores them in responses and offers topic-warning acknowledgement. Its support for custom rules/topics and model replacement also means that it should not be characterized as uniformly static. Hide and Seek (HaS) anonymizes entities before cloud inference and locally recovers them from responses, with trained adversaries testing recovery resistance~\cite{HaS2024}. ProSan selectively replaces prompt words using task-importance and privacy proxies, then distils a local sanitizer~\cite{ProSan2024}. Together, these systems establish both local processing and training as prior art, while PromptShield studies enterprise role/policy classification~\cite{PromptShield2025}.

\textit{A different measured contract.} PriVoke's browser interface acts on requests: BLOCK cancels a supported request, whereas WARN and ALLOW send its original body. Although the detector returns masked spans, these do not rewrite traffic, and the system has no response-recovery stage. Its distinctive engineering focus is instead the guarded path from a candidate tied to an exact model revision through publication to client refresh. To assess this path, we measure paired gains and losses, including those of candidates refused by the guard. The separate telemetry path limits randomized event fields without treating them as training feedback. These are bounded systems contributions, not claims of superior sanitization or uniquely local inference.

\textit{Comparison boundaries.} Because the reviewed systems evaluate different targets, their published metrics cannot be placed on one privacy-accuracy axis: Casper reports entity/topic detection, HaS reports resistance to whole-text recovery alongside downstream task proxies, and ProSan reports item replacement and automatic utility. We retain version-qualified outcomes and interaction details in Appendix~\ref{app:interaction}. Our executed Presidio references use disclosed native entity-recognizer settings~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026}; these serve as component references rather than reproductions of Casper, HaS or ProSan and therefore support no matched system superiority claim.

\section{System Design and Privacy Boundary}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
The system aims to reduce inadvertent personal, third-party or organizational disclosure through supported outgoing prompts, under a trust boundary that includes the workstation, enabled extension, local runtime/supervisor and authenticated model publisher. Compromised clients, malicious publishers, deliberately replaced hooks and provider-held historical information lie outside this boundary. Recognized prompt POST formats through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} are supported; workers, attachments, other transports and changed endpoints need separate validation. Inspection is bypassed when protection is disabled, while optional hosted inference changes the disclosure boundary and is therefore excluded from local-inference measurements.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} distinguishes three data paths: prompt inspection remains on the workstation; labelled examples enter a separate training service, from which accepted parameters return to the local detector; and coarse, locally randomized event fields reach a telemetry collector without a path to training. This separation describes what each service receives; it does not establish that every policy decision is correct.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
A page hook extracts supported prompt text and sends it through the extension background process and a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to a workstation-local Python runtime. A native-messaging supervisor manages the bridge/runtime lifecycle, while local user inference remains separate from the server-side training runtime and does not fall back to it. Figure~\ref{fig:enforcement} shows the resulting request contract.

The runtime normalizes text while preserving offsets for original-text evidence. Its default cascade runs regular-expression rules first, followed by enabled named entity recognition (NER) and the semantic classifier. A rule-based BLOCK skips later detectors; other findings are aggregated with the strongest action retained. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW result, while valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. Although unavailable analysis blocks supported requests, a parameter-stream outage can leave other enabled layers operating. This distinction means that fallback may reduce semantic coverage rather than block every full-pipeline request.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{architecture-enforcement.pdf}
\caption{Pre-transmission enforcement on supported hooks. WARN forwards unchanged content after attempting a notice; it does not wait for user acknowledgement. Analysis failure and reduced-layer stream fallback are distinct conditions. Disabled protection and unsupported traffic lie outside this decision path.}
\label{fig:enforcement}
\end{figure}

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic classifier returns three dimensions: sensitivity (none to highly sensitive), visibility (public to personal-private, or unspecified) and a set of ten information categories, such as health, identity and location. A deterministic policy converts these labels and confidence into an action. Highly sensitive content normally maps to BLOCK and potentially harmful personal information to WARN; identity/location combinations can raise restrictions. Low confidence can moderate the action, but neither these labels nor the confidence score independently establish disclosure authorization or calibrated risk. The exact mapping is given in Appendix~\ref{app:policy}.

We compare historical small classifiers with later packages retaining pretrained BERT encoders/poolers~\cite{googleBertL4H128A2,googleBertL2H256A4,googleBertL12H256A4,turc2019WellReadStudents}. These models classify prompts rather than generate conversational responses and are offered in three package sizes, called efficient, balanced and quality. Since their tokenizers, capacities, thresholds and histories differ, the names imply no universal performance ordering and package comparisons cannot isolate pretraining or architecture.

\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
The update process separates candidate computation from the decision about whether a candidate may replace its base. To support these responsibilities, a training controller constructs bounded labelled batches and held-out guard cases, excluding protected rows/text/groups. A separate server runtime fetches the specified model, executes training and returns model-bound parameter changes and candidate metrics. The updater checks exact base identity, allowed trainable tensors and shapes, finite values, magnitude limits and strategy-configured publication guards (Figure~\ref{fig:updates}). Passing an integrity check or successfully receiving an update therefore establishes neither accuracy nor protection.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{architecture-updates.pdf}
\caption{A candidate is evaluated before publication. Compare-and-swap rejects stale/conflicting bases; durable receipts prevent an identical retry from applying twice. A passing retention guard covers its declared cases and criteria only. Head and underlying-model stages are separate publications, so a later rejection need not undo an earlier accepted head stage.}
\label{fig:updates}
\end{figure}

Validated artifacts are published atomically using compare-and-swap: publication succeeds only if the current base still matches the candidate's declared parent. Durable replay receipts make repeated submissions idempotent for each artifact, while streaming serves versioned parameters whose artifact identities clients verify before refreshing cached weights without an application release. This is centralized training from supplied examples, not federated learning, collection of user gradients or evidence of cloud deployment. This design establishes the ability to update the detector, leaving RQ2 to examine whether particular updates preserve protection.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
Telemetry reports five coarse fields: action, an action/sensitivity risk bucket, one precedence-selected category, a bounded release label and a four-hour time-of-day bin without date. Each field is randomized locally before release, and packets exclude raw prompts, identifiers, exact confidence scores and per-layer findings. This reporting path remains separate from training: no telemetry field supplies a corrected label or enters the training path.

For an event budget $\varepsilon$, each of five fields receives $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. A field with $K_i$ possible values reports truth with probability
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
for each alternative. Under ideal independent sampling and common public configuration, an event tuple has likelihood ratio at most $e^\varepsilon$. Because actual finite arithmetic can differ from this ideal mechanism, it requires a separate audit. A persistent local ledger reserves budget before construction, defaults to event budget one and total eight per UTC day, and does not refund failures. This accounting requires trusted clock/state, while report presence, contribution selection, delivery and network metadata remain outside the content bound. The collector inverts nominal probabilities and clips marginal estimates; randomization can therefore impose substantial estimation error (RQ3).

\textit{Contextual policies.} The authored workload contains 1,560 cases: 810 sensitive and 750 clean, with 260 per audience class. Its sensitivity, audience, category and allowed-action targets are assistant-provisional and have not undergone independent human adjudication. For binary scoring, a positive means nonzero sensitivity or any category. Semantic joint correctness requires all three label dimensions; product joint correctness additionally requires an allowed action. An under-restricted action falls below the allowed-action set and an over-restricted action above it. These classification and action outcomes are assessed separately from receiver-observed transmission.

The workload has 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups. Although sealed for an earlier semantic comparison, it was subsequently opened and reused for product and recognizer studies, so it provides PriVoke-authored development evidence rather than independent validation. Repeated passes establish repeatability without increasing the independent sample. Separate paired cluster resampling by family or expression describes dependence sensitivity; it does not certify population coverage.

\textit{Annotation-based checks.} A once-only final endpoint contains 498 English rows with 236 annotation-positive and 262 annotation-negative targets from PIIMB revision \texttt{4a13e9f}~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026}. Models, sources and scoring were frozen before release, with no subsequent tuning, reselection or promotion. The resulting prompt-level presence task differs both from contextual authorization and from PIIMB's official character-masking/F2 task. Its 464 observed source-group keys do not certify template independence. A separate external reference uses 2,000 AdvPIIBench rows: 1,000 injected positives, 750 ordinary negatives and 250 hard negatives~\cite{arpaly2026advpiibench}. Because official source categories are not exhaustive privacy annotations, the selection, grouping and prediction proxies are detailed separately in Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}.

\subsection{Comparison and Retention Protocols}
Historical and updated native packages are evaluated on the same contextual cases through the product handler, with unchanged rules and policy. Since differences between packages remain confounded, a matched balanced-package ablation provides a narrower contrast by removing individual detectors while fixing texts, weights and policy. Configured small/large English Presidio models provide same-text entity-recognizer references at threshold 0.5~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026,SpacyLarge371Release2026}, with unsupported contextual/action outputs left unmeasured.

For RQ2, a fixed-base experiment compares head-only and full-model continuation across three seeds. Each treatment starts with byte-identical intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads, receives the same ordered 4,096 training exposures over 64 steps, and is assessed on the same 1,080 reused provisional cases after complete training/guard group and text exclusions. Full-model continuation alone enables encoder optimization, including the consequent joint-gradient-clipping changes. Assessment includes all terminal candidates, including guard failures, and is not used to select a checkpoint. The 16-case retention guard requires joint improvement and zero action harms against its derived target actions. This guard is smaller and differently scored than the 1,080-case assessment, which permits declared allowed-action sets; neither the weak zero-head control nor the small guard certifies broader safety. Recipes and historical unsuccessful branches remain in Appendices~\ref{app:lineage} and~\ref{app:protocols}.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Telemetry Protocols}
Installed Linux Chromium probes use the built extension, standard native-host installer, native messaging, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector. An HTTPS synthetic receiver records original bodies and late sends. Thirty-three correctness probes cover forwarding, blocking, faults, cancellation and recovery, using a synthetic receiver rather than an authenticated LLM provider. Full-pipeline and sole-semantic execution are checked separately, outside the resource windows.

The cost experiment requests rules/NER and measures 360 decisions across twelve fresh profiles, two per transport/action cell. While page-observed latency excludes the receiver response, whole-container CPU and sampled charged memory include resident infrastructure/cache and the observer. These windows provide neither an unprotected baseline nor incremental client attribution, and they do not measure semantic-layer cost. Appendix~\ref{app:browser_protocol} specifies boundaries and sampling.

The telemetry audit enumerates finite sampling kernels for one fingerprinted host build, assuming independent uniform OS bits. Separate fixed-contributor simulations quantify estimator error, while real ledger checks exercise persistence, concurrency and failure accounting. The resulting evidence supports conditional mechanism checks and synthetic utility measurements rather than a deployed monitoring study.

\subsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
The complete installed-browser capture observed eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes across thirty-three declared correctness probes. These outcomes were supported by runtime-call correlation and late receiver checks without a decision broker, while healthy full and sole-semantic probes verified execution. An interrupted parameter stream blocked semantic-only requests; configured full-mode fallback executed rules/NER and forwarded, demonstrating reduced coverage. Restarting restored full execution, whereas disabling protection/layers forwarded requests by design. The completed result is reported separately from nine earlier failed captures, which are not pooled with it.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke detects many provisional sensitive cases and enforces its declared supported-request contract, including explicit blocking. However, this effectiveness remains conditional, because updated packages still make action errors, WARN transmits original content, independent contextual ground truth is missing, and separate presence/category tasks reveal weak specificity. Receiver probes establish bounded enforcement rather than provider compatibility, prevented disclosure on every sensitive prompt or practical usability.
\section{RQ2: Updates and Previously Protected Behavior}
\label{sec:adaptation_results}
\subsection{Mechanism Versus Useful Adaptation}
Audited server computation, bounded publication and client refresh demonstrate that served parameters can change, although these mechanics alone do not establish that an update is worth retaining. Earlier contextual, curriculum and accelerated training studies failed their declared qualification criteria. A pretrained package improved provisional disclosure agreement but failed a serious-action veto. These negative results and their attempted budgets are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:history}, rather than displaced by later package gains.

Later supervised packages improved authored contextual classification, with component and action regressions retained. Because the RQ1 comparison measures the resulting packages rather than automatic online-training benefit, its differences in capacity, tokenizer, threshold and supervision prevent attribution to one update mechanism. The controlled continuation below asks a narrower question with a common base and exposure budget.

\section{RQ3: Systems and Privacy Overhead}
\subsection{Measured Systems Cost}
The completed installed-browser protocol recorded 360 rules/NER decisions in twelve fresh-profile windows. Across transport/action cells, page-observed decision medians ranged from 27.9 to 35.0 ms, with each reported p99 corresponding to that cell's observed maximum. All 240 ALLOW/WARN decisions executed rules and NER, while 120 BLOCK decisions executed rules and legitimately skipped NER. These measurements exclude semantic-layer and receiver-response cost.

Across 126.40 seconds of summed windows, aggregate container CPU was 193.29 CPU-seconds, including 0.69 observer CPU-seconds. Maximum sampled charged memory was 1,267.91 MiB. The container is shared by resident services/cache, the driver and the receiver, while a maximum sampling gap of 622.84 ms leaves open the possibility of missed memory spikes. Although final-read and cleanup checks passed, these scope limitations remain. Without an unprotected baseline or per-role attribution, these totals describe the instrumented system and do not isolate incremental PriVoke overhead. Startup intervals and cell tails appear in Appendix~\ref{app:browser_cost}.

Updated packages had lower observed host-call medians but larger server-process peak RSS than historical packages. Fixed execution order, warming, different backends and cascade shortcuts prevent a controlled speedup or model-only interpretation (Appendix~\ref{app:product_cost}). Training times and allocated CUDA tensor peaks likewise do not establish comparative training speed or whole-process memory requirements.

\subsection{Privacy Cost and Telemetry Utility}
\label{sec:telemetry_results}
The host finite-kernel audit found that maximum event privacy loss exceeds nominal budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 by approximately $(1.294,1.503,1.226)\times10^{-15}$. The exact nominal bound therefore fails despite the tiny excess, and eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$ under the same independent uniform-entropy assumptions. These assumptions leave other builds and deployed entropy uncertified, as discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction}, which distinguishes the implemented finite kernel from the ideal formula.

The utility assessment reveals a further cost of randomization: fixed-contributor simulations found substantial clipping bias and root-mean-square error, together with incoherent marginal totals. For eleven-category reports with event budget one and eighty contributors, a true first-category count of seven had clipped bias +22.84 and RMSE 42.32 counts. Marginal estimates failed to sum to eighty in 99.99\% of trials, indicating a limitation of the conditional synthetic estimator rather than providing a population monitoring accuracy estimate.

Real ledger checks enforced the daily cap across persistence and concurrent requests, and failed construction/delivery still consumed reservations. Reversing a constructed event sequence changed first-eight BLOCK participation from 100\% to 0\%. Randomizing report contents therefore does not remove contribution-selection bias or protect whether an event participated.

\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} PriVoke introduces measurable local processing and infrastructure costs, but incremental and semantic-pipeline overhead remain unmeasured. For randomized telemetry, the nominal content bound is conditional and narrowly exceeded, while synthetic estimation error remains substantial. These findings establish neither deployment utility nor protection of participation and network metadata.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
The central contribution is a complete protection lifecycle with explicit boundaries: inspect locally, enforce a request decision, evaluate a version-bound candidate before publication, refresh clients, and randomize separate telemetry. The experiments show why those boundaries matter. For example, semantic improvements cannot undo a rule-based early BLOCK, while WARN results still allow exposure. Similarly, a candidate with a larger average gain may be rejected because of a single retained protection failure, and telemetry can support conditional aggregate estimation without becoming a source of training truth.

This contribution complements sanitization and recovery systems rather than replacing their utility questions. Casper, HaS and ProSan motivate what a user may need after detection: an acknowledged intervention, a usable rewritten prompt or a restored answer. PriVoke currently offers a request-action contract, so claims about these additional functions or superiority over the reviewed systems would require separate implementation and evidence.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
The largest scientific gap is independently adjudicated contextual privacy truth. Because our sensitivity, audience, category and action targets are assistant-provisional, they do not establish authorization or human agreement. Source annotation presence and injected category labels provide useful checks without filling this gap, since entity detection, contextual disclosure, appropriate action and task utility require different labels. The large drop from authored clean specificity to final annotation-negative specificity is an observed limitation, not evidence that one target should be dismissed.

The dependence and reuse of the authored cases further limit generalization: final source-group keys and external lexical exclusions do not certify template independence, cover paraphrases or remove unknown upstream pretraining exposure. Final rows are source-imbalanced: 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA. These English synthetic/machine-generated workloads do not represent other languages or organizational policies, and the separate cluster sensitivities remain descriptive and unadjusted. Neither repeated runs nor three seeds create independent populations. Native-package differences further limit causal attribution. A new protected successor is needed for future tuning after the opened final endpoint.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
A retention guard can protect only the behavior it tests, leaving considerable uncertainty when the matched guard is small and the zero-head control is weak. Rejected candidates do not become served improvements, and the broader negative studies likewise do not establish useful automatic adaptation. Future studies need common pinned bases, independent retention cases and explicit casewise harm criteria, retaining failed and effective artifacts separately.

Fail-closed behavior also introduces availability costs, while the installed Linux probes cover only the declared historical-detector Chromium/native receiver paths. Authenticated providers, Windows, updated-model installation, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified; confidence scores are heuristic rather than calibrated joint correctness, and WARN does not wait for informed acknowledgement. No study measures authorized task completion, unnecessary-intervention cost, warning comprehension or reformulation effort, so practical utility and human benefit remain unestablished.

Later instrumentation exposes ephemeral notice-rendering and local request-disposition events. Although fifty-three synthetic host tests validate that contract, its fields postdate the reported browser capture and have not undergone installed-browser or overhead remeasurement. Rendering does not prove comprehension, native handoff does not prove receipt, and page scripts can forge or suppress the events. These events are neither persisted nor sent as telemetry, and measuring utility still requires independent expected actions together with joined user/task outcomes.

\subsection{Measurement and Reproduction Boundaries}
The cost measurements have deliberately narrow scopes, since instrumented container totals do not isolate incremental client overhead and rules/NER timings do not measure semantic processing. Similarly, the telemetry bounds depend on retained local state, a trusted clock and independent uniform OS bits. Delivery selection, participation and metadata remain outside these bounds, while synthetic estimator error has no deployment-usefulness threshold and telemetry supplies no corrected contextual labels.

A sealed local source/model capsule reproduced twenty-four invented semantic requests across six packages with matching identities and outcomes. This is bounded replay using existing host dependencies, not public Git-only/fresh-environment reproduction or a protected-final rerun (Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction}). Ignored pinned model/source assets and licensing constraints remain relevant to redistribution. These limits guide the next work: independent contextual adjudication, protected generalization studies, broader receiver coverage, baseline-relative semantic cost and human utility, rather than additional branches of uncontrolled fitting.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke integrates local prompt inspection, pre-transmission request actions, guarded version-bound detector updates and separate randomized telemetry. Across the three research questions, the evidence yields a qualified systems result. Detection improves on authored cases while specificity transfers poorly, and supported enforcement is observable even though WARN forwards original content. Aggregate gains do not establish useful updates, as illustrated by the rejection of all full-model candidates in the matched study despite their better averages. The measured costs and privacy bounds likewise retain substantial limits in scope and utility. The system provides an auditable mechanism for protection and revision, without established independent contextual accuracy, safe automatic improvement or human utility.

\section{Exact Policy Representation}
\label{app:policy}
The sensitivity scale S0--S3 represents no identified risk, low-risk personal context, potentially harmful personal information and highly sensitive/identifying details or secrets. Correspondingly, visibility P0--P4/PU represents public, semi-public, authentication-restricted, group-private, personal-private and unspecified. The ten multilabel categories are HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY and THIRD\_PARTY.

The deterministic policy maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless IDENTITY and LOCATION coexist, or either occurs with P2--P4 visibility. Returned confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY/LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications map to ALLOW. Aggregation preserves individual enforcement decisions, unions categories and takes the strongest sensitivity/known visibility. Together, these rules define the implemented policy without independently validating disclosure authorization.
