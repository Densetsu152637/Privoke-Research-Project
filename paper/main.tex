\begin{abstract}
Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) can receive sensitive information through outgoing prompts. We present \privoke, a client-side firewall combining rules, NER and semantic classification with bounded cloud training and guarded parameter redistribution. In the tested local-inference configuration, user-text inspection remains local; training uses supplied labeled examples. We separate contextual labels, annotation presence, actions, request enforcement and telemetry. On a reused 1,560-case assistant-provisional contextual workload, V3 product joint correctness improved by 27.37--41.86 percentage points over V1, while retaining 139 shared over-enforced cases and three/six new efficient/balanced action failures. Balanced regex removal corrected those 139 cases but lost eighteen previously correct actions. Matched FULL continuation improved aggregate joint agreement over HEADS, yet every FULL candidate failed the retention guard. On a once-only 498-case annotation-presence final, the same V3 artifacts yielded recall 80.51--93.64\% and specificity 13.36--21.76\%, versus 98.40--99.87\% clean specificity on the authored semantic fixture. An external official-category reference and native Presidio configurations exposed further tradeoffs under explicit task/proxy mismatches. A complete instrumented Linux Chromium capture observed declared receiver behavior and 360 regex/NER decisions; whole-container resources include infrastructure and observer. Conditional host telemetry auditing found a tiny excess over nominal privacy budgets and substantial clipping error on synthetic workloads. These results establish auditable mechanics and bounded package gains, while leaving independent contextual truth, usability and deployment generalization unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly shaping modern AI-assisted workflows, particularly in writing, coding, analysis, and document-centric professional tasks~\cite{Gupta2024, LIANG2025101366, koraag2025comparing}. Their widespread adoption is largely driven by the capabilities of large-scale hosted models, which often provide substantially greater model capacity than locally deployable alternatives such as LLaMA-style models~\cite{Florian2025, MEDEC2024}. This increased capacity enables higher-quality responses, broader task handling, and greater productivity benefits, encouraging users to rely on web-hosted LLMs for everyday work activities~\cite{Crane2025, Zahra2025}. However, this convenience also introduces a critical privacy risk: users may include personal, organizational, or contextually sensitive information in prompts without fully recognizing the implications of sending that data to an external service~\cite{Athey2017}. While major LLM providers such as OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft offer privacy policies, retention controls, and enterprise settings, prompt processing remains largely provider-dependent, and user-submitted data may still be processed, logged, or reused within service improvement pipelines depending on the provider's policy and configuration~\cite{barbera2025ai,King2025}. As a result, prompt-based interaction with web-hosted LLMs poses a practical security challenge: sensitive information can leave the client before effective privacy enforcement can occur.

Recent incidents show that prompt privacy is no longer a hypothetical concern. In 2023, Samsung employees reportedly exposed confidential source code, internal meeting notes, and engineering information by submitting them to Chat-GPT for work-related assistance, prompting the company to restrict the use of generative AI tools within its workplace~\cite{SamsungBans2023}. In the same year, a Chat-GPT service bug exposed parts of users' conversation-history metadata and limited billing-related information for a subset of subscribers, demonstrating that prompt ecosystems can fail not only through user mistakes but also through platform-side vulnerabilities~\cite{BBCChatGPTHistoryBug2023}. Google reportedly warned employees not to enter confidential material into AI chatbots, including its own Bard/Gemini system, and its privacy notice cautioned users against including confidential or sensitive information in Bard conversations~\cite{GoogleWarns2023}. Similar risks have appeared in high-stakes public-sector settings, where sensitive child-protection information was entered into Chat-GPT during the preparation of a court-related report, leading regulators to impose restrictions on generative AI use within the department~\cite{ChatGPTChildProtection}. Claude.ai has also been shown to face prompt-injection and data-exfiltration risks, where chained vulnerabilities could enable sensitive information from conversation history to be extracted through provider-side workflows~\cite{ClaudeDay2026}. Beyond individual incidents, prior studies and provider-side analyses show that sensitive information supplied through prompts may be retained, reproduced, analyzed, or insufficiently sanitized by LLM-based systems~\cite{tamkin2024clio,priyanshu2023chatbots}. These cases highlight a critical gap in current LLM usage: privacy protection still depends on user caution, provider policy, or post-submission controls, while the most effective point of intervention is before sensitive content leaves the client.

Motivated by this gap, we propose \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that shifts prompt protection to the moment sensitive information is about to leave the user's device. In addition to user caution, organizational policy, and remote service controls, \privoke inspects outgoing prompts locally before they are transmitted to supported web-hosted LLM endpoints and applies warning or blocking decisions. Its design combines real-time prompt analysis on the client with a supporting microservices architecture for model management, privacy-oriented prompt fuzzing, controlled parameter updates, and telemetry protected by Local Differential Privacy (LDP). The current implementation forwards the original request on ALLOW or WARN and cancels a supported request on BLOCK; span masking returned by the runtime is not automatically substituted into browser traffic. This separation allows cloud-side training and robustness experiments to proceed independently of interactive client inference, while deployment cost and usability remain empirical questions. In this way, \privoke provides an endpoint-level intervention within existing web-hosted LLM workflows, with protection bounded by the supported request paths and enabled detector layers.

The principal contribution of \privoke is the design and evaluation of a guarded update path that retrains the privacy classifier in the cloud and distributes revised parameters to deployed clients while continuing to inspect user prompts locally. Labeled generated or prepared examples are processed by a server-side instance of the runtime, and accepted updates are published as versioned artifacts. A client retrieves the selected model and refreshes its cached parameters, so further training does not require an application release or client-side collection of training prompts. Successive updates can therefore support adaptation to newly supplied examples, but improvement is a hypothesis to evaluate: an updated classifier may improve one outcome while regressing another.

Client-side layered inspection has precedent in Casper~\cite{Casper2024}. We build on that established design and examine the additional training, publication, and redistribution path. The resulting research questions concern the contribution of each layer, the effect of controlled cloud-side updates, and whether the extension enforces the intended request decisions. The incident examples above motivate minimizing avoidable outgoing disclosure; billing exposure and exfiltration of information already held by a provider also illustrate downstream risks that a prompt interceptor cannot generally prevent.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We frame prompt-level privacy leakage as an endpoint security problem in web-hosted LLM workflows, where sensitive information may leave the client before effective safeguards are applied.

    \item We present \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that inspects supported outgoing prompts and applies ALLOW, WARN, or BLOCK decisions before transmission, with explicit forwarding and failure behavior.

    \item We combine rule-based matching, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and semantic classification with a sensitivity, visibility, and category policy, and distinguish entity-presence detection from contextual disclosure and action correctness.

    \item We introduce a cloud-training and local-inference architecture in which a privacy-focused fuzzer supplies labeled batches, the server runtime computes bounded updates, and validated revisions are streamed to deployed clients. This enables controlled evaluation of adaptation as new data become available, including detection gains and regressions.

    \item We integrate locally randomized categorical telemetry for aggregate deployment monitoring, with an explicit event-tuple privacy scope, composition budget, and exclusions; telemetry is independent of the model-training data path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Privacy Representation}
\label{sec:threat_model}
We protect supported outgoing prompt requests from inadvertent personal, third-party or organizational disclosure. The workstation, enabled extension, local runtime/supervisor and authenticated model publisher are trusted. Compromised clients, malicious publishers, deliberately replaced hooks and provider-held historical information are outside this boundary. Coverage is limited to recognized prompt POST formats through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest}; worker traffic, unsupported transports, attachments and endpoint changes require separate validation. Disabling protection bypasses inspection. Optional hosted inference changes the disclosure boundary and is excluded from local-inference measurements.

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
Classifications retain three dimensions: sensitivity S0--S3, visibility P0--P4/PU, and a multilabel category set. S0 means no identified risk, S1 low-risk personal context, S2 potentially harmful personal information and S3 highly sensitive/identifying details or secrets. P0--P4 denote public, semi-public, authentication-restricted, group-private and personal-private context; PU denotes unspecified visibility. The ten categories are HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY and THIRD\_PARTY. These policy labels do not independently establish disclosure authorization.

The deterministic policy maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless IDENTITY and LOCATION coexist, or either occurs with P2--P4 visibility. Returned confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY/LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications map to ALLOW. Confidence is not established as calibrated probability. Aggregation preserves individual enforcement decisions, unions categories and takes the strongest sensitivity/known visibility. Entity presence, contextual labels and appropriate action are therefore distinct targets.

\section{Architecture and Update Path}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Local Inspection and Enforcement}
A browser page hook extracts supported prompt text and sends it through the WebExtension background process and loopback gRPC-Web bridge to a workstation-local Python runtime. A native-messaging supervisor manages bridge/runtime lifecycle. User inference does not fall back to the server-side training runtime. ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels it. A returned masked span is evidence rather than automatically substituted browser content. Unavailable analysis blocks supported requests; a streaming outage may retain other enabled layers, reducing semantic coverage rather than guaranteeing it.

The runtime normalizes Unicode, case, separators, digit spacing and whitespace while retaining offsets for original-text evidence. Regex runs first by default; regex BLOCK skips later requested detectors. Otherwise enabled regex, spaCy NER and semantic findings are aggregated. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW response; valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. Semantic-only experiments explicitly select layer 4 and verify actual sole-layer execution. Product experiments explicitly select [2,3,4] and retain legitimate skip/error records. An empty list implicitly selects the complete pipeline and cannot establish semantic-only evidence.

Historical V1 profiles are repository-owned transformers with seeded-bootstrap encoders. Balanced has 36,756 parameters, two blocks, hidden size 32, vocabulary 512 and a 96-token window; head-only training changes 660 parameters. Later V3 profiles retain complete pretrained Google BERT encoders/poolers~\cite{googleBertL4H128A2,googleBertL2H256A4,googleBertL12H256A4,turc2019WellReadStudents}. They remain classifiers rather than conversational generators. Native tokenizer, capacity, threshold and training-history differences are preserved in package comparisons.

\subsection{Guarded Cloud Training and Redistribution}
The fuzzer constructs bounded labeled TRAIN and held-out batches, excludes protected rows/text/groups, and requests head or admitted full-model gradients from a separate server runtime. That runtime performs model fetching, execution and differentiation, returning model-bound deltas and candidate metrics. The fuzzer submits eligible updates to an updater that validates exact base identity, trainable tensor inventory/shapes, finite values, magnitude limits and publication guards. Checksums or accepted receipts alone are not efficacy evidence.

The updater atomically publishes each validated artifact revision using compare-and-swap base checks and durable replay receipts. Conflicting/stale bases fail; identical retries do not apply twice. Head and underlying stages are separate publications, so an accepted head revision may remain if the later full stage rejects. Streaming serves versioned parameters and clients refresh cached weights without an application release. The path is labeled examples $\rightarrow$ server computation $\rightarrow$ guarded publication $\rightarrow$ streamed parameters, separate from local user-text inspection. This is centralized training, not federated learning, user-gradient collection or a demonstrated cloud deployment. Experimental retention objectives, samplers and optimizer variants remain documented engineering treatments rather than assumed sources of quality gain.

\subsection{Telemetry Boundary}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
Telemetry is independent of training. Randomized packets contain action, a coarse action/sensitivity risk bucket, one precedence-selected category, bounded release label and four-hour UTC time-of-day bin without date; they exclude raw prompts, identifiers, exact scores and per-layer findings. For public event budget $\varepsilon$, five independently randomized fields receive $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. A field with domain size $K_i$ returns its true value with probability
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\qquad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
for each alternative. Ideal product sampling bounds the event-tuple likelihood ratio by $e^{\sum_i\varepsilon_i}=e^\varepsilon$ under common public configuration. Actual binary64 thresholds differ from that ideal mechanism. Let $\widetilde p_i$ denote the implemented host binary64 threshold. In the audited CPython sampler, a fresh uniform 53-bit integer $J$ selects truth when $J/2^{53}<\widetilde p_i$, giving
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil 2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\qquad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{finite}}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Alternative selection uses rejection sampling. This conditional finite-kernel calculation assumes independent uniform OS bits; it does not certify OS entropy or deployed builds. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves nominal budget before construction, defaults to event epsilon 1 and total epsilon 8 per UTC day, and does not refund failures. Report presence, accepted counts, delivery selection and network metadata are excluded; retained state and a trusted clock are required. The collector inverts nominal response probabilities and independently clips each marginal to $[0,n]$, so estimates need not form a coherent histogram.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Update Mechanics and Adaptation Limits}
Audited runtime computation, bounded publication and client refresh demonstrate that the path can change served parameters, not sustained improvement. Six early contextual grids completed 126 attempts with no retained qualifier. \subsection{Finite Telemetry Privacy and Synthetic Utility}
\label{sec:telemetry_results}
A fingerprinted Windows CPython 3.11.9 audit enumerated exact rational finite kernels for the actual five domains, $K=3,4,11,2,6$. Outward 100-digit logarithm enclosures place maximum event loss above nominal $\varepsilon=0.5,1,2$ by approximately $1.294\times10^{-15}$, $1.503\times10^{-15}$ and $1.226\times10^{-15}$, respectively. Thus the exact nominal bound fails in these settings, despite the tiny excess. Eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$ under the same entropy assumptions. Other configurations and deployed builds remain uncertified.

Across 180 fixed-truth simulation cells and 10,000 repetitions per cell, inversion and clipping produced substantial conditional error. For balanced integer category counts with $K=11$, event $\varepsilon=1$ and $n=80/8000$ contributing reports, first-category true counts were $7/727$, clipped bias $+22.84/+237.50$ and RMSE $42.32/1028.47$ counts. Clipping occurred in $85.62/28.32\%$ of trials; independently estimated marginals failed to sum to $n$ in $99.99/99.78\%$. Larger $n$ pools installation-days, each capped at eight default reservations. These are errors around realized contributor counts, excluding population sampling; simulator probabilities are binary64 approximations of the exact finite kernel. Numerical moment/collector checks support implementation consistency, not a deployment-usefulness threshold.

Real isolated ledger checks accepted eight reservations and denied the ninth, preserved the cap across process reopen, reset next UTC day, failed closed on rollback/corruption and accepted sixteen of forty concurrent epsilon-0.5 requests. Build, queue and delivery failures consumed reservations. A constructed 32-event sequence with 25\% BLOCK yielded 100\% versus 0\% BLOCK among first-eight contributors when event order reversed. Randomization therefore does not remove contribution-selection bias; no real deployment monitoring accuracy was measured.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The evidence distinguishes enabling adaptation from demonstrating a useful revision. Early guarded updates changed parameters without satisfying retention criteria; later supervised pretrained packages improved contextual agreement while retaining errors and action harms. Neither publication nor a better average guarantees protection for a previously covered case. Fixed-base component metrics and paired regressions are necessary acceptance evidence.

The balanced detector counterfactual demonstrates a concrete enforcement tradeoff: removing regex resolves 139 unnecessary restrictions but introduces eighteen additional action failures. Improved semantic classification cannot remove a regex-first BLOCK within the ordinary cascade. Unchanged outputs after NER removal are specific to this fixture and label filtering. AU3 semantic-only, PIPE3 product and detector-removal results answer different questions despite shared text; actual execution must be labeled where other layers recover or prevent semantic decisions.

The frozen final annotation-presence endpoint and external source-category reference expose recall/specificity tradeoffs beyond the authored contextual grid. The AU3-to-final contrast holds artifacts and the non-S0-or-category response projection fixed while changing inputs and target labels; the external reference additionally changes the response proxy. These differences do not isolate a cause or explain away poor agreement on the new endpoints. High AU3 clean-control agreement does not establish broader generalization. Different targets prevent a shared privacy-accuracy ranking; neither new endpoint supplies independently reviewed contextual truth. The same-case Presidio reference shows larger V3 binary agreement here, not universal recognizer superiority. Entity detection, contextual disclosure, sanitization and downstream utility require different truth and measurements. PriVoke-authored synthetic cases and native-package differences matter even when IDs match. WARN's original-content forwarding also separates risk communication from prevention; sanitization needs distinct implementation and utility evidence.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textit{Label validity and task alignment.} Contextual sensitivity, audience, categories and allowed actions remain assistant-provisional, without independent adjudication or disclosure authorization. PIIMB annotations instead identify entities; our custom prompt-level annotation-presence projection is neither contextual truth nor its official character-masking metric. AdvPIIBench CATEGORY describes injected identifiers and source-negative constructions, with incomplete prompt annotation and no human labels. Broader semantic findings can disagree without establishing privacy errors. Its any-finding proxy and five-type recognizer differ from the other studies' projections/configurations. Small/large native Presidio settings expose tradeoffs rather than an optimized strongest baseline. Published sanitization/utility outcomes supply no common privacy-accuracy ranking.

\textit{Dependence and selection.} AU3 cases were authored for PriVoke and opened before PIPE3: 35 families and 24 expression groups generate dependent observations, while repeat passes provide repeatability. Reused validation/development informed exploratory work; later freezing cannot retrospectively make them untouched confirmation. Final scoring used 464 observed source-group keys, not certified independent templates, and was source-imbalanced (308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, 80 Gretel, twenty Privy, one MAPA row). External lexical components exclude declared links, not unknown templates, punctuated identifier variants, paraphrases or upstream pretraining. Separate cluster sensitivities are descriptive and unadjusted; they do not establish crossed independence, human-label uncertainty or population prevalence. English synthetic/machine-generated sources do not represent broader languages and organizational policies.

\textit{Attribution and retention.} Native packages differ in capacity, tokenizer, threshold, window, initialization, backend and training history; selected task epochs exclude unknown upstream pretraining. PH3/EN3/AU3 also change supervision and exposure. Matched HEADS/FULL fixes data/order and head settings, but FULL includes encoder optimization and joint-gradient clipping; its zero-head ORIGINAL is a weak task baseline, and three seeds reuse one control and assessment. Guard-rejected candidates are observations, not served gains. Package comparisons and offline continuation do not establish useful automatic fuzzer updates, a universal tier ordering or preserved casewise protection. Aggregate gains coexist with category/audience errors and new action failures.

\textit{Policy, confidence and enforcement.} Allowed-action agreement does not establish consent or appropriate warning/blocking costs. Confidence diagnostics cover only 443--1,207 outcome-selected emitted findings per package. In V3's $[0.9,1]$ bin, sensitivity exactness is about 97--99\% versus category exactness 78--85\%, despite mean scores near 0.99; this heuristic is no calibrated presence or joint-correctness probability. Installed Linux probes cover the declared V1 Chromium/native receiver paths, with supported-hook trust and enabled settings assumed. Real providers, Windows, V3 installation, attachments/workers and unsupported transports remain unverified. WARN forwards original content; stream fallback can reduce actual layers. The completed regex/NER [2,3] cost protocol cannot establish semantic-pipeline latency. Instrumented whole-container totals include resident infrastructure/cache and observer, with sampled memory gaps and no unprotected baseline or per-role attribution. Fail-closed behavior also imposes availability costs; invented probes do not measure usability.

\textit{Telemetry and reproduction.} Finite-kernel bounds assume independent uniform OS bits and fingerprinted host arithmetic, not certified deployed entropy. Participation, delivery selection and transport metadata remain excluded; clock and retained installation budget state are trusted. Synthetic estimator error is conditional on fixed contributors, without deployment population sampling or an application-usefulness threshold. Telemetry is independent of training and provides no corrected contextual labels. Byte audits and the isolated capsule support local invented-input replay using existing dependencies; public Git-only/fresh-environment reproduction and deployed performance remain unestablished. Ignored model/source assets are still required. Dataset/model licenses and attribution constrain redistribution; no real-user consent or warning-frequency evidence was collected.
\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}
\textit{Reviewed supervision and independent evaluation.} Prioritize independently reviewed sensitivity, audience, complete category sets and allowed actions, distinguishing actual disclosures from discussion/fiction and retaining unknown audience. Adjudicate disagreement rather than turn missing annotations into clean labels. Freeze inclusion, protected source/template exclusions and analysis before fitting. Future mixed-class contextual source-heldout data need difficult clean controls, sensitive disclosures, broader language/policy coverage and casewise required-BLOCK errors. The opened final 498 endpoint now requires a new protected successor; it must not become another tuning pool.

\textit{Adaptation and detector studies.} Use common pinned bases, matched exposure and multiple realizations to separate supervision, sampler, objective and optimizer treatments. Expand independently reviewed guards and held-out harms beyond the present sixteen cases, preserving raw rejected candidates, effective artifacts and both original/parent comparisons. Keep sole-semantic execution distinct from product ablation. Balanced regex removal motivates targeted rule/policy review rather than unconditional disabling. Further recognizer comparisons require a common justified task and transparent adaptation, rather than selecting by model size or aggregate accuracy.

\textit{Installed protection and sanitization.} Extend the observed Linux receiver matrix to authenticated provider sandboxes, Windows and additional formats/transports, retaining receiver bytes, late-send checks and client outcomes. Repeat instrumented cost measurements across hardware and semantic pipelines, with actual layer scope and an unprotected baseline to estimate incremental overhead. Warning comprehension and unnecessary restrictions need human usability evidence. Request-body masking remains a distinct future feature: reliable span/body reconstruction must prevent original-secret transmission across formats and preserve downstream task utility. Returned masked evidence alone does not implement browser sanitization.

\textit{Monitoring and redistribution.} Verify deployed finite-sampler/entropy assumptions and accounting, then evaluate coherent histogram estimates, contribution selection and application-specific monitoring utility. Telemetry-guided training needs separate consent, justified label signals and data-flow/privacy analysis. Fresh dependency-locked reproduction and licensed artifact access remain separate from the local capsule. Compression/pruning should be explored only with export checks, native parity, measured download/memory effects and retained publication/client-refresh guards. These directions are prospective, not demonstrated improvements.
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke implements bounded cloud-side training and guarded redistribution with local prompt inspection. Reused contextual product gains coexist with paired action failures; balanced detector removal exchanges 139 corrected restrictions for eighteen lost correct actions. Matched FULL fits improve aggregate agreement but all fail their retention guard. The same V3 artifacts yield 98.40--99.87\% clean specificity on the authored semantic fixture but 13.36--21.76\% against final annotation absence; this changed-data/target contrast leaves its causes unisolated. External source-category/recognizer comparisons likewise reveal different recall/specificity tradeoffs rather than a common privacy ranking. Installed Linux probes and a 24-request local capsule replay provide bounded enforcement/reconstruction evidence; instrumented regex/NER cost scope and existing dependencies limit those conclusions. The telemetry audit discloses a tiny nominal-budget excess and synthetic clipping error. The contribution is an auditable adaptation mechanism with measured gains and failures, without established safe automatic improvement. Independent contextual adjudication, human usability, broader provider/Windows coverage and deployment monitoring remain necessary.
