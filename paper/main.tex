\begin{abstract}
Useful prompts often contain information that users may not intend to disclose to an external language-model provider. \privoke inspects supported requests locally and applies explicit allow, warn or block actions before transmission. A separate training path checks version and retention constraints before distributing accepted parameters, while independently randomized telemetry provides no training data. Across 1,560 authored cases with provisional targets, updated packages improved contextual policy agreement. Targeted detector removal nevertheless exposed a tradeoff between unnecessary restrictions and missed protection, and a frozen 498-row semantic annotation-presence check yielded specificity of only 13.36 to 21.76\%. Thirty-three installed receiver probes verified the declared request behavior. Every matched full-model candidate improved aggregate agreement but failed retention. Installed pretrained-model measurements isolated resident detector-activation cost, although a quality-tier probe blocked a benign prompt. A detector-layer precision revision moved regex specificity on a previously opened endpoint, scored once for the detector layers, from 98.5 to 99.6\% at a recall cost of 1.3 points; each shift rests on three rows and is not statistically significant (exact McNemar $p=0.25$), the rules-and-NER union showed no detectable change (which does not establish equivalence), and no net NER benefit was established. A full-product rerun with the revised detectors reduced the 139 shared unnecessary restrictions to 108 without new binary misses, while the removal tradeoff persisted. Controlled telemetry tests verified delivery and accounting; separate audits identified a small excess over nominal privacy loss and substantial synthetic estimation error. Together, these findings demonstrate an inspectable protection and revision mechanism while leaving independent contextual accuracy, adaptation without lost protection, real-user monitoring usefulness and practical user benefit unresolved.
\end{abstract}

Local differential privacy, local inference, model regression, prompt privacy, randomized telemetry, request enforcement.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Web-hosted large language models (LLMs) support writing, coding and analysis by processing users' instructions and contextual material~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing}. That context can extend well beyond the immediate question. A person seeking help with a report, for example, might include a colleague's details, health information or confidential organizational material. Information that helps the model complete the task can therefore also disclose something the user intended to keep private. Reported workplace incidents and company warnings illustrate this risk during legitimate work~\cite{SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Controls on subsequent storage or use cannot prevent the initial transfer, whereas inspection on the user's device offers an opportunity to intervene before local control is lost.

Local execution of the application model is one alternative to sending a prompt to a hosted service. Research releases such as LLaMA demonstrate that foundation models can be made available for separate deployment~\cite{Llama2023}, but moving the whole task locally changes the chosen application and its computational requirements. PriVoke instead studies intervention within an existing hosted workflow: a compact local detector decides whether a supported request may proceed to the external model. This choice makes detector cost, endpoint coverage and the consequences of interruption relevant alongside classification accuracy. It does not assume that hosted models are universally more capable than local alternatives.

The point of intervention also determines which privacy risks the system can address. Priyanshu et al. investigate the reproduction of sensitive prompt information in chatbot outputs and the effect of instructions intended to sanitize those outputs~\cite{priyanshu2023chatbots}. Such work concerns information already supplied to the model. Pre-transmission inspection can prevent a particular submission, but cannot recover a previous disclosure or protect information held by a provider through an unrelated channel. The motivating workplace examples therefore establish a reason to examine outgoing disclosure, without making PriVoke a remedy for every platform-side privacy failure.

Deciding when to intervene involves more than recognizing names or identifiers. Prompts about the same subject can differ in whose information they contain, their intended recipients and the appropriateness of disclosure. Nissenbaum's account of contextual integrity likewise relates privacy to the norms governing information flows within a particular context~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}. Detector labels can represent some of these distinctions without establishing that a disclosure is authorized. The resulting decision must also affect the outgoing request: alerting a user to sensitive content does not necessarily prevent its transmission. An evaluation must therefore distinguish contextual predictions, policy decisions and what actually leaves the device.

This distinction shaped the variables used in the research. Sensitivity describes the information's potential for harm; intended audience describes the claimed disclosure context; and information categories identify overlapping types of content. The requested detector layers and the order in which they execute determine which findings reach the policy. ALLOW, WARN and BLOCK then determine the request disposition. Treating these variables separately permits a useful diagnosis: a system may recognize sensitive content but choose an inappropriate action, or choose the intended action while describing the content incorrectly. Neither outcome can be inferred from an entity-presence score alone. Model capacity, input windows, pretraining and decision thresholds may also affect the balance between coverage, restriction and local cost. The compact-model precedent~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents} motivates examining such configurations, but the native-package comparison changes several together; only the matched comparisons can narrow the attribution.

We address these connected decisions through \privoke, an implemented client-side privacy firewall for supported outgoing prompts. It combines rules, named entity recognition (NER) and semantic classification to characterize sensitivity, intended audience and information categories, then selects ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. ALLOW and WARN release the original request; BLOCK cancels it. WARN attempts to display a notice but does not wait for acknowledgement, so the warning itself demonstrates neither consent nor prevented disclosure. This explicit request contract makes it possible to assess classification and enforcement as related, but distinct, outcomes.

Maintaining the detector raises a further difficulty: a revision can improve average accuracy while becoming incorrect on cases its predecessor handled correctly~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. PriVoke therefore separates learning a candidate from accepting it. A server-side runtime computes a candidate update from supplied labelled examples; the candidate is gated on declared retention criteria before the update service applies it only to its exact parent model. Clients can verify accepted artifacts and refresh local parameters without an application release. Reporting follows a separate path, with coarse event fields randomized on the workstation. Neither intercepted prompts nor telemetry supplies training labels, keeping maintenance and reporting independent of user traffic as a source of supervision.

These separations also constrain what adaptation can mean. The model's base version, trainable parameters, exposure to labelled examples and retention cases are experimental variables, rather than evidence that a revision will help. A changed input distribution may motivate supplying new examples, but a lower training loss cannot demonstrate preserved protection. Operational reporting introduces a different set of variables: which events are admitted, how their fields are randomized, the privacy budget consumed and the error in the resulting estimates. Reporting can be implemented correctly while failing to represent the events or users of interest. We therefore evaluate maintenance and reporting as distinct mechanisms, with no automatic learning loop inferred from their coexistence.

Casper, Hide and Seek and ProSan establish substantial precedent for local protection and learned sanitization~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}. Our contribution lies in the integration and empirical analysis of request enforcement, guarded updates and separate reporting; local detection, regression analysis and randomized response are established techniques.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A client-side semantic privacy firewall that combines local rule-, NER-, and semantic-based inspection with explicit ALLOW, WARN, and BLOCK enforcement before supported prompts leave the workstation.
\item A guarded model-update lifecycle that binds candidate updates to their parent versions and applies retention checks before publication, showing that aggregate accuracy gains do not necessarily preserve previously protected behavior.
\item A privacy-bounded telemetry mechanism that releases only randomized coarse event information under a local privacy-budget ledger, while remaining separate from user-prompt inspection and model training.
\item An end-to-end empirical evaluation of detection, request enforcement, model-update retention, runtime overhead, and telemetry privacy, revealing trade-offs between improved agreement, unnecessary restriction, lost protection, and monitoring utility.
\end{enumerate}


We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1}: Does PriVoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively?
\item \textbf{RQ2}: Can PriVoke update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior?
\item \textbf{RQ3}: What systems and privacy overhead does PriVoke introduce?
\end{enumerate}
RQ1 examines the relationship between contextual prediction and enforcement, including situations in which better classification or fewer restrictions do not mean better protection. RQ2 tests whether aggregate improvements satisfy a casewise retention requirement. RQ3 measures systems costs while distinguishing report-content privacy from estimation error and contribution selection. Together, the questions establish what successful execution, training and accounting can demonstrate, and where the evidence remains limited by provisional contextual labels.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local intervention and contextual decisions.} Casper places prompt protection on the user's device through a browser extension combining rules, entity recognition and local topic analysis~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024}. In the reviewed prototype, a keyboard shortcut initiates inspection; identifiers are replaced with placeholders, users can request their restoration in responses, and topic warnings require acknowledgement. Custom rules, topics and compatible replacement models make the protection configurable. PriVoke adopts the motivation for layered local inspection but implements a different request contract: supported hooks apply decisions automatically, and WARN releases unchanged content without waiting for a user response.

Hide and Seek (HaS) seeks to preserve useful interaction by hiding entities before cloud inference and recovering them in the response~\cite{HaS2024}. Its generative variant trains hiding and recovery models, whereas its label-based variant uses an entity recognizer for hiding. ProSan selects word replacements through task-importance and privacy proxies, then trains a sanitizer for local execution~\cite{ProSan2024}. PromptShield approaches enterprise protection through role/policy classification~\cite{PromptShield2025}. Together, these systems show why detecting information, controlling its disclosure and completing the user's task warrant separate evaluation. PriVoke's labels describe its policy, but cannot by themselves validate contextual authorization.

\textit{Retaining behavior during model revision.} Model-update research distinguishes improved average performance from preservation of individual predictions. Yan et al. examine \emph{negative flips}, in which an updated image classifier becomes incorrect on a previously correct example, and propose positive-congruent training to reduce these regressions~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. This motivates measuring both gains and losses, although image-classification findings do not establish privacy-policy correctness. PriVoke applies the distinction to publication: it compares labels and actions against the declared base and can reject an improved candidate through a configured retention guard. Linking this decision to a versioned artifact and verified client refresh separates successful optimization from acceptance for use. The evaluated connection is the contribution; the guard remains limited to its declared cases and criteria.

This framing does not require earlier systems to lack configurable or learned components. Casper permits custom rules and topics, and HaS and ProSan explicitly study learned transformations. The narrower question is how to connect a proposed classifier change to an identifiable parent, a declared behavioral acceptance rule and the artifact that would be served. That question remains meaningful even when the local detector, learning algorithm and publication machinery each use established techniques. It also makes a rejection informative: an update can reveal a useful learning direction while remaining ineligible under the chosen harm criterion.

\textit{Privacy of operational reporting.} Local randomization supports aggregate collection without directly releasing each underlying value. RAPPOR applies randomized response to client-side statistics and addresses the additional privacy cost of repeated reporting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}. PriVoke instead combines categorical event reports with a local budget ledger, without adopting RAPPOR's memoization. Its evaluation must consequently establish both whether the implemented sampler and accounting meet the stated event-content bounds and whether the estimates are useful. We examine finite arithmetic, estimation error and contribution selection separately because a content bound protects neither participation nor network metadata. Nor do the reported fields provide corrected contextual labels or establish a need to retrain the detector.

\textit{Contribution and comparison boundaries.} These strands address different outcomes: sanitization and recovery concern disclosure and continued use, negative-flip analysis concerns changed predictions, and randomization constrains released observations. RQ1 connects layered decisions to receiver-observed transmission, without claiming Casper's entity/topic, HaS's recovery-resistance or ProSan's task-utility results. RQ2 distinguishes aggregate improvement from eligibility for version-bound replacement, while RQ3 examines cost, finite content privacy, estimation and admission selection. Making these decisions jointly inspectable reveals where they diverge. Their different targets and denominators, however, provide no basis for a numerical superiority claim.

\section{System Design and Privacy Boundary}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
\privoke intervenes when personal, third-party or organizational information is about to leave the workstation in a supported prompt request. It trusts the enabled extension, local runtime and supervisor, workstation, and authenticated publisher. Protection therefore depends on that boundary: compromised or deliberately replaced page hooks can bypass inspection, and blocking a later request cannot recover information already disclosed.

The interception path recognizes prompt POST formats sent through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} (XHR). Workers, attachments, other transports and changed endpoints need separate validation. Users may also disable protection, deliberately bypassing inspection. An optional hosted-inference configuration moves text beyond the workstation and changes the disclosure boundary; it is consequently excluded from this paper's local-inference measurements.

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} distinguishes prompt inspection from training on supplied examples and randomized reporting. A permitting action releases the prompt to the hosted LLM, but maintenance need not collect that prompt and the collector supplies no training labels. This separation defines the information paths without establishing that the resulting decisions are appropriate.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
The page hook extracts supported prompt text and passes it through the extension background and loopback gRPC-Web remote-procedure-call bridge to the workstation's Python runtime. Native messaging starts the supervisor that manages this path. The returned action determines whether the original request is released or cancelled; the separate server-side runtime instance that computes training updates never serves as an inference fallback.

The runtime normalizes text while retaining offsets linking findings to the original input. It produces two views with the same normalization procedure, run once per view: case-preserved text for the regular-expression and NER layers, and lowercased text for the semantic models, which were trained on lowercased normalized input. Unicode normalization, whitespace and obfuscation handling are shared, so both views map findings to the same original intervals and differ only in letter case. The rule and NER layers also apply the precision controls of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision_design}. The runtime evaluates regular-expression rules before enabled NER and semantic layers, ending the cascade immediately if a rule returns BLOCK. Otherwise, it combines findings using the strongest action. As a result, a semantic classifier cannot reverse an earlier rule-based restriction, and an early BLOCK prevents the remaining requested detectors from running.

Failure handling also depends on which layers actually execute. A failed requested layer turns an otherwise ALLOW result into BLOCK, while valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. The extension blocks supported requests if analysis itself is unavailable. Loss of the parameter stream can instead leave rules and NER running in a reduced configuration, which may release a request without semantic inspection. The action and execution record must therefore be interpreted together.



\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
Semantic classification describes sensitivity, audience and information categories. Sensitivity ranges from no identified risk (S0), through low-risk personal context (S1) and potentially harmful personal information (S2), to highly sensitive information, identifiers or secrets (S3). Audience, termed \emph{visibility} in the implementation, distinguishes public (P0), semi-public (P1), authentication-restricted (P2), group-private (P3), personal-private (P4) and unspecified (PU) contexts. Categories may overlap and cover health, politics, religion, criminal, financial, sexual, child, location, identity and third-party information.

The policy ordinarily maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 allow unless identity and location occur together, or either occurs with visibility P2--P4; these exceptions produce WARN. S0 without categories always allows. Numeric confidence below 0.5 overrides this mapping: S3 becomes WARN, S2 remains WARN only when identity or location is present, and other cases become ALLOW. Confidence summarizes sensitivity and category heads rather than a calibrated probability of joint correctness or authorized disclosure. Neither the labels nor their deterministic mapping has been independently validated for contextual authorization. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} states the decision order explicitly.

Consider a hypothetical S2 prediction with HEALTH, confidence 0.7 and no earlier restriction. The policy returns WARN, allowing the supported request to send its original body without acknowledgement. If a rule had already returned BLOCK, the request would instead be cancelled before semantic inspection. This example illustrates the mapping; it adds no observed prediction, independently labelled case or test.

These models classify prompts rather than generate conversational responses. Historical V1 packages use randomly initialized frozen encoders with fitted heads; V3 packages retain pretrained Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) encoders and poolers\footnote{\scriptsize \href{https://huggingface.co/google/bert_uncased_L-4_H-128_A-2/blob/c29bee83fc7f003ac8c5e6e135529da4ecddb7c3/README.md}{BERT L-4/H-128/A-2, revision c29bee83fc7f003ac8c5e6e135529da4ecddb7c3}; \href{https://huggingface.co/google/bert_uncased_L-2_H-256_A-4/blob/4e937a8675e5afd9a4836735c186ec01695bc3ea/README.md}{BERT L-2/H-256/A-4, revision 4e937a8675e5afd9a4836735c186ec01695bc3ea}; \href{https://huggingface.co/google/bert_uncased_L-12_H-256_A-4/blob/2ab9a0f41435a4d23d4cbc11cc3e7c922545f13d/README.md}{BERT L-12/H-256/A-4, revision 2ab9a0f41435a4d23d4cbc11cc3e7c922545f13d}.}~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}. Both families offer efficient, balanced and quality packages, whose names imply no guaranteed performance ordering. The installed default now selects V3 balanced, alongside explicit V3 tiers and labelled older V1 choices. Its CPU loader verifies binary packages from the model service without falling back to a random or V1 model. Differences in tokenizers, capacity, thresholds and training history mean that comparisons assess the complete package change, not pretraining or architecture alone.

\subsection{Detector Precision Controls}
\label{sec:detector_precision_design}
Rules and NER run first, and a rule BLOCK ends the cascade, so their false detections cannot be repaired by a later semantic layer. Three controls therefore limit how often these layers fire without personal context. They change detector findings only; the policy mapping, the semantic models and the update path are unchanged.

\textit{Rule gates.} A narrative heuristic that matched on length alone was removed. Kinship, family and child cues now require a possessive determiner, optionally with modifier words, or an age expression combined with a person noun. Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns after a possessive, and location keywords require a connector followed by a name rather than a closed-class word. Structured identity values remain on one short line. Rules derived from Presidio patterns regained validators: Luhn checksums~\cite{Luhn1960Patent} for card numbers, mod-97 for IBANs~\cite{ISO13616Part1_2020}, mod-11 for NHS numbers, issuable ranges for US social security numbers and whole-word context terms for weakly shaped licence and date patterns. Placeholder passwords, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. Appendix~\ref{app:detprec} lists the gates.

\textit{Case-preserved input.} All rules are compiled case-insensitively, so letter case does not determine which rules match. The small English spaCy NER model~\cite{Honnibal2020SpaCy}, however, uses capitalization as evidence for proper nouns, and lowercasing the input suppresses that evidence. Passing case-preserved text to NER therefore increases the entities it returns, but it also admits capitalized non-names such as headings, acronyms and ordinary words, which motivates the filter below.

\textit{NER post-filter.} A first stage removes entity spans that are unlikely to be personal: masking placeholders, identifier-shaped tokens, spans inside structural headings, short acronyms without a locating or affiliation preposition, and single-token person candidates that an offline lexicon and tagger read as ordinary words. A second stage assigns sensitivity from the combination of surviving categories, following a quasi-identifier principle: a lone place or organization does not identify the speaker and is dropped unless a relation cue such as ``I live in'' ties it to the speaker; a person linked with a corroborated place or organization, or a corroborated name (multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring), yields S2; and an uncorroborated single name yields S1. The filter records the deciding rule for each emitted entity and uses only resources installed with the model.

The rule repairs ship unconditionally. The case-preserved input and the post-filter are on by default and can be switched off individually (\path{PRIVOKE_CASE_PRESERVING_DETECTORS} and \path{PRIVOKE_NER_POSTFILTER}, set to 0), which preserves the pre-change behavior for ablation and rollback. A later action-level check (Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision}) found that removing the child cue had downgraded identified adult student records from BLOCK to WARN. A student-identifier rule (S3 identity; a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student) was therefore added to the shipped rules after the evaluations below were scored, and none of them includes it.

\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
Before accepting a candidate, the update path checks both its model identity and its behavior. In the legacy route, the updater periodically requests a cycle from a fuzzer, which prepares bounded labelled batches and held-out guard cases, excluding protected rows, texts and groups from training. The fuzzer never loads model weights. Instead, the server-side runtime computes bounded parameter changes against its cached copy of the specified base, obtained from the model-streaming service, and returns them with base and candidate metrics bound to that base. The fuzzer forwards only candidates satisfying its configured quality and retention guards. The updater then checks parent identity, permitted tensors and shapes, finite values and magnitude limits before applying the update. Artifact checks establish that a candidate is well formed; retention checks assess its behavior on declared cases. Neither successful fitting nor parameter transfer establishes acceptance.



Publication uses atomic compare-and-swap: the current base must still match the candidate's declared parent. This prevents a decision about one model from replacing an intervening revision. Durable replay receipts connect identical retries to their earlier artifact outcomes, avoiding duplicate application. Head and underlying-model stages are separate publications, however, so rejection of a later stage does not undo an accepted head update. Clients verify delivered artifacts before refreshing cached weights. Standard V3 instead uses a pinned read-only package store and a configuration-owned alias, beyond the separately enabled legacy training/publication route. Unsupported V3 legacy-gradient calls are rejected; installed V3 inference thus provides no evidence of deployed V3 learning or guarded publication.

Training is centralized and uses supplied examples, without user gradients or federated learning. The implemented service path establishes neither cloud deployment nor the value of a particular revision.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
Telemetry reports five coarse event fields: action, an action/sensitivity risk bucket, one category chosen by fixed precedence, a bounded release label and a four-hour time-of-day bin without a date. Their domains have sizes $K_i=3,4,11,2,6$, including a no-category value. Each field is randomized locally; raw prompts, identifiers, exact confidence and per-layer findings are omitted. This reduces disclosure without making the remaining fields harmless. The collector estimates lifetime marginals for usage and development inspection, but cannot estimate latency or error rates: it has no analysis/user denominator, date/window query or joint-event record, and distinguishes only v0.3.0 from OTHER. A separate Health call checks store writability. Neither channel produces corrected training labels.

Local differential privacy limits how much an output probability can vary between possible event contents~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. This comparison fixes the reporting opportunity and public configuration, leaving the presence of a report visible. The event budget $\varepsilon$ is divided equally across fields as $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. For a domain of size $K_i$, the true value has reporting probability $p_i$ and each alternative has probability $q_i$:
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}.
\end{equation}
Under independent ideal sampling, composition multiplies the field likelihood ratios to bound the event ratio by $e^\varepsilon$~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. The implementation compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw, $J/2^{53}$, with a binary64 threshold $\widetilde p_i$, choosing alternatives by rejection sampling. The resulting finite probabilities and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The calculation depends on host arithmetic and independent uniform random bits, so the ideal formula alone cannot certify the sampler. A persistent ledger reserves budget before report construction, defaulting to one per event and eight per Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day. Failed construction or delivery does not refund that reservation; accounting therefore requires retained state and a trusted clock. Report presence, contribution selection, delivery and network metadata fall outside the content guarantee. The collector estimates marginals by inverting nominal response probabilities and clipping counts to their feasible range, leaving their usefulness after randomization and clipping to empirical assessment.

\label{sec:methodology}
\subsection{Questions and Evidence Units}

Product calls request rules, NER and semantics [2,3,4], recording any legitimate skips after a rule-based BLOCK. Sole-semantic calls explicitly request [4] and assert that it alone executes successfully. Detector-layer calls for the precision study request [2,3] and assert that both executed successfully. This distinction prevents product outcomes from being attributed to semantic classification alone, and detector-layer outcomes from being read as either.

The unit of analysis changes with the question. A contextual case is one authored situation with provisional targets, regardless of how many packages process it. A final annotation row is one frozen source example. A receiver probe is a specified request-handling scenario, while a browser timing observation is a repeated execution nested within a profile. A training seed identifies a fit from the common base, and a telemetry repetition samples reports for a fixed contributor histogram. We retain those units when interpreting counts and uncertainty; execution volume does not turn repeated observations into additional independently sourced cases.

\textit{Contextual targets and measures.} The authored workload contains 1,560 cases, comprising 810 sensitive and 750 clean examples, with 260 per audience class, 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups. Targets for sensitivity, audience, categories and allowed actions were prepared provisionally with assistant support, without independent human adjudication. Cases sealed for an earlier semantic comparison were subsequently opened and reused for the product comparison and ablation. These remain development results on authored material, even when a later protocol was fixed prospectively.

A positive binary prediction requires nonzero sensitivity or a nonempty category set. Label-joint agreement requires exact matches for sensitivity, audience and category set; the \emph{label-and-action joint} measure additionally requires an allowed action. We also measure allowed-action agreement separately. An action below the allowed set is under-restrictive, while one above it is over-restrictive. Receiver arrival provides a different observation: it establishes transmission rather than classification or policy agreement.

Keeping these outcomes separate prevents a single accuracy figure from concealing the mechanism of a change. Correct sensitivity with an incorrect audience fails exact label agreement; correct labels with an inadmissible action fail label-and-action agreement. Conversely, a different label tuple may still produce an allowed action. Binary recall and specificity deliberately discard these distinctions and answer only the declared presence question. We report paired gains and losses because the same net change can arise from preserving almost all earlier successes or replacing many of them with different successes. Appendix~\ref{app:measurement} defines the projections and paired measures precisely.

\textit{Final annotation target.} The once-only final endpoint comprises 498 English rows from the Personally Identifiable Information Masking Benchmark (PIIMB), revision \texttt{4a13e9f}: 236 annotation-positive and 262 annotation-negative~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026}. Source inputs, all six packages and scoring were frozen before opening, with no subsequent tuning, reselection or promotion. Sole-semantic execution evaluates the same nonzero-sensitivity-or-category projection against annotation presence. This target differs from contextual authorization and PIIMB's official character-masking/F2 task. The 464 observed source-group keys capture declared relationships, without proving template independence.

For contextual uncertainty, we use 2,000 paired whole-cluster draws with seed 42 and linear 95\% percentile ranges, separately grouping by family and audience expression. Ablation expression groups were reconciled after collection. Final ranges use 2,000 paired source-group draws with the same seed and percentiles, retaining row weighting. These unadjusted ranges show sensitivity to known dependence; they establish neither crossed independence, template separation nor population coverage. Repeated calls and seeds likewise add no independent situations.

The grouping analyses address an identified dependence problem rather than repair the provenance of the targets. Resampling whole groups keeps their member rows together, but each analysis uses one declared grouping at a time. Family and audience-expression intervals cannot be combined as if their groupings were crossed-independent replications. Likewise, holding out rows by normalized text and known scenario group does not rule out common templates or semantic paraphrases. These limits apply even where source and model hashes establish that the same bytes were used.

\subsection{Comparison and Retention Protocols}
Historical and updated efficient, balanced and quality packages processed the same contextual cases through the product handler, with rules and policy fixed. Updated packages descend from frozen-encoder head fitting, joint encoder/pooler/head continuation and audience-supervised rehearsal; checkpoints were selected on validation alone before the sealed comparison. Historical packages used 64/96/128-token windows and category threshold 0.38, compared with 512 WordPieces and threshold 0.5 for updated packages. Their respective parameter counts were 0.019/0.037/0.054 and 4.785/9.596/17.494 million. These differences prevent attribution to pretraining or architecture alone, while repeatability passes add no cases. Rules and NER in this comparison and in the ablation below were first measured in the pre-change configuration of Section~\ref{sec:detprec_protocol}. Both were later rerun unchanged except for the revised detectors (Section~\ref{sec:product_rerun}); the tables of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_comparison_results} keep the historical measurements.

The comparison consequently asks whether a complete updated package behaves differently under the same product policy, rather than estimating a controlled effect of one training stage. Its compact lineage identifies how the evaluated artifact was reached; it supplies no additional result catalogue. The updated package name must also be distinguished from the default installed selection: the earlier comparison evaluates frozen packages, whereas the later browser study verifies the standard V3 loading path. Sharing a family name does not make measurements from those environments interchangeable.

The ablation holds the updated balanced package, inputs and policy constant across full [2,3,4], no-rules [3,4] and no-NER [2,4] configurations. Execution checks distinguish requested layers from legitimate skips, allowing detector contributions on this workload to be examined without assuming that removal is safe elsewhere.

RQ2 holds the initial model and training exposure constant. Both head-only and full-model fits start from byte-identical official pretrained balanced encoder/pooler weights with zero task heads, rather than either RQ1 package. Training used Python 3.11, Torch 2.12/CUDA 13.0 and Transformers 4.57.6 on an RTX 5080. Seeds 42/43/44 received the same ordered 4,096 exposures over 64 steps, minimizing sensitivity and audience cross-entropy plus mean category binary cross-entropy with dropout disabled. AdamW used head/encoder rates $10^{-3}/2\times10^{-5}$, zero weight decay, norm-one clipping and microbatch 4/effective batch 64. Enabling encoder optimization in full-model training also changed the scope of joint-gradient clipping.

Excluding all declared training/guard groups and normalized texts removed 480 validation rows, leaving 1,080 reused provisional cases across 25 families: 570 sensitive and 510 clean. Each terminal candidate and the unchanged base underwent native in-process sole-semantic assessment; assessment scores selected no checkpoint. Sixteen separate guard cases required improved joint correctness and zero action harms against one derived target action per case, whereas the broader assessment accepted declared action sets. Guard acceptance and assessment improvement thus answer different questions. No candidate was published: effective-artifact mappings show what the rule would retain, conditional on the small guard and weak zero-head control.

The matched design reduces several sources of ambiguity: both treatments share the exact initial weights, ordered training exposures, head settings, objective and terminal assessment rule. It does not hold the effective optimization trajectory fixed, because allowing encoder gradients changes both the representation and joint clipping. The guard and broader assessment also serve different roles. A candidate's assessment gains describe behavior on the retained workload; its guard result determines eligibility under a separately declared rule. Neither result establishes what an installed client actually received. Appendix~\ref{app:retention} records the resulting artifact and decision distinctions.

\subsection{Detector Precision Protocol}
\label{sec:detprec_protocol}
The eighth evidence unit asks whether repaired rules, case-preserved input and the NER post-filter reduce false annotation-presence detections without an unacceptable loss of recall. It is a detector-layer study: each call explicitly requests the regex and NER layers, [2,3], and asserts that both executed successfully. The semantic layer is never requested, so the outcomes are neither semantic-only nor full-product evidence and are not comparable with Tables~\ref{tab:pipeline_v1_v3} or~\ref{tab:final_presence}. The pre-change configuration is a clean export of the commit preceding the revision, with the original rules, lowercased detector input and unfiltered NER output used by the earlier units. The installed configuration is the shipped default with all three controls of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision_design}, as it stood when the endpoint was scored; the later student-identifier rule is not part of it. Single-control arms (case preservation alone, the post-filter on lowercased input and variants that ignore organizations) are development-only ablations reported in Appendix~\ref{app:detprec}; they are not held out.

Splits reuse existing material. The 502-row development set is divided by the parity of a hash of its source-group identifier into dev-TUNE (267 rows, 148 annotation-positive) and dev-CHECK (235 rows, 116 positive). Two synthetic sets complete the design: a personalized set of 2,000 rows (1,000 positive) and an OpenAI-generated set of 1,000 rows (500 positive). The post-filter rules were designed using only dev-TUNE and the personalized set, and were committed before any dev-CHECK or OpenAI score was computed. A subsequent review using author-written probe sentences produced a revised filter; because dev-CHECK and OpenAI had already been scored, the revised filter's results on them are post-review and no longer strictly held out. The data consulted while designing the regex repairs are not separately recorded, so the development and synthetic splits are treated as design material for them. The held-out set is the 498-row final endpoint of Section~\ref{sec:final_annotation} (236 annotation-positive rows). It was scored once for both configurations after the installed defaults were frozen, as aggregate counts and timing only, with no row inspected and no rule or code changed in response to its rows; the student-identifier rule was diagnosed on the personalized set and added afterwards. The endpoint had already been opened for the semantic arms of unit 3 under its own protocol, so this is a one-shot detector-layer scoring rather than a new protected confirmation. The same frozen pair was later re-derived once more, storing per-row outcomes without text so that outcomes could be paired; its counts equal the first run exactly, and the endpoint is now consumed for this pair.

A prediction is positive when any finding has nonzero sensitivity or a nonempty category set, the projection of Appendix~\ref{app:measurement}. Because S1 findings are positive, downgrading an uncorroborated name from S2 to S1 changes no confusion cell; only dropping a finding does. We report true/false positives and negatives, recall, specificity and mean latency per prompt for regex, NER and their union. Labels are the source annotation-presence labels, not contextual authorization.

\textit{Paired uncertainty.} A follow-up analysis re-derived per-row outcomes for the two frozen configurations on every split and checked that the confusion counts equalled those of Table~\ref{tab:detprec} exactly. Because both configurations score the same rows, differences are tested on discordant rows only: an exact two-sided McNemar test, separately for annotation-positive and annotation-negative rows, with rows that became correct called gained and rows that became incorrect called lost. Intervals for differences come from a paired percentile bootstrap (10,000 replicates, fixed seed) that resamples source documents on the development and held-out splits. The synthetic sets carry no template identifier, so their primary analysis resamples rows, which is likely anti-conservative for template-generated text; a conservative sensitivity analysis resamples the 20 personalized and 10 OpenAI scenario labels. No multiplicity correction is applied, so isolated small $p$-values are exploratory. The same tests are applied to the runtime action of the union (WARN or BLOCK, and BLOCK only), a different construct from annotation presence, and to costs weighted by false-negative to false-positive ratios of 2, 4 and 9.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Telemetry Protocols}
Thirty-three installed Linux Chromium probes exercised the built extension, standard native installer, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector with the pre-change rules and NER. A local Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS) receiver checked original bodies and late sends during forwarding, blocking, faults, cancellation and recovery. Product and sole-semantic probes outside the resource windows asserted actual layer execution. These synthetic receiver observations do not establish authenticated-provider or cross-platform compatibility.

Historical cost measurements used three fixed synthetic cases, one per action, over fetch and XHR. Two fresh profiles per transport/action cell yielded twelve profiles, each completing a first decision, five warmups and thirty measured rules/NER requests, for 360 measured decisions. Page latency ran from analysis request to result, excluding receiver response; nearest-rank percentiles pool only two profile clusters per cell. Resource windows began at browser launch and ended after request/readiness checks, before teardown. Whole-container central processing unit (CPU) time and sampled charged memory include infrastructure, cache, driver, receiver and observer. Charged memory differs from process resident set size (RSS) and proportional set size (PSS)~\cite{LinuxCgroupV2Interfaces2026}. Sampling requested 100-ms intervals and recorded actual gaps. The historical V1 study included neither semantics, an unprotected baseline nor per-role resource attribution.

The V3 study used two fresh Linux Chromium profiles per transport and the synthetic prompt ``Please explain the steps in this process.'' Each profile retained its normal launcher, supervisor, bridge and model service across detectors-off, rules/NER, semantic and full arms, with order reversed in the second replicate. Five warmups and thirty measurements per arm produced 80 warmups and 480 timed requests. Because every timed request forwarded, request-to-receiver completion could be compared through within-profile median subtraction. The off baseline retained interception and infrastructure. Exact layers and packages were asserted on Torch 2.11 CPU with four threads, Python 3.12.3 and Chromium 153.0.8010.12; telemetry was disabled. Ten untimed post-cost probes checked the three V3 tiers, older V1 balanced and return to default over both transports.

The telemetry audit enumerated exact rational kernels on a fingerprinted Windows CPython 3.11.9 build, enclosing logarithms with outward 100-digit arithmetic under independent uniform operating-system (OS) bits. Budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 cover the declared settings. Estimator simulation crossed these budgets with $n=8,80,800,8000$, all five field domains, and balanced-integer, point-mass and minimum-one 1\%-target histograms. Each of the resulting 180 prespecified cells had 10,000 repetitions with seed 20261014. NumPy 1.26.4/PCG64 sampled binary64 approximations of finite probabilities; the collector applied nominal inversion and independent $[0,n]$ clipping. Large $n$ pools installation-days, still limited to eight default reservations each. The estimand is a fixed contributor histogram, excluding population-sampling variation, and no deployment-usefulness threshold was specified.

Ledger checks examined persistence, concurrent admission and failure accounting. A separate controlled Windows host study exercised the actual reporter, randomizer and ledger with a separate SQLite-backed collector, using generated analysis-shaped fixtures without a detector. Twenty-three ledger fixtures covered caps, reopening, ordered inputs, outage/recovery and malformed packets. Synchronous report-call timing excluded background delivery and was kept separate from Linux browser timing. Neither study involved real-user traffic.

We therefore use the operational fixtures to test report delivery and budget accounting, and the simulation to study estimator behavior conditional on known contributors. They cannot be pooled into a user study. The browser experiment likewise evaluates an installed local path to a synthetic receiver; its instrumentation and resident services are part of the observation boundary. Appendix~\ref{app:browser} distinguishes the timing endpoints, and Appendix~\ref{app:reporting} explains why reservation counts, received reports and potential users require different denominators.

\textit{Artifact availability.} Tracked recipes in \texttt{evaluation/} and dated records in \texttt{paper/research/} identify configurations through source, model and data fingerprints and environment receipts. They distinguish product/ablation dependencies, including historical uncommitted sources, from matched continuation, final semantics, historical and standard-V3 client captures, telemetry, and the detector-precision unit with its follow-up (\path{detector-precision-20261024.md} and \path{detector-precision-followup-20261024.md}). Some pinned checkpoints and raw run artifacts remain local and ignored. Exact reruns therefore require more than a Git checkout, and public distribution of those assets has not been established.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke improved agreement with provisional contextual policy and enforced its declared request contract on the tested installed paths. The results do not yet establish independent contextual accuracy, provider compatibility, prevention for every sensitive prompt or practical usability.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
Detector placement is part of the policy decision, not simply an implementation choice. An early rule-based BLOCK prevents semantic contribution, so a better downstream model cannot repair it. Historically, removing rules corrected 139 unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen previously correct actions. The appropriate response is therefore to examine both restriction and protection costs, rather than infer that higher aggregate agreement justifies removal. This implication remains conditional on the provisional policy and tested fixture, and both counts come from the pre-change rules. The precision repairs of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision_design} were designed to reduce such restrictions, and the full-product rerun of Section~\ref{sec:product_rerun} found that they did so in part: the shared unnecessary restrictions fell from 139 to 108 without new binary misses. Removing rules nevertheless still corrected more actions than it lost (net 85) at the cost of the same eighteen lost actions, so the tradeoff survived the repair. A policy-correct WARN presents a related distinction: because it releases the original content, classification correctness, appropriate intervention and prevented transmission each require their own evidence.

Comparison with local sanitization clarifies who controls continuation. Casper's acknowledged warnings leave a decision to the user, while HaS and ProSan transform content to support continued use~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}. PriVoke instead proceeds after WARN without acknowledgement and leaves task completion unresolved after BLOCK; it neither masks prompts nor restores responses. Receiver compliance verifies that contract, but not its desirability. Establishing the latter would require evidence about authorized task completion, warning comprehension and the effort needed to reformulate a prompt.

The ablation and execution traces provide complementary explanations. Traces identify where a decision arose and show when downstream layers did not execute. The matched removal changes one detector configuration and observes the resulting paired actions on the same fixture. The trace alone cannot establish what the removed layer would have changed; the removal result alone does not establish that a new policy would be acceptable to users. Together they localize a tradeoff that a future policy study would need to resolve. In particular, fewer unnecessary restrictions and more missed protections should remain visible as different consequences, rather than being collapsed into a recommendation to remove the rules.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
Contextual integrity concerns appropriate information flows~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}, for which entity recognition supplies only part of the judgment. Independent contextual ground truth remains the largest scientific gap because PriVoke's authored targets are assistant-provisional. Freezing the separate annotation-presence endpoint before opening limits post-selection, but neither its different target nor its sole-semantic execution validates contextual authorization. Improved authored-target agreement and poor final specificity consequently answer different questions, neither establishing human agreement, appropriate intervention or task utility.

A stronger contextual evaluation would specify who may disclose which information to whom, for what purpose, before annotators see model outputs. It would need to preserve legitimate disagreements rather than force every situation into a single unexamined target. The current allowed-action sets accommodate some policy latitude, but their provisional construction is not a substitute for that review. The annotation-presence endpoint is still informative: it exposes behavior on a separately frozen target. Its limitations do not justify dismissing adverse specificity, just as its frozen status does not convert it into evidence of contextual authorization.

Group and text exclusions reduce declared overlap, while paired cluster analyses show sensitivity to known dependence. Neither establishes template independence, paraphrase exclusion or absence of pretraining exposure. The final endpoint comprises 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row. These English synthetic or machine-generated cases do not represent other languages or organizational policies, and neither unadjusted intervals nor three seeds provide a population guarantee. Confirmation after opening this endpoint requires a new protected successor.

The detector-precision unit adds its own limits. It uses a single small English spaCy model, and its cue lists for honorifics, relations and roles are English-only. Annotation-presence labels are source annotations with unquantified noise, and a prediction is positive for any nonzero sensitivity, so S1 findings count as positives. The filter was designed on dev-TUNE and the personalized set; dev-CHECK and the OpenAI set were scored before the post-review revision and are no longer strictly held out, so only the one-shot run on the final endpoint is free of selection on its rows, and that endpoint was already opened for unit 3 and has since been used a second time, for paired statistics on the same frozen pair, so it cannot inform further decisions. The student-identifier rule was added after the endpoint scoring and rests on a single template, and no reported measurement includes it. The datasets are synthetic or benchmark-derived, and small splits make single rows move recall by about a percentage point. Pets, fictional or historical people and organizations that resemble given names cannot be separated from private people without world knowledge. The held-out run has 236 positive and 262 negative rows, so a single row moves recall by about 0.4 points and specificity by about 0.4 points; its regex differences are net differences of three rows and are not statistically significant. Percentile bootstrap intervals are optimistic when few rows change, so the exact tests are the primary evidence for small counts; no multiplicity correction was applied, and the synthetic sets lack template identifiers, which makes their row-level inference likely anti-conservative. Timing is one sequential pass on one host.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
Matching the base and exposure reduces RQ2 configuration confounding without retrospectively isolating the RQ1 package effect. Consistent with negative-flip research~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, all matched full-model candidates improved aggregate assessment yet failed the guard. Under the configured rule, higher scores cannot override casewise rejection, and no candidate was published. The weak zero-head base and small guard also constrain the significance of a pass, motivating independently reviewed retention cases and explicit criteria for harm.

The meaning of a harm criterion matters as much as its pass rate. A label change that preserves an allowed action differs from a change that releases previously blocked content; an unnecessary BLOCK imposes a different cost from under-restriction. The maintained guard uses a single derived action and the assessment uses allowed sets, so their disagreements need not be contradictions. A larger, independently justified guard could alter eligibility in either direction. The present result establishes the configured decision and the measured gains and losses, without establishing that the guard represents every relevant harm or that rejected weights lack all useful information.

Operational version checks address another failure mode. Binding a candidate to its exact parent can prevent an update evaluated against one model from overwriting a different revision, but cannot make its labels correct. Likewise, a verified download shows artifact integrity, not behavioral suitability. Standard V3's pinned inference path and the separately enabled legacy update service must remain distinguishable: this paper reports installed V3 inference and bounded continuation evidence, not a deployed V3 learning loop.

Blocking when analysis is unavailable can prevent disclosure while interrupting an authorized task; WARN avoids that interruption but proceeds without informed acknowledgement. Heuristic confidence cannot resolve the choice because it is not calibrated joint correctness. Installed Linux evidence now covers historical V1 enforcement and standard V3 loading and cost, yet the quality-tier benign-prompt BLOCKs demonstrate why installation alone is not accuracy validation. Authenticated providers, Windows, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified. Without evidence on authorized task completion, unnecessary intervention, warning comprehension and reformulation effort, practical utility and human benefit remain open.

\subsection{Measurement Boundaries and Next Steps}
Matched resident arms quantify semantic and full-detector activation, but retain instrumentation and omit extension-absent and cold-load comparisons needed to establish total overhead. For reporting, RAPPOR motivates repeated-report accounting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}; PriVoke's ledger depends on retained state and a trusted clock, and its finite bound assumes independent uniform OS bits. Accounting leaves estimation error and admission-order effects unresolved, so privacy, estimation and representative admission require separate justification. The current deployment has too few concurrent users for a live-usage usefulness study, and more users alone would not repair the missing fields. Lifetime categorical marginals and an operator Health probe cannot replace latency/error and usage-denominator instrumentation, nor has a deployment-usefulness threshold been established. Participation, delivery selection and metadata remain outside the bound, and aggregates supply no corrected training labels.

A reporting design must consequently specify its intended estimand before increasing collection. The distribution among admitted, successfully delivered events can differ from the distribution among all attempted decisions, installations or users. A daily cap selects early events; delivery failures can further select which reservations become visible. Even an exact estimator of the admitted histogram would not correct those differences without additional assumptions. Adding richer fields or denominators could help answer operational questions, but would change both the disclosure surface and the privacy analysis. Such a redesign and its validation are future work, rather than benefits established by the current collector.

Reproducibility has a related boundary. A readable recipe and a fingerprint allow an available artifact to be identified, while a successful fresh rerun additionally requires the referenced weights, inputs, compatible dependencies and permissions. The retained records disclose local and ignored assets so that absence from a public checkout is not mistaken for a complete reproduction package. Future release work should resolve distribution and environment requirements independently of the manuscript's empirical conclusions.

Across the lifecycle, early restrictions can survive later model improvements, improved candidates can remain ineligible, and admitted reports can remain unrepresentative. These findings motivate separate acceptance evidence rather than validate a remedy. Contextual adjudication, broader receiver and retention checks, total-cost measurements, and user/task evidence are needed to support stronger claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke integrates local prompt inspection, explicit request enforcement, version-bound guarded revision and independently randomized reporting. Its evaluation demonstrates why each mechanism needs distinct acceptance evidence. Agreement with authored contextual targets improved, while action errors and poor specificity on a separate annotation-presence task persisted. Installed probes verified supported request handling, including unchanged-content forwarding under WARN. All matched full-model candidates improved aggregate agreement but failed retention, making average gain insufficient to justify publication. Installed V3 tests measured resident activation cost while revealing an adverse quality-tier decision; controlled telemetry verified delivery and accounting without establishing live monitoring usefulness. The contribution is an inspectable lifecycle and an empirical account of its limits. A detector-layer precision revision shifted held-out regex specificity upward and recall downward by amounts too small to distinguish from chance (three rows each), with significant changes only on synthetic sets and no measured net NER benefit. A full-product rerun found fewer unnecessary restrictions (139 to 108) without new binary misses, yet the rule-removal tradeoff persisted and the NER layer began converting findings. Independent contextual accuracy, useful adaptation without lost protection and human benefit remain to be established.

\section{Policy Decision Order and Observable Outcomes}
\label{app:policy}
The contextual attributes in Section~\ref{sec:pii_categorisation} describe a finding before enforcement. For a finding with numeric confidence below 0.5, the low-confidence rule takes precedence: S3 yields WARN; S2 yields WARN only if IDENTITY or LOCATION is present; all remaining cases yield ALLOW. Missing or nonnumeric confidence does not activate that rule. This is a decision threshold, not an empirical confidence calibration.

For other findings, the baseline checks are ordered. S0 with an empty category set yields ALLOW. S3 yields BLOCK and S2 yields WARN. For the remaining S0/S1 cases, IDENTITY together with LOCATION yields WARN; either category with visibility P2, P3 or P4 also yields WARN. Otherwise the finding yields ALLOW. PU denotes an unspecified audience and is not treated as restricted/private by this comparison. A category set is multilabel, so retaining one sensitive category is not equivalent to matching the exact target set.

The cascade then constrains what reaches aggregation. An early rule BLOCK ends inspection; later requested layers have not evaluated the text. Otherwise, aggregation preserves the strongest enforcement decision rather than replacing it with the last detector's action. A failed requested layer changes an otherwise ALLOW result to BLOCK, while a valid non-ALLOW finding remains visible. These semantics differ from a configuration in which an unavailable parameter stream leaves fewer enabled layers: a successfully completed reduced cascade is not a successful semantic execution.

Finally, a policy action and a receiver observation are different records. ALLOW and WARN release the original supported request, while BLOCK cancels it. Notice rendering under WARN does not imply acknowledgement, consent or removal of information from the body. For a probe expecting a send, the relevant observation is receiver arrival with the original body; for a no-send probe, the observation is absence of arrival within the declared observation window, including checks for a late send. The installed probes establish these bounded outcomes, not an absence of leakage through every browser transport.

\section{Measurement Definitions and Reused Evidence}
\label{app:measurement}
Let case $j$ have target sensitivity $s_j$, audience $v_j$, category set $C_j$ and allowed-action set $\mathcal A_j$. Let the corresponding prediction be $\hat s_j,\hat v_j,\hat C_j,\hat a_j$. Define the three indicators
\begin{align}
L_j&=\mathbf1\{\hat s_j=s_j,\ \hat v_j=v_j,\ \hat C_j=C_j\},\\
A_j&=\mathbf1\{\hat a_j\in\mathcal A_j\},\\
J_j&=L_j A_j.
\end{align}
Label agreement, action agreement and label-and-action joint agreement average $L_j$, $A_j$ and $J_j$, respectively, over the declared cases. Exact category agreement counts both omitted and spurious categories as mismatches. For action direction, the ordering is ALLOW $<$ WARN $<$ BLOCK; the retained contextual analysis distinguishes an action below the allowed set from one above it.

The binary projection is positive when $\hat s_j\ne\mathrm{S0}$ or $\hat C_j$ is nonempty. Recall is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ and specificity is $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$, with denominators fixed by the declared positive and negative targets. On the contextual workload those targets are provisional sensitivity labels; on the final endpoint they are source annotation presence. The same prediction projection does not make those reference tasks equivalent.

For any binary correctness indicator $Z_j$, the paired gain count is the number of cases with $Z_j^{\rm old}=0$ and $Z_j^{\rm new}=1$; the loss count reverses those values. The net rate difference is $(G-L)/N$. Reporting only that difference would hide the number of previous successes lost. The case identity must remain paired when a cluster is resampled, and repeated appearances of a sampled group carry its rows with their multiplicities. Row weighting is retained, so a larger group contributes more cases; the method does not give each group equal influence.

The 1,560 contextual cases are shared by the package comparison and targeted ablation. The 1,080-case continuation assessment is a reused provisional subset after the declared exclusions. These are related development observations. The 498-row final endpoint supplies a different frozen target and source composition, with all six arms retained, but its 464 observed group keys do not certify independent templates. A later analysis of already opened material cannot retroactively make it protected confirmation. The full confusion counts and adverse outcomes remain in the main results, rather than being displaced by this methodological detail.

\section{Candidate Identity, Guarding and Reproduction}
\label{app:retention}
The matched continuation comparison starts both HEADS and FULL from the same intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads. That ORIGINAL control is distinct from both the historical V1 classifier and the selected updated package used in RQ1. The shared 4,096 exposures describe repeated training occurrences, not 4,096 independently sampled situations. Each seed produces a terminal candidate; the assessment selects no intermediate checkpoint.

\section{Installed-Browser Observation Boundaries}
\label{app:browser}
The historical rules/NER cost capture and standard-V3 activation study use different timing endpoints. Historical page-observed decision latency ends when the analysis result returns and excludes the receiver response. V3 request-to-receiver completion includes a successful send to the synthetic receiver. Their distributions should therefore not be subtracted to estimate a generation speedup. In the V3 study, subtraction occurs only within a profile between its resident detector arm and its detectors-off baseline, using the same endpoint and fixed prompt.

The off baseline retains the extension hooks, native launcher, supervisor, bridge and model service. It estimates the increment from activating a detector in that arrangement, not the total cost of installing the extension. Reversing the arm order in the second profile replicate reduces reliance on one fixed ordering without creating an extension-absent control or a cold-load experiment. The warmup requests are excluded from the timed distributions, and the untimed tier-selection probes remain separate from those distributions.

Historical resource windows include browser startup and request/readiness activity before teardown, with resident services and instrumentation already present. Cgroup CPU counters aggregate descendants, while charged memory includes cache and kernel charges~\cite{LinuxCgroupV2Interfaces2026}. They are not per-detector CPU or process RSS/PSS measurements. Positive sampling gaps remain part of the record; a sampled maximum does not establish the continuous peak. Observer cost is retained rather than subtracted through an unverified attribution model.

Each historical transport/action cell pools thirty measurements from each of two profiles. Its nearest-rank p99 is the observed maximum of sixty measurements, rather than evidence of a well-estimated population tail. The V3 distributions likewise contain repeated executions within only two profiles per transport. These restrictions matter when interpreting apparent latency differences and when extrapolating beyond the fixed synthetic prompts. The installed evidence records are \path{installed-client-evidence-20261014.md} and \path{v3-standard-installed-evidence-20261017.md} under \texttt{paper/research/}.

\section{Telemetry Estimation and Admission}
\label{app:reporting}
The ideal mechanism and audited finite implementation in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism} are analyzed for the content of a fixed reporting opportunity under public configuration. The finite bound includes the reported excess over nominal privacy loss. For a category whose true contributor count is $h$ among $n$ reports, and observed randomized count $Y$, the nominal inverse-response estimate before clipping is
\begin{equation}
\widehat h=\frac{Y-nq}{p-q}.
\end{equation}
The collector clips each estimated marginal separately to $[0,n]$. Inverting the nominal probabilities is not the same as inverting the exact finite kernel, and clipping is nonlinear: the expected clipped value need not equal $h$. Independently clipped categories also need not sum to $n$. These properties explain why privacy loss, bias, RMSE and total coherence are separate checks.

The synthetic trials fix the contributor histogram before sampling the randomized reports. Their error therefore excludes uncertainty about which people or events enter that histogram. Increasing $n$ means more admitted reports, pooled across installation-days where necessary, rather than relaxing the per-installation daily cap. A precision requirement for an operational decision was not prespecified; the two illustrative category cells cannot establish one after the fact.

The operational accounting also has distinct denominators. An opportunity may be suppressed before queuing, a reservation may be consumed without a delivered packet, and an accepted report represents a received randomized event. The observed totals reconcile as $308=184+124$ opportunities and $184=168+16$ reserved events, where the latter partition is accepted reports plus submission failures in the controlled fixtures. These are not counts of users, unique prompts or detector decisions in a deployment.

The ledger reserves before construction and does not refund failed construction or delivery. That ordering protects accounting against retries within retained state, but cannot hide whether a report was sent. A cap can also change event composition before randomization, as the retained order-reversal example demonstrates. Correcting the randomized counts would still leave that admission mechanism to justify. The finite audit and controlled operation are recorded separately in \path{telemetry-evidence-20261014.md} and \path{rq3-telemetry-evidence-20261017.md}; neither supplies training examples or establishes useful live monitoring.

\section{Detector Precision Rules and Reproduction}
\label{app:detprec}
This appendix defines the controls evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision}. The record \path{detector-precision-20261024.md} under \texttt{paper/research/} holds source revisions, split definitions and results.

\textit{Rule gates.} Kinship terms and the word ``family'' require a possessive determiner (my, our, his, her, their), optionally followed by up to two hyphen- or space-joined modifier words, so ``my ex-wife'' matches while a non-possessive self-statement does not. Child cues require a possessive and a child noun, an age expression, or an age followed by a person noun; object ages such as a five-year-old laptop do not match. Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns (for example salary, loan, mortgage, savings, credit score) after a possessive with up to two modifiers; a bare reference to an account does not match. Location keywords require a connector such as ``live in'' followed by a capture that is not a closed-class word. Structured identity values stay on one line and capture at most five tokens, and a phone label needs a value that begins like a number. Checksum validators reject shape-only hits for card numbers (Luhn), IBANs (mod-97), NHS numbers (mod-11) and US social security numbers (issuable area, group and serial ranges), and context matching is word-bounded rather than substring-based. Password placeholders, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. A bare reference to a student is not a child cue; a separate student-identifier rule (S3 identity) needs a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student, so generic student and identifier mentions stay negative. It postdates every reported measurement.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 1.} Five span rules apply in turn. The placeholder rule removes masks (repeated letters, spans without letters, a pluralized capital letter, and spans inside template delimiters). The identifier rule removes e-mail, URL, underscore and code-like tokens, which belong to the regex layer. The structural rule removes spans inside headings, except multi-token or cue-introduced person names. The short-acronym rule requires a locating or affiliation preposition before a place of at most three letters or an organization of at most four. The common-phrase rule removes a single-token person candidate when the model's installed word list contains it and the tagger, reading the lowercased token in isolation, finds no proper-noun reading and no context cue corroborates it.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 2.} The first matching rule decides: (1) a person linked with a place or organization, when either side is independently corroborated, yields S2; (2) the corresponding place or organization yields S2; (3) a corroborated person yields S2; (4) a place or organization tied to the speaker by a relation cue keeps its default sensitivity; (5) one in the same prompt as such a tied entity keeps its default; (6) a facility or location directly beside a number yields S2; (7) a single uncorroborated person yields S1; and (8) a lone place or organization is dropped. Independent corroboration means evidence about the entity itself: a multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring person, or a place or organization with a personal-relation cue. A bare locating preposition does not corroborate.

\textit{Development-only ablations.} Table~\ref{tab:detprec_ablation} holds the repaired rules fixed and varies only the NER input and filter, using union recall/specificity from the source repository's experiment records. These arms were run on design and development splits; dev-CHECK and OpenAI are post-review, and none is held out. Case preservation alone raises or preserves union recall but lowers specificity on every split with a margin to lose (to 0.824, 0.832 and 0.814 on dev-TUNE, dev-CHECK and OpenAI, and to 0.919 on the personalized set), because capitalized non-names enter the NER output. The post-filter recovers most of that specificity on case-preserved input, and on lowercased input it lowers recall slightly while raising specificity. Counting only non-organization entities as NER positives moved development NER-only recall from 0.170 to 0.333 and specificity from 0.996 to 0.937 between lowercased and case-preserved input.
