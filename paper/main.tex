\begin{abstract}

Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) can receive sensitive information through outgoing prompts. We present \privoke, a client-side privacy firewall combining rules, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and semantic classification. Its guarded cloud-training path computes bounded updates from supplied labeled examples and redistributes accepted parameters for local inference without collecting users' raw prompts for training. We distinguish update execution, annotation presence, contextual classification and request enforcement. Early contextual grids completed 126 attempts without retaining a candidate; later pretrained-BERT supervision improved agreement on provisional synthetic labels. The latest native product comparison evaluated original V1 and audience-trained V3 packages on the same 1,560 previously opened cases. Joint sensitivity, audience, category and allowed-action correctness increased from 266 to 693, 160 to 717 and 226 to 879 cases for efficient, balanced and quality. All V3 packages nevertheless over-enforced the same 139 cases; efficient/balanced introduced three/six newly incorrect actions. A configured Presidio reference uses shared binary contextual targets with its entity-task mismatch explicit. ALLOW or WARN forwards original requests; BLOCK cancels supported requests. These results demonstrate auditable adaptation and package gains on the tested workload, while leaving independent generalization, contextual safety and complete installed-client validation unresolved.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly shaping modern AI-assisted workflows, particularly in writing, coding, analysis, and document-centric professional tasks~\cite{Gupta2024, LIANG2025101366, koraag2025comparing}. Their widespread adoption is largely driven by the capabilities of large-scale hosted models, which often provide substantially greater model capacity than locally deployable alternatives such as LLaMA-style models~\cite{Florian2025, MEDEC2024}. This increased capacity enables higher-quality responses, broader task handling, and greater productivity benefits, encouraging users to rely on web-hosted LLMs for everyday work activities~\cite{Crane2025, Zahra2025}. However, this convenience also introduces a critical privacy risk: users may include personal, organizational, or contextually sensitive information in prompts without fully recognizing the implications of sending that data to an external service~\cite{Athey2017}. While major LLM providers such as OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft offer privacy policies, retention controls, and enterprise settings, prompt processing remains largely provider-dependent, and user-submitted data may still be processed, logged, or reused within service improvement pipelines depending on the provider's policy and configuration~\cite{barbera2025ai,King2025}. As a result, prompt-based interaction with web-hosted LLMs poses a practical security challenge: sensitive information can leave the client before effective privacy enforcement can occur.

Recent incidents show that prompt privacy is no longer a hypothetical concern. In 2023, Samsung employees reportedly exposed confidential source code, internal meeting notes, and engineering information by submitting them to Chat-GPT for work-related assistance, prompting the company to restrict the use of generative AI tools within its workplace~\cite{SamsungBans2023}. In the same year, a Chat-GPT service bug exposed parts of users' conversation-history metadata and limited billing-related information for a subset of subscribers, demonstrating that prompt ecosystems can fail not only through user mistakes but also through platform-side vulnerabilities~\cite{BBCChatGPTHistoryBug2023}. Google reportedly warned employees not to enter confidential material into AI chatbots, including its own Bard/Gemini system, and its privacy notice cautioned users against including confidential or sensitive information in Bard conversations~\cite{GoogleWarns2023}. Similar risks have appeared in high-stakes public-sector settings, where sensitive child-protection information was entered into Chat-GPT during the preparation of a court-related report, leading regulators to impose restrictions on generative AI use within the department~\cite{ChatGPTChildProtection}. Claude.ai has also been shown to face prompt-injection and data-exfiltration risks, where chained vulnerabilities could enable sensitive information from conversation history to be extracted through provider-side workflows~\cite{ClaudeDay2026}. Beyond individual incidents, prior studies and provider-side analyses show that sensitive information supplied through prompts may be retained, reproduced, analyzed, or insufficiently sanitized by LLM-based systems~\cite{tamkin2024clio,priyanshu2023chatbots}. These cases highlight a critical gap in current LLM usage: privacy protection still depends on user caution, provider policy, or post-submission controls, while the most effective point of intervention is before sensitive content leaves the client.

Motivated by this gap, we propose \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that shifts prompt protection to the moment sensitive information is about to leave the user's device. In addition to user caution, organizational policy, and remote service controls, \privoke inspects outgoing prompts locally before they are transmitted to supported web-hosted LLM endpoints and applies warning or blocking decisions. Its design combines real-time prompt analysis on the client with a supporting microservices architecture for model management, privacy-oriented prompt fuzzing, controlled parameter updates, and telemetry protected by Local Differential Privacy (LDP). The current implementation forwards the original request on ALLOW or WARN and cancels a supported request on BLOCK; span masking returned by the runtime is not automatically substituted into browser traffic. This separation allows cloud-side training and robustness experiments to proceed independently of interactive client inference, while deployment cost and usability remain empirical questions. In this way, \privoke provides an endpoint-level intervention within existing web-hosted LLM workflows, with protection bounded by the supported request paths and enabled detector layers.

The principal contribution of \privoke is the design and evaluation of a guarded update path that retrains the privacy classifier in the cloud and distributes revised parameters to deployed clients while continuing to inspect user prompts locally. Labeled generated or prepared examples are processed by a server-side instance of the runtime, and accepted updates are published as versioned artifacts. A client retrieves the selected model and refreshes its cached parameters, so further training does not require an application release or client-side collection of training prompts. Successive updates can therefore support adaptation to newly supplied examples, but improvement is a hypothesis to evaluate: an updated classifier may improve one outcome while regressing another.

Client-side layered inspection has precedent in Casper~\cite{Casper2024}. We build on that established design and examine the additional training, publication, and redistribution path. The resulting research questions concern the contribution of each layer, the effect of controlled cloud-side updates, and whether the extension enforces the intended request decisions. The incident examples above motivate minimizing avoidable outgoing disclosure; billing exposure and exfiltration of information already held by a provider also illustrate downstream risks that a prompt interceptor cannot generally prevent.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We frame prompt-level privacy leakage as an endpoint security problem in web-hosted LLM workflows, where sensitive information may leave the client before effective safeguards are applied.

    \item We present \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that inspects supported outgoing prompts and applies ALLOW, WARN, or BLOCK decisions before transmission, with explicit forwarding and failure behavior.

    \item We combine rule-based matching, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and semantic classification with a sensitivity, visibility, and category policy, and distinguish entity-presence detection from contextual disclosure and action correctness.

    \item We introduce a cloud-training and local-inference architecture in which a privacy-focused fuzzer supplies labeled batches, the server runtime computes bounded updates, and validated revisions are streamed to deployed clients. This enables controlled evaluation of adaptation as new data become available, including detection gains and regressions.

    \item We integrate locally randomized categorical telemetry for aggregate deployment monitoring, with an explicit event-tuple privacy scope, composition budget, and exclusions; telemetry is independent of the model-training data path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Privacy Representation}
\label{sec:threat_model}
We protect supported outgoing prompt requests from inadvertent personal, third-party or organizational disclosure. The workstation, enabled extension, local runtime/supervisor and authenticated model publisher are trusted. Compromised clients, malicious publishers, deliberately replaced hooks and provider-held historical information are outside this boundary. Coverage is limited to recognized prompt POST formats through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest}; worker traffic, unsupported transports, attachments and endpoint changes require separate validation. Disabling protection bypasses inspection. Optional hosted inference changes the disclosure boundary and is excluded from local-inference measurements.

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
Classifications retain three dimensions: sensitivity S0--S3, visibility P0--P4/PU, and a multilabel category set. S0 means no identified risk, S1 low-risk personal context, S2 potentially harmful personal information and S3 highly sensitive/identifying details or secrets. P0--P4 denote public, semi-public, authentication-restricted, group-private and personal-private context; PU denotes unspecified visibility. The ten categories are HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY and THIRD\_PARTY. These policy labels do not independently establish disclosure authorization.

The deterministic policy maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless IDENTITY and LOCATION coexist, or either occurs with P2--P4 visibility. Confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY/LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications map to ALLOW. Confidence is not established as calibrated probability. Aggregation preserves individual enforcement decisions, unions categories and takes the strongest sensitivity/known visibility. Entity presence, contextual labels and appropriate action are therefore distinct targets.

\section{Architecture and Update Path}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Local Inspection and Enforcement}
A browser page hook extracts supported prompt text and sends it through the WebExtension background process and loopback gRPC-Web bridge to a workstation-local Python runtime. A native-messaging supervisor manages bridge/runtime lifecycle. User inference does not fall back to the server-side training runtime. ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels it. A returned masked span is evidence rather than automatically substituted browser content. Unavailable analysis blocks supported requests; a streaming outage may retain other enabled layers, reducing semantic coverage rather than guaranteeing it.

The runtime normalizes Unicode, case, separators, digit spacing and whitespace while retaining offsets for original-text evidence. Regex runs first by default; regex BLOCK skips later requested detectors. Otherwise enabled regex, spaCy NER and semantic findings are aggregated. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW response; valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. Semantic-only experiments explicitly select layer 4 and verify actual sole-layer execution. Product experiments explicitly select [2,3,4] and retain legitimate skip/error records. An empty list implicitly selects the complete pipeline and cannot establish semantic-only evidence.

Historical V1 profiles are repository-owned transformers with seeded-bootstrap encoders. Balanced has 36,756 parameters, two blocks, hidden size 32, vocabulary 512 and a 96-token window; head-only training changes 660 parameters. Later V3 profiles retain complete pretrained Google BERT encoders/poolers~\cite{googleBertL4H128A2,googleBertL2H256A4,googleBertL12H256A4,turc2019WellReadStudents}. They remain classifiers rather than conversational generators. Native tokenizer, capacity, threshold and training-history differences are preserved in package comparisons.

\subsection{Guarded Cloud Training and Redistribution}
The fuzzer constructs bounded labeled TRAIN and held-out batches, excludes protected rows/text/groups, and requests head or admitted full-model gradients from a separate server runtime. That runtime performs model fetching, execution and differentiation, returning model-bound deltas and candidate metrics. The fuzzer submits eligible updates to an updater that validates exact base identity, trainable tensor inventory/shapes, finite values, magnitude limits and publication guards. Checksums or accepted receipts alone are not efficacy evidence.

The updater atomically publishes each validated artifact revision using compare-and-swap base checks and durable replay receipts. Conflicting/stale bases fail; identical retries do not apply twice. Head and underlying stages are separate publications, so an accepted head revision may remain if the later full stage rejects. Streaming serves versioned parameters and clients refresh cached weights without an application release. The path is labeled examples $\rightarrow$ server computation $\rightarrow$ guarded publication $\rightarrow$ streamed parameters, separate from local user-text inspection. This is centralized training, not federated learning, user-gradient collection or a demonstrated cloud deployment. Experimental retention objectives, samplers and optimizer variants remain documented engineering treatments rather than assumed sources of quality gain.

\subsection{Telemetry Boundary}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
Telemetry is independent of training. Randomized packets contain action, a coarse action/sensitivity risk bucket, one precedence-selected category, bounded release label and four-hour UTC time-of-day bin without date; they exclude raw prompts, identifiers, exact scores and per-layer findings. For public event budget $\varepsilon$, five independently randomized fields receive $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. A field with domain size $K_i$ returns its true value with probability
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\qquad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
for each alternative. Ideal product sampling bounds the event-tuple likelihood ratio by $e^{\sum_i\varepsilon_i}=e^\varepsilon$. This nominal pure-LDP statement assumes public input-independent configuration and compliant sampling; exact finite-randomness privacy loss is uncertified. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves budget before construction, defaults to event epsilon 1 and total epsilon 8 per UTC day, and does not refund failures. Report presence, accepted counts, delivery selection and network metadata are excluded; retained state and a trusted clock are required. Clipped inverse-response estimates can be biased, and monitoring utility is unmeasured.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
\subsection{Questions, Data and Measurement Units}
We ask whether controlled updates improve classifier behavior while preserving coverage, how semantic and product decisions differ, and whether supported enforcement matches policy. Per-prompt annotation presence, contextual label exactness, allowed-action correctness and receiver-observed prevention remain separate. A contextual binary positive means non-S0 sensitivity or any category; joint semantic classification requires exact sensitivity, audience and category set, while product joint additionally requires an allowed action. Errors are incorrect in full accuracy/class denominators, with coverage and successful confusion reported separately.

Historical English PIIMB experiments use revision \texttt{4a13e9f}, exact-text/conflict exclusion and source-document grouping. The selected 1,000 rows comprise development 502 (264 positive/238 negative) and protected final 498 (236/262); additional reused validation has 968 rows (475/493). Source annotations define binary presence, not contextual privacy or action truth. Custom within-corpus fitting and threshold selection do not establish untouched official benchmark results. Positive-only source-heldout studies cannot estimate specificity. Final 498 remains unscored.

Authored contextual targets are assistant-provisional rather than human-adjudicated. PH3 initially sealed 1,560 comparison rows after fitting; EN3 reused them as development. AU3 separately sealed a new 1,560-row FRESH workload after all fit/export/native checks. Its 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups introduce dependence. PIPE3 and Presidio reuse that already opened AU3 workload: 810 sensitive/750 clean cases and 260 per audience P0--P4/PU. Repetition or a later prospective protocol does not make reused cases independent confirmation.

\subsection{Training Lineage and Selection}
Historical online studies bind artifacts, curricula, trainable tensors, seeds, budgets and safety/retention predicates before assessment, retaining accepted and rejected attempts separately. Later paired semantic-only HEADS/FULL and representation studies use explicit execution scopes; publication does not imply qualification. Full specifications and unsuccessful attempts remain in repository research records.

PH3 fits affine privacy heads over frozen pretrained encoders/poolers using 6,240 TRAIN and 1,560 validation rows, AdamW rate 0.001 and validation-selected checkpoints. EN3 continues each selected parent by jointly fitting encoder/pooler/heads on the same data. AU3~\cite{privokeAU3Audience20261013} adds 3,120 audience rows to unchanged 6,240-row PH3 rehearsal and uses new 1,560-row validation. EN3/AU3 CPU-four-thread float32 recipes use encoder/pooler rate $2\times10^{-5}$, head rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01, dropout 0.1 and microbatch 16/effective batch 64. The final incomplete batch uses its actual loss denominator. AU3 warms up 147 updates, allows 5--30 epochs and stops after five validation epochs without strict $10^{-4}$ loss improvement. Only validation selects trained checkpoints; sealed comparisons select no model, threshold or recipe.

\subsection{Product and External-Reference Protocol}
\label{sec:pipeline_comparison_methods}
PIPE3 compares original V1 and AU3-selected V3 efficient/balanced/quality packages through the native regex-first handler and a narrow inference-only V3 adapter. Requests explicitly select [2,3,4], no audience hints and no presence gate; identities, actual layer status and errors are checked. Real installed regex/spaCy detectors are used. First-pass same-case accuracy is primary. Three passes/package yield 28,080 accuracy RPCs plus 24 separate warmups; later identical passes measure repeatability and do not increase independent sample size.

Native V1 windows 64/96/128 and category threshold 0.38 are retained; V3 uses 512 WordPieces and threshold 0.5. Three rows exceed V1 efficient's cap: one regex-skips semantic and two use native truncation; none is removed. Windows, tokenizers, capacities, thresholds and histories prevent causal architecture/pretraining attribution. The CPU host uses Python 3.11.9, Torch 2.11.0, NumPy 1.26.4, spaCy 3.7.5 and \texttt{en\_core\_web\_sm} 3.7.1 with four model threads. V3 runs before V1 in fixed tier order, one loaded process at a time. Host RPC includes product/transport overhead; peak RSS covers the server process. Fixed order, backends, warming and regex shortcuts make cost descriptive.

Independent Presidio 2.2.363 uses the same ordered texts, spaCy 3.7.5, \texttt{en\_core\_web\_sm} 3.7.1, native recognizers and threshold 0.5~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026}. Any finding predicts positive. Only text/language enter inference: no target hints, PriVoke rules, mapping, tuning or exclusions. Its entity task deliberately differs from contextual binary truth. Severity, audience, category exactness, joint and actions remain unmeasured. Raw same-ID pairing uses existing PIPE3 first passes without rerunning them. Direct analyzer timing is not compared with product RPC latency.

Source closures, package/data/recognizer hashes, raw journals and independent audits bind the studies. The tracked compact aggregate/generator reproduce figures without prompts or weights; inference reproduction additionally requires ignored pinned assets, including historical dirty source bytes. Where reported, separate family/expression bootstrap sensitivities use 2,000 paired draws and describe dependence rather than crossed-cluster independence. New recognizer comparisons use checked point estimates/paired counts without a new interval claim.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Update Mechanics and Adaptation Limits}
Audited runtime computation, bounded publication and client refresh demonstrate that the path can change served parameters, not sustained improvement. Six early contextual grids completed 126 attempts with no retained qualifier. A later semantic-only curriculum/representation comparison completed 900 live attempts and 360 offline steps without qualification. An accelerated normal-batch study completed 2,016 attempts; none of nine revised realizations met its promising criterion. Recall/specificity tradeoffs and casewise harms remained, including zero disclosure joint correctness throughout that accelerated experiment. A frozen pretrained MiniLM package improved provisional disclosure agreement but failed its serious-action veto. These bounded negative results are preserved rather than replaced by later gains.

Supervised PH3/EN3 packages subsequently improved task coverage with component regressions retained. On sealed AU3 semantic-only FRESH, EN3-to-AU3 joint classification rose 262/267/429 to 922/972/1209 of 1,560. Joint improvements/regressions were 680/20, 732/27 and 792/12. Quality had no observed action distance but retained classification regressions; efficient/balanced had new under-restriction harms. These provisional synthetic package outcomes do not establish automatic online-training benefit. PIPE3 below reuses AU3 cases and adds cascade and allowed action to its joint measure.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The evidence distinguishes enabling adaptation from demonstrating a useful revision. Early guarded updates changed parameters without satisfying retention criteria; later supervised pretrained packages improved contextual agreement while retaining errors and action harms. Neither publication nor a better average guarantees protection for a previously covered case. Fixed-base component metrics and paired regressions are necessary acceptance evidence.

The shared 139-case burden locates restrictions that semantic improvement cannot remove on regex-skipped requests. It motivates separately authorized rule review and matched detector analysis without turning trace provenance into causal ablation. AU3 semantic-only and PIPE3 product results answer different questions despite shared text. Actual execution must be labeled where other layers can recover or prevent semantic decisions.

The same-case Presidio reference shows larger V3 binary agreement here, not universal recognizer superiority. Entity detection, contextual disclosure, sanitization and downstream utility require different truth and measurements. PriVoke-authored synthetic cases and native-package differences matter even when IDs match. WARN's original-content forwarding also separates risk communication from prevention; sanitization needs distinct implementation and utility evidence.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\textit{Label validity and task alignment.} Contextual targets remain assistant-provisional rather than independently adjudicated privacy truth. Sensitivity, intended audience, category membership and appropriate action can disagree for different reasons; their joint score does not identify which judgment is valid. Unknown audience is particularly important when text supplies too little context. PIIMB annotations instead establish entity presence: an annotated name can occur in an acceptable disclosure, while an unannotated statement can still reveal sensitive circumstances. The same-case Presidio reference removes case-mix differences but does not resolve this ontology mismatch. Its disclosed small-English setting is one configuration, not an optimized or strongest recognizer baseline. Published systems add different sanitization and utility targets, sometimes with unavailable per-metric supports. None of these comparisons establishes a common privacy-accuracy ranking.

\textit{Dependence and selection.} The latest workload was developed for PriVoke and was already opened before PIPE3 and the recognizer reference. Its 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups generate related observations; three repeated passes provide repeatability rather than independent test examples. Separate cluster bootstrap sensitivities do not establish crossed independence, human-label validity or variation across independently collected populations. Validation/development have also informed repeated exploratory studies. A later frozen protocol cannot retrospectively make those data untouched confirmation. Custom within-corpus PIIMB fitting, uneven source composition and positive-only external diagnostics further limit generalization and specificity claims. English synthetic wording does not represent organizational policies, language variation or deployment prevalence. The protected final 498 rows remain unscored.

\textit{Attribution and retention.} Native packages differ in capacity, tokenizer, threshold, window, initialization, runtime and training history. Selected task epochs quantify retained fine-tuning ancestry, not unknown upstream pretraining or a common training dose. PH3, EN3 and AU3 change supervision and optimization exposure across stages; offline continuation does not isolate the benefit of automatic fuzzer updates. Fixed budgets and publication guards constrain an experiment but do not guarantee contextual safety. Aggregate gains coexist with PU and multilabel errors, newly incorrect actions and cases where an external reference was correct. A no-new-failure condition would still leave pre-existing failures unresolved. These results do not justify promotion or a universal tier ordering.

\textit{Policy and enforcement coverage.} Allowed-action agreement evaluates the configured policy, not whether an affected person authorized disclosure or whether the policy's costs are appropriate. Returned confidence has not been established as calibrated, and the consequences of missed credentials and unnecessary warnings are not interchangeable. The shared regex-associated restrictions locate observed behavior without establishing a detector-removal counterfactual. Supported-hook trust, enabled settings and recognized endpoint/body formats constrain actual prevention. WARN forwards original content; unsupported transports and deliberate hook replacement remain outside coverage. The controlled receiver fixture used a decision broker, while the incomplete installed-client attempt does not establish the full native-messaging path, provider compatibility, outage recovery or representative cold/warm latency. Fail-closed behavior also has an availability cost that detector accuracy does not measure.

\textit{Telemetry and reproducibility.} Nominal event-tuple LDP excludes participation, delivery selection and transport metadata; it does not establish anonymous communication or protection of training data. It assumes public configuration, compliant sampling, a trusted clock and retained installation-local budget state. Exact finite-sampler privacy loss, estimator bias under clipping and reporting-selection effects remain unmeasured. Model adaptation consumes supplied labels, not corrected labels inferred from aggregate telemetry. Separately, source/artifact hashes and raw audits support computational integrity but not clean-room reproduction or deployed performance. Ignored assets and historical dirty source bytes are necessary to reproduce inference; tracked aggregates reproduce figures only. Source licenses and attribution constrain redistribution of text-bearing corpora and fitted artifacts. Controlled probes use invented identifiers and supply no evidence about real-user usability or acceptable warning frequency.

\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}
\textit{Reviewed supervision and independent evaluation.} The first priority is an independently reviewed contextual corpus with explicit sensitivity, audience, full category sets and allowed actions. Reviewers should distinguish actual disclosures from benign discussion and fiction, retain unspecified audience where evidence is absent, and adjudicate disagreements rather than silently convert missing source annotations into clean labels. A prospective protocol should freeze the rubric, inclusion rules, protected exclusions, source/template groups and analysis before fitting. Mixed-class source-heldout evaluation should include difficult clean controls as well as sensitive disclosures, with broader language and organizational-policy coverage. Report class/slice denominators, errors and casewise required-BLOCK failures alongside aggregate scores. Model and threshold selection must remain on training/validation; the protected final partition should open only after a configuration and analysis are frozen. This would test transfer under clearer supervision rather than extend tuning on observed assessment failures.

\textit{Controlled adaptation and detector studies.} Follow-up training should start from independently pinned common bases and separate supervision, sampler, objective and optimizer changes. Matched data exposure, attempted budgets and multiple realizations are needed to interpret differences between head-only, full-model and unchanged controls. Raw rejected candidates, accepted publications and selected checkpoints should remain distinguishable, with component and action regressions checked against both the fixed original and immediate parent. Semantic studies must request and verify sole-layer execution. A separate product experiment could examine the shared rule-associated restrictions using independently reviewed cases and explicit detector configurations, while measuring lost protection as well as reduced false alarms. Such an ablation should not be inferred from normal-cascade traces. Stronger recognizer configurations could also be evaluated under a predeclared common task, keeping entity detection, contextual decisions and any task-specific adaptation transparent.

\textit{Installed-path protection and sanitization.} The complete browser, native-messaging supervisor, bridge and runtime should be tested together against an instrumented receiver on declared supported request formats. The matrix should include ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK, missing analysis, streaming interruptions, restart/recovery and cancellation, retaining both receiver-observed original-body transmission and client-visible outcomes. Cold/warm latency, resource use and timeout behavior need declared hardware and repeated sessions; partial capture should remain diagnostic. Warning comprehension and unnecessary restriction require separate usability evidence. Request-body masking is a distinct alternative to current original-content forwarding: it would need reliable span/body reconstruction across supported formats, verification that original secrets do not reach the receiver, and a downstream utility comparison. A returned masked span alone cannot establish this feature.

\textit{Monitoring and efficient redistribution.} Telemetry work should first audit finite-randomness privacy loss, ledger persistence and reporting-selection effects under the stated release mechanism, then measure marginal estimator error and clipping bias on declared workloads. Any future telemetry-guided training needs a separate justified label signal, consent/data-flow design and privacy analysis; categorical counts are not corrected examples. Parameter compression or pruning could be explored only with bounded export checks, native prediction parity and measured download/memory effects, retaining regression guards through publication and client refresh. These are prospective studies, not implemented remedies or demonstrated improvements. Independently reviewed labels, generalization and installed-path prevention remain prerequisites for stronger deployment claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke implements bounded cloud-side training and guarded redistribution with local prompt inspection. Latest V1/V3 product measurements show joint gains of 427/557/653 cases on a reused 1,560-case synthetic workload, together with 139 shared over-enforced cases and three/six new efficient/balanced action failures. Configured Presidio comparisons preserve the entity-task boundary and paired losses; published outcomes supply context rather than a ranking. The result is auditable adaptation with measured package gains and remaining failures, not established contextual safety or deployment readiness. Independent labels, external generalization and installed-client prevention evidence remain necessary.
