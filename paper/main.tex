\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to web-hosted language models can disclose personal and organizational information before a provider's controls apply. \privoke inspects supported prompts on the user's workstation, decides whether to allow, warn or block, and enforces that decision before transmission. A separate service trains detector candidates from supplied labelled examples, checks version and retention constraints, and streams accepted parameters to clients. Independently randomized telemetry does not feed training. We evaluate detection and enforcement, retention during updates, and systems and privacy costs. Updated packages improve agreement on an authored contextual workload, but retain action failures and perform poorly on a separate annotation-presence task, where specificity is only 13.36--21.76\%. All full-model candidates in a matched continuation study fail the retention guard despite aggregate gains. Installed-browser probes confirm bounded request handling; cost and telemetry audits expose measurement and estimator limits. The contribution is an implemented, auditable protection and update lifecycle. Independent contextual ground truth, practical utility and reliable generalization remain open.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Prompt privacy, local inference, request enforcement, guarded model updates, local differential privacy.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Web-hosted large language models support writing, coding and analysis~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing}. The context that makes a prompt useful can also disclose a person's health, a colleague's details or an organization's secrets. Reported workplace disclosures illustrate this risk~\cite{SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Once transmitted, sensitive content is already available to the provider; provider-side safeguards address a different point in the disclosure path~\cite{Athey2017,barbera2025ai,King2025}. A local intervention can inspect a supported request before it leaves the browser.

Local inspection alone does not resolve the problem. A detector can recognize an entity without knowing whether its disclosure is authorized. A correct warning may still transmit the original content. A detector update can improve average accuracy while losing a previously protected case. Even monitoring the system can disclose information unless its reporting path has a separate privacy boundary. These distinctions motivate \privoke, a client-side prompt firewall that joins local inspection, explicit request enforcement, guarded detector updates and separately randomized telemetry.

\privoke assigns contextual sensitivity, intended audience and information categories, then maps these findings to ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. On supported requests, ALLOW and WARN forward the original body, while BLOCK cancels transmission. WARN attempts to display a notice without waiting for acknowledgement; it is not evidence of consent or prevented disclosure. The detector runs locally in the configuration studied here. A distinct server-side training path uses supplied labelled examples, validates candidate updates against their exact base, and distributes accepted parameters without rebuilding the extension. User prompts and telemetry do not supply training labels.

The closest work already establishes local prompt protection. Casper combines rules, entity recognition and topic detection in a browser extension~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}; Hide and Seek trains local hiding and recovery models~\cite{HaS2024}; ProSan trains a local sanitizer that trades privacy hiding against task utility~\cite{ProSan2024}. Our contribution is narrower than inventing local inspection or trainable protection: an implemented, version-bound detector lifecycle coupled to observable request actions, and evidence that distinguishes useful gains from regression and rejection. A retention check is a bounded gate, not a guarantee of safe adaptation.

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1: Does PriVoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively?}
\item \textbf{RQ2: Can PriVoke update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior?}
\item \textbf{RQ3: What systems and privacy overhead does PriVoke introduce?}
\end{enumerate}
The answers are conditional. Authored contextual cases show substantial package gains and remaining enforcement harms; separate annotation-based tasks expose weak specificity. The update path works mechanically, but aggregate improvement does not ensure retention. Browser and telemetry measurements establish bounded behavior and costs rather than deployment utility. Keeping these answers together makes the system's contribution and its unresolved gaps explicit.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local protection and sanitization.} Casper's three layers directly precede PriVoke's layered inspection~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024}. Casper also substitutes identifiers, restores them in responses and offers topic-warning acknowledgement. It supports custom rules/topics and model replacement; it should not be characterized as uniformly static. Hide and Seek (HaS) anonymizes entities before cloud inference and locally recovers them from responses, with trained adversaries testing recovery resistance~\cite{HaS2024}. ProSan selectively replaces prompt words using task-importance and privacy proxies, then distils a local sanitizer~\cite{ProSan2024}. These systems establish both local processing and training as prior art. PromptShield studies enterprise role/policy classification~\cite{PromptShield2025}.

\textit{A different measured contract.} PriVoke's browser interface acts on requests: BLOCK cancels a supported request, whereas WARN and ALLOW send its original body. Returned masked spans do not rewrite traffic, and there is no response-recovery stage. Its distinctive engineering focus is the guarded path from a candidate tied to an exact model revision to publication and client refresh. We measure paired gains and losses, including candidates that the guard refuses. The separate telemetry path limits randomized event fields without treating them as training feedback. These are bounded systems contributions, not claims of superior sanitization or uniquely local inference.

\textit{Comparison boundaries.} The reviewed systems evaluate different targets. Casper reports entity/topic detection, HaS reports resistance to whole-text recovery plus downstream task proxies, and ProSan reports item replacement and automatic utility. Their published metrics cannot be placed on one privacy-accuracy axis. We retain version-qualified outcomes and interaction details in Appendix~\ref{app:interaction}. Our executed Presidio references use disclosed native entity-recognizer settings~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026}; they are component references, not reproductions of Casper, HaS or ProSan. No matched system superiority claim follows.

\section{System Design and Privacy Boundary}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
The aim is to reduce inadvertent personal, third-party or organizational disclosure through supported outgoing prompts. We trust the workstation, enabled extension, local runtime/supervisor and authenticated model publisher. Compromised clients, malicious publishers, deliberately replaced hooks and provider-held historical information lie outside this boundary. Recognized prompt POST formats through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} are supported; workers, attachments, other transports and changed endpoints need separate validation. Disabling protection bypasses inspection. Optional hosted inference changes the disclosure boundary and is excluded from local-inference measurements.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates three paths. Prompt inspection stays on the workstation. Labelled training examples enter a separate training service and accepted parameters return to the local detector. Coarse, locally randomized event fields go to a telemetry collector, with no path from telemetry to training. This separation describes what each service receives; it does not establish that every policy decision is correct.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
A page hook extracts supported prompt text and sends it through the extension background process and a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to a workstation-local Python runtime. A native-messaging supervisor manages bridge/runtime lifecycle. Local user inference does not fall back to the server-side training runtime. Figure~\ref{fig:enforcement} shows the request contract.

The runtime normalizes text while preserving offsets for original-text evidence. Its default cascade runs regular-expression rules first, followed by enabled named entity recognition (NER) and the semantic classifier. A rule-based BLOCK skips later detectors; other findings are aggregated with the strongest action retained. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW result, while valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. Unavailable analysis blocks supported requests. A parameter-stream outage can leave other enabled layers operating, so fallback may reduce semantic coverage rather than block every full-pipeline request.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{architecture-enforcement.pdf}
\caption{Pre-transmission enforcement on supported hooks. WARN forwards unchanged content after attempting a notice; it does not wait for user acknowledgement. Analysis failure and reduced-layer stream fallback are distinct conditions. Disabled protection and unsupported traffic lie outside this decision path.}
\label{fig:enforcement}
\end{figure}

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic classifier returns three dimensions: sensitivity (none to highly sensitive), visibility (public to personal-private, or unspecified) and a set of ten information categories, such as health, identity and location. A deterministic policy converts these labels and confidence into an action. Highly sensitive content normally maps to BLOCK and potentially harmful personal information to WARN; identity/location combinations can raise restrictions. Low confidence can moderate the action. These labels and a confidence score do not independently establish disclosure authorization or calibrated risk. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives the exact mapping.

We compare historical small classifiers with later packages retaining pretrained BERT encoders/poolers~\cite{googleBertL4H128A2,googleBertL2H256A4,googleBertL12H256A4,turc2019WellReadStudents}. They classify prompts rather than generate conversational responses. Three package sizes are called efficient, balanced and quality; the names do not imply a universal performance ordering. Their tokenizers, capacities, thresholds and histories differ, so package comparisons do not isolate pretraining or architecture.

\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
The update process has two responsibilities: compute a candidate and decide whether that candidate may replace its base. A training controller constructs bounded labelled batches and held-out guard cases, excluding protected rows/text/groups. A separate server runtime fetches the specified model, executes training and returns model-bound parameter changes and candidate metrics. The updater checks exact base identity, allowed trainable tensors and shapes, finite values, magnitude limits and strategy-configured publication guards (Figure~\ref{fig:updates}). A checksum or successful receipt establishes neither accuracy nor protection.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{architecture-updates.pdf}
\caption{A candidate is evaluated before publication. Compare-and-swap rejects stale/conflicting bases; durable receipts prevent an identical retry from applying twice. A passing retention guard covers its declared cases and criteria only. Head and underlying-model stages are separate publications, so a later rejection need not undo an earlier accepted head stage.}
\label{fig:updates}
\end{figure}

Validated artifacts are published atomically using compare-and-swap: publication succeeds only if the current base still matches the candidate's declared parent. Durable replay receipts make repeated submissions idempotent. Streaming serves versioned parameters; clients verify identities and refresh cached weights without an application release. This is centralized training from supplied examples, not federated learning, collection of user gradients or evidence of cloud deployment. The design enables updates; RQ2 asks whether particular updates preserve protection.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
Telemetry reports five coarse fields: action, an action/sensitivity risk bucket, one precedence-selected category, a bounded release label and a four-hour time-of-day bin without date. Packets exclude raw prompts, identifiers, exact confidence scores and per-layer findings. Each field is randomized locally before release. No telemetry field supplies a corrected label or feeds the training path.

For an event budget $\varepsilon$, each of five fields receives $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. A field with $K_i$ possible values reports truth with probability
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
for each alternative. Under ideal independent sampling and common public configuration, an event tuple has likelihood ratio at most $e^\varepsilon$. Actual finite arithmetic requires a separate audit. A persistent local ledger reserves budget before construction, defaults to event budget one and total eight per UTC day, and does not refund failures. Trusted clock/state are required. Report presence, contribution selection, delivery and network metadata are outside this content bound. The collector inverts nominal probabilities and clips marginal estimates; randomization can therefore impose substantial estimation error (RQ3).

\textit{Contextual policies.} The authored workload contains 1,560 cases: 810 sensitive and 750 clean, with 260 per audience class. Sensitivity, audience, categories and allowed actions are assistant-provisional, without independent human adjudication. A binary positive means nonzero sensitivity or any category. Semantic joint correctness requires all three label dimensions; product joint correctness additionally requires an allowed action. An under-restricted action falls below the allowed-action set and an over-restricted action above it. These outcomes are distinct from receiver-observed transmission.

The workload has 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups. It was sealed for an earlier semantic comparison, then opened and reused for product and recognizer studies. It is PriVoke-authored development evidence, not independent validation. Repeated passes establish repeatability, not a larger independent sample. Separate paired cluster resampling by family or expression describes dependence sensitivity; it does not certify population coverage.

\textit{Annotation-based checks.} A once-only final endpoint contains 498 English rows with 236 annotation-positive and 262 annotation-negative targets from PIIMB revision \texttt{4a13e9f}~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026}. Models, sources and scoring were frozen before release; no later tuning, reselection or promotion followed. This prompt-level presence task differs from contextual authorization and from PIIMB's official character-masking/F2 task. Its 464 observed source-group keys do not certify template independence. A separate external reference uses 2,000 AdvPIIBench rows: 1,000 injected positives, 750 ordinary negatives and 250 hard negatives~\cite{arpaly2026advpiibench}. Official source categories are not exhaustive privacy annotations. Detailed selection, grouping and prediction proxies are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}.

\subsection{Comparison and Retention Protocols}
Historical and updated native packages are evaluated on the same contextual cases through the product handler, with unchanged rules and policy. Package differences remain confounded. A matched balanced-package ablation instead removes individual detectors while fixing texts, weights and policy; this isolates the local detector-removal contrast. Configured small/large English Presidio models provide same-text entity-recognizer references at threshold 0.5~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026,SpacyLarge371Release2026}, with unsupported contextual/action outputs left unmeasured.

For RQ2, a fixed-base experiment compares head-only and full-model continuation across three seeds. Each treatment starts with byte-identical intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads, receives the same ordered 4,096 training exposures over 64 steps, and is assessed on the same 1,080 reused provisional cases after complete training/guard group and text exclusions. Full-model continuation alone enables encoder optimization, including the consequent joint-gradient-clipping changes. All terminal candidates are assessed, including guard failures; no assessment selects a checkpoint. The 16-case retention guard requires joint improvement and zero action harms against its derived target actions. It is smaller and differently scored than the 1,080-case assessment, which permits declared allowed-action sets. Neither a weak zero-head control nor a small guard certifies broader safety. Recipes and historical unsuccessful branches remain in Appendices~\ref{app:lineage} and~\ref{app:protocols}.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Telemetry Protocols}
Installed Linux Chromium probes use the built extension, standard native-host installer, native messaging, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector. An HTTPS synthetic receiver records original bodies and late sends. Thirty-three correctness probes cover forwarding, blocking, faults, cancellation and recovery; this receiver is not an authenticated LLM provider. Full-pipeline and sole-semantic execution are checked outside resource windows.

The cost experiment requests rules/NER and measures 360 decisions across twelve fresh profiles, two per transport/action cell. Page-observed latency excludes receiver response. Whole-container CPU and sampled charged memory include resident infrastructure/cache and the observer. There is no unprotected baseline or incremental client attribution, and these windows do not measure semantic-layer cost. Appendix~\ref{app:browser_protocol} specifies boundaries and sampling.

The telemetry audit enumerates finite sampling kernels for one fingerprinted host build, assuming independent uniform OS bits. Separate fixed-contributor simulations quantify estimator error, while real ledger checks exercise persistence, concurrency and failure accounting. These are conditional mechanism checks and synthetic utility measurements, not a deployed monitoring study.

\subsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
The complete installed-browser capture observed eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes across thirty-three declared correctness probes. Runtime-call correlation and late receiver checks supported the outcomes, without a decision broker. Healthy full and sole-semantic probes verified execution. An interrupted parameter stream blocked semantic-only requests; configured full-mode fallback executed rules/NER and forwarded, demonstrating reduced coverage. Restart restored full execution. Disabled protection/layers forwarded by design. Nine earlier failed captures remain separate, not pooled into the completed result.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke detects many provisional sensitive cases and enforces its declared supported-request contract, including explicit blocking. Its effectiveness is conditional: updated packages still make action errors, WARN transmits original content, independent contextual ground truth is missing, and separate presence/category tasks reveal weak specificity. Receiver probes establish bounded enforcement rather than provider compatibility, prevented disclosure on every sensitive prompt or practical usability.
\section{RQ2: Updates and Previously Protected Behavior}
\label{sec:adaptation_results}
\subsection{Mechanism Versus Useful Adaptation}
Audited server computation, bounded publication and client refresh demonstrate that served parameters can change. They do not establish that an update is worth retaining. Earlier contextual, curriculum and accelerated training studies failed their declared qualification criteria. A pretrained package improved provisional disclosure agreement but failed a serious-action veto. These negative results and their attempted budgets are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:history}, rather than displaced by later package gains.

Later supervised packages improved authored contextual classification, with component and action regressions retained. The RQ1 package comparison measures the resulting packages, not automatic online-training benefit. Its capacity, tokenizer, threshold and supervision differences prevent attribution to one update mechanism. The controlled continuation below asks a narrower question with a common base and exposure budget.

\section{RQ3: Systems and Privacy Overhead}
\subsection{Measured Systems Cost}
The completed installed-browser protocol recorded 360 rules/NER decisions in twelve fresh-profile windows. Page-observed decision medians ranged from 27.9 to 35.0 ms across transport/action cells; each reported p99 is that cell's observed maximum. All 240 ALLOW/WARN decisions executed rules and NER, while 120 BLOCK decisions executed rules and legitimately skipped NER. These measurements exclude semantic-layer and receiver-response cost.

Across 126.40 seconds of summed windows, aggregate container CPU was 193.29 CPU-seconds, including 0.69 observer CPU-seconds. Maximum sampled charged memory was 1,267.91 MiB. Resident services/cache, driver and receiver share the container. A maximum sampling gap of 622.84 ms can miss memory spikes. Final-read and cleanup checks passed. Without an unprotected baseline or per-role attribution, these totals describe the instrumented system and do not isolate incremental PriVoke overhead. Startup intervals and cell tails appear in Appendix~\ref{app:browser_cost}.

Updated packages had lower observed host-call medians but larger server-process peak RSS than historical packages. Fixed execution order, warming, different backends and cascade shortcuts prevent a controlled speedup or model-only interpretation (Appendix~\ref{app:product_cost}). Training times and allocated CUDA tensor peaks likewise do not establish comparative training speed or whole-process memory requirements.

\subsection{Privacy Cost and Telemetry Utility}
\label{sec:telemetry_results}
The host finite-kernel audit found that maximum event privacy loss exceeds nominal budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 by approximately $(1.294,1.503,1.226)\times10^{-15}$. Thus the exact nominal bound fails, despite the tiny excess. Eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$ under the same independent uniform-entropy assumptions. Other builds and deployed entropy remain uncertified. Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction} distinguishes the implemented finite kernel from the ideal formula.

Randomization also reduces estimate usefulness. Fixed-contributor simulations found substantial clipping bias, root-mean-square error and incoherent marginal totals. For eleven-category reports with event budget one and eighty contributors, a true first-category count of seven had clipped bias +22.84 and RMSE 42.32 counts. Marginal estimates failed to sum to eighty in 99.99\% of trials. This conditional synthetic result is not a population monitoring accuracy estimate.

Real ledger checks enforced the daily cap across persistence and concurrent requests, and failed construction/delivery still consumed reservations. Reversing a constructed event sequence changed first-eight BLOCK participation from 100\% to 0\%. Randomizing report contents therefore does not remove contribution-selection bias or protect whether an event participated.

\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} PriVoke introduces measurable local processing and infrastructure costs, but incremental and semantic-pipeline overhead remain unmeasured. Randomized telemetry has a conditional, narrowly exceeded nominal content bound and substantial synthetic estimation error. Neither telemetry's deployment utility nor protection of participation and network metadata is established.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
The central contribution is a complete protection lifecycle with explicit boundaries: inspect locally, enforce a request decision, evaluate a version-bound candidate before publication, refresh clients, and randomize separate telemetry. The experiments show why those boundaries matter. A semantic improvement cannot undo a rule-based early BLOCK. A WARN result does not prevent exposure. A larger average gain can be rejected for a single retained protection failure. Telemetry can support conditional aggregate estimation without becoming a source of training truth.

This contribution complements sanitization and recovery systems rather than replacing their utility questions. Casper, HaS and ProSan motivate what a user may need after detection: an acknowledged intervention, a usable rewritten prompt or a restored answer. PriVoke currently offers a request-action contract. It needs separate implementation and evidence before claiming those additional functions or superiority over them.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
The largest scientific gap is independently adjudicated contextual privacy truth. Our sensitivity, audience, category and action targets are assistant-provisional; they do not establish authorization or human agreement. Source annotation presence and injected category labels are useful checks, but cannot fill that gap. Entity detection, contextual disclosure, appropriate action and task utility need different labels. The large drop from authored clean specificity to final annotation-negative specificity is an observed limitation, not evidence that one target should be dismissed.

The authored cases are dependent and reused. Final source-group keys and external lexical exclusions do not certify template independence, cover paraphrases or remove unknown upstream pretraining exposure. Final rows are source-imbalanced: 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA. English synthetic/machine-generated workloads do not represent other languages or organizational policies. Separate cluster sensitivities are descriptive and unadjusted; repeated runs and three seeds do not create independent populations. Native-package differences further limit causal attribution. A new protected successor is needed for future tuning after the opened final endpoint.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
A retention guard can protect only what it tests. Our small matched guard and weak zero-head control leave considerable uncertainty. Rejected candidates are not served improvements, and the broader negative studies do not establish useful automatic adaptation. Future studies need common pinned bases, independent retention cases and explicit casewise harm criteria, retaining failed and effective artifacts separately.

Fail-closed behavior also has availability costs. Installed Linux probes cover declared historical-detector Chromium/native receiver paths; authenticated providers, Windows, updated-model installation, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified. Confidence scores are heuristic, not calibrated joint correctness. WARN does not wait for informed acknowledgement. No study measures authorized task completion, unnecessary-intervention cost, warning comprehension or reformulation effort, so practical utility and human benefit remain unestablished.

Later instrumentation exposes ephemeral notice-rendering and local request-disposition events. Fifty-three synthetic host tests validate that contract, but the fields postdate the reported browser capture. They have no installed-browser or overhead remeasurement. Rendering does not prove comprehension, native handoff does not prove receipt, and page scripts can forge or suppress the events. They are not persisted or sent as telemetry; measuring utility still needs independent expected actions and joined user/task outcomes.

\subsection{Measurement and Reproduction Boundaries}
Cost scopes are deliberately narrow: instrumented container totals are not incremental client overhead, and rules/NER timings are not semantic timings. Telemetry bounds assume retained local state, trusted clock and independent uniform OS bits. Delivery selection, participation and metadata are excluded, and synthetic estimator error has no deployment-usefulness threshold. Telemetry supplies no corrected contextual labels.

A sealed local source/model capsule reproduced twenty-four invented semantic requests across six packages with matching identities and outcomes. This is bounded replay using existing host dependencies, not public Git-only/fresh-environment reproduction or a protected-final rerun (Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction}). Ignored pinned model/source assets and licensing constraints remain relevant to redistribution. These limits guide the next work: independent contextual adjudication, protected generalization studies, broader receiver coverage, baseline-relative semantic cost and human utility, rather than additional branches of uncontrolled fitting.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke integrates local prompt inspection, pre-transmission request actions, guarded version-bound detector updates and separate randomized telemetry. The three research questions yield a qualified systems result: detection improves on authored cases but specificity transfers poorly; supported enforcement is observable, while WARN forwards original content; useful updates cannot be inferred from aggregate gains; and measured costs and privacy bounds retain substantial scope and utility limits. All full-model candidates in the matched study were rejected despite better averages. The system provides an auditable mechanism for protection and revision, without established independent contextual accuracy, safe automatic improvement or human utility.

\section{Exact Policy Representation}
\label{app:policy}
Sensitivity S0--S3 means no identified risk, low-risk personal context, potentially harmful personal information and highly sensitive/identifying details or secrets. Visibility P0--P4/PU means public, semi-public, authentication-restricted, group-private, personal-private and unspecified. The ten multilabel categories are HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY and THIRD\_PARTY.

The deterministic policy maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN. S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless IDENTITY and LOCATION coexist, or either occurs with P2--P4 visibility. Returned confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY/LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications map to ALLOW. Aggregation preserves individual enforcement decisions, unions categories and takes the strongest sensitivity/known visibility. These are policy definitions, not independently validated disclosure authorization.

\subsection{Implemented Finite Kernel}
Let $\widetilde p_i$ be the binary64 truth threshold. In the audited CPython sampler, fresh uniform 53-bit $J$ selects truth when $J/2^{53}<\widetilde p_i$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Alternative selection uses rejection sampling. The calculation assumes independent uniform OS bits and the fingerprinted host arithmetic; it does not certify deployed entropy or other builds.
