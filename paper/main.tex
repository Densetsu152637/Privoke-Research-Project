\begin{abstract}

Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) introduce a privacy leakage surface in which sensitive user information can be disclosed through outgoing prompts. This paper presents \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that combines rule-based detection, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and a compact semantic classifier to inspect supported requests before transmission. Its principal contribution is the design and evaluation of a guarded cloud-training and parameter-redistribution path for a locally executed prompt-privacy classifier: a server-side fuzzer supplies labeled examples, a cloud runtime computes bounded model updates, and accepted parameter revisions are streamed to clients for local inference. This architecture permits successive retraining as new examples become available without requiring the collection of users' raw prompts for training. We investigate whether these updates improve detection while preserving existing coverage, distinguishing annotation-presence detection, contextual privacy decisions, and request enforcement. The current extension forwards the original request on ALLOW or WARN and cancels supported requests on BLOCK. Locally randomized categorical telemetry supports aggregate monitoring under a conditional event-tuple privacy guarantee and remains separate from the training path. Six contextual grids completed on 6 October recorded 126 update attempts and no retained candidate under their criteria. A separate six-hour study measured a combined synthetic curriculum against a binary annotation-presence endpoint: balanced pipeline specificity increased by 14.29 percentage points as recall fell by 6.06 points, while balanced semantic recall fell by 25 points. A later semantic-only curriculum and representation comparison completed 900 live attempts and 360 separate offline steps, with no qualifying candidate. Specificity and contextual clean-control gains coexisted with annotation-presence recall loss. A subsequent accelerated semantic-only experiment completed 2,016 normal-batch attempts; none of nine revised realizations met its prespecified promising criterion. Efficient traded recall for specificity, balanced traded specificity and contextual agreement for recall relative to its control, and quality reached a publication plateau. A subsequent frozen pretrained package improved provisional synthetic disclosure agreement but failed a casewise serious-action veto; reused secondary evidence exposed a separate credential regression. These exploratory development results support reporting the tested adaptation limits, but do not establish contextual privacy safety or justify model promotion; protected final evaluation remains pending.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Web-hosted Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly shaping modern AI-assisted workflows, particularly in writing, coding, analysis, and document-centric professional tasks~\cite{Gupta2024, LIANG2025101366, koraag2025comparing}. Their widespread adoption is largely driven by the capabilities of large-scale hosted models, which often provide substantially greater model capacity than locally deployable alternatives such as LLaMA-style models~\cite{Florian2025, MEDEC2024}. This increased capacity enables higher-quality responses, broader task handling, and greater productivity benefits, encouraging users to rely on web-hosted LLMs for everyday work activities~\cite{Crane2025, Zahra2025}. However, this convenience also introduces a critical privacy risk: users may include personal, organizational, or contextually sensitive information in prompts without fully recognizing the implications of sending that data to an external service~\cite{Athey2017}. While major LLM providers such as OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft offer privacy policies, retention controls, and enterprise settings, prompt processing remains largely provider-dependent, and user-submitted data may still be processed, logged, or reused within service improvement pipelines depending on the provider’s policy and configuration~\cite{barbera2025ai,King2025}. As a result, prompt-based interaction with web-hosted LLMs poses a practical security challenge: sensitive information can leave the client before effective privacy enforcement can occur.

Recent incidents show that prompt privacy is no longer a hypothetical concern. In 2023, Samsung employees reportedly exposed confidential source code, internal meeting notes, and engineering information by submitting them to Chat-GPT for work-related assistance, prompting the company to restrict the use of generative AI tools within its workplace~\cite{SamsungBans2023}. In the same year, a Chat-GPT service bug exposed parts of users' conversation-history metadata and limited billing-related information for a subset of subscribers, demonstrating that prompt ecosystems can fail not only through user mistakes but also through platform-side vulnerabilities~\cite{BBCChatGPTHistoryBug2023}. Google reportedly warned employees not to enter confidential material into AI chatbots, including its own Bard/Gemini system, and its privacy notice cautioned users against including confidential or sensitive information in Bard conversations~\cite{GoogleWarns2023}. Similar risks have appeared in high-stakes public-sector settings, where sensitive child-protection information was entered into Chat-GPT during the preparation of a court-related report, leading regulators to impose restrictions on generative AI use within the department~\cite{ChatGPTChildProtection}. Claude.ai has also been shown to face prompt-injection and data-exfiltration risks, where chained vulnerabilities could enable sensitive information from conversation history to be extracted through provider-side workflows~\cite{ClaudeDay2026}. Beyond individual incidents, prior studies and provider-side analyses show that sensitive information supplied through prompts may be retained, reproduced, analyzed, or insufficiently sanitized by LLM-based systems~\cite{tamkin2024clio,priyanshu2023chatbots}. These cases highlight a critical gap in current LLM usage: privacy protection still depends on user caution, provider policy, or post-submission controls, while the most effective point of intervention is before sensitive content leaves the client.

Motivated by this gap, we propose \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that shifts prompt protection to the moment sensitive information is about to leave the user's device. In addition to user caution, organizational policy, and remote service controls, \privoke inspects outgoing prompts locally before they are transmitted to supported web-hosted LLM endpoints and applies warning or blocking decisions. Its design combines real-time prompt analysis on the client with a supporting microservices architecture for model management, privacy-oriented prompt fuzzing, controlled parameter updates, and telemetry protected by Local Differential Privacy (LDP). The current implementation forwards the original request on ALLOW or WARN and cancels a supported request on BLOCK; span masking returned by the runtime is not automatically substituted into browser traffic. This separation allows cloud-side training and robustness experiments to proceed independently of interactive client inference, while deployment cost and usability remain empirical questions. In this way, \privoke provides an endpoint-level intervention within existing web-hosted LLM workflows, with protection bounded by the supported request paths and enabled detector layers.

The principal contribution of \privoke is the design and evaluation of a guarded update path that retrains the privacy classifier in the cloud and distributes revised parameters to deployed clients while continuing to inspect user prompts locally. Labeled generated or prepared examples are processed by a server-side instance of the runtime, and accepted updates are published as versioned artifacts. A client retrieves the selected model and refreshes its cached parameters, so further training does not require an application release or client-side collection of training prompts. Successive updates can therefore support adaptation to newly supplied examples, but improvement is a hypothesis to evaluate: an updated classifier may improve one outcome while regressing another.

Client-side layered inspection has precedent in Casper~\cite{Casper2024}. We build on that established design and examine the additional training, publication, and redistribution path. The resulting research questions concern the contribution of each layer, the effect of controlled cloud-side updates, and whether the extension enforces the intended request decisions. The incident examples above motivate minimizing avoidable outgoing disclosure; billing exposure and exfiltration of information already held by a provider also illustrate downstream risks that a prompt interceptor cannot generally prevent.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We frame prompt-level privacy leakage as an endpoint security problem in web-hosted LLM workflows, where sensitive information may leave the client before effective safeguards are applied.

    \item We present \privoke, a client-side semantic privacy firewall that inspects supported outgoing prompts and applies ALLOW, WARN, or BLOCK decisions before transmission, with explicit forwarding and failure behavior.

    \item We combine rule-based matching, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and semantic classification with a sensitivity, visibility, and category policy, and distinguish entity-presence detection from contextual disclosure and action correctness.

    \item We introduce a cloud-training and local-inference architecture in which a privacy-focused fuzzer supplies labeled batches, the server runtime computes bounded updates, and validated revisions are streamed to deployed clients. This enables controlled evaluation of adaptation as new data become available, including detection gains and regressions.

    \item We integrate locally randomized categorical telemetry for aggregate deployment monitoring, with an explicit event-tuple privacy scope, composition budget, and exclusions; telemetry is independent of the model-training data path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Large Language Models and Prompt-Based Interaction}

LLMs are widely used in modern AI-assisted workflows, including writing, coding, summarization, information retrieval and decision-support tasks. Most contemporary web-hosted LLM services, such as Chat-GPT, Microsoft Copilot, and Google Gemini, are built on Transformer-based architectures that support scalable pre-training and effective modeling of long-range textual dependencies~\cite{courseraTransformersConvolutional,liu2025understanding}. Transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs), when trained on large-scale corpora and adapted for interactive use, form the foundation of many current LLM systems~\cite{zhong2023chatradio}.

Users interact with these systems primarily through prompts, which are natural-language instructions, questions or contextual inputs submitted to the model. In practical workflows, prompts often contain more than simple queries: users may paste personal details, organizational information, code fragments, legal or medical context, internal documents, or other sensitive content to obtain useful responses. Although LLMs can also be deployed locally using smaller open-weight models such as LLaMA-style systems~\cite{Llama2023}, web-hosted LLM services remain widely adopted because they reduce local computational requirements and provide convenient access to high-capacity models. This deployment model, however, shifts prompt processing outside the user's direct control.

\subsection{Privacy Risks in External LLM Systems}

The widespread adoption of web-hosted LLMs has raised privacy concerns about the processing, retention, and reuse of user-submitted prompts. Recent studies highlight risks associated with prompt collection, provider-side logging, and policy-dependent data handling in external LLM services~\cite{li2025security, de2025lifecycle}. Prior systems such as Casper~\cite{Casper2024} and PromptShield~\cite{kelecspromptshield} further emphasize that web-hosted LLM services may process, store, or share user prompts depending on their privacy policies and service configurations. Once a sensitive prompt is submitted, users may have limited visibility into how the content is handled, retained, analyzed, or used in downstream service-improvement pipelines.

A further concern is that sensitive content may remain difficult to fully control after submission. Studies on memorization and training-data reconstruction show that information used during model training can, under certain conditions, be partially recovered or reproduced~\cite{haim2022reconstructing}. While not every submitted prompt is necessarily used for model training, these findings reinforce the broader risk that sensitive data entering an LLM pipeline may create long-term privacy exposure. This motivates privacy controls that operate before disclosure, rather than relying only on provider-side policies or post-submission deletion mechanisms.

\subsection{Privacy Preservation Techniques}

Existing frameworks, including Casper \cite{Casper2024}, HaS \cite{HaS2024}, and ProSan~\cite{ProSan2024}, use techniques such as prompt sanitization, data anonymisation, NER, semantic risk detection, semantic substitution, local inference, and client-side filtering to reduce privacy risks in LLM workflows. Their detector, transformation and deployment choices differ, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:literature_review}.

Prompt sanitization refers to the process of inspecting, cleaning, or transforming user inputs before they are passed to a language model or downstream service~\cite{Mousumi_Bakshi2025}. In security settings, sanitization may remove malicious instructions to reduce prompt-injection risks; in privacy settings, it may redact or replace PII before disclosure. While server-side systems may apply sanitization to protect their own infrastructure, they may not always prevent user-sensitive information from reaching the provider in the first place~\cite{pradhan2025zero}. This motivates client-side filtering approaches, where sensitive content is detected and controlled before the prompt leaves the user's device.

Data anonymisation focuses on reducing the identifiability of user-submitted content, typically through two steps: PII detection followed by text replacement or transformation. Rule-based detection uses regular expressions and predefined patterns to identify common sensitive fields such as email addresses, phone numbers, or identification numbers. Although effective for explicit and well-structured identifiers, rule-based methods are limited when sensitive information appears in unusual formats or depends on context. NER addresses part of this limitation by identifying structured entities such as persons, organizations, locations, and dates from unstructured text~\cite{munnangi2024brief}. However, sensitive information may also arise from the semantic meaning of a prompt rather than from explicit identifiers alone. Semantic risk detection, therefore, uses Natural Language Processing (NLP) methods to infer privacy risk from context, enabling detection of disclosures that may not be captured by surface-level patterns or entity labels. Once sensitive content is identified, it may be redacted, replaced, masked, or semantically rewritten to reduce privacy leakage.

DP provides a formal framework for limiting privacy risk during data collection and analysis. A randomized mechanism $M$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differentially private when, for every pair of neighboring datasets $D,D'$ and every measurable output set $O$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[M(D)\in O] \leq e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[M(D')\in O]+\delta.
\end{equation}
The neighboring relation specifies the protected change in the data; $\varepsilon$ bounds privacy loss and $\delta$ permits a bounded exceptional probability. These parameters do not by themselves specify which data or observable channels a system protects.

It is widely used for large-scale telemetry and usage statistics collection by organizations such as Google, Apple, and Microsoft~\cite{cormode2018privacy, cortes2016differential}. Local Differential Privacy (LDP) extends this idea by perturbing information on the user's device before transmission, reducing reliance on a trusted central collector. In the context of client-side LLM privacy tools, LDP can protect released categorical telemetry, such as risk buckets, action types, and model-release statistics. Payload minimization and a value-level privacy guarantee must be distinguished from hiding report presence or identifying metadata in the transport.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature_review}

\subsection{PII Detection}

Existing privacy-preserving LLM systems commonly combine rule-based, entity-based and semantic approaches to identify sensitive information prior to disclosure. Casper~\cite{Casper2024} provides a browser-local layered detector, whereas HaS~\cite{HaS2024} and ProSan~\cite{ProSan2024} study prompt transformation and the utility of subsequent responses. These systems establish relevant detection and sanitization precedents, but their tasks and reported measurements are not interchangeable with request blocking or binary annotation-presence evaluation.

As privacy-sensitive content depends on application context, domain-specific terminology, and evolving input distributions, a fixed classifier can encounter examples outside its training coverage. Updating the classifier is a candidate response to this problem. In \privoke, the question is whether new labeled cloud-side batches produce useful changes in the locally executed detector under controlled comparisons. This formulation distinguishes a learned update from manual rule customization and does not assume that every existing prompt-privacy system is incapable of adaptation.

\subsection{Continuous Model Training Methods}

Feedback-driven refinement, active learning, and adversarial testing offer different ways to identify or generate additional training examples. Their benefit depends on the correctness of labels, the representation being updated, and the separation of training from evaluation. A reduction in training loss or success on generated variants is insufficient to establish performance on unseen sources.

\privoke uses centrally orchestrated training and versioned redistribution to support successive classifier updates after client deployment. The fuzzer supplies labeled examples to a server-side runtime rather than collecting user gradients. Its optional transformations permit controlled robustness experiments, while each executed study records whether transformations were enabled. The current evidence addresses bounded updates to repository-owned classifiers; it does not establish autonomous learning from live user behavior, federated learning, or monotonically improving detection.

\subsection{Differential Privacy \& Telemetry Collection}

Differentially private telemetry supports aggregate analytics by randomizing data before release. Telemetry privacy and prompt sanitization protect different disclosures: one bounds information released through monitoring fields, while the other changes or prevents the content sent to an LLM. Text anonymization and reconstruction, as studied by HaS~\cite{HaS2024}, also involve utility tradeoffs that differ from categorical monitoring.

\privoke applies local randomization to bounded event fields and uses the resulting summaries for monitoring. The current telemetry path does not provide prompt features, corrected labels, or gradients to the fuzzer. We therefore describe monitoring and model refinement as separate architectural functions, each with its own assumptions and evaluation requirements. A future telemetry-guided training mechanism would need an explicit learning signal and a separate privacy argument.

\subsection{Research Gap}

The architectural precedent of local layered filtering leaves an empirical question about maintaining a deployed privacy classifier: can centrally trained, bounded revisions improve detection while preserving the existing protection afforded by other layers? Answering this question requires fixed-base comparisons, separate semantic and full-pipeline measurements, and explicit accounting for clean-input regressions.

\privoke addresses this question through a cloud-training path coupled to local inference and guarded parameter publication. Its contribution is the design and evaluation of this update path in a prompt-privacy system, including its limits. Comparisons with prior sanitizers must state differences in task, intervention and data; the existence of the integration alone does not establish measured superiority or a universally novel training technique.

\section{PII Categorization}
\label{sec:pii_categorisation}

To support context-aware privacy decisions, \privoke classifies detected information according to public visibility (P0--P4, PU), sensitivity (S0--S3), and information category. Category labels are stored as bit-mask flags covering domains such as health, financial information, location data, criminal history, religion, and personal identifiers. This multi-dimensional representation enables efficient storage while allowing privacy decisions to consider both the sensitivity and public exposure of detected information.

Sensitivity expresses the configured disclosure-risk level: S0 denotes no identified risk, S1 low-risk personal context, S2 personal information associated with targeting or harm, and S3 high-risk sensitive or identifying details, including credentials and secrets. Visibility distinguishes public (P0), semi-public (P1), authentication-restricted (P2), group-private (P3), and personal-private (P4) contexts; PU denotes unknown visibility. These are policy labels and model outputs, not independent evidence that a person has authorized disclosure. Unknown visibility should remain explicit when the text or supplied context does not support a more precise class.

The ten categories are HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY, and THIRD\_PARTY. Multiple categories may coexist, and each detector returns evidence, confidence, and a span where available. Normalized spans are mapped back to the original prompt. A public biography containing a name and a privately supplied account credential can both contain annotated identifiers, yet require different disclosure decisions.

The policy maps S3 to BLOCK and S2 to WARN, while S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless both IDENTITY and LOCATION occur, or either category occurs with restricted/private visibility (P2--P4). Confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY or LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications also map to ALLOW. This threshold is an implemented policy choice; returned confidence is not established as a calibrated probability. Aggregation preserves individual enforcement decisions and combines categories and the strongest sensitivity and known visibility. Accordingly, entity presence, contextual labels, and policy actions are evaluated separately.

\section{Threat Model and Scope}
\label{sec:threat_model}

We consider a user who intends to interact with a hosted LLM but may inadvertently include private personal, third-party, or organizational information in a prompt. The protected opportunity is the outgoing request before transmission on a supported interception path. We trust the workstation, browser and extension configuration, local supervisor/runtime, and authenticated model publisher. A compromised client, malicious model publisher, adversarial local process, or page code that deliberately replaces the interception hook lies outside this boundary. Deliberate prompt evasion requires separate testing from accidental disclosure.

The extension covers recognized prompt POST endpoint and body formats through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest}. Unsupported transports, worker traffic, unrecognized attachments and future endpoint changes are not covered by this claim. Disabling the master toggle or all detector layers explicitly bypasses protection. A warning communicates a risk while forwarding the original request; a block cancels that request. Historical provider records and billing data are outside the outgoing-prompt boundary.

In streamed mode, user prompt inspection occurs on the workstation and parameter downloads carry weights rather than user text. Cloud-side training processes labeled generated or prepared research examples in a separate runtime instance. Optional inference through a hosted model changes the disclosure boundary and is excluded from the local-inference evaluation. Telemetry introduces a further release channel with the restricted guarantee specified in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism}.

\section{System Architecture \& Data Pipelines}
\label{sec:system_architecture}

\subsection{Client Runtime Service}

The browser component is a TypeScript WebExtension backed by a workstation-local Python runtime. A page hook intercepts supported outgoing requests and sends extracted prompt text through the extension's background process and a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to the detector. A separate supervisor, started through native messaging, owns both the bridge and detector process and provides lifecycle control. The extension instance is independent of the server-side runtime used for training and evaluation; it does not fall back to that server for user prompt inspection.

The extension displays warning or blocking notices and enforces the returned action. ALLOW and WARN preserve the original request body, whereas BLOCK prevents forwarding on the supported path. The runtime can return a masked primary span as evidence, but the extension does not substitute this text into the request. If the bridge or runtime is unavailable, the supported request is blocked. A streaming outage can temporarily use other enabled detector layers; when semantic detection is the only enabled layer, unavailable analysis blocks the request. Such fallback preserves the available layers rather than guaranteeing semantic coverage.

\subsection{\privoke Runtime}

The Python runtime hosts normalization, rule matching, spaCy NER and the selected semantic classifier. It applies Unicode compatibility normalization, lowercasing, email-separator normalization, digit-space removal and whitespace normalization, retaining offsets to remap evidence to the original text. Regex runs first by default and a regex BLOCK short-circuits the remaining requested layers. Otherwise the runtime executes the enabled layers and aggregates their classification evidence and errors. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW decision; valid non-ALLOW results remain visible rather than being replaced by a clean default.

The streamed semantic component is a compact repository-owned transformer classifier, not a general-purpose conversational LLM. Efficient, balanced and quality profiles differ in depth, context and vocabulary; these names do not establish relative model quality. The shipped historical balanced bootstrap artifact has two encoder blocks, hidden size 32, a 512-entry vocabulary, a 96-token context and 36,756 parameters; that count excludes the additional position rows in the later prepared 256-token release. Its original encoder is initialized from seeded randomness, with six sensitivity/visibility/category head tensors bootstrapped on 43 authored examples. Head-only training updates 660 parameters while retaining the encoder. Separately declared experimental treatments can train the last encoder block. A bounded offline research fitter now supports matched \texttt{head\_only} and \texttt{end\_to\_end} Adam training and validated tensor export; the historical offline experiment remains separate from the later versioned online full-Tiny training contract. That contract adds a supervised encoder-and-head endpoint alongside the head endpoint; it does not introduce general language-model pretraining or external pretrained weights.

The runtime reconstructs validated streamed tensors and performs inference locally, using available acceleration or CPU execution. It caches a model snapshot for the configured refresh interval and coalesces concurrent loads. The client can select a model identifier; \texttt{latest} resolves to the configured release channel, currently balanced, rather than identifying the best-scoring candidate. A snapshot is identified by architecture, configuration, version, checksum and tensor fingerprint.

A separate word/character TF-IDF classifier predicts binary annotation presence and exposes distinct inference and training RPCs. An explicit research-only cascade can use its ABSENT decision to suppress semantic findings before fusion, while retaining regex/NER findings. Presence does not supply sensitivity, visibility or action truth, and ABSENT is not a declaration that the prompt is safe. This cascade is opt-in and is not the default deployed policy.

The alternative hosted and local conversational backends share a classification prompt that now requires supported textual evidence rather than speculative external linkage. It distinguishes general discussion and explicit fiction from asserted personal disclosures, without exempting actual facts embedded in quotations or hypothetical frames. Sensitivity, visibility and category definitions remain separate, with unknown visibility represented by PU and public availability not erasing sensitivity. Analyzed text is encoded as a JSON string and designated as data rather than instructions. The response contract requests one JSON object containing a results array; the exact empty array envelope is an explicit no-risk response, while malformed or partial findings remain errors. These prompt and parser changes have compatibility checks, but their accuracy and injection resistance remain unmeasured. The streamed tensor classifier and its training/publication path do not consume this conversational prompt.

\subsection{Fuzzer Service}

The fuzzer is a server-side orchestration worker that generates or loads labeled prompt batches and requests training through the cloud runtime. It does not download model weights or execute the classifier itself. For a training request it reserves distinct clean and sensitive held-out examples, excludes their normalized texts and declared source groups from training, and submits the bounded batches to \texttt{ComputeSemanticGradients} for head training or \texttt{ComputeUnderlyingModelGradients} for the explicit full-Tiny strategy. Both requests require exactly the semantic layer and return checked training, base-held-out and candidate-held-out execution records; empty or mixed layer selections fail. Model fetching, execution and gradient computation occur inside the server runtime. The fuzzer receives model-bound deltas and candidate metrics, and submits eligible updates to the update service.

The head endpoint updates the sensitivity, visibility and category heads on a full-capability artifact. The underlying endpoint updates embeddings, all encoder blocks and the task heads using explicit contextual targets. Each keeps its own replay identity and validates exact model/version/checksum, tensor inventory and bounded deltas before publication. Frozen MiniLM online training remains unsupported. Optional transformed variants are generated according to the declared experiment; the presence of transformation support does not imply that every study uses adversarial examples. The runtime uses the same canonical normalization for training and inference and evaluates the exact bounded, float32 candidate on held-out examples without mutating its serving cache. Publication requires finite metrics, both held-out classes, nonregressing exact match/recall/specificity, and the configured casewise severity/action guard. These internal checks are distinct from public evaluation and do not establish generalization.

Repeated training can be requested by the update service or experiment controller. Current deployment defaults request 32 prompts per stage at startup and subsequent cycles at one-hour intervals. Each automatic cycle sequences head then underlying training on the same model ID. The second stage requires the acknowledged head version as its base; these are independent compare-and-swap publications, so a second-stage rejection leaves an accepted head update intact. A durable journal retains stage IDs, seed and acknowledgments across restart. Uncertain stages resume their original identity, definite rejections terminate the cycle, and subsequent periodic cycles advance the generation/trainer seed. A completed one-shot is not repeated on restart. For prepared curricula, the periodic caller also accepts \texttt{FUZZER\_CURRICULUM\_SAMPLER\_POLICY} and \texttt{FUZZER\_CURRICULUM\_SAMPLER\_SEED}; the allocation seed remains stable independently of the changing trainer seed. The default policy retains legacy deterministic allocation. Controlled quality-study and general product-smoke overrides disable background training to preserve fixed inputs. The dedicated semantic-training CI job deliberately enables one automatic cycle on an isolated fixed fixture; enabling a requester does not establish improved detection. The current source of adaptation is supplied labeled data, not user telemetry or gradients collected from deployed browsers. The separate annotation-presence training path updates its own logistic head under binary labels and is not contextual transformer training. Both dedicated presence RPCs now require explicit semantic-only selection and actual returned execution evidence: one inference execution or the three nonempty training and guard phases. Missing or failed evidence prevents prediction admission or publication. Durable attempt records preserve actual wire responses and guard diagnostics separately from logical training-round counts; this protocol change does not revise historical measurements.

An optional synthetic curriculum prepares grammar examples, offline assistant-authored teacher paraphrases and fact-preserving evolved variants under provisional contextual targets. Parent families are permanently separated into training, replay and a fixed sixteen-example publication guard. The default \texttt{deterministic\_v1} sampler preserves legacy ordering; \texttt{seeded\_family\_v1} permutes families and their descendants per epoch while retaining role/class quotas and within-batch uniqueness. Durable cursors have separate model/policy/seed namespaces, and content- and configuration-bound reservations preserve a batch across retries. A declared quarter of batch rows are replay examples. Their configurable relative example weight, \texttt{FUZZ\_TRAINING\_REPLAY\_WEIGHT}, defaults to 0.35, corresponding to approximately 10.45\% of nominal total example weight rather than a measured gradient contribution. The revised version retains the original 672-row training budget: 28 separately authored situations, four sibling values, two contextual classes and three roles. It supplies complete evolved renderings rather than only prefixes and suffixes, while preserving the original 64 replay and sixteen guard rows. Visibility and category exposure also change, so a comparison tests a revised curriculum package rather than semantic diversity alone.

A separately authored contextual regression assessment contains 64 rows in 32 paired scenario groups, frozen outside training, replay, mining and the publication guard. Its targets remain assistant provisional, and semantic archetypes overlap training; withheld IDs, declared groups and normalized texts do not establish broad semantic generalization. It includes actual disclosures in public, quoted, hypothetical and mixed-discussion wording. The isolated continual-study controller separately archives exact checkpoints and paired semantic/pipeline predictions on byte-pinned annotation-presence development data; that task does not supply contextual-policy truth. Failure-guided sampling, when enabled, selects only training IDs. The completed duration-based study used a two-hour elapsed window per profile, with measurement, mining and pauses under a cycle safety cap rather than two hours of compute. Its historical results in Section~\ref{sec:results} do not qualify a model; the reused sixteen-row guard cannot establish generalization.

The maintained server image includes pinned Linux CPU Torch 2.10.0 and checks CPU autograd readiness. Before serving, a locked bootstrap checkpoints update receipts and migrates the selected current learned artifact to the full-training contract, preserving all existing coordinates. The default prepared context is 256 total tokens (255 content tokens plus one start token); new position coordinates use a versioned deterministic SHA-256 initializer and the artifact receives a distinct version/checksum. An already-capable artifact with sufficient context is reused unchanged. Shipped historical artifacts and the separate MiniLM 256/512-token profiles remain unchanged. Preparation and mechanical checks do not establish improved detection or cloud deployment.

\subsection{Telemetry Collector Service}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}

The telemetry collector receives locally randomized categorical reports and stores marginal aggregate counts stratified by declared epsilon. The official packet contains action, an action/sensitivity-derived risk bucket, one category selected by fixed precedence, a bounded model-release label, and a four-hour UTC time-of-day bin without a date. Raw prompts, direct identifiers, exact scores and per-layer results are absent. Training suffixes are removed from the reported model release. The fields can reveal information about an event, so omission of identifiers alone is not described as de-identification.

For a public event budget $\varepsilon$, each of the five fields receives $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. Generalized randomized response on field $i$, with domain size $K_i$, outputs its true value with probability $p_i$ and each alternative with probability $q_i$, where
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},
\quad q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
With independent draws, the product of the five field likelihood ratios is at most $e^{\sum_i\varepsilon_i}=e^\varepsilon$. This establishes pure LDP for the nominal event-tuple randomizer at a fixed reporting opportunity, under public input-independent configuration, compliant execution and ideal sampling. The implementation uses floating-point probabilities and finite randomness; its exact sampler privacy loss has not been certified.

The default event epsilon is 1, configurable from 0.5 to 2. An installation-local persistent ledger reserves budget before packet construction, caps nominal expenditure at epsilon 8 per UTC day by default, and does not refund failed submissions. This bound assumes retained state and a trusted clock; it is neither a cross-device user budget nor a rolling 24-hour guarantee. Report presence, exact accepted counts, delivery selection and network metadata remain outside the guarantee. The collector validates domains and declared epsilon but cannot attest client randomization. Inverse-response marginal estimates are clipped, which can introduce bias, and monitoring utility requires separate measurement. Telemetry remains independent of model training.

\subsection{Model Streaming Service}

The Go model-streaming service supplies versioned artifacts through gRPC tensor streams. A consumer validates ordered chunks, offsets, shapes, finite values and complete model identity before constructing a model. Each stream is pinned to one artifact snapshot so that a concurrent publication cannot mix versions within a download. Workstation cloud connections use the configured authenticated TLS boundary; internal Compose gRPC remains confined to the trusted service network. The transport is not a browser WebSocket connection.

The streaming service reads the persistent artifact catalog, while the update service publishes accepted revisions to that catalog. A subsequent streaming request can receive the new artifact, and the client checks for revisions after its configured cache interval. Consequently training and publication do not require a browser-extension release or remote execution of the user's prompt. The refresh mechanism does not guarantee instantaneous fleet-wide synchronization; experiment records must identify the actual snapshot used by each runtime.

\subsection{Model Update Service}

The Python update service requests fuzzer cycles when enabled and validates submitted model-bound deltas. It checks the configured model identifier, exact base version, allowed trainable tensors, shapes, finite values and magnitude limits, then applies the bounded deltas and atomically publishes a revised artifact. It does not perform backpropagation; that computation has already occurred in the runtime. The artifact revision increments as \texttt{release+train.N}, and durable receipts bind a request identity to its committed outcome so that an identical retry does not apply the update twice. A stale base or conflicting request reuse is rejected. The explicit full-Tiny strategy admits exact validated shapes up to 24,576 values per tensor and 65,536 total; legacy artifacts retain the 4,096 per-tensor limit. Full encoder deltas form one atomic artifact publication rather than separately committed tensor fragments.

Training and publication are centralized server operations. The architecture therefore supports cloud-side retraining and distribution while retaining local user inference, but does not implement federated learning or privately trained user gradients. Historical offline encoder-training measurements remain separate from this later online publication contract. The implemented capability and its local mechanical validation do not establish that it has been deployed to a cloud VM or improved detection quality.

\subsection{Cloud Training and Client Refresh Cycle}

The complete update path is: labeled examples to the fuzzer; bounded training/held-out batches to the server runtime; candidate deltas and metrics back to the fuzzer; eligible deltas to the updater; an atomic artifact revision to streaming; and refreshed weights to client runtimes. User prompt inspection follows a separate path from the browser to the workstation runtime and then, on an allowed request, to the hosted LLM. This separation is the mechanism that permits adaptation over successive cloud-side training cycles. Whether a given cycle improves detection is evaluated against its fixed base and held-out data, rather than inferred from the existence of a new version.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Research Questions and Measurement Units}

We investigate three questions: what each detection layer contributes; whether controlled cloud-side updates improve detection without unacceptable regressions; and whether browser enforcement matches the returned policy action. Client cost and telemetry utility are separate supporting questions. Annotation-presence detection is measured per prompt, contextual sensitivity/visibility/categories are separate label dimensions, action correctness depends on the configured policy, and prevention is measured by whether the original request reaches a receiver. These outcomes are not combined into a single privacy-accuracy score.

\subsection{Data Provenance, Labels and Partition Use}

The initial public binary study uses English PIIMB sentences at revision \texttt{4a13e9ffe6fd0d275efbde8afd4d8d8f1ffc2133}. Selection uses seed 3102026, exact-text deduplication, conflict exclusion and source-document grouping. The selected 1,000 rows are split into 502 development rows (264 annotation-positive, 238 annotation-negative) and 498 protected final rows (236 positive, 262 negative), with disjoint source groups. An additional reused validation partition contains 968 rows (475 positive, 493 negative). Its exact preparation and artifact commitments are retained with each later study.

For the binary metric, a valid source PII annotation defines a positive reference. A prediction is positive when the returned classification has S1--S3 or any privacy category, regardless of action. This proxy does not establish whether a prompt is contextually private, whether every span was found, or whether a warning/block is appropriate. Positive-only external sources supply recall evidence and cannot independently establish specificity. Absence of a source annotation is not silently converted into independently verified contextual harmlessness.

Synthetic development examples and authored contextual contrasts provide controlled checks, with their shared template/family structure retained. The separate 48-case contextual fixture comprises 24 controls, 17 disclosure candidates and seven ambiguous exclusions; 41 cases enter quantitative rubric analysis. Its labels remain assistant-provisional pending independent human review and adjudication. Agreement between two human annotators is not claimed. Public/private visibility and desired action require explicit supplied context rather than inference from a topic keyword alone.

Validation and development have informed repeated exploratory studies. A prospectively frozen choice within a later study does not make those reused data untouched confirmation. Training exclusions track groups, row identifiers and normalized text, and known overlap in a historical trained control limits that comparison to diagnostics. Training on unused source documents from PIIMB's upstream \texttt{test} corpus creates a custom within-corpus experiment, not an untouched official test score. The protected final partition remains unscored and is unavailable for fitting, threshold choice or error-driven revision.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The figures expose why update mechanics, detection quality and privacy protection require distinct conclusions. Figure~\ref{fig:layer_ablation} shows that the original pipeline's high recall coincided with low specificity. Figure~\ref{fig:update_outcomes} shows that successful computation and publication did not ensure validation eligibility or complete retention. These are controlled study outcomes, not a measured learning trajectory over successive production months. They support evaluating each revision against its fixed base and checking semantic and pipeline behavior separately.

The cascade illustrates a substantial reduction in annotation-negative detections, accompanied by additional missed positives (Figure~\ref{fig:cascade_errors}). The efficient gate removes 122 false positives and adds five false negatives relative to its ordinary control; those outcomes cannot be collapsed into a context-independent benefit. A missed credential and an unnecessary warning can carry different costs, while the binary dataset does not supply those costs or independent action truth. The provisional fixture preserves two required-BLOCK failures, so fewer negative flags alone cannot justify enabling the gate by default.

More training data also did not yield uniform improvements (Figure~\ref{fig:external_specificity}). Expanded profiles detected every sampled positive source-heldout example, yet validation specificity decreased for efficient and showed mixed, selection-conditioned changes for the other profiles. Source style, positive-only evaluation, provisional contextual targets and representation limits remain competing explanations to investigate. The present evidence motivates independently reviewed supervision and matched representation controls; it does not establish that any particular extension will produce sustained cloud adaptation.

The accelerated normal-batch experiment (Section~\ref{sec:accelerated_results}) supplies a direct iteration decision for the tested revised package: measurable changes did not meet the frozen promising rule. Its efficient and balanced tradeoffs, quality publication plateau and absent disclosure joint correctness support closing this experiment and reporting a bounded negative result rather than repeating the same schedule without a new hypothesis. They do not establish that adaptation is generally ineffective, that the LLM layer is ready for deployment, or that the conversational prompt revision improved accuracy. Tiny inference and training do not consume that prompt.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Task and Label Validity}

The principal public labels measure annotation presence rather than contextual privacy truth. A public entity can carry a valid annotation, and an unannotated prompt can still contain sensitive context. Binary recall and specificity therefore cannot establish correct sensitivity, visibility, category, action, masking or complete span recovery. The observed specificity burden remains relevant under the declared task; annotation mismatch does not justify dismissing all false positives. The 48-case authored fixture uses assistant-provisional labels, excludes seven ambiguous cases and awaits independent human adjudication. Relative retention of 15/17 minimum private actions leaves two required-BLOCK failures visible. The separate 64-row assessment uses assistant-provisional targets in 32 paired groups, with shared semantic archetypes across training and assessment; withheld IDs and normalized text do not establish broad semantic-family generalization. The revised curriculum changes wording, visibility and category exposure together, preventing attribution to semantic diversity alone.

\subsection{Adaptation and Representation}

The six contextual grids completed on 6 October produced 126 attempts and no retained qualifier under their respective criteria. A separate six-hour combined-curriculum study showed mixed changes on binary development outcomes, including a 25-point semantic recall loss for balanced and a 6.06-point full-pipeline recall loss despite higher specificity. Nine separate presence-head updates also failed their strict specificity-improvement gate. These findings do not establish that cloud training cannot improve a privacy detector; they constrain claims about the executed curricula, objectives, representations and update budgets. The contextual encoder originates in seeded initialization, and head-only updates train 660 parameters while freezing the representation. Short contexts, few sensitivity-positive training examples, provisional targets and heterogeneous loss scales limit interpretation. In that six-hour study, a single trajectory per profile, a finite reused curriculum, one fixed publication guard and the lack of a matched old-sampler control leave training variation and curriculum contributions unresolved. Local-SGD observations do not support clipping as the limiting factor for accepted heads, but do not settle other optimization or data explanations.

The accelerated experiment uses repeated exposure to a fixed pool and the same guard. Its revised curriculum and seeded allocation form a combined package, preventing attribution to wording, visibility/category coverage or allocation alone. One deterministic control per profile supports conditional revised-seed comparisons, not independent control replication; source-group/scenario intervals do not measure control-seed uncertainty. Compressing hourly waits does not simulate real deployment aging or isolate the effect of update frequency. Its numeric rejection audit reconstructs gate predicates from reported service metrics and commits candidate identities; it does not independently recompute rejected-candidate metrics from tensors. The original raw audit binds the raw summary, while the publication manifest separately binds the public aggregate projection.

The pretrained study has only ten assessment families with sixteen correlated descendants each. Assistant-provisional targets, formulaic framing and shared abstract contexts limit the result to synthetic family-heldout transfer. Schema checks and split-disjoint wording/entities bound some shortcuts but cannot establish their absence. The new disclosure ontology cannot be pooled with historical topic-category exactness. The package confounds pretraining with representation dimension, tokenizer and normalization; matched head optimization does not isolate capacity. Reused secondary data are guards, not fresh independent evaluation. The observed child-care and credential regressions prohibit a safety or promotion claim despite aggregate improvement.

The all-surface protocol in Section~\ref{sec:all_surfaces_methods} remains prospective here: source mechanics establish neither completed fitting nor quality gains. Fresh assistant-provisional transfer targets need independent adjudication, and their disclosure ontology cannot be pooled with historical topic-category exactness. Reused secondary fixtures replay text without stored audience hints, so their action labels are transfer targets rather than fresh contextual truth. Exact overlap exclusions do not establish mechanism independence. Scratch and sparse arms use different native training cohorts, capacities and budgets; deterministic sparse repetitions are not independent seed corroboration. Frozen MiniLM versus projected Tiny features compares representation packages rather than isolating pretraining or model size. Fixed budgets, immutable-baseline scoring and execution assertions support an auditable comparison, but do not establish generalization, conversational-prompt effects or deployment readiness before actual accepted results.

\subsection{Selection, Contamination and Generalization}

Development and validation have informed repeated exploratory research. Even prospectively specified later grids reuse those partitions, so small changes and bootstrap intervals remain descriptive. Historical training overlap with validation limits the trained-control cascade branch to diagnostics. Threshold search and profile/C selection condition the reported comparisons; repeated seeds, profiles and RPCs are not independent prompts. The initial frozen rules, revised-rule original control and later trained checkpoints differ and cannot be pooled to imply progress. The amended semantic-only criterion changed after fifteen observed cells, and the offline S0/nonS0 breakdown was post-observation; neither is untouched confirmation. Final performance remains unknown.

The PIIMB studies use a custom within-corpus split, not an untouched official benchmark test. Source-family balance is uneven, tiny strata can lack a class, and source style may help binary separation. External source-heldout diagnostics contain positives only and synthetic or machine-generated text; their perfect observed detection cannot establish specificity, population-level perfect recall or real-clinical-record performance. Meddies metadata families are coarse proxies for independence. English data and authored examples do not represent all languages, organizations or deployed privacy decisions. Added positive data produced mixed validation-specificity effects, including a substantial decrease for the efficient sparse profile.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Monitoring}

A trusted, enabled client and recognized request path are required. WARN forwards original sensitive content; unsupported transports, unmatched bodies, attachments and deliberate hook tampering remain outside coverage. Partial-layer fallback can reduce detection coverage, while blocking unavailable analysis imposes an availability cost. The eight-case controlled page-hook fixture does not establish the complete installed extension/native-messaging path. The incomplete installed-client attempt and detector-only elapsed times cannot establish representative end-to-end latency, memory, reliability or usability.

Telemetry's nominal event-tuple guarantee excludes report presence, exact accepted counts, transport identifiers and training data. It assumes public input-independent configuration, compliant randomization, retained ledger state and a trusted clock. Exact finite-sampler privacy loss, aggregate utility and reporting-selection effects remain unverified. The telemetry mechanism is independent of model training and supplies no evidence that live user behavior improves the classifier.

\subsection{Ethics and Artifact Availability}

Controlled request-capture probes use invented identifiers rather than private user prompts. Generated, source-provided and assistant-provisional labels remain distinguished from independently reviewed contextual truth. Nemotron and Meddies source attribution and applicable restrictions must accompany reuse; Meddies' non-commercial license constrains redistribution. Text-bearing inputs, clinical examples and fitted vocabularies require appropriate handling even when aggregate reports omit identifiers.

The repository records protocols, aggregate reports, source/image identities, model commitments, attempted updates and restoration checks. Recorded audits support computational integrity within their stated scope, while clean-room reproduction, final release packaging and applicable license review remain unfinished. Protected final prompts and labels have not been used in the reported exploratory analyses. AI-assisted editing and provisional example construction require author verification and disclosure under the selected venue's policy.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\privoke implements a cloud-training and guarded parameter-publication path for a locally executed prompt-privacy classifier. The server runtime computes bounded updates from labeled fuzzer batches, the updater validates and publishes revisions, and client runtimes refresh streamed parameters. Completed studies demonstrate that this path changes model parameters and can publish accepted revisions; successful transport and training do not establish better detection.

The exploratory evidence exposes that distinction. The original layered detector attained high annotation-presence recall with substantial false positives. Across six contextual grids completed on 6 October, none of 126 attempts yielded a retained candidate under the study-specific rules. A separate six-hour synthetic-curriculum study produced mixed binary endpoint changes: balanced semantic recall fell by 25 points, and pipeline recall fell by 6.06 points as pipeline specificity rose to 36.55\%. Efficient pipeline specificity ended at 27.31\%, and quality at 18.91\%; quality's one accepted update changed none of the sampled predictions. The fixed, reused gate and single trajectory per profile do not establish generalization or training-seed variation. A historical public-negative update improved specificity while losing recall, and an additional accepted cycle crossed below the recall floor. Separate fitted sparse models and the presence cascade substantially reduced annotation-negative detections, but their binary task, validation-based selection and provisional action checks prevent a conclusion about contextual privacy safety or sustained adaptation.

The completed semantic-only matrix adds a controlled curriculum/sampler comparison and a separate matched encoder experiment, with zero qualifiers. Its specificity and contextual-control gains coexist with annotation-presence recall loss and fixture harms; no disclosure achieved joint correctness in the offline assessment. These findings establish audited execution of the update mechanisms, not promotion or contextual safety.

The separate accelerated study completed 2,016 normal-batch attempts with 1,270 publications and 746 guard rejections, but no revised realization met its prespecified promising criterion and no cell qualified. Relative to shared controls, specificity/recall tradeoffs and a quality publication plateau coexisted with zero disclosure category-set exact and joint correctness at every checkpoint. We therefore close this experiment as a negative and tradeoff result for the tested package; additional improvement work requires a distinct hypothesis and fresh evaluation, rather than a claim of readiness or sustained learning.

The subsequent frozen pretrained package improved provisional disclosure joint agreement, including 19/80 serious rows versus zero for each matched random control, but failed its serious-action veto. Poor reused-development specificity and a distinct credential-action regression reinforce the negative qualification decision. The optional runtime and separate 512-token functional extension are engineering results; neither establishes deployment readiness. A useful next study requires independently reviewed contextual labels and casewise policy coverage, rather than tuning on these assessment failures.

The practical implication is that cloud training makes adaptation possible while the usefulness of each revision must be measured against its fixed base and retained coverage. Further work should address representation and independently reviewed contextual supervision, alongside complete installed-client measurement. Protected final scoring, human adjudication, cascade uncertainty and telemetry utility remain TODO; the present conclusion applies to the documented exploratory studies.

\section{Extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

\subsection{TODO: Full-Encoder and Contextual Training}

A bounded offline fitter and completed matched comparison support training the repository-owned encoder and heads end to end against a head-only Adam control with the same data, initialization and evaluation boundary. Validated exports and isolated research loading enabled the measured tradeoffs in Section~\ref{sec:curriculum_results}; those results do not establish independently reviewed generalization or justify promotion. The subsequent versioned online full-Tiny contract now admits exact full tensor shapes and complete atomic candidate publication; separate sequential head/full stages are not an atomic pair. That implementation does not change the historical results or satisfy the pending reviewed-data quality study. Teacher-model paraphrases, external generation and hard-case mining were not tested in this matrix and require separately frozen experiments.

Contextual sensitivity, visibility, categories and minimum actions need independently reviewed labels rather than conversion from annotation presence. Freeze example families, target construction, budgets and regression criteria before fitting; include casewise clean interventions and required-BLOCK failures. Future LLM-layer comparisons must isolate the semantic component and verify actual layer execution. Full-pipeline measurements belong to separately authorized product or detector analyses so other layers cannot conceal semantic regressions.

\subsection{TODO: Reviewed Data and Independent Generalization}

The pinned AdvPIIBench structural/protection scan is complete, but broad whole-prompt review, quota-feasible grouped partitions, fitting and scoring remain pending. Native empty identifier annotations do not establish that a whole prompt is harmless. Any clean-data extension must preserve the full protected union, keep template/transformation families together and stop before fitting if independently reviewed labels and disjoint quotas cannot be obtained. Source-heldout evaluation should include both positive and negative examples and broader language/domain coverage. The original final partition must remain protected until the selected configuration and analysis are frozen.

\subsection{TODO: Deployment, Uncertainty and Monitoring}

Complete the installed-browser/native-messaging enforcement and outage matrix, then measure cold/warm end-to-end latency and resource distributions on declared hardware. Compute the pending source-group paired cascade intervals without treating reused development estimates as confirmatory evidence. Telemetry studies should measure estimator bias/error and reporting-selection effects under the declared privacy mechanism, while separately auditing finite-randomness implementation and budget persistence.

\subsection{Additional Design Directions}

Automated request-body masking, pruning, federated learning and telemetry-guided example selection remain possible design directions. Automatic masking requires verified body/span reconstruction and a separate utility comparison with current ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK behavior. Telemetry-guided training needs an explicit label or learning signal and its own privacy analysis; aggregate categorical counts do not provide corrected labels. None of these directions is an implemented consequence of the current cloud updater or an assumed solution to the measured detection failures.
