\begin{abstract}
Web-hosted language models rely on user-supplied context, but the same information that makes a response useful may disclose personal or organizational information to an external provider. We present \privoke, an implemented client-side privacy firewall that inspects supported prompts locally and applies an allow, warn or block decision before transmission. A separate training service computes detector updates from supplied labelled examples, checks their version and retention constraints, and distributes accepted parameters to clients without an application release. Locally randomized telemetry follows an independent reporting path and does not supply training data. We evaluate the system through three questions concerning detection and enforcement, retention during detector updates, and systems and privacy overhead. Updated packages improved agreement on an authored contextual workload, but action failures remained and specificity on a separate annotation-presence task was only 13.36 to 21.76\%. In a matched continuation study, every full-model candidate failed the retention guard despite improving aggregate agreement. Installed-browser probes confirmed the declared request behavior on the tested paths, while cost measurements and telemetry audits exposed limits in attribution and estimation. The results establish an auditable protection and update lifecycle, with independent contextual accuracy, useful adaptation and practical user benefit still requiring further evidence.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Prompt privacy, local inference, request enforcement, guarded model updates, local differential privacy.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Web-hosted large language models support writing, coding and analysis by processing the instructions and contextual material supplied in a prompt~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing}. In practical use, this context may include a person's health information, a colleague's details or confidential organizational material. Users can therefore disclose sensitive information while pursuing an otherwise legitimate task, as reported workplace incidents illustrate~\cite{SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Provider-side policies and controls influence how submitted information is handled, but they operate after the content has reached the provider~\cite{Athey2017,barbera2025ai,King2025}. Inspecting an outgoing request on the user's device creates an earlier opportunity to intervene, before that disclosure occurs.

This opportunity introduces several distinct technical questions. Recognizing a name or identifier does not establish whether its disclosure is authorized, because the decision also depends on the sensitivity of the information and its intended audience. A detector's output must then produce an observable request action: displaying a warning and preventing transmission have different consequences. Maintaining the detector adds a further difficulty, since an update that improves average accuracy can lose protection on a previously correct case. Finally, collecting information about these decisions creates its own disclosure channel. \privoke addresses these concerns through a common system design that connects local inspection and explicit enforcement with guarded updates and separately randomized telemetry.

\privoke represents a prompt using contextual sensitivity, intended audience and information categories, and maps those findings to ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. The extension forwards the original request body for ALLOW and WARN, whereas BLOCK cancels a supported request. WARN attempts to display a notice but does not wait for acknowledgement, so its presence establishes neither consent nor prevented disclosure. In the configuration evaluated here, prompt inspection runs on the workstation. A distinct server-side runtime trains candidates from supplied labelled examples, and an update service validates each candidate against its exact base before publishing accepted parameters. Clients verify the distributed artifact and refresh their detector without rebuilding the extension. Neither intercepted user prompts nor telemetry supplies training labels.

Local prompt protection has substantial prior precedent. Casper combines rules, entity recognition and topic detection in a browser extension~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}; Hide and Seek trains local models to hide and recover sensitive information~\cite{HaS2024}; and ProSan trains a local sanitizer to balance privacy hiding with task utility~\cite{ProSan2024}. Our contribution builds on these established approaches by implementing and evaluating a version-bound detector lifecycle together with explicit request actions. The evaluation follows a candidate from its fixed base through retention assessment and publication, preserving both gains and regressions. This makes the conditions for accepting a revision inspectable, while avoiding the assumption that a passing check guarantees safe adaptation beyond the cases it covers.

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1: Does PriVoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively?}
\item \textbf{RQ2: Can PriVoke update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior?}
\item \textbf{RQ3: What systems and privacy overhead does PriVoke introduce?}
\end{enumerate}
Together, these questions connect contextual findings to request actions, test aggregate improvement against casewise retention, and examine the resources and information released by protection itself. The following sections explain the implemented mechanisms before presenting the detection, update and overhead evidence. This organization keeps the system's operational capabilities distinct from the accuracy and utility that require empirical support.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local protection and sanitization.} Casper provides a direct precedent for layered browser-local inspection, combining rules, entity detection and topic analysis~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024}. Its prototype replaces identifiers with placeholders and supports user-triggered restoration in responses, while topic warnings require acknowledgement. Users trigger analysis through a keyboard shortcut; this interaction should not be conflated with automatic interception of every outgoing request. Casper also permits custom rules and topics and compatible model replacement, so it is not accurately described as uniformly static.

Hide and Seek (HaS) modifies the prompt before cloud inference and recovers hidden response entities locally~\cite{HaS2024}. Its generative variant trains hiding and recovery models, while the label-based variant reuses an entity recognizer for hiding; trained attackers assess how much of the original text they recover. ProSan uses task-importance and privacy proxies to guide selective word replacement, then distils a sanitizer for local execution~\cite{ProSan2024}. These systems establish learned local protection as well as local processing. PromptShield addresses a related enterprise problem through role/policy classification~\cite{PromptShield2025}.

\textit{A different measured contract.} PriVoke acts directly on the outgoing request: BLOCK cancels it on a supported path, while ALLOW and WARN release its original body. Masked spans returned by the detector remain evidence and are not substituted into traffic; there is also no response-recovery stage. The system's distinctive engineering focus is the lifecycle connecting a candidate tied to an exact model revision with guarded publication and verified client refresh. We evaluate that lifecycle using paired gains and losses, including candidates that the guard refuses. A separate telemetry mechanism limits the contents of randomized event reports without treating those reports as training feedback. This focus supports a bounded systems contribution, rather than a claim of uniquely local inference or superior sanitization.

\textit{Comparison boundaries.} The reviewed systems measure different aspects of protection. Casper evaluates entity and topic detection; HaS measures resistance to whole-text recovery alongside downstream task proxies; ProSan measures replacement of privacy items and automatic task utility. These outcomes cannot be placed on one privacy-accuracy scale, because their targets and denominators differ. Appendix~\ref{app:interaction} retains the version-qualified results and interaction details. Our executed external references use Presidio with disclosed native entity-recognizer configurations~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026}. They are component references, rather than reproductions of Casper, HaS or ProSan, and do not support a matched claim of system superiority.

\section{System Design and Privacy Boundary}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
\privoke inspects supported outgoing prompts to reduce inadvertent disclosure of personal, third-party or organizational information. The protection boundary includes the workstation, enabled extension, local runtime and supervisor, and authenticated model publisher. The system consequently assumes that these components operate as configured. A compromised client, a malicious publisher or page code that deliberately replaces the interception hooks lies outside that assumption; information already held by a provider is also outside the outgoing-request boundary.

The extension recognizes prompt POST formats submitted through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest}. This coverage does not automatically extend to workers, attachments, other transports or changed endpoints, each of which requires separate validation. Disabling protection bypasses inspection by design. An optional hosted-inference configuration also changes the disclosure boundary because it sends text beyond the workstation, and it is excluded from the local-inference measurements reported here.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates the system into three data paths. In the first, supported prompt text passes from the browser to the workstation detector and is released to the hosted LLM only when the request action permits it. In the second, supplied labelled examples enter the training service, and accepted model parameters return to the local detector. In the third, coarse event fields are randomized locally and sent to a telemetry collector that has no training input role. Keeping these paths distinct identifies what each service receives and prevents model maintenance from requiring collection of intercepted prompts. It does not, by itself, establish that the resulting privacy decisions are correct.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
The local inspection path begins when a page hook extracts the prompt from a supported outgoing request. The extension background process passes this text through a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to the Python runtime on the workstation. A supervisor started through native messaging manages the bridge and runtime lifecycle. The server-side runtime used for training is a separate instance and is not a fallback destination for local user inference. After analysis, the returned action controls whether the hook releases or cancels the original request, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:enforcement}.

Before detection, the runtime normalizes the input while retaining offsets that map evidence back to the original text. Its default cascade evaluates regular-expression rules first, followed by enabled named entity recognition (NER) and the semantic classifier. A rule-based BLOCK stops the cascade before later detectors run. Otherwise, the runtime combines the findings and retains the strongest action, so a less restrictive semantic prediction does not override a stronger rule finding.

Failure handling distinguishes unavailable analysis from loss of the parameter stream. A failed requested layer blocks an otherwise ALLOW result, while valid non-ALLOW findings remain visible. If analysis itself is unavailable, the extension blocks supported requests. A parameter-stream outage, however, can leave the remaining enabled rules and NER layers operating. Such fallback reduces semantic coverage and therefore need not block every full-pipeline request; the actual layers that execute are part of the evidence needed to interpret the decision.

\caption{Pre-transmission enforcement on supported hooks. WARN forwards unchanged content after attempting a notice; it does not wait for user acknowledgement. Analysis failure and reduced-layer stream fallback are distinct conditions. Disabled protection and unsupported traffic lie outside this decision path.}

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic classifier describes a prompt along three dimensions: sensitivity, ranging from none to highly sensitive; visibility, ranging from public to personal-private or unspecified; and a set of ten information categories, including health, identity and location. These dimensions distinguish the presence of information from the context in which it is disclosed. A deterministic policy then converts the labels and returned confidence into a request action. Highly sensitive content normally maps to BLOCK and potentially harmful personal information to WARN, while combinations involving identity and location can increase the restriction. Low confidence can moderate the action. These are implemented policy choices, rather than independent evidence of authorization or calibrated risk; Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives their exact mapping.

The evaluated semantic models are classifiers, rather than language models that generate conversational responses. We compare historical small classifiers with later packages that retain pretrained BERT encoders and poolers~\cite{googleBertL4H128A2,googleBertL2H256A4,googleBertL12H256A4,turc2019WellReadStudents}. Each generation is offered in efficient, balanced and quality packages. The names identify configurations and do not imply a universal ordering of performance. Because tokenizers, capacities, thresholds and training histories also differ between the native packages, their comparison measures the combined package change rather than isolating pretraining or architecture.

\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
The update path separates the computation of a candidate from permission to replace the model on which it was based. A training controller constructs bounded labelled batches and held-out guard cases, with protected rows, texts and groups excluded from training. The server runtime fetches the specified model, performs training and returns parameter changes and candidate metrics tied to that base. The updater then checks exact base identity, permitted trainable tensors and shapes, finite values, magnitude limits and the publication guards configured for the strategy (Figure~\ref{fig:updates}). These checks serve different purposes: artifact checks establish that the update is well formed and belongs to the expected model, whereas retention checks test its behavior on declared cases. Successful computation or transport alone establishes neither accuracy nor protection.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{architecture-updates.pdf}
\caption{A candidate is evaluated before publication. Compare-and-swap rejects stale/conflicting bases; durable receipts prevent an identical retry from applying twice. A passing retention guard covers its declared cases and criteria only. Head and underlying-model stages are separate publications, so a later rejection need not undo an earlier accepted head stage.}
\label{fig:updates}
\end{figure}

Publication uses an atomic compare-and-swap operation: the candidate is committed only if the currently published base still matches its declared parent. Durable replay receipts bind repeated submissions to the same artifact outcome, preventing an identical retry from applying the update twice. Head and underlying-model stages remain separate publications, so rejection of a later stage does not undo an earlier accepted head update. The streaming service supplies versioned parameters, and clients verify artifact identities before refreshing their cached weights without an application release.

This implemented path supports centralized training from supplied examples while preserving local user inference. It does not collect user gradients or implement federated learning, and its existence is not evidence of cloud deployment. RQ2 therefore evaluates whether particular candidates deserve retention separately from the mechanical ability to compute, publish and receive them.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
The telemetry path reports five coarse event fields: action, an action/sensitivity risk bucket, one category selected by fixed precedence, a bounded release label and a four-hour time-of-day bin without a date. Each field is randomized on the workstation before release. Packets exclude raw prompts, identifiers, exact confidence scores and per-layer findings, although the retained fields still describe aspects of an event. The collector has no role in supplying corrected labels or training inputs; telemetry and detector learning remain separate processes.

For an event budget $\varepsilon$, each of five fields receives $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. A field with $K_i$ possible values reports truth with probability
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}
\end{equation}
for each alternative. With independent ideal samples and common public configuration, the product of the field likelihood ratios bounds an event tuple by $e^\varepsilon$. The implementation uses finite arithmetic, so agreement with this ideal expression requires a separate numerical audit. A persistent local ledger reserves budget before constructing a report, with defaults of one per event and eight per UTC day; failed construction or delivery does not refund the reservation. This accounting depends on retained state and a trusted clock. It bounds released content under its assumptions, while report presence, contribution selection, delivery and network metadata remain outside the guarantee. The collector estimates marginal counts by inverting the nominal response probabilities and clipping the results, which introduces a separate estimation cost examined in RQ3.

\textit{Contextual policies.} The authored workload contains 1,560 cases, of which 810 are sensitive and 750 clean, with 260 per audience class. Sensitivity, audience, category and allowed-action targets were constructed provisionally with assistant support and have not undergone independent human adjudication. They therefore support measurements of agreement with the authored policy, rather than independently established privacy or authorization accuracy. For binary scoring, nonzero sensitivity or any category defines a positive. Semantic joint correctness requires exact agreement on all three label dimensions, while product joint correctness additionally requires an allowed action. We record under-restriction when an action falls below the allowed-action set and over-restriction when it exceeds that set. Receiver-observed transmission is measured separately from both forms of policy agreement.

The workload comprises 35 scenario families and 24 audience-expression groups. It was sealed for an earlier semantic comparison, then opened and reused in the product and recognizer studies. Those later results are consequently PriVoke-authored development evidence, even when their comparison procedures were fixed in advance. Repeated passes check whether the same inputs produce the same outputs; they do not increase the independent sample. Separate paired resampling by scenario family or audience expression describes sensitivity to these dependencies without certifying population coverage.

\textit{Annotation-based checks.} The once-only final endpoint contained 498 English rows from PIIMB revision \texttt{4a13e9f}, comprising 236 annotation-positive and 262 annotation-negative targets~\cite{PIIMBPinned2026}. Models, source inputs and scoring were frozen before its release, and no tuning, reselection or promotion followed the results. This endpoint tests whether a prompt contains a source annotation. It is distinct both from contextual authorization and from PIIMB's official character-masking/F2 task. The 464 observed source-group keys describe grouping conventions and do not certify template independence. A separate reference used 2,000 AdvPIIBench rows: 1,000 injected positives, 750 ordinary negatives and 250 hard negatives~\cite{arpaly2026advpiibench}. Its official source categories are not exhaustive privacy annotations. Appendix~\ref{app:protocols} therefore retains the selection rules, grouping and prediction proxies needed to interpret each check.

\subsection{Comparison and Retention Protocols}
We evaluated historical and updated native packages on the same contextual cases through the product handler, holding rules and policy unchanged. This pairing exposes gains and losses on individual cases, but differences between the packages still prevent attribution to one model property. A narrower ablation removed individual detectors from the balanced package while fixing texts, weights and policy. We also ran configured small and large English Presidio models as same-text entity-recognizer references at threshold 0.5~\cite{PresidioNlpConfiguration2026,SpacyLarge371Release2026}. These references do not produce the contextual labels and actions required by our complete policy, so those unsupported outputs were left unmeasured.

For RQ2, a fixed-base experiment compared head-only and full-model continuation across three seeds. Each treatment began with byte-identical intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler weights and zero task heads, received the same ordered 4,096 training exposures over 64 steps, and was assessed on the same 1,080 reused provisional cases after complete training/guard group and text exclusions. Only full-model continuation enabled encoder optimization, including the resulting changes to joint-gradient clipping. We assessed every terminal candidate, including guard failures, without using assessment results to select a checkpoint.

The 16-case retention guard required improved joint correctness and zero action harms against its derived target actions. The broader 1,080-case assessment instead permitted its declared sets of allowed actions. Thus, a guard outcome and an assessment score refer to different cases and action criteria. The small guard and weak zero-head control limit what either a pass or an improvement establishes. Appendices~\ref{app:lineage} and~\ref{app:protocols} retain the recipes and historical unsuccessful branches.

\subsection{Enforcement, Cost and Telemetry Protocols}
The installed-browser study exercised the built extension in Linux Chromium through the standard native-host installer, native messaging, supervisor, bridge and historical balanced detector. An HTTPS synthetic receiver recorded arriving original bodies and checked for late sends. Thirty-three correctness probes tested forwarding, blocking, faults, cancellation and recovery. They used the synthetic receiver rather than an authenticated LLM provider, so they establish behavior along declared request paths. Full-pipeline and sole-semantic execution were checked separately outside the resource-measurement windows.

The cost experiment requested rules and NER and measured 360 decisions across twelve fresh profiles, with two profiles for each transport/action cell. Page-observed latency ended at the analysis result and excluded the receiver response. CPU and sampled charged memory were measured for the whole container, including resident infrastructure, cache and the observer. Because the experiment supplied neither an unprotected baseline nor attribution to individual processes, these measurements describe the instrumented system rather than incremental client overhead. They also exclude semantic-layer cost. Appendix~\ref{app:browser_protocol} specifies the observation windows and sampling boundaries.

The telemetry study combined a mechanism audit with separate utility and accounting checks. The audit enumerated finite sampling kernels for one fingerprinted host build under the assumption of independent uniform OS bits. Fixed-contributor simulations measured the error introduced by estimation, while real ledger checks exercised persistence, concurrency and failure accounting. Together, these procedures examine a conditional privacy mechanism and synthetic utility; they do not constitute a deployed monitoring study.

\section{RQ1: Detection and Enforcement}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Contextual Policy Agreement}
\label{sec:pipeline_comparison_results}
The updated packages agreed more often with the authored contextual targets than their historical counterparts (Table~\ref{tab:pipeline_v1_v3}). All six product runs completed without failed calls or layer errors, and joint correctness increased by 27.37--41.86 percentage points. A common restriction pattern arose from the cascade rather than differences among the updated semantic models. All updated packages over-restricted the same 139 cases. Of these, 109 clean controls received a rule-based BLOCK, which prevented later detectors from running, and thirty low-risk controls received a rule-based WARN. \label{sec:detector_ablation}
The balanced-package ablation tested the contribution of these rules while holding the remaining configuration fixed. Removing them increased allowed-action agreement from 1,409 to 1,530 and joint agreement from 717 to 972. It corrected all 139 shared unnecessary restrictions, but also lost eighteen previously correct actions; under-restricted cases increased from twelve to thirty. Thus, the 121 net action gains conceal additional lost protection under the provisional policy targets. Removing NER left full-product outputs unchanged on this fixture, consistently with the absence of converted findings. Neither result justifies universally disabling a detector, since both depend on this workload and policy. All 10,920 ablation calls retained valid execution records.

\label{sec:external_comparison}
The external source-category check showed another recall/specificity tradeoff under a different response proxy. Updated balanced recall was 92.9\%, compared with historical 63.0\%, but source-negative specificity was 0.5\%, compared with 48.6\%. This check counted any semantic finding as positive and used labels for injected identifier categories. Because those labels do not exhaustively describe privacy throughout the prompt, the 0.5\% specificity cannot be converted into an established 99.5\% broad-privacy false-alarm rate. It does show poor agreement with the declared source target. Restricted five-type Presidio exhibited the opposite tradeoff, with 99.1\% specificity and only 3.9\% recall. All 6,000 model/recognizer analyses completed without errors, and no threshold or row was changed afterwards.

The same-text recognizer comparison further illustrates why model size and aggregate scores are insufficient for ranking privacy systems. On the authored contextual cases, small-English Presidio achieved 45.31\% recall and 71.87\% specificity. Its larger configuration reduced recall to 29.63\% while increasing specificity to 88.80\%. Both classified 906 of 1,560 cases correctly, but the larger model gained 192 correct cases and lost 192 others. Updated product packages achieved recall of 99.01--100.00\% and specificity of 83.87--85.47\% under the contextual targets. The entity/contextual task mismatch and PriVoke-authored data prevent interpreting this advantage as a general privacy ranking (Appendix~\ref{app:reference_detail}).

\subsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
The completed installed-browser capture connected request decisions to receiver observations. Across thirty-three declared correctness probes, the receiver observed eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes. Runtime-call correlation and checks for late arrivals supported these outcomes without a decision broker, while healthy full-pipeline and sole-semantic probes verified actual execution.

The fault probes also made the limits of fallback visible. Interrupting the parameter stream blocked semantic-only requests, but the configured full-mode fallback executed rules and NER and forwarded the request. Restarting restored full execution. Disabling protection or all layers forwarded requests by design. These observations distinguish loss of semantic coverage from complete analysis failure; they do not establish that fallback preserves the original protection. The completed capture remains separate from nine earlier failed captures, which were not pooled with it.

\textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke detected many provisionally sensitive prompts and enforced the declared request contract on the tested installed paths. Its effectiveness is nevertheless conditional: action errors remained, WARN transmitted the original content, and separate presence/category tasks exposed weak specificity. The receiver probes establish bounded enforcement, while independent contextual accuracy, provider compatibility, prevention on every sensitive prompt and practical usability remain unestablished.
\section{RQ2: Updates and Previously Protected Behavior}
\label{sec:adaptation_results}
\subsection{Mechanism Versus Useful Adaptation}
Audited server computation, bounded publication and client refresh established that the implemented path changes served parameters. Whether those changes improve protection is a separate empirical question. Earlier contextual, curriculum and accelerated training studies failed their declared qualification criteria, and a pretrained package that improved provisional disclosure agreement failed a serious-action veto. Appendix~\ref{app:history} retains these negative outcomes and their attempted budgets so that later gains do not obscure unsuccessful adaptation.

Later supervised packages improved authored contextual classification while retaining component and action regressions. The RQ1 comparison measures these resulting packages, not the benefit of automatic online training in isolation. Capacity, tokenizer, threshold and supervision changed together, so the observed difference cannot identify which change caused a gain. The matched continuation study addresses a narrower question by fixing the initial model and training exposure and varying whether the encoder is optimized.

\section{RQ3: Systems and Privacy Overhead}
\subsection{Measured Systems Cost}
The installed-browser cost protocol recorded 360 rules/NER decisions across twelve fresh-profile windows. Median page-observed decision latency ranged from 27.9 to 35.0 ms across transport/action cells; each reported p99 was that cell's observed maximum. All 240 ALLOW/WARN decisions executed both rules and NER. The 120 BLOCK decisions executed rules and legitimately skipped NER, so their timings describe a shorter cascade path. These measurements end before the receiver response and do not include semantic-layer processing.

The summed observation windows covered 126.40 seconds and recorded 193.29 CPU-seconds for the container, including 0.69 observer CPU-seconds. Maximum sampled charged memory was 1,267.91 MiB. These totals include resident services and cache, the driver and the receiver, rather than only the extension and detector. A maximum sampling gap of 622.84 ms also leaves memory spikes potentially unobserved. Final-read and cleanup checks passed, but they do not resolve this attribution limit. Without an unprotected baseline or per-role measurements, the totals describe the instrumented system and cannot isolate incremental PriVoke overhead. Appendix~\ref{app:browser_cost} reports startup intervals and cell tails.

The separate product measurements recorded lower host-call medians and larger server-process peak RSS for updated packages than for historical packages. Their fixed execution order, warming, different backends and cascade shortcuts prevent interpreting the result as a controlled speedup or a model-only cost comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:product_cost}). Training times and allocated CUDA tensor peaks have similarly bounded meanings: they do not establish comparative training speed or whole-process memory requirements.

\subsection{Privacy Cost and Telemetry Utility}
\label{sec:telemetry_results}
The finite-kernel audit found that maximum event privacy loss on the fingerprinted host exceeded nominal budgets 0.5, 1 and 2 by approximately $(1.294,1.503,1.226)\times10^{-15}$. Although the excess is tiny, the exact nominal bound fails for the audited implementation. Eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$ under the same independent uniform-entropy assumptions. Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction} distinguishes this finite calculation from the ideal formula and states the host assumptions. It does not certify other builds or the entropy available in deployment.

The utility simulations exposed a larger practical estimation problem. Inversion followed by clipping produced substantial bias and root-mean-square error, and the separately estimated marginals did not necessarily form a coherent total. For eleven-category reports with event budget one and eighty contributors, a true first-category count of seven had clipped bias +22.84 and RMSE 42.32 counts. Estimated marginals failed to sum to eighty in 99.99\% of trials. These results describe error conditional on the synthetic contributors and estimator; they do not measure population monitoring accuracy or establish an acceptable error threshold for deployment.

Real ledger checks preserved the daily cap across persistent state and concurrent requests. Reservations were still consumed when construction or delivery failed, as required by the accounting rule. However, limiting which events contribute creates a distinct selection effect: reversing a constructed event sequence changed BLOCK participation among the first eight reports from 100\% to 0\%. Randomizing report contents does not correct that selection bias and does not protect whether an event participated.

\textbf{Answer to RQ3.} The tested configuration incurred measurable local processing and infrastructure costs, but the measurements do not isolate incremental overhead or semantic-pipeline cost. Randomized telemetry has a conditional content bound that the finite implementation narrowly exceeds, together with substantial synthetic estimation error. Neither the privacy calculation nor the utility simulation establishes useful deployment monitoring or protection of participation and network metadata.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
The experiments show why the implemented lifecycle requires separate measurements of detection, enforcement and update acceptance. A better semantic classifier cannot reverse a rule-based early BLOCK within the cascade, and a correct WARN still releases the original content. Similarly, improved average candidate performance does not compel publication when a retained protection fails. The design makes these relationships inspectable by tying each action to its execution path and each candidate to its versioned base. Separate randomization limits telemetry content without making it a source of training truth.

Sanitization and recovery systems address additional consequences of intervention. Casper supports acknowledged warnings and local restoration, HaS recovers hidden response entities, and ProSan evaluates task utility after selective replacement. Their functions motivate asking whether a user can still complete an authorized task after protection is applied. PriVoke's current request-action contract does not answer that question: it neither rewrites outgoing content nor restores an answer. Implementing those functions and evaluating their usefulness would require evidence beyond correct request disposition, rather than an inference from local detection alone.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
Independent contextual ground truth remains the largest scientific gap. The sensitivity, audience, category and action targets used here are assistant-provisional; agreement with them does not establish disclosure authorization or human agreement. Annotation presence and injected-category labels provide additional checks, but they answer different questions and cannot replace contextual adjudication. In particular, identifying an entity, deciding whether its disclosure is appropriate, choosing an intervention and measuring task utility each require their own targets. The contrast between high authored clean specificity and poor final annotation-negative specificity shows the limits of the available evidence without justifying dismissal of either task.

Dependence and reuse further constrain generalization. Source-group keys and lexical exclusions track declared relationships, but do not certify template independence, cover paraphrases or remove unknown upstream pretraining exposure. The final endpoint is also source-imbalanced, with 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row. These English synthetic or machine-generated workloads do not represent other languages and organizational policies. Separate cluster sensitivities remain descriptive and unadjusted, while repeated runs and three seeds do not create independent populations. Native-package differences also limit causal attribution. Because the final endpoint has now been opened, further tuning requires a new protected successor rather than reuse of its observed failures.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
A retention guard protects only the behavior represented by its cases and criteria. This limitation is particularly consequential when the guard is small and improvement is measured against a weak zero-head control. The matched experiment retained rejected candidates as observations, not as served improvements, and the earlier negative studies likewise supplied no evidence of useful automatic adaptation. Future studies need common pinned bases, independently reviewed retention cases and explicit casewise harm criteria. Failed candidates and effective artifacts should remain distinguishable so that a successful fit is not mistaken for an accepted model.

Blocking unavailable analysis imposes an availability cost as well as a protection policy. The installed Linux probes verified declared Chromium/native receiver paths using the historical detector, leaving authenticated providers, Windows, updated-model installation, workers, attachments and other transports unverified. Returned confidence is heuristic rather than calibrated joint correctness, and WARN does not wait for informed acknowledgement. More fundamentally, no study measured authorized task completion, the cost of unnecessary intervention, warning comprehension or reformulation effort. The observed request contract therefore does not yet establish practical utility or human benefit.

The current client exposes ephemeral events for notice rendering and local request disposition, which provide a starting point for observing intervention. Fifty-three synthetic host tests validated this contract, but the fields were added after the reported browser capture and have not undergone installed-browser or overhead remeasurement. Their meanings are deliberately narrow: rendering does not prove that a user understood a notice, and native handoff does not prove that a server received the request. Page scripts can also forge or suppress the events. They are neither persisted nor sent as telemetry. A utility study still requires independently specified expected actions and joined user or task outcomes, rather than treating these diagnostic events as evidence of benefit.

\subsection{Measurement and Reproduction Boundaries}
The resource and telemetry studies also require careful interpretation of their observation boundaries. Container totals include infrastructure and instrumentation, so they do not isolate incremental client overhead; rules/NER timings likewise do not measure semantic processing. Telemetry accounting assumes retained local state and a trusted clock, while the finite privacy calculation assumes independent uniform OS bits. Participation, delivery selection and metadata remain outside those bounds. The synthetic estimator study has no deployment-usefulness threshold, and its aggregate fields supply no corrected contextual labels for training.

The sealed local source/model capsule reproduced twenty-four invented semantic requests across six packages with matching identities and outcomes. This closes a bounded local replay gap using existing host dependencies, rather than establishing public Git-only installation, reproduction in a fresh environment or a rerun of protected-final data (Appendix~\ref{app:telemetry_reconstruction}). Ignored pinned source and model assets and licensing constraints remain relevant to redistribution. The next evaluation steps follow from these limits: independently adjudicated contextual targets, protected generalization studies, broader receiver coverage, baseline-relative semantic cost and human utility. Those studies would test the unresolved claims directly.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke implements local prompt inspection and explicit request actions with guarded, version-bound updates and separate randomized telemetry. Detection improved on authored contextual cases, but action failures and poor specificity on separate targets limited its effectiveness. Installed-browser probes verified supported request handling, including WARN's original-content forwarding. Every matched full-model candidate improved aggregate agreement yet failed retention, demonstrating why average gains are insufficient grounds for publication. Resource measurements and telemetry audits established bounded costs and conditional privacy behavior without demonstrating deployment utility. The system provides an auditable mechanism for protection and revision; independent contextual accuracy, safe automatic improvement and human benefit remain questions for further evaluation.

\section{Exact Policy Representation}
\label{app:policy}
The policy uses sensitivity and visibility as separate dimensions so that information content and its disclosure context remain distinguishable. Sensitivity S0--S3 denotes no identified risk, low-risk personal context, potentially harmful personal information, and highly sensitive/identifying details or secrets. Visibility P0--P4/PU denotes public, semi-public, authentication-restricted, group-private, personal-private and unspecified context. A prompt also receives a multilabel set drawn from ten categories: HEALTH, POLITICS, RELIGION, CRIMINAL, FINANCIAL, SEXUAL, CHILD, LOCATION, IDENTITY and THIRD\_PARTY. More than one category may apply to the same prompt.

The deterministic mapping assigns BLOCK to S3 and WARN to S2. S0/S1 remain ALLOW unless IDENTITY and LOCATION coexist, or either category occurs with P2--P4 visibility. Returned confidence below 0.5 moderates S3 to WARN and S2 without IDENTITY/LOCATION to ALLOW; other low-confidence classifications also map to ALLOW. During aggregation, the runtime preserves individual enforcement decisions, unions the categories and retains the strongest sensitivity and known visibility. These rules specify how the implemented policy produces an action. They do not independently validate disclosure authorization or establish confidence as a calibrated probability.

\section{Telemetry and Local Reconstruction Details}
\label{app:telemetry_reconstruction}
The audit enumerated exact rational finite kernels for the actual five domains, $K=3,4,11,2,6$, on a fingerprinted Windows CPython 3.11.9 build. Outward 100-digit logarithm enclosures placed maximum event loss above nominal $\varepsilon=0.5,1,2$ by approximately $1.294\times10^{-15}$, $1.503\times10^{-15}$ and $1.226\times10^{-15}$, respectively. The excess is tiny but sufficient to invalidate the exact nominal bound in these settings. Under the same entropy assumptions, eight default reservations compose to approximately $8+1.202\times10^{-14}$. The calculation remains specific to the audited host and settings; other configurations and deployed builds are uncertified.

The utility experiment used 180 fixed-truth simulation cells with 10,000 repetitions per cell. For balanced integer category counts with $K=11$, event $\varepsilon=1$ and $n=80/8000$ contributing reports, first-category truth was $7/727$, clipped bias was $+22.84/+237.50$, and RMSE was $42.32/1028.47$ counts. Clipping occurred in $85.62/28.32\%$ of trials, while independently estimated marginals failed to sum to $n$ in $99.99/99.78\%$. The larger $n$ pools installation-days, each subject to the default eight-reservation cap.

These errors are conditional on realized contributor counts and exclude population sampling. The simulator also uses binary64 approximations of the exact finite-kernel probabilities. Numerical moment and collector checks therefore establish consistency within the implemented estimation procedure, without specifying how much error would be acceptable for a deployment use case.

The isolated ledger checks accepted eight reservations and denied the ninth, retained the cap across process reopen, and reset it on the next UTC day. They failed closed on rollback or corruption and admitted sixteen of forty concurrent epsilon-0.5 requests. Build, queue and delivery failures also consumed their reservations. A constructed 32-event sequence with 25\% BLOCK produced 100\% versus 0\% BLOCK among the first-eight contributors when its order was reversed. This demonstrates selection sensitivity before content randomization; it is not a measurement of real deployment monitoring accuracy.

\subsection{Implemented Finite Kernel}
The finite calculation follows the actual sampler rather than substituting the ideal randomized-response probabilities. Let $\widetilde p_i$ denote the binary64 truth threshold. In the audited CPython implementation, a fresh uniform 53-bit integer $J$ selects truth when $J/2^{53}<\widetilde p_i$. The resulting truth probability and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Alternative selection uses rejection sampling. The expression assumes independent uniform OS bits and the arithmetic of the fingerprinted host, so it does not independently certify the entropy source in deployment or the behavior of other builds.
