\title{\privoke: A Client-Side Privacy Firewall for LLM Prompts}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Prompts sent to hosted language models can disclose personal or confidential information before a provider processes them. We examine how local inspection, request enforcement, guarded model revision and operational reporting connect in one workflow. \privoke combines local inspection, ALLOW/WARN/BLOCK handling on supported requests (ALLOW and WARN forward the original body; BLOCK cancels), version-bound detector updates with a retention guard, and separate randomized telemetry. On 1,560 authored cases with provisional targets, updated packages improved agreement; 33 installed receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths. All matched full-model candidates improved aggregate agreement but failed the configured retention guard. Registered fresh detector-layer tests found that the shipped rework passed the 0.5-point BLOCK-recall margin (difference $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$) while its union-recall result stayed inconclusive and a 1.05-point loss against the original detectors did not meet the promotion rule. Controlled telemetry fixtures verified delivery and accounting; finite-arithmetic loss slightly exceeded nominal budgets and simulated estimation error was substantial. The findings support bounded mechanism claims, not validated contextual judgments, universal interception, an accepted update, useful live monitoring or user benefit. Adaptive end-to-end evasion and communication-authentication tests are pending.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Hosted large language models (LLMs) support writing, coding and analysis, but prompts may include another person's details or confidential material~\cite{Gupta2024,LIANG2025101366,koraag2025comparing,SamsungBans2023,GoogleWarns2023}. Provider controls on retention act after the initial transfer. Running the application model locally is one alternative, but changes the chosen service and its computational demands~\cite{Llama2023}. We study the intervening point: inspecting selected requests on the user's device before handoff.

The privacy question is contextual: the same information may be appropriate or inappropriate to disclose depending on whose information it is, the audience and purpose~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}. Recognition alone cannot establish authorization. Nor does a warning necessarily prevent transfer. PriVoke combines rules, named entity recognition (NER) and semantic classification to represent sensitivity, audience and overlapping categories, then selects ALLOW, WARN or BLOCK. ALLOW and WARN send the original body; BLOCK cancels a recognized request. WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. Because an early rule BLOCK ends the detector cascade, both detector order and request enforcement affect observed behavior. Work on reproducing sensitive prompt information in chatbot outputs concerns a later point in the lifecycle~\cite{priyanshu2023chatbots}; pre-transmission inspection cannot reverse that disclosure.

The system also separates detector maintenance from reporting. A server runtime fits candidates from supplied labelled examples, and a version-bound retention guard may reject a candidate before publication. This addresses the risk that aggregate gains conceal regressions on previously correct cases~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, but only for the guard's declared examples and criteria. A separate client randomizes coarse telemetry under a local budget ledger; neither prompts nor telemetry train the detector. The contribution is the evaluation of these connected but distinct mechanisms in one workflow, not a new detector or privacy primitive.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt]
\item An implemented client path connecting local layered inspection to explicit request handling on supported browser requests, specified as a workflow (Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow}) and a decision policy.
\item A version-bound update workflow with a casewise retention guard and a formal acceptance predicate (Definition~\ref{def:accept}), distinguished from the read-only standard V3 path and evaluated through matched candidates that improved aggregate agreement but did not qualify for publication.
\item A separate randomized telemetry path evaluated for finite event-content privacy, budget accounting, delivery and estimation error.
\item Explicit security goals and threats linked to the experiments that test them, with untested properties marked pending, and an empirical account of trade-offs across prediction, enforcement, update eligibility and reporting that does not treat provisional targets as contextual truth.
\end{enumerate}
The implementation, evaluation recipes and research records are available at an anonymous repository (link withheld for review).

We organize the evaluation around three questions:
\textbf{RQ1}, does \privoke detect and enforce privacy-sensitive prompt policies effectively; \textbf{RQ2}, can it update its detector without sacrificing previously protected behavior; and \textbf{RQ3}, what systems and privacy overhead does it introduce? RQ1 separates agreement with declared targets from observed transmission; RQ2 asks whether aggregate gains satisfy a casewise retention rule; RQ3 separates resident cost and event-content privacy from estimation error and selection. These distinctions define what the evidence can support and where independent contextual judgment remains absent.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
\label{sec:literature_review}
\textit{Local intervention and contextual decisions.} Casper combines browser-side rules, entity recognition and topic analysis; its reviewed prototype replaces identifiers and requires acknowledgement for topic warnings~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,Casper2024}. Hide and Seek (HaS) hides entities before cloud inference and recovers them in responses, while ProSan learns selective word replacement from task-importance and privacy proxies~\cite{HaS2024,ProSan2024}. PromptShield classifies enterprise prompts by role and policy~\cite{PromptShield2025}. These works address sanitization, task continuation or policy classification. PriVoke instead measures a request contract in which supported hooks act automatically and WARN forwards unchanged content without acknowledgement. Across these approaches, detector labels alone do not establish contextual authorization; that question requires its own targets and evaluation.

\textit{Retaining behavior during model revision.} Negative-flip research measures when an update turns a previously correct prediction into an error; Yan et al. propose positive-congruent training for image classifiers~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}. This motivates paired gains and losses, but does not establish privacy-policy correctness. PriVoke applies the distinction to an update decision: candidates are compared with their declared base and can be rejected under a configured retention guard. The measured candidates show why aggregate improvement is insufficient for acceptance; the guard remains limited to its declared cases and harm rule.

\textit{Privacy of operational reporting.} Local randomization supports aggregate collection without directly releasing each underlying value. RAPPOR applies randomized response to client-side statistics and addresses the additional privacy cost of repeated reporting~\cite{ErlingssonRappor2014}. PriVoke instead combines categorical event reports with a local budget ledger. We examine finite arithmetic, estimation error and contribution selection separately: event-content protection does not conceal participation or network metadata, and accurate marginals would not by themselves establish representative monitoring.

\textit{Contribution and comparison boundaries.} PriVoke does not claim a new detector, regression method or privacy primitive. Its contribution is the empirical connection of established mechanisms: RQ1 pairs local decisions with receiver observations; RQ2 separates learning gains from version-bound update eligibility; RQ3 measures system cost and distinguishes event-content privacy from estimation and admission. The cited systems use different tasks, targets and denominators, so no numerical ranking is justified.

\section{Threat Model and Security Goals}
\subsection{Scope and Trust}
\label{sec:threat_model}
\privoke addresses prompt text in recognized browser requests before handoff to an external language-model provider. Protected assets are the request text, local detector and model state, update artifacts and credentials, and randomized telemetry with its budget ledger. The user, enabled extension, page hook, local runtime and workstation are trusted to inspect and to preserve local state; compromised local components are out of scope and could expose prompts or alter decisions and accounting. The provider and network observer are not trusted: the provider receives the original prompt after ALLOW or WARN, and an observer may see report presence and transport metadata that event-content randomization does not hide.

We consider two active adversaries. A \emph{prompt author} modifies text to evade detection. An \emph{update sender} submits stale, replayed, duplicate, malformed or unauthorized candidates, or presents an unauthorized service identity. The implementation has normalization and detector layers for prompt variation, and parent-version, tensor-bound, serialized-publication and receipt checks for updates (Section~\ref{sec:updates}); the inspected proxy requires client certificates on model-streaming and telemetry routes. These are implemented controls, not executed attack results: end-to-end authentication and authorization, particularly for update submission, are not established, and Section~\ref{sec:security_eval} reserves their evaluation.

The boundary covers recognized prompt-bearing POST requests sent through page \texttt{fetch} and asynchronous \texttt{XMLHttpRequest} (XHR). Workers, attachments, unsupported endpoints and other transports are outside measured coverage, users can disable protection, and the optional hosted-inference configuration is excluded. Each goal in Table~\ref{tab:security_goals} is therefore conditional, and none implies correct contextual decisions or comprehensive protection. Goals marked pending name the section that will evaluate them; they are not omitted because they are untested.

\subsection{Local Inspection and Request Enforcement}
\label{sec:enforcement}
\textit{Request enforcement (G1).} Algorithm~\ref{alg:workflow} gives the client workflow. When the active page hook recognizes a supported request and the runtime returns BLOCK, the hook cancels it before the page's native sender hands it off. ALLOW and WARN release the original body, and WARN does not wait for acknowledgement. The installed probes in Section~\ref{sec:installed_probes} test this contract on selected paths; they do not establish coverage of unsupported transports.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\privoke request workflow. Layers are ordered rules $<$ NER $<$ semantic; $\max$ uses ALLOW $<$ WARN $<$ BLOCK.}
\label{alg:workflow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require intercepted request $r$ with prompt text $x$; requested layers $\Lambda$
\State \textbf{if} protection is disabled or $r$ is unsupported \textbf{then return} forward $r$
\State $x_c\gets\textsc{Normalize}(x)$ with offsets; $x_\ell\gets\textsc{Lowercase}(x_c)$
\State $a\gets\text{ALLOW}$;\; $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{false}$
\If{rules $\in\Lambda$}
  \State $a\gets\textsc{Rules}(x_c)$
  \If{$a=\text{BLOCK}$} \textbf{goto} line~\ref{alg:enforce} \EndIf \Comment{skip NER and semantics}
\EndIf
\ForAll{$\ell\in\Lambda\setminus\{\text{rules}\}$ \textbf{concurrently}}
  \State $F\gets\ell(x_c)$ for NER; $\ell(x_\ell)$ for semantic
  \If{$\ell$ failed} $\mathit{failed}\gets\text{true}$
  \Else{} $a\gets\max\bigl(a,\max_{f\in F}\pi(f)\bigr)$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$\mathit{failed}\wedge a=\text{ALLOW}$} $a\gets\text{BLOCK}$ \EndIf
\State \textbf{enforce:}\label{alg:enforce} \textbf{if} $a=\text{BLOCK}$ cancel $r$ before transmission
\State \textbf{else} release the original body (WARN first shows a notice, without waiting)
\State offer a coarse event to the separate telemetry channel (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The runtime normalizes text while retaining offsets. Regex and NER receive case-preserved text; semantic models receive lowercased normalized text. Rules run first and an early rule BLOCK ends the cascade; otherwise the strongest action is retained. A failed requested layer turns an otherwise-ALLOW result into BLOCK. If the parameter stream is lost, however, full mode can continue with rules and NER and forward without semantic inspection. We therefore distinguish the returned action, executed layers and receiver observation.

\label{sec:pii_categorisation}
The semantic model predicts sensitivity $s\in\{\mathrm{S0},\dots,\mathrm{S3}\}$ (S0 no identified risk; S3 highly sensitive information, identifiers or secrets), audience (visibility) $v\in\{\mathrm{P0},\dots,\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{PU}\}$ (public, semi-public, restricted/group-private/personal-private, unspecified) and a category set $C$ (health, politics, religion, criminal, financial, sexual, child, location, identity and third-party information). With $c$ the finding confidence and $I=C\cap\{\mathrm{IDENTITY},\mathrm{LOCATION}\}$, the policy $\pi$ maps a finding to an action:
\begin{equation}
\pi(f)=\begin{cases}
\mathrm{WARN} & c<0.5\wedge\bigl(s=\mathrm{S3}\vee(s=\mathrm{S2}\wedge I\neq\varnothing)\bigr)\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & c<0.5\ \text{otherwise}\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & s=\mathrm{S0}\wedge C=\varnothing\\
\mathrm{BLOCK} & s=\mathrm{S3}\\
\mathrm{WARN} & s=\mathrm{S2}\ \vee\ |I|=2\ \vee\ (I\neq\varnothing\wedge v\in\{\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4}\})\\
\mathrm{ALLOW} & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with rows evaluated in order and a missing confidence treated as $c\ge0.5$. The score $c$ is not calibrated joint confidence or evidence of authorization. Appendix~\ref{app:policy} gives the order and its observable consequences. The compact-model precedent motivates the package comparisons~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}, but they change several factors and do not isolate an architectural cause. The pretrained V3 packages use BERT encoders~\cite{turc2019WellReadStudents}; the installed default is V3 balanced.

\subsection{Detector Precision Controls}
\label{sec:detector_precision_design}
Rules and NER precede semantic classification, and a rule BLOCK ends the cascade. The precision revision therefore targets unnecessary early findings while retaining the possibility of missed detections. The policy mapping and semantic models are unchanged.

\subsection{Guarded Detector Updates}
\label{sec:updates}
\textit{Standard V3 versus the legacy update path.} Two model paths coexist and must not be conflated (Table~\ref{tab:paths}). The \emph{standard V3 path} serves the installed default: pinned, read-only pretrained packages streamed as hash-verified assets, with no gradient route; the streaming service's default alias is configuration-owned, and a legacy publication cannot replace it. The \emph{legacy update path} applies version-bound gradient updates to a mutable V1 artifact; it is opt-in (an explicit training profile), the updater and fuzzer services exist only there, and standard V3 identifiers are rejected by it. All guarded-update evidence in this paper concerns the legacy mechanism and controlled continuation studies; the paper reports installed V3 inference, not a deployed V3 learning loop.

In the legacy workflow, the updater requests labelled batches and held-out guard cases; the server runtime computes a bounded change against its cached base. Training is centralized on supplied examples, not user gradients or federated data. A candidate $u=(\mathit{src},\mathit{mid},v_b,G,\mu)$ carries a source, model identity, base version $v_b$, named gradients $G=\{(n_i,\sigma_i,w_i)\}$ with shape $\sigma_i$ and values $w_i$, and metadata $\mu$. Let $A$ be the active artifact with version $v_A$, parameters $P$ and configured bounds: $B$ (default 1.0) per value, $L$ values per tensor (4,096; larger for the full-encoder strategy) and $T=65{,}536$ values in total.

\begin{definition}[Acceptance of a candidate]\label{def:accept}
Candidate $u$ replaces its parent $A$ if and only if all of the following hold, where (a)--(c) are checked by the update service, re-evaluated on the artifact reloaded under the writer lock, and (d) is checked by the trainer before submission:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,label=(\alph*),itemsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Valid.} $\mathit{mid}$ equals the service's model and $A$'s; $\mathit{src}$ and $v_b$ are non-empty; $1\le|G|\le256$, names are distinct, $\sum_i|w_i|\le T$ and $|\mu|\le64$; for every $i$, $\sigma_i$ is positive with $\prod\sigma_i=|w_i|\le L$, every value is finite with $|x|\le B$, and $n_i$ names a trainable parameter of $A$ with shape $\sigma_i$; and $A$'s architecture admits gradient updates (scratch-presence and pretrained-context artifacts allow only offline release fitting).
\item \emph{Fresh.} $v_b=v_A$ (compare-and-swap on the exact parent).
\item \emph{Not a conflicting retry.} Let $\kappa=H(\mathit{src},\mathit{request\_source},\mathit{request\_id})$. If a receipt for $\kappa$ exists, $u$ is not re-applied: an identical payload digest returns the recorded outcome and a different digest is rejected. Updates without a request identifier create no receipt.
\item \emph{Guarded.} With held-out exact match $e$, sensitive recall $r$, clean specificity $p$, minimum exact match $\theta$ and target-capped regression rate $\rho$ (a row regresses when its severity or action falls below the lower of its target and the base prediction), $e_{\mathrm{train}}>\theta\wedge e_u\ge e_A\wedge r_u\ge r_A\wedge p_u\ge p_A\wedge\rho_u=0$. Retention policies additionally need zero per-row ORIGINAL violations; the sixteen-row window guard needs non-decreasing counts and $\rho=0$, with its selected-checkpoint evidence bound to the artifact.
\end{enumerate}
On acceptance, $A'=A\oplus u$ has version \texttt{release+train.}$(k{+}1)$ and a recomputed checksum, is written to a temporary file, flushed and atomically renamed over $A$, and its receipt is committed; a best-effort audit record follows. Otherwise $A$ is unchanged.
\end{definition}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:update} gives the control flow. The service recovers a receipt left by an interrupted commit before serving the next update, so a crash between rename and receipt does not allow a second application. Rejection at a later stage does not undo an earlier head stage already accepted. Clients verify delivered artifacts before refreshing.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Guarded update of the legacy path (Definition~\ref{def:accept}).}
\label{alg:update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require candidate $u$ with base version $v_b$, request identifier, gradients $G$
\State \textbf{trainer:} fit $u$ on supplied examples; \textbf{if not} Guarded$(u)$ \textbf{then} discard $u$
\State submit $u$ to the update service; \textbf{if not} Valid$(u,A)$ \textbf{then reject}
\State \textbf{lock} writer;\; recover any receipt left by an interrupted commit
\State reload $A$; \textbf{if} $v_b\ne v_A$ \textbf{then reject} (stale)
\State \textbf{if} receipt for $\kappa(u)$ exists \textbf{then} \textbf{return} recorded outcome \textbf{or reject} (digest differs)
\State revalidate $u$ against reloaded $A$; $A'\gets A\oplus u$; new version and checksum
\State write $A'$ to a temporary file; flush; \textbf{atomically rename} over $A$; commit receipt
\State \textbf{unlock}; \textbf{return} $A'$ version; clients verify and refresh later
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The checks constrain stale, duplicate and malformed updates but do not authenticate the sender, bound the aggregate change beyond the per-value limit $B$, or guarantee behavioral safety. Test code covers validation and receipt scenarios but is not itself a passing security campaign; attack outcomes are reserved for Section~\ref{sec:eval_update}. In the matched RQ2 study, all full-model candidates failed the configured guard and none was published.

\subsection{Separate Randomized Telemetry}
\label{sec:telemetry_mechanism}
The official client emits five coarse randomized fields: action, risk bucket, precedence-selected category, model-version bucket, and four-hour time-of-day bin. The schema omits the prompt, identifiers, exact confidence and per-layer findings. Client and collector test code checks the field set and aggregate storage; controlled operation tests delivery and accounting, not absence of prompt text from every local log, memory surface or channel. The collector estimates lifetime marginals but lacks denominators and time-window queries needed for latency or error rates. Telemetry does not provide training labels.

\textit{Event-content privacy (G4).} The randomized-response mechanism is intended to limit how much the distribution of a released tuple can change between possible event contents~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. This comparison fixes a reporting opportunity and public configuration; it does not randomize whether a report exists. The event budget $\varepsilon$ is divided equally across fields as $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon/5$. For a domain of size $K_i$, the true value has reporting probability $p_i$ and each alternative has probability $q_i$:
\begin{equation}
p_i=\frac{e^{\varepsilon_i}}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1},\quad
q_i=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_i}+K_i-1}.
\end{equation}
Under independent ideal sampling, composition multiplies the field likelihood ratios to bound the event ratio by $e^\varepsilon$~\cite{DworkRothFoundations2014}. That is the nominal ideal mechanism, not an exact guarantee for the finite implementation. The client compares a fresh uniform 53-bit integer draw, $J/2^{53}$, with a binary64 threshold $\widetilde p_i$, choosing alternatives by rejection sampling. Its finite probabilities and event loss are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_i&=\frac{\lceil2^{53}\widetilde p_i\rceil}{2^{53}},\quad Q_i=\frac{1-P_i}{K_i-1},\\
\varepsilon_{\rm finite}&=\sum_i\log\max\{P_i/Q_i,Q_i/P_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The finite audit in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_results} found $\varepsilon_{\rm finite}$ slightly above nominal $\varepsilon$ for the audited host settings. It therefore supports only the corresponding implementation-specific finite bound under independent uniform randomness, not exact nominal epsilon across builds or environments. A persistent installation-local ledger reserves nominal budget before packet construction, defaulting to one per event and eight per Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) day. Failed construction or delivery does not refund that reservation; accounting depends on retained ledger state and a trusted clock, and the cap is not user-level accounting across devices or days.

Report presence, exact accepted counts, timing, source IP, contribution selection, delivery and other network metadata remain observable or outside this content guarantee. The collector estimates marginals by inverting nominal response probabilities and clipping counts to their feasible range, so the privacy mechanism alone does not establish useful or representative monitoring.

\subsection{Implementation}
The browser extension injects page hooks for supported \texttt{fetch} and XHR POST requests. The extension background routes extracted text through a loopback gRPC-Web bridge to the local Python runtime; native messaging launches the supervisor, and the native-host manifest restricts launches to the registered extension identity. The default local package is the verified, pretrained V3 balanced model, and standard V3 inference is read-only. The legacy updater, fuzzer and parameter service implement candidate fitting, validation, version-bound publication and artifact refresh. The official reporter randomizes its five coarse fields and reserves budget in installation-local state before delivery; the collector stores lifetime marginals. These are distinct runtime paths: neither prompt inspection nor telemetry supplies detector-training labels.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
For case $j$ with target sensitivity $s_j$, audience $v_j$, category set $C_j$ and allowed-action set $\mathcal A_j$, and prediction $(\hat s_j,\hat v_j,\hat C_j,\hat a_j)$, label, action and joint agreement average
\begin{equation}
L_j=\mathbf1\{\hat s_j=s_j,\hat v_j=v_j,\hat C_j=C_j\},\ \
A_j=\mathbf1\{\hat a_j\in\mathcal A_j\},\ \
J_j=L_jA_j .
\end{equation}
A case is binary-positive when $\hat s_j\ne\mathrm{S0}$ or $\hat C_j\neq\varnothing$; recall is $\mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN})$ and specificity $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$ over targets that are provisional labels on the contextual workload and source annotation presence on the final endpoint. For a paired correctness indicator $Z$, gains $G$ and losses $\Lambda$ count cases with $Z^{\rm old}=0,Z^{\rm new}=1$ and the reverse; the net change is $(G-\Lambda)/N$, always reported with both counts. Exact McNemar tests use the discordant rows, and cluster bootstraps resample whole groups.

\textit{Attack success rate.} For a frozen labelled sensitive set $D$ and a prompt transformation $\tau$, let $d(x)=1$ when the evaluated pipeline's action on $x$ is WARN or BLOCK. A prompt \emph{evades} when it was detected before the transform and is missed after it. The conditional and unconditional rates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
E(\tau)&=\{x\in D:d(x)=1,\ d(\tau(x))=0\},\\
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mid}(\tau)&=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|\{x\in D:d(x)=1\}|},\qquad
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm u}(\tau)=\frac{|E(\tau)|}{|D|},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
each reported with a Wilson interval for $k$ successes in $n$ trials, $\bigl[\hat p+\tfrac{z^2}{2n}\pm z\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}+\tfrac{z^2}{4n^2}}\,\bigr]\big/\bigl(1+\tfrac{z^2}{n}\bigr)$ with $\hat p=k/n$ and $z=1.96$. Baseline detection $|\{d(x)=1\}|/|D|$ on untransformed prompts accompanies every rate. This is a product end-to-end measure and is never pooled with LLM-only results; its results are reserved for Section~\ref{sec:eval_evasion}.

\subsection{Does a Decision Control Transmission?}
\label{sec:installed_probes}
Across thirty-three correctness probes, the installed-browser capture connected decisions to eighteen unchanged-body sends and fifteen zero-send outcomes. \textbf{Answer to RQ1.} PriVoke improved agreement with provisional contextual policy and enforced its declared request contract on the tested installed paths. The results do not yet establish independent contextual accuracy, provider compatibility, prevention for every sensitive prompt or practical usability.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{What the System Evidence Supports}
RQ1 shows why detector order is part of the policy: an early rule BLOCK prevents later semantic evidence from changing the action. Historically, removing rules corrected 139 unnecessary restrictions but lost eighteen previously correct actions; the revised full-product rerun lowered shared unnecessary restrictions to 108, yet rule removal still gained 85 actions while losing the same eighteen. Both results rely on provisional targets. Fresh detector-layer tests found a specificity/recall trade-off: the first revision improved union specificity but failed its recall margin; K passed H8's BLOCK-recall margin (estimate $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$) while losing 1.05 points of union recall against the original detectors and failing H7. That loss is source-dependent, some specificity checks reused negatives seen during rework, and the owner shipped K despite its failure to meet the registered promotion rule. These findings describe trade-offs, not a universally suitable detector setting.

Comparison with local sanitization clarifies who controls continuation. Casper's acknowledged warnings leave a decision to the user, while HaS and ProSan transform content to support continued use~\cite{CasperPreprintV12024,HaS2024,ProSan2024}. \privoke sends unchanged content after WARN and leaves task completion unresolved after BLOCK. Receiver compliance verifies that contract, not whether it is desirable; that judgment needs evidence about disclosure authorization, warning comprehension and reformulation effort (Section~\ref{sec:eval_warn}). Execution traces locate the layer that produced an action, whereas matched removal measures the resulting action changes on the fixture; fewer unnecessary restrictions and more missed protections remain distinct outcomes.

\subsection{Ground Truth and Generalization Remain Open}
\label{sec:limitations}
Contextual integrity concerns appropriate information flows~\cite{NissenbaumContextualIntegrity2004}, for which entity recognition supplies only part of the judgment. Independent contextual ground truth remains the largest scientific gap because \privoke's authored targets are assistant-provisional. Freezing the separate annotation-presence endpoint before opening limits post-selection, but neither its different target nor its sole-semantic execution validates contextual authorization, so improved authored-target agreement and poor final specificity answer different questions. A stronger evaluation should define whose information may flow to which audience and for what purpose before annotators see system outputs, with at least two independent annotators labelling a fresh sample and reporting agreement and adjudication; the prepared double-annotation protocol has not been run.

Group and text exclusions reduce declared overlap, and paired cluster analyses show sensitivity to known dependence, but neither establishes template independence, paraphrase exclusion or absence of pretraining exposure. The final endpoint comprises 308 Nemotron, 89 AI4privacy, eighty Gretel, twenty Privy and one MAPA row; these English synthetic or machine-generated cases do not represent other languages or organizational policies, and neither unadjusted intervals nor three seeds provide a population guarantee. Confirmation after opening this endpoint requires a new protected successor.

The detector-layer studies use one small English spaCy model and source annotation labels with unquantified noise; S1 findings count as positive. The post-filter was developed on design material, dev-CHECK and OpenAI became post-review after revisions, and the 498-row endpoint was already opened for another unit and is consumed. The student-identifier rule rests on one template. F4 contains positives only, so K's specificity rests on negatives that O and N had been scored on and that informed the rework, and on unlabelled natural prompts whose flag rates are not error rates. The benchmark pool is exhausted, annotation is incomplete by construction and includes attribute spans (age, title, gender) for which \privoke has no detector, label noise was not audited, and F2 adjudication remains unperformed. The K qualifier vocabulary mirrors labels in the development spans, so labelled-identifier gains are tied to the benchmark's conventions. K's recall cost against the original detectors is real, source dependent and partly attributable to the NER layer it shares with the first revision; exploratory analyses cannot confirm anything. Neither the full-product rerun nor the installed probes and cost studies include K, and the default-off robustness fixes and larger NER models are not shipped. No multiplicity correction was applied.

\subsection{Retention, Availability and Practical Utility}
Matching the base and exposure reduces RQ2 configuration confounding without isolating the RQ1 package effect. Consistent with negative-flip research~\cite{YanPositiveCongruent2021}, all matched full-model candidates improved aggregate assessment yet failed the guard; under the configured rule, higher scores cannot override casewise rejection, and the weak zero-head base and small guard constrain the significance of a pass. A label change that preserves an allowed action differs from one that releases blocked content, and an unnecessary BLOCK costs differently from under-restriction, so the guard's single derived action and the assessment's allowed sets need not agree. A larger, independently justified guard could change eligibility in either direction.

Operational version checks address another failure mode: binding a candidate to its exact parent prevents an update evaluated against one model from overwriting a different revision, but cannot make its labels correct, and a verified download shows integrity, not behavioral suitability. Standard V3's pinned path and the separately enabled legacy update service must remain distinguishable (Table~\ref{tab:paths}); this paper reports installed V3 inference and bounded continuation evidence, not a deployed V3 learning loop. Blocking when analysis is unavailable can prevent disclosure while interrupting an authorized task, whereas WARN avoids that interruption but proceeds without informed acknowledgement; heuristic confidence cannot resolve the choice because it is not calibrated joint correctness. The quality-tier benign-prompt BLOCKs show that installation alone is not accuracy validation. Authenticated providers, Windows, workers, attachments and other transports remain unverified, and without evidence on task completion, unnecessary intervention, warning comprehension and reformulation effort, practical utility and human benefit remain open.

\subsection{Measurement Boundaries and Next Steps}
Matched resident arms quantify semantic and full-detector activation but omit extension-absent and cold-load comparisons needed for total overhead. The telemetry ledger depends on retained state and a trusted clock, its finite bound assumes independent uniform OS bits, and accounting leaves estimation error and admission-order effects unresolved. The current deployment has too few concurrent users for a live-usage usefulness study, and more users alone would not repair the missing fields; lifetime categorical marginals and an operator Health probe cannot replace latency, error and denominator instrumentation. A reporting design must specify its estimand before increasing collection, because the distribution among admitted, delivered events can differ from that among all decisions, installations or users, and richer fields would change the disclosure surface and the privacy analysis. Reproducing results requires the referenced weights, inputs, compatible dependencies and permissions, not only a recipe and a fingerprint; the records disclose local and ignored assets so that their absence from a public checkout is not mistaken for a complete package.

Across the lifecycle, early restrictions can survive later model improvements, improved candidates can remain ineligible, and admitted reports can remain unrepresentative. These findings motivate separate acceptance evidence rather than validate a remedy. Contextual adjudication, running the security evaluations of Section~\ref{sec:security_eval}, broader receiver and retention checks, total-cost measurements, and user and task evidence are needed to support stronger claims.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\privoke connects local prompt inspection to supported request enforcement, guarded candidate updates and separate randomized telemetry. Receiver probes observed the declared send/block behavior on tested paths, including unchanged-body sends under WARN. On 1,560 authored cases, updated packages improved agreement with provisional targets, but a separate annotation-presence evaluation had low specificity. All matched full-model candidates failed retention. Installed V3 measurements isolated resident detector activation; controlled telemetry fixtures verified delivery and accounting, while the finite audit found privacy loss slightly above nominal and simulations showed substantial estimation error.

The detector revisions expose the central trade-off. The first revision improved union specificity but failed its registered recall test. K, shipped by owner decision, passed H8's 0.5-point margin for BLOCK recall (difference $+0.01$ points; 95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$), but H7 for union recall remained inconclusive and K retained a 1.05-point union-recall loss against the original detectors (95\% interval $-1.70$ to $-0.41$), concentrated in Privy. The evidence supports bounded, inspectable mechanisms, not independently validated contextual accuracy, universal interception, an accepted retention-qualified update, representative monitoring or user benefit. Human annotation, the pending security evaluations of Section~\ref{sec:security_eval}, total cost, and user and task outcomes remain outstanding.

\section{Policy Decision Order and Observable Outcomes}
\label{app:policy}
The contextual attributes in Section~\ref{sec:pii_categorisation} describe a finding before enforcement. For a finding with numeric confidence below 0.5, the low-confidence rule takes precedence: S3 yields WARN; S2 yields WARN only if IDENTITY or LOCATION is present; all remaining cases yield ALLOW. Missing or nonnumeric confidence does not activate that rule. This is a decision threshold, not an empirical confidence calibration.

For other findings, the baseline checks are ordered. S0 with an empty category set yields ALLOW. S3 yields BLOCK and S2 yields WARN. For the remaining S0/S1 cases, IDENTITY together with LOCATION yields WARN; either category with visibility P2, P3 or P4 also yields WARN. Otherwise the finding yields ALLOW. PU denotes an unspecified audience and is not treated as restricted/private by this comparison. A category set is multilabel, so retaining one sensitive category is not equivalent to matching the exact target set.

The cascade then constrains what reaches aggregation. An early rule BLOCK ends inspection; later requested layers have not evaluated the text. Otherwise, aggregation preserves the strongest enforcement decision rather than replacing it with the last detector's action. A failed requested layer changes an otherwise ALLOW result to BLOCK, while a valid non-ALLOW finding remains visible. These semantics differ from a configuration in which an unavailable parameter stream leaves fewer enabled layers: a successfully completed reduced cascade is not a successful semantic execution.

Finally, a policy action and a receiver observation are different records. ALLOW and WARN release the original supported request, while BLOCK cancels it. Notice rendering under WARN does not imply acknowledgement, consent or removal of information from the body. For a probe expecting a send, the relevant observation is receiver arrival with the original body; for a no-send probe, the observation is absence of arrival within the declared observation window, including checks for a late send. The installed probes establish these bounded outcomes, not an absence of leakage through every browser transport.

\section{Candidate Identity, Guarding and Reproduction}
\label{app:retention}
The matched continuation comparison starts both HEADS and FULL from the same intact pretrained balanced encoder/pooler and zero task heads. That ORIGINAL control is distinct from both the historical V1 classifier and the selected updated package used in RQ1. The shared 4,096 exposures describe repeated training occurrences, not 4,096 independently sampled situations. Each seed produces a terminal candidate; the assessment selects no intermediate checkpoint.

A candidate, a guard decision and an effective artifact must remain distinguishable in the record. The candidate is the fitted set of weights, even if rejected. The decision applies the fixed sixteen-case criterion of this study: improved joint correctness and zero action harms against its derived target actions. This study mapping differs from the maintained service predicate of Definition~\ref{def:accept}(d), which requires non-decreasing exact, recall and specificity counts and no target-capped regression, with progress required separately at selection. The effective-artifact mapping describes which artifact that rule would retain: a rejected candidate maps to the unchanged ORIGINAL. Because this experiment publishes no candidate, that mapping does not establish a server replacement or a client refresh. Counting only effective artifacts would erase the rejected candidates' measured gains and harms; counting every fitted candidate as an update would erase the guard.

For reproduction, the maintained records identify the exact base and candidate artifacts, parameter scope, ordered exposure schedule, exclusions, scoring target and actual sole-semantic execution. The model-family label alone is insufficient to recover those inputs. The same requirement applies to the RQ1 package comparison and final endpoint: source, tokenizer, backbone and task-head identities must match the recorded configuration. Some checkpoints and raw run receipts remain local and ignored, so the public repository does not by itself establish that every input can be obtained and redistributed.

\section{Telemetry Estimation and Admission}
\label{app:reporting}
The ideal mechanism and audited finite implementation in Section~\ref{sec:telemetry_mechanism} are analyzed for the content of a fixed reporting opportunity under public configuration. The finite bound includes the reported excess over nominal privacy loss. For a category whose true contributor count is $h$ among $n$ reports, and observed randomized count $Y$, the nominal inverse-response estimate before clipping is
\begin{equation}
\widehat h=\frac{Y-nq}{p-q}.
\end{equation}
The collector clips each estimated marginal separately to $[0,n]$. Inverting the nominal probabilities is not the same as inverting the exact finite kernel, and clipping is nonlinear: the expected clipped value need not equal $h$. Independently clipped categories also need not sum to $n$. These properties explain why privacy loss, bias, RMSE and total coherence are separate checks.

The synthetic trials fix the contributor histogram before sampling the randomized reports. Their error therefore excludes uncertainty about which people or events enter that histogram. Increasing $n$ means more admitted reports, pooled across installation-days where necessary, rather than relaxing the per-installation daily cap. A precision requirement for an operational decision was not prespecified; the two illustrative category cells cannot establish one after the fact.

The operational accounting also has distinct denominators. An opportunity may be suppressed before queuing, a reservation may be consumed without a delivered packet, and an accepted report represents a received randomized event. The observed totals reconcile as $308=184+124$ opportunities and $184=168+16$ reserved events, where the latter partition is accepted reports plus submission failures in the controlled fixtures. These are not counts of users, unique prompts or detector decisions in a deployment.

The ledger reserves before construction and does not refund failed construction or delivery. That ordering protects accounting against retries within retained state, but cannot hide whether a report was sent. A cap can also change event composition before randomization, as the retained order-reversal example demonstrates. Correcting the randomized counts would still leave that admission mechanism to justify. The finite audit and controlled operation are recorded separately in \path{telemetry-evidence-20261014.md} and \path{rq3-telemetry-evidence-20261017.md}; neither supplies training examples or establishes useful live monitoring.

The synthetic-set regex changes are significant: personalized false positives fell on 23 rows with none added ($p<0.001$, although an interval that resamples its 20 scenario labels touches zero, [0.000, 0.073]), and the OpenAI set gained specificity on 121 rows against 1 ($p<0.001$; scenario-label interval [0.174, 0.329]) while losing recall on 12 rows against none ($p<0.001$).



The union shows no detectable change on held-out data: recall 0.767 to 0.763 (181 to 180 true positives; 13 rows gained and 14 lost, $p=1.0$) and specificity 0.958 to 0.962 (11 to 10 false positives; 7 gained and 6 lost, $p=1.0$). This supports no detectable change, not equivalence: the intervals for the differences, [$-$0.047, 0.039] for recall and [$-$0.023, 0.031] for specificity, remain compatible with shifts of three to five points either way. The development-set rise in union recall from 0.731 to 0.761 (12 rows gained, 4 lost) is not significant by the exact test ($p=0.077$) and did not reproduce on the held-out endpoint. The NER layer alone did not improve there: false positives rose from 7 to 9 and recall fell from 0.237 to 0.212. It also lost recall on both synthetic sets (personalized 0.800 to 0.695, OpenAI 0.290 to 0.170), and gained recall on the development set (0.208 to 0.246, with specificity 0.975 to 0.966) and on dev-CHECK, where its false positives stayed at four while recall rose from 0.172 to 0.207. The NER differences on real text are inside the noise and have opposite signs (development 26 rows gained against 16 lost, $p=0.16$; held-out 23 against 29, $p=0.49$), whereas the synthetic recall losses are significant (personalized 52 against 157, OpenAI 11 against 71, $p<0.001$ each). A net NER benefit is therefore not established. The NER changes are installed on design and development evidence, with an off switch, and the union result is dominated by the regex layer. The direction of the trade is regex specificity for a small recall cost, but on real text neither side is statistically established; only the synthetic-set changes are.

Action-level analysis of the union separates a change in annotation presence from a change in the runtime action. On the held-out endpoint no WARN-or-BLOCK difference is distinguishable from zero (recall 0.606 to 0.602, specificity 0.977 to 0.973), BLOCK-only recall moves from 0.398 to 0.386 (none gained, three lost, $p=0.25$), and no difference in cost-weighted error at false-negative to false-positive ratios of 2, 4 and 9 is distinguishable from zero. The largest action-level change appeared on the personalized set: BLOCK-only recall fell from 1.000 to 0.928 because 72 positives moved from BLOCK to WARN (72 against 0, $p<0.001$). Annotation presence cannot show this, since recall stays at 1.000. The cause was removal of a regex child category that had matched records of adult students by accident. The student-identifier rule added afterwards (Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision_design}) restored BLOCK on those rows without new false positives, but it rests on a single template, so the mechanism is confirmed and its generalization is not. The tables of this subsection and of the full-product rerun do not include the rule; the fresh-data evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:heldout} does.

Mean detector latency per prompt, from one sequential pass, was 44.3 to 52.4 ms for the pre-change configuration and 46.1 to 51.5 ms for the installed one across splits, with no meaningful difference. Detector-layer results do not by themselves establish the effect on contextual actions; the next subsection reports a full-product rerun of the comparison and ablation. The installed probes and the cost increments reported later were not rerun and remain pre-change measurements. The comparison also shares the limits listed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}: one small spaCy model, annotation-presence labels with their noise, S1 findings counted as positive, and partly reused design material.

Five further tests ran in parallel with the registrations. None changes the shipped configuration.

\textit{NER models.} Two larger spaCy models and a GLiNER-based personal-data model were compared with the shipped small model on dev-CHECK and the OpenAI set, with the GLiNER label preset and threshold frozen on the design splits first. No union difference among the candidates with the post-filter on was statistically reliable (smallest exact $p=0.125$). The larger spaCy model had the most consistent gains on those post-review splits (dev-CHECK 5 true positives gained and 1 lost, 4 false positives removed; OpenAI 1 gained, 3 removed) and was neutral on the design splits. The GLiNER model lost NER recall (dev-CHECK $-$0.103, $p=0.008$), left the union level or lower, and documents no training data, so it was dropped. The transformer spaCy model is a comparator at roughly six times the NER-layer latency. On the fresh F1 set the larger model raised union specificity over N by 1.71 points (1.17 to 2.24) and union recall by 1.86, but it lowered specificity in the Privy sentences (union $-$4.34, NER-only $-$7.59), so the pooled gain depends on the source mix. It also adds about 550 MB on disk and 762 MB resident. None of the evaluated models changed a BLOCK decision on the post-review splits, because their entities are at most S2. The owner held the larger model, and the shipped NER model is unchanged.

\textit{NER as amplifier.} A study on the development splits asked whether NER is better used only to corroborate or extend regex findings. Corroboration-only policies never beat the shipped union on the design splits, and amplifier-only policies cannot create a positive by construction, so they surrender NER's recall gain (negative result for NER as an amplifier-only detector). Modest independent recall gains from the shipped NER layer remain.

\textit{Robustness probe and fixes.} A perturbation probe applied seeded transformations to the development, personalized and OpenAI sets. Digit obfuscation caused the largest recall gap: splitting digits with dashes or spelling them out cost the revised union about 20 to 29 points on development and OpenAI text, and on OpenAI the precision repairs made it worse than O (regex recall fell 34.4 points against 15.2 under dashes). Zero-width characters cost 11 points of development union recall and inflated NER false positives; lowercase and uppercase text reduced NER recall; digit spacing, full-width digits and bracketed at-signs were already neutralized. Four default-off canonicalizations were then built and scored on F1's 58,899 perturbed items: stripping zero-width characters ($+$9.11 points of union recall), rejoining split or spelled digits ($+$17.82), spelled e-mail separators ($+$30.90) and a caseless gate on NER ($+$1.30), together $+$11.77, all passing their registered tests. These passes are largely circular. Stripping restores the unperturbed result on every item by construction (zero mismatches), the digit gain comes from the dash and spelled transforms, the others having been handled already, the caseless gain is entirely in uppercase text, and the no-harm test passes because no clean sentence changed outcome. These are detector-layer transformation results, not an end-to-end attack-success rate: no adaptive attacker, intended-action denominator or receiver-observed attack outcome was evaluated, and the tests do not estimate how often these inputs occur. The documentation prerequisite registered for promotion was unmet at the frozen commit; the owner waived it, and the fixes remain off and outside K.

\textit{Latency.} In a host-process, detector-layer timing (1,000 prompts, three repeats, fresh worker per run), the combined regex and NER call took a median 27.3 ms per prompt for O, 27.5 for N and 27.76 for K against 27.77 for N in the same session. The robustness flags and the larger spaCy model changed latency by less than the run-to-run spread of about 1 ms, though the larger model raised resident memory from 387 to 1,149 MB. The transformer model added 50.1 ms at the median and 2.4 GB of memory, a 211\% increase in mean latency against O, which fails the registered 20\% criterion. This is not an installed-browser measurement.

\textit{Annotation study.} A blinded human double-annotation protocol for the contextual targets, with sampling, agreement statistics and a check of the provisional targets, has been written and its scripts tested on synthetic inputs. It has not been run, and no contextual claim depends on it.

\subsection*{Detector-layer protocol details}
\label{sec:detprec_protocol}
This detector-layer study explicitly requests regex and NER, never semantics. It compares pre-change detectors with the first revision; the later student-identifier rule and K rework are absent. The 502-row development data are split by source-group hash into dev-TUNE (267; 148 positive) and dev-CHECK (235; 116 positive); design material also includes 2,000 personalized and 1,000 OpenAI-generated rows. The post-filter used dev-TUNE and personalized data; dev-CHECK and OpenAI results for its revised version are post-review. The inputs consulted while designing regex repairs were not separately recorded, so those splits are treated as design material. The initial endpoint contains 498 rows and was scored once per configuration after defaults froze, but had already been opened in another unit; paired outcomes were later reconstructed and the endpoint is now consumed. The student rule was diagnosed after this scoring. Labels are source annotation presence, not contextual authorization.

A positive is any nonzero sensitivity or nonempty category. We report confusion counts, recall, specificity and latency for regex, NER and their union. Paired McNemar tests use discordant rows; paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 replicates, fixed seed) resample documents on real sets and rows on synthetic sets, with scenario-label sensitivity analyses (20 personalized and 10 OpenAI scenarios). No multiplicity correction is applied. Runtime action and weighted-error comparisons are separate outcomes.

\textit{Rule gates.} Kinship terms and the word ``family'' require a possessive determiner (my, our, his, her, their), optionally followed by up to two hyphen- or space-joined modifier words, so ``my ex-wife'' matches while a non-possessive self-statement does not. Child cues require a possessive and a child noun, an age expression, or an age followed by a person noun; object ages such as a five-year-old laptop do not match. Financial keywords cover personal financial-state nouns (for example salary, loan, mortgage, savings, credit score) after a possessive with up to two modifiers; a bare reference to an account does not match. Location keywords require a connector such as ``live in'' followed by a capture that is not a closed-class word. Structured identity values stay on one line and capture at most five tokens, and a phone label needs a value that begins like a number. Checksum validators reject shape-only hits for card numbers (Luhn~\cite{Luhn1960Patent}), IBANs (mod-97~\cite{ISO13616Part1_2020}), NHS numbers (mod-11) and US social security numbers (issuable area, group and serial ranges), and context matching is word-bounded rather than substring-based. Password placeholders, chat mentions and standards identifiers are excluded. A bare reference to a student is not a child cue; a separate student-identifier rule (S3 identity) needs a value of four or more characters containing a digit tied to the word student, so generic student and identifier mentions stay negative. It postdates the measurements of Section~\ref{sec:detector_precision} and Section~\ref{sec:product_rerun} and is part of N and K in Section~\ref{sec:heldout}.

\textit{Labelled identifiers and downgrades (K).} Two rules, S3 and S2 identity, match a label followed by a value. The label noun is id, identifier, number, no, num, nr, code or reference, in words, snake case, camel case or key-value form, or a hash sign directly after the qualifier. S3 qualifiers are passport, licence, national, social security or insurance, tax, identity, identification, citizenship, voter, visa, residence permit, immigration, military, birth certificate, patient, medical record, health plan, insurance, Medicare, Medicaid, beneficiary, bank account and payment card; the acronyms SSN, TFN, NINO, EIN, ITIN, MRN and NRIC need no noun, and a colon after Passport, ID card, identity card, Medicare or Medicaid labels its own value. S2 qualifiers are customer, client, account, member, subscriber, employee, staff, personnel, user, policy, claim, registration, plate, store, loyalty, library, rewards, club and gift cards, and reference. A value is tokens of letters, digits and underscores joined by hyphens or single spaces; its first token carries a digit or is a one- to three-letter prefix before a digit group, every spaced group carries a digit, it contains five to twenty-four compact characters and it is not a date. Software licence names, order, build, ticket, part and promotion codes are not qualifiers. Downgrades keep a hit whose checksum fails when a card, banking or social-security word is in context (S3) and, after an IBAN check fails without a banking word, keep a token of eight or more characters with five or more digits and a letter at S2. Because digit spaces are removed before matching, a spaced card number reaches the rule as one digit run.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 1.} Five span rules apply in turn to the output of the small English spaCy model~\cite{Honnibal2020SpaCy}, which uses capitalization as evidence for names. The placeholder rule removes masks (repeated letters, spans without letters, a pluralized capital letter, and spans inside template delimiters). The identifier rule removes e-mail, URL, underscore and code-like tokens, which belong to the regex layer. The structural rule removes spans inside headings, except multi-token or cue-introduced person names. The short-acronym rule requires a locating or affiliation preposition before a place of at most three letters or an organization of at most four. The common-phrase rule removes a single-token person candidate when the model's installed word list contains it and the tagger, reading the lowercased token in isolation, finds no proper-noun reading and no context cue corroborates it.

\textit{Post-filter, stage 2.} The first matching rule decides: (1) a person linked with a place or organization, when either side is independently corroborated, yields S2; (2) the corresponding place or organization yields S2; (3) a corroborated person yields S2; (4) a place or organization tied to the speaker by a relation cue keeps its default sensitivity; (5) one in the same prompt as such a tied entity keeps its default; (6) a facility or location directly beside a number yields S2; (7) a single uncorroborated person yields S1; and (8) a lone place or organization is dropped. Independent corroboration means evidence about the entity itself: a multi-token, cue-introduced or recurring person, or a place or organization with a personal-relation cue. A bare locating preposition does not corroborate.

\textit{Development-only ablations.} Table~\ref{tab:detprec_ablation} holds the repaired rules fixed and varies only the NER input and filter, using union recall/specificity from the source repository's experiment records. \textit{The first revision failed its recall test.} N raised regex specificity by 1.75 points and union specificity by 3.32 points on F1, but its union recall fell 1.37 points against O, and the whole interval lies below zero as well as below the $-1.0$ margin ($p=2\times10^{-6}$). The loss is larger at the BLOCK level: 190 positives moved from BLOCK to a lesser action and none moved the other way, all through the regex layer. By the registered rule N was not promotable; N was already the installed default, and the registration's stated consequence was the trade-off itself, not a revert. An exploratory exact layer swap placed the recall loss in the regex layer: keeping the old regex layer with the new NER layer gave $+$0.08 points of union recall ($-$0.43 to $+$0.61), whereas the new regex layer with the old NER layer gave $-$1.49. The rules behind the 146 positives lost through the regex layer were mainly the weakly shaped licence recognizer, IBAN and its validator, concentrated-sensitive-data, the card and social-security recognizers, and the child and family cues; the child and family hits fell mostly on text the annotation does not mark. This attribution is post hoc and uses no new data. It prompted the owner's decision to rework the regex rules that cost recall.

\textit{The rework recovered part of the loss and not all of it.} On F4, K lost 1.05 points of union recall against O (342 gained, 433 lost). The estimate sits 0.05 points beyond the margin, the interval straddles it, and H7 is inconclusive. Relative to the margin that means the data neither show a loss within 1.0 point nor show that the loss exceeds it; relative to zero, the upper limit $-0.41$ shows K is significantly worse than O on union recall (exact McNemar $p=0.0012$). For BLOCK recall, K passed H8's 0.5-percentage-point non-inferiority margin: the estimated K$-$O difference is +0.01 points (95\% CI $-0.33$ to $+0.35$); this does not establish equality. Against N, K dominates on every recall rule: 69 union gains and no losses, and 135 BLOCK gains and none lost. K restores about 43\% of N's union-recall loss on F4 ($-1.85$ for N, $-1.05$ for K) and meets the H8 margin after N's BLOCK-level loss ($-1.55$). The 5 clean F1 rows K adds against N, all from the generic labelled-identifier rule, are the cost of the new rules, and the G2 pass is a second look. By the registered rule K was not promoted, because H7 did not pass. It is nonetheless the shipped configuration: the owner decided on 25 October 2026 to ship the rework, and an independent critic reviewing the registered cells advised that if the first revision shipped, K was the better version. The critic's summary concluded that K's union-recall loss against O is real and that reverting to O would buy back about one point of union recall through both layers (in Privy mainly through O's noisy NER; the critic's corrected wording, w3-critic-3), at a cost of about three points of union specificity.

\textit{Flag rates on natural prompts.} Of 6,000 unlabelled prompts, O flagged 24.4\% (1,465), whereas N and K each flagged 16.7\% (1,001), and K flagged exactly the prompts N did. BLOCK fell from 130 prompts to 65. These are flag rates, not error rates; whether flagged prompts contain personal data awaits human adjudication, so no specificity claim is made on natural prompts. The generic labelled-identifier rule fires at S2 on technical text such as a user-identifier key and value, and its qualifier vocabulary mirrors the labels of the development spans, so its gains may not transfer to text that does not use such labels.

\textit{Spans.} Counting a prompt as detected when any rule fires overstates masking. On F1, N's union detects 80.92\% of positive sentences by that definition, yet fully masks every annotated span in only 37.16\% of them (37.31\% for O). Span-level scoring on the 502 development sentences showed the same gap (union any-hit recall 0.761, every mention masked in 107 of 264 prompts, 0.405, and 0.625 of gold characters covered). Span measures also moved differently from the presence measures: on F1, N's union overlap recall rose 1.19 points and its over-masking fell by 12.75 characters per thousand, whereas on F4, K raised gold-character coverage by 5.00 points over O but masked 10.82 more characters per thousand outside annotated spans than N. Letting NER extend masking only where the regex layer already fired recovers part of the NER span gain without creating a positive.

\textit{Full-product rerun detail.} The comparison and ablation of Section~\ref{sec:pipeline_comparison_results} were measured with the pre-change detectors. They were rerun as full-product evidence (rules, NER and semantics, actual cascade execution recorded), not as detector-layer or semantic-only results. The rerun used the same 1,560 cases, six packages, policy, rule-first ordering and one first pass per cell, and compared a clean export of the pre-change commit with the revised detectors without the student-identifier rule. The pre-change arm reproduced every historical outcome and action on all 1,560 cases for all six packages and seven layer sets, with no discordant pair, so differences arise from the detectors rather than the harness. The semantic layer is unchanged by construction, and its sole-semantic outcomes were identical in both arms. All 40,560 scored requests completed without errors. Intervals resample the 35 scenario families.



The revised detectors reduced over-restriction in every package, and the 139 unnecessary restrictions shared by the three V3 tiers fell to 108 (112 in union); 103 of the 108 lie among the original 139. The residual shows that the over-enforcement was largely, but not entirely, a rule artefact. Allowed-action agreement rose for every package, for example V3 balanced from 1,409 to 1,438 (36 cases improved, 7 regressed, $p<0.001$), and binary false positives for that package fell from 119 to 95 with false negatives unchanged at 8; no V3 tier gained a binary miss. The mechanism for V3 balanced was 36 cases moving from BLOCK to ALLOW as rule false positives disappeared (early rule BLOCKs fell from 289 to 253), against 7 clean cases moving from ALLOW to WARN because NER now emitted findings. Joint agreement did not change significantly for V3 ($p$ from 0.26 to 0.81) and fell by five for V1 balanced (2 improved, 7 regressed, $p=0.18$). Two effects cancelled: the rule repairs alone added 28 joint-correct cases without a loss, while the new NER findings altered the label set on 25 cases, each in favour of removing NER. The updated V3 packages remained ahead of historical V1 in every tier, with allowed-action gains of 388 against 5 losses for efficient, 413 against 12 for balanced and 337 against 3 for quality, compared with 389/3, 389/6 and 348/0 historically.



The central ablation result persists but shrinks. Removing rules versus the full product corrected 139 actions and lost 18 historically (net 121), and with the revised detectors corrected 103 and lost 18 (net 85, $p<10^{-14}$; family-resampled interval for the action-rate gain 2.4 to 8.9 percentage points, against 3.5 to 12.7 historically). The lost actions are again 18, and under-restriction again rises from 12 to 30, so the tradeoff between unnecessary restriction and missed protection remains. The revised detectors also change the NER findings. Historically NER produced no converted findings on this fixture and removing it left every outcome unchanged. With case-preserved input and the post-filter it converts 48 findings, all identity names: alone it flags 36 of the 810 sensitive-labelled cases and 12 of the 750 clean cases. No-NER now equals the full product on 1,526 of 1,560 cases, and removing NER improves 7 action outcomes and 25 joint outcomes with no regression. On this fixture the NER layer is therefore a net negative at the label level, which does not establish its value or harm elsewhere.

These are full-product measurements on the same assistant-provisional labels and correlated families as before. The detector changes were tuned on development and personalized sets rather than on these cases, but this partition has now been scored twice, so the rerun is not a fresh confirmation. The ablation uses one balanced package, the intervals use the family grouping only, and neither the student-identifier rule nor the later rework (K) is part of the rerun: the shipped configuration has not been run through the full product.

\textit{The result depends on the source.} Pooled F4 averages gains with a large loss in one source (Table~\ref{tab:heldout_source}, Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}). In the Privy sentences K lost 8.00 points of union recall against O (interval $-$9.57 to $-$6.41), the same loss as N ($-$8.04), so K's rework leaves it unchanged: K differs from N on one Privy row. The F1 draw showed the same Privy loss for N ($-$6.57) and a larger loss in Nemotron-PII ($-$4.86, against $-$2.35 on F4). Outside Privy, K gains 1.25 points ($+$0.59 to $+$1.92; exploratory). F4 is Privy-heavy (25\% of rows) and Nemotron-light (8\%), the mix the registration flagged as unfavourable; reweighting to the F1 mix gives $-$0.43 ($-$1.06 to $+$0.24), whereas equal source weights give $-$1.45. Exploratory analysis of the Privy losses found that 164 of 241 lost rows had old NER spans that touch no annotated span, and about 77 had a span touching an annotated one; on F1 the old union also flagged 48\% of Privy negatives against 29\% for N and K. Much of the old Privy recall is therefore indiscriminate flagging, but because the benchmark's annotation is incomplete by construction, unannotated personal data in those rows cannot be excluded, so the loss may be partly false-positive reduction and partly real misses. In exploratory strata, losses concentrate in long sentences (203 or more characters, $-$3.21 points) and in location, organization and person annotations, categories carried mainly by the NER layer, while K gains in given-name, surname, street and building-number annotations. The NER layer shared by N and K lost 4.58 points of NER-only recall against O on F4.
