# External PII training-data results

## Current status and scope

The amended source preparation and offline profile fits are complete. Live scoring through the runtime RPC is complete and aggregate-verified. Attempt v1 failed before scoring because a protobuf import was missing and produced zero scores; the preserved v2 study is at `evaluation/results/external_pii_rpc_20261004_v2/`. All 18 matched runs (17,802 prompt requests) completed with zero errors. The final partition remains unscored.

This study measures **binary annotated-PII presence**. It does not measure contextual privacy risk, sensitivity, visibility, severity, category correctness, exact span recovery, or the appropriateness of `ALLOW`/`WARN`/`BLOCK`. Empty or malformed annotations were treated as unknown unless valid positive annotation evidence was available; they were not converted to clean negatives.

## Sources and protocol

The source audit used pinned public source metadata, schemas, licenses, Parquet footer counts, and repository parser checks; it did not inspect final examples. The pinned views are Nemotron-PII `default/train` at `b70ffaf5ff39e079776134c5bf4381f00a9fd1ed` (100,000 rows, CC-BY-4.0) and Meddies PII `english/train` at `6a5c8f5441e3b421d983c9741770262365acdd77` (47,744 rows, CC-BY-NC-4.0). The Nemotron README says 50,000 train rows while the pinned train Parquet footer reports 100,000; the discrepancy is preserved in the audit record. The pinned Meddies English training view was used; mixed/BIOES derivatives were excluded. See [the source audit](../paper/research/external-pii-source-audit.md) and the [data-expansion evidence ledger](../paper/research/data-expansion-ledger.md).

The frozen protocol is [dataset-expansion-protocol.md](../paper/research/dataset-expansion-protocol.md), SHA-256 `962198b384aaed7fd5fb98e10c778b295ac6403c0dd1ba5ea376c28ec786eafe`. It retains parent Nemotron UIDs as protected groups and permits only valid positive `us`/`intl` pairs with matching source metadata. A variant row ID is derived from the parent UID and SHA-256 of canonical JSON `[locale, training_text_key(text)]`; the shared NFKC-based `training_text_key` controls global duplicate and protected-text checks. Meddies has no stable row/document ID, so its four-field metadata family is a coarse grouping proxy, not verified document identity. The protocol fixes the source sampling, group split, fitter seeds, three capacity profiles, C candidates `{1, 10}`, selection rule, and 20,000-row maximum before fitting.

The first v1 preparation used a blanket ambiguous-UID exclusion: it retained 2,993 Meddies train rows and 999 Meddies held-out rows, but zero Nemotron train rows and one held-out row (6,825 total training rows including the original 3,832). This was a filtering outcome, not a model result. A count-only audit found 50,000 two-row Nemotron UID pairs; 41,718 met the exact positive two-locale and matching-metadata rule, and 41,717 of those pairs had different normalized text keys. The protocol amendment preserves the parent UID group and permits only those verified pairs. The v2 preparation was interrupted during SQLite staging before fitting or scoring. Both attempts remain preserved; neither is evidence of model performance.

## Interpretation and limits

The experimental sparse profiles were installed and measured through live RPC for the fixed comparison, then the prior experimental presence artifacts were restored byte-for-byte and verified; the contextual artifact remained unchanged throughout. The current selected contextual model retains its earlier 90.53% recall and 29.41% specificity at a separate operating point. It does not jointly meet the development targets of at least 90% recall and specificity; those targets are not IEEE acceptance criteria. The comparison does not establish that an expanded profile should replace it.

The 968-row validation set is a fixed project selection control, not an external test. The project’s original PIIMB-derived fitting reference comes from the benchmark test population and is exploratory within-corpus evidence, not benchmark-clean training. The source-heldout corpora are synthetic or machine-generated, positive-only, and not representative evidence about real clinical records or deployment. The Meddies metadata families do not establish document independence. Final remains locked and unscored.

Meddies is CC-BY-NC-4.0 and Nemotron is CC-BY-4.0 at the pinned revisions. Retain source attribution and the non-commercial restriction. Sparse vocabularies may encode generated names or identifiers; keep all text-bearing inputs, clinical text, entity lists, exclusion/group records, and fitted vocabularies in restricted ignored experiment storage. Public documentation should contain only reviewed aggregate counts and metrics.

## Conditional next data direction

The aggregate nonsemantic bound does not rule out 90% specificity, while the observed cascade remains far below it. One possible next step is a small, prospectively fixed clean-data study using the pinned [AdvPIIBench source](https://huggingface.co/datasets/roei-ar/AdvPIIBench/blob/02741d9f99a91b8fdcf48f4316a2c73be7a7449a/README.md) (`02741d9f99a91b8fdcf48f4316a2c73be7a7449a`), but only if its labels can be reviewed against PriVoke's broader whole-prompt target. The card's negative and hard-negative classes cover five identifier types; an empty span list does not prove that a prompt contains no other sensitive information. Positive rows label attacked identifiers, so few-shot exemplars and surrounding prompt content also need an explicit inclusion and scoring policy. The card reports a USENIX Security 2026 poster and says a full paper is forthcoming; it should not be cited as a published paper.

Freeze the source, protocol, inclusion rules, and exclusion-key algorithms before accessing candidate rows. After retrieval, blinded reviewers must determine which candidates are genuinely clean under the declared whole-prompt ontology before fitting. Grouping must keep base prompts and transformation/template families together, resolve duplicate-text components and reject conflicting labels. The exclusion union must protect original train, validation and bootstrap rows; the opaque original development/final selection; and the external Nemotron/Meddies train and source-heldout groups and normalized text keys. The earlier original protected index alone does not cover the later external expansion. If sufficient reviewed negatives and disjoint groups do not remain, stop before fitting. Any resulting comparison needs a fresh grouped split and a predeclared test; the existing validation and development samples cannot be promoted to new untouched evidence.

This remains a conditional design direction: a complete pinned-source structural scan and protected-key union now exist, but broad whole-prompt labeling, fresh quota-feasible partitions, fitting, and scoring have not occurred. The observed 90/90 development target remains unmet, and final remains locked and unscored. Any future sparse-presence result must be evaluated separately from a fixed-threshold full-contextual-pipeline result; presence-only scores cannot establish the project's pipeline target. Retention and action regressions also need separately frozen contextual cases.

## Prospective clean-data study (structural scan complete; review pending)

The [prospective protocol](../paper/research/clean-augmentation-protocol.md) and the [whole-prompt labeling guide](../paper/research/clean-augmentation-labeling.md) define a single fixed comparison on the original balanced contextual pipeline. The protocol proposes preserving the original 3,832 training rows and adding at most 4,000 reviewed examples: 2,000 broad-target clean negatives and 2,000 audited positives. It separately proposes fresh grouped validation and test partitions of 2,000 rows each, with 1,000 examples per class in each partition. Negative quotas are fixed at 750 ordinary and 250 hard negatives in each validation/test partition, and 1,600 ordinary plus 400 hard negatives in training (900 hard negatives total). At least 200 distinct connected components per class are required in validation and test. These are required quotas, not observed counts; any shortfall stops the study before fitting.
